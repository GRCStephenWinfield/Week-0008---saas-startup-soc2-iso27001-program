## 1. Two populations, one register

Clause 7.5.1 defines the scope of documented information in two parts: what the standard **requires**, and
what **the organisation determines is necessary** for the effectiveness of the management system. The two
are treated identically once registered — same identification, same approval route, same retention
discipline — but they arrive by different authority, and the register records which is which.

**Fourteen items are required by ISO/IEC 27001:2022. CloudNimbus has determined a further twenty-three
necessary. Thirty-seven in total.** `CNB-C-145` is the control that operates the register.

The second population is where the judgement sits. A register of only the fourteen omits the subordinate
policies, the control library and the inventories every control depends on; a register of everything the
company has written dilutes the fourteen it exists to protect. The test applied is **whether an unapproved
change to the item, or its loss, would impair the management system** — which admits the control library
and the asset inventory and excludes runbooks, agendas and the engineering handbook.

## 2. Clause 7.5.2 — creating and updating

Three requirements, each answered by a mechanism.

**Identification and description.** Every item carries a `DI-nn` identifier, a title, an individual owner,
a basis and a version; programme narrative documents also carry the `CNB-TRUST-2026-NNN` identifier and the
classification line. **Identifiers are not reused**: a withdrawn item keeps its number, because a reused
identifier makes a historical reference silently wrong.

**Format and media.** All thirty-seven are Markdown or structured data in version-controlled repositories,
rendered to PDF only at external release. Formats that carry their change history in a proprietary binary
make the *system* the record of change rather than the *document*, and the record then cannot be produced
separately from the tool.

**Review and approval for suitability and adequacy.** Every item names an approver distinct from its owner,
and approval is recorded against a specific version, on a date, by a named person — not as a standing
authority to publish.

## 3. Clause 7.5.3 — control of documented information

Clause 7.5.3 requires documented information to be **available and suitable for use where and when
needed** and **adequately protected**, and names **four** activities to be addressed as applicable —
c) distribution, access, retrieval and use; d) storage and preservation, including preservation of
legibility; e) control of changes; f) retention and disposition.

| Limb | How CloudNimbus addresses it |
|---|---|
| **c) Distribution, access, retrieval and use** | The policy suite is readable by all 187 staff without a login wall; the risk register, Statement of Applicability and evidence register are restricted by role. Every item is retrievable by identifier and the register is the index |
| **d) Storage and preservation, including preservation of legibility** | Plain-text formats in version control, replicated by the hosting provider, with a quarterly repository export into the evidence store. Legibility is preserved by format choice, not by migration promises |
| **e) Control of changes** | A change is a pull request, reviewed by the named approver, effective only on merge. The merge record carries who approved, what changed and when. See section 4 |
| **f) Retention and disposition** | Every row states a retention period. Disposition is by withdrawal, not deletion: the item is marked withdrawn with a date and reason and its versions remain |

**Documented information of external origin is a separate requirement, not a fifth activity.** Clause 7.5.3
states it in its own sentence: documented information of external origin determined by the organisation to
be necessary for the planning and operation of the ISMS shall be **identified as appropriate and
controlled**. It is not conditioned by "as applicable" in the way the four activities are, and folding it
into the list makes it look like one more thing that might not apply.

| Separate requirement | How CloudNimbus addresses it |
|---|---|
| **Documented information of external origin** | Identified in the register as external, held read-only, with a named individual responsible for noticing a revision. Six items; see section 5 |

## 4. The limb most often satisfied badly

**Clause 7.5.3's "control of changes" is the limb that gets a tick it has not earned**, and it gets it
because the phrase "such as version control" appears in the standard. An organisation with a document store
showing "version 7, edited by J. Smith, 14 March" believes it has controlled its changes. It has recorded
them.

**A document store with version history is not the same as a document whose changes were reviewed and
approved before they took effect.** The difference is the order of two events. In the first, the change is
live from the moment it is saved and the history is a trail of what has already happened to a document
people are relying on. In the second, the change is a proposal until an approver acts, and the approval is
what makes it effective. Both produce a version history. Only the second produces one in which every
version was approved.

The consequence is not theoretical. An engineer corrects what they believe is an error in a standard — a
threshold, a retention period, a severity definition — the store dutifully records version 8, and six weeks
later a control is being operated against a rule nobody with authority over it agreed to. The auditor's
question is not *"can you show me the version history?"* but *"who approved this version, and was that
before or after it took effect?"* — and in the first arrangement that question has no answer.

CloudNimbus's arrangement makes the answer mechanical. **A change to any of the thirty-seven is a pull
request; the named approver's approval is the merge condition; the merge is the effective date.** Approval,
content and effective date are one record and cannot come apart, because no route reaches the main branch
without passing the approval. That is `CNB-C-145`, evidence class **EC-24**.

**The ML-1 lesson applies here too.** The 2025 management letter recorded access review evidence retained
as email threads and screenshots — a control that worked and left nothing a sampler could use. Documented
information has the same failure available to it: a policy that exists, is correct, is being followed, and
cannot be produced in a form that shows when its current version became current. **Documented information
that cannot be produced in the form a sampler needs is documented information that will fail a test**, and
it will fail while being substantively right.

## 7. Three notes on the inventory

**The programme narrative documents are not separate rows.** Phases 01 to 09 produce numbered documents,
each version-controlled, owned and approved; they are controlled **as a repository** under POL-19 rather
than enumerated, and the items above that live in them are named by document reference.

**Retention of seven years is a decision, not an inherited default.** It covers the examination period, the
three-year certification cycle and a reasonable look-back for a user entity, and POL-19 states that it is
not a legal retention determination. It coexists with, and does not override, RT-01 to RT-08, which govern
**customer and personal data** rather than management-system records.

**The Statement of Applicability, DI-05, is the item with the most movement.** Issued at v1.0 on 2026-04-24
with status stated as 62 implemented, 21 partially implemented and 8 planned, it was **reissued on
2026-06-15** when the ISMS was declared operational, under DEC-412. An item whose content is expected to
change is exactly where "approved before it took effect" earns its keep.
