## 1. Fifteen controls the SOC 2 side of the programme gets no credit for

`CNB-C-134` to `CNB-C-148` are the fifteen controls whose **Criteria column carries a dash**. They serve no
trust services criterion, will not appear in Section IV of the examination report, and would not exist in
this form if CloudNimbus were pursuing SOC 2 alone.

They divide two ways, and the division is the chapter's point.

| Group | Count | What it is | Criteria | Annex A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unmapped Annex A** | **9** | Controls implementing an Annex A control that corresponds to no criterion | `—` | Populated |
| **ISMS machinery** | **6** | Controls implementing a requirement of clauses 4 to 10 for which **Annex A contains no control at all** | `—` | `—` |

Nine plus six is fifteen, and the six are the more interesting half. A row with both trailing columns empty looks like a data-entry error. It is not: it records that the
requirement being implemented is a **clause**, and that the reference set of controls in Annex A does not
reach it.

leftovers of the four controls argued for exclusion and refused.** Exactly one, A.7.13, came from that
argument; two of the most conspicuous — A.5.6 and A.8.23 — were kept without any proposal for exclusion
ever reaching the table, which is the opposite of having survived one.

Five of these deserve a sentence each, because their justification is not obvious from the row — and two of
the five because the row is weaker than the list it sits in.

**`CNB-C-136` and A.5.30 — the weakest member of the nine.** A.5.30 requires ICT readiness for business
continuity to be **planned, implemented, maintained and tested** against continuity objectives derived from
a business impact analysis. `CNB-C-136` carries the planning limb: requirements expressed per service tier
as recovery time and recovery point objectives, re-derived annually and on a tier change. It carries no
testing limb, and until this issue it was the only row citing A.5.30 — which meant the library asserted
implementation of a control whose testing requirement nothing implemented. The testing limb is
**`CNB-C-100`**, the annual disaster recovery exercise that fails the platform to `us-west-2` and measures
achieved recovery time and recovery point against the four-hour and fifteen-minute objectives. It now cites
A.5.30 alongside A.5.29.

That correction has a consequence for this chapter's own framing, and it is better stated than left to be
noticed. **A.5.30 is now implemented in part by a criteria-bearing control** — `CNB-C-100` serves A1.3 —
which makes it the weakest member of the nine unmapped Annex A controls. 03.12 §5.1 said as much in
advance: it named A.5.30 as "the contestable one" and "the member of the nine most open to challenge", and
kept it unmapped on the basis that A.5.30's management-system activity **sits alongside but does not
coincide with** A1.2 and A1.3. That reasoning still holds for the planning limb. It holds much less well for
the testing limb, because the exercise that tests ICT readiness and the exercise A1.3 asks about are the
same exercise producing the same record. The programme keeps A.5.30 in the nine and records here that one
of its two limbs is now indistinguishable from a mapped control.

**`CNB-C-138` and A.7.13 — a pair that was a duplicate and is not now.** As first drafted, `CNB-C-138` and
`CNB-C-062` were the same mechanism written twice: mobile device management enforcing a monthly baseline on
the 231 managed devices, with a fourteen-day threshold and loss of access. The control change log records
nine draft duplicates removed for exactly that reason, and this pair survived the sweep. It mattered more
than an ordinary duplicate would, because this chapter and 03.12 both present A.7.13 as mapping to no
criterion — while the identical mechanism in `CNB-C-062` maps to CC6.6 and CC7.1. A claim that an Annex A
control has no criterion behind it cannot rest on a row that duplicates one that does.

The two are now differentiated by subject rather than by wording. **`CNB-C-062` is patch deployment and
remediation targets**: operating system and third-party application patches on the monthly cycle, Critical
remediated inside fourteen days, compliance signal lost past target. **`CNB-C-138` is hardware maintenance
and lifecycle**, which is what A.7.13 equipment maintenance actually concerns: firmware and hardware
maintenance, battery and storage health, repair and replacement through the managed service, and withdrawal
of devices past end of support. **The maintenance limb is genuinely separate from the patching limb** — a
laptop with a failing battery and a swollen cell is fully patched, and a device four operating system
versions behind is in perfect physical health — which is why the pair is not a duplicate now. It was one
when it was drafted, and the differentiation is a correction rather than a design that was always this way.

Three more deserve a sentence each.

**`CNB-C-139` — clock synchronization.** A.8.17 is the control most often dismissed as housekeeping. Here it
is evidential infrastructure: the calculation engine derives paid hours from the difference between two
timestamps, so clock drift is not a logging inconvenience, it is a wrong number.

**`CNB-C-142` — protection of information systems during audit testing.** A.8.34 has no counterpart because
the criteria do not concern themselves with the disruption an audit causes. CloudNimbus will be audited and
tested repeatedly — penetration testing, internal audit, the certification body's fieldwork, the service
auditor's procedures — and each is an outsider touching production with credentials. The control is the
authorisation record agreed before testing starts and the revocation that closes it.

**`CNB-C-137` — intellectual property rights.** A.5.32 is justified in the Statement of Applicability as a
contractual and legal requirement rather than from the register, and the control makes the mechanics plain:
the licence list is the authority, the software bill of materials the population, the build gate the
enforcement. **Whether a licence obligation has been met in law is not a question this control answers**,
which is why the annual review is held with the General Counsel.

## 3. The six — requirements Annex A does not contain

**One substitution from Phase 03 is made here rather than silently.** 03.12 §5.3 and 01.06 §4 listed the
six as the clause 9.2 internal audit programme, the clause 9.3 management review, **Statement of
Applicability maintenance under clause 6.1.3**, the clause 6.1.2 risk assessment process, clause 7.5
documented information control and the clause 10.2 corrective action process. This library substitutes the
**clause 6.2 objectives** for Statement of Applicability maintenance, and keeps the count at six.

The substitution is the better cut, for a reason that is about where a requirement lives rather than about
tidiness. **Maintaining the Statement of Applicability is an activity of the 6.1.2 and 6.1.3 process, not a
separate requirement** — clause 6.1.3 d) requires the SoA to be produced and clause 6.1.3 requires the
treatment process to be applied, so a control that operates the process and reissues the SoA when a
determination changes is one control, and that is `CNB-C-143`. **Clause 6.2 objectives are a requirement in
their own right**, with their own limbs — measurable where practicable, monitored, communicated, updated,
and supported by a plan — and Annex A contains no objectives control, which is exactly the property that
puts a row in this half of the chapter. Splitting the SoA out would have produced two rows over one
process; leaving 6.2 out would have left a clause requirement with no control against it.

### 3.1 Why both columns are empty

Take `CNB-C-146`. Search Annex A for an internal audit control and there is nothing: A.5.35 requires an
independent review of the approach to managing information security, a different activity with a different
scope and no programme, plan, criteria or auditor-competence requirement attached. The internal audit
obligation lives in **clause 9.2**, alongside the requirements that the programme consider the importance
of the processes concerned, that auditors be objective and impartial, and that results be reported to
relevant management.

The same is true of the other five. **Clause 6.1.2** requires a defined risk assessment process producing
consistent, valid and comparable results; Annex A has no risk assessment control, and 03.12 called that the
clearest structural demonstration available that the annex is a reference set. **Clause 6.2** requires
objectives that are measurable where practicable, monitored, communicated and updated, and supported by a
plan; Annex A has no objectives control. **Clause 7.5** requires documented information to be identified,
formatted, reviewed, approved, distributed, protected, version-controlled and retained; Annex A has A.5.33
protection of records and A.5.37 documented operating procedures, and neither is that. **Clause 9.3**
requires a management review with **seven** named inputs, a) to g), and specified outputs; Annex A has no
management review control. **Clause 10.2** requires reaction, evaluation of cause, corrective action and evaluation of
effectiveness; A.5.27 learning from information security incidents is about incidents, not about
nonconformities of the management system.

**Three of the six have a twin in the Common Criteria families, and the distinction between them is thinner
than the three above.** It is worth setting out honestly, because a sampler who draws both members of a
pair and receives the same artefact has found a duplicate however the rows are worded.

| Pair | What a sampler receives for the CC row | What a sampler receives for the ISO row | Is the sampling unit different? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Risk process — `CNB-C-017`, `CNB-C-018` against `CNB-C-143` | One quarterly register review: the register snapshot, the entries re-tested against the movement rules, and each risk owner's confirmation of rating, treatment decision and status | One quarterly execution of the documented **process**: the same CAL-06 record, read for whether the process defined under clauses 6.1.2 and 6.1.3 was followed, plus the Statement of Applicability reissue decision where a determination changed | **Partly.** The SoA reissue decision is a distinct artefact. The register snapshot and the owner confirmations are the same record read twice |
| Objectives — `CNB-C-016` against `CNB-C-144` | One annual objective-setting record: OBJ-01 to OBJ-08 with owner, measure and target, minuted at the Trust Committee | One quarterly report of each objective's measure read from the evidence store, with the withdraw-or-remeasure decision minuted | **Yes.** Setting an objective and evaluating its measure are different acts, at different cadences, producing different records |
| Corrective action — `CNB-C-025` against `CNB-C-148` | One corrective action tracker item raised by a monitoring activity: owner, root cause, due date, and its appearance on the monthly past-due report | One improvement register record: the immediate reaction, the cause analysis, the corrective action, the accountable person, the due date and the **effectiveness check** that gates closure | **Partly.** The effectiveness check is a distinct artefact; the item, owner and due date are the same record |

So one of the three — the objectives pair — is genuinely two mechanisms, and **two of the three are not**.
`CNB-C-143` and `CNB-C-017`/`CNB-C-018` draw the same CAL-06 record, and `CNB-C-148` and `CNB-C-025` draw
the same tracker item; what differs is one additional field or decision on the same artefact. That is a
**management-system-process versus outcome distinction**: the CC row asks whether the activity happened and
produced its result, the ISO row asks whether the defined process was followed and closed properly. It is a
real distinction and it is thinner than the distinctions drawn against A.5.35 and A.5.27 above, where the
Annex A control is about a different subject entirely.

Two consequences follow and both are stated rather than left. **A deviation on the shared artefact lands on
both rows**, so the library's redundancy here is smaller than the row count suggests. And **the sampler
should be told the artefact is shared** rather than discovering it — which is why the evidence architecture
puts `CNB-C-017`, `CNB-C-018`, `CNB-C-143`, `CNB-C-025` and `CNB-C-148` in EC-23 together instead of
inventing separate classes that would have concealed the overlap.

### 3.2 The argument this chapter exists to make

**Annex A is not the requirements.** That sentence appears in the fact base, in Phase 01, in Phase 03's
Statement of Applicability methodology, and it is easy to nod at and hard to see the consequences of. These
six rows are the consequence, and they make the point better than any restatement of clause 6.1.3 c) can.

Consider an organisation that builds its information security management system the way most first-time
certifications are attempted: open Annex A, work down the ninety-three controls, implement each one, write
a Statement of Applicability, book the audit. That organisation ends up with a genuinely creditable set of
controls. It has access control, cryptography, logging, backup, supplier management, secure development and
physical security. What it does not have is:

- a **defined risk assessment process** — because no Annex A control asks for one;
- **information security objectives** with measures and owners — because no Annex A control asks for them;
- **control of documented information** in the clause 7.5 sense — because no Annex A control asks for it;
- an **internal audit programme** — because no Annex A control asks for one;
- a **management review** — because no Annex A control asks for one;
- a **nonconformity and corrective action process** — because no Annex A control asks for one.

It therefore has no management system at all. It has a control set. **Clauses 4 to 10 are the difference**,
and a certification audit is conducted against those clauses, with Annex A reached only through clause
6.1.3 c) as a comparison verifying that no necessary control has been omitted. An organisation in that
position does not fail on a technicality: it fails because the thing being certified — a management system
— was never built, and the six most likely findings against it are the six rows above.

The inverse holds too, and it is why these six sit in a **control library** rather than in a procedure
manual. Clause requirements not expressed as controls with an owner, a cadence and a declared evidence
artefact do not get operated; they get intended. The library treats "the management review happens" with
the same machinery it applies to "the nightly calculation run writes a reconciliation record". **A
requirement without an operating cadence is a requirement nobody has scheduled**, and clauses 9.2 and 9.3
are the two most commonly discovered, in the fourth quarter, to have been nobody's diary entry.

### 3.3 What these six do for the other framework, indirectly

None of the six serves a criterion, and the library does not pretend otherwise by mapping them. But 01.11
made a related observation that survives here: a functioning internal audit and a functioning management
review are **monitoring activities of the kind CC4.1 and CC4.2 contemplate**, and evidence produced by
`CNB-C-146` and `CNB-C-147` will be of interest to the service auditor even though the controls themselves
are not in Section IV.

That is a statement about evidence, not about mapping, and the distinction is the discipline of
[04.03](04.03-mapping-methodology-and-its-limits.md). Citing CC4.1 against `CNB-C-146` would assert that
the criterion requires an ISO internal audit programme, which it does not. **The first is a mapping error;
the second is the integration dividend the programme was designed to collect.**

**Five of the fifteen operate annually**, which in a six-month window means a population of one or zero.
Three — `CNB-C-141`, `CNB-C-146` and `CNB-C-147` — fall inside the window as they stand, the last two
because CAL-14 and CAL-15 are placed in 2026-09. `CNB-C-136` and `CNB-C-137` do not, and are two of the
**ten** annual controls across the library being re-scheduled into the window and disclosed. That is dealt
with in [04.11](04.11-control-ownership-and-operating-cadence.md) rather than glossed here.

**No control in this chapter is owned by a function.** `CNB-C-147` is owned by Elise Fontaine personally,
because clause 9.3 is a top-management obligation and assigning it to "the executive team" is an assignment
to nobody. ADR-0020 records the rule, and the ISMS machinery is its sharpest application: this is exactly
where a library drifts into naming committees, and a committee cannot be asked in an interview what it did
last quarter.
