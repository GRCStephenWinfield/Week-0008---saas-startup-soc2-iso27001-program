## 1. What this chapter carries

`CNB-C-094` to `CNB-C-133` — **forty controls** covering the four trust services categories that sit
outside the Common Criteria: **Availability (9)**, **Processing Integrity (11)**, **Confidentiality (5)**
and **Privacy (15)**. The Common Criteria are in
[04.04](04.04-controls-for-the-common-criteria-cc1-to-cc5.md) and
[04.05](04.05-controls-for-the-common-criteria-cc6-to-cc9.md); the ISO-only controls are in
[04.07](04.07-iso-only-controls-and-isms-machinery.md).

These four categories have **never been examined**. The 2025 Type I covered Security alone, so every row
below is a control whose operating effectiveness no service auditor has ever tested and whose design no
service auditor has ever opined on. That is the honest starting position, and it is why this chapter is
written control by control rather than category by category.

**Thirty-two of the forty are dual** — they serve a trust services criterion and implement one or more of
the 91 Annex A controls determined necessary. **Eight are SOC 2-only**: four in Processing Integrity and
four in Privacy, for substantially the reason 03.12 §5.2 gave in advance. No Annex A control asks whether a
derived amount is arithmetically correct, and Annex A treats the protection of personally identifiable
information as one control, A.5.34, facing eighteen Privacy criteria. Where a control's subject matter has
no Annex A counterpart it has nothing to cite, and the row says so with a dash rather than by reaching for
the nearest plausible Annex A number. **A mapping made to avoid an empty cell is the worst kind of
mapping**, because it is invisible: it looks like coverage.

One statement 03.12 made, and this chapter previously repeated, does not survive contact with the rows: a
criterion is not SOC 2-only because *some* control serving it has nothing to cite. Eleven of the seventeen
criteria 03.12 recorded as having no Annex A analogue are, in this library, served by at least one control
that does cite Annex A. §3 and §5 below say which, and 04.03 §4.1 reconciles the divergence.

## 2. Availability — `CNB-C-094` to `CNB-C-102`

The commitment is **99.9% monthly** (SC-01), the recovery objectives are **RTO 4 hours and RPO 15 minutes**
(SR-10), and the constraint that shapes the design is obligation **O8**: personal data of the 41
EU-residency customers stays at rest in `eu-central-1`, so `eu-central-1` recovers **intra-region across
availability zones only**. A control set that quietly assumed cross-region recovery everywhere would
contradict a contractual commitment, which is why `CNB-C-099` names region pinning in the same breath as
multi-availability-zone placement.

Nine controls is not many for an availability category, and the reason is the carve-out. **AWS performs the
environmental protections, the facility power and the physical redundancy** that A1.2 contemplates, and
those are disclosed as **CSOC-01** (physical and environmental protections at the facilities supporting the
three regions), **CSOC-05** (redundancy of power, cooling and network at region and availability-zone
level) and **CSOC-08** (durability and integrity of stored objects, snapshots and configured cross-region
replication) rather than claimed here. The other eight of the eleven AWS complementary controls concern
different subjects — physical and logical access restriction, tenant isolation of the virtualisation and
managed service planes, key material protection, infrastructure patching, control-plane integrity, AWS
personnel access, and incident notification — and citing the whole range against availability would
overstate what the carve-out disclosed. What remains in CloudNimbus's hands is capacity, backup, recovery
configuration, recovery testing and incident correction — and all five are in the table.

## 3. Processing Integrity — `CNB-C-103` to `CNB-C-113`

**This is where the product is.** The calculation engine derives overtime, differential, accrual balances
and reimbursement amounts for roughly 1.24 million end users, and the output leaves CloudNimbus as an
export to a payroll provider. A wrong number here is a wrong paycheque, and it is a wrong paycheque that
CloudNimbus's own software produced from data the individual supplied directly. Processing Integrity in
most SaaS examinations is a formality bolted on to satisfy a questionnaire. Here it is the thing the
company sells.

That has a consequence for how the controls are written. **The commonest defect in a Processing Integrity
control library is a row that restates PI1.3** — "processing is complete, accurate and timely" — and calls
itself a control. Every row below names a mechanism and states what happens when the mechanism fires.
`CNB-C-107` is the model: the reconciliation record is an artefact, the tolerance is a threshold, and the
block on export is an observable consequence with a timestamp.

**Four of the eleven are SOC 2-only** — `CNB-C-103`, `CNB-C-107`, `CNB-C-108` and `CNB-C-112` — and the
claim that goes with them has to be made more carefully than 03.12 §5.2 made it, because this chapter's own
rows contradict the blunt version.

**PI1.1 and PI1.5 are principally SOC 2-only, not wholly.** PI1.1 is served by `CNB-C-103`, which cites
nothing, and by `CNB-C-104`, which cites **A.8.26** — and that citation reaches only the part of `CNB-C-104`
that validates a submitted rule set against the published schema and refuses what fails it. That is input
validation. It is not a statement that Annex A asks whether the rule catalogue is complete or its
definitions current, which is what PI1.1 is about and what `CNB-C-103` answers with nothing to cite. PI1.5
has the same shape: `CNB-C-112` cites nothing, and `CNB-C-113` cites **A.8.26** for the constraint-set
conformance check, which is output and storage validation and reaches no further. **PI1.3** is served by
`CNB-C-107` and `CNB-C-108`, which cite nothing, and by `CNB-C-109`, which cites **A.5.33** for the
immutability of the run record and its pinned input snapshot — protection of a record from falsification,
which is a genuinely different claim from the timeliness and completeness of the processing itself.

What remains true, and is the point worth keeping, is narrower than "no Annex A analogue at all": **no Annex
A control asks whether a derived amount is arithmetically correct**, and the reconciliation that would
detect an error in a derivation has no counterpart in the annex. Where a row in this family cites A.8.26 or
A.5.33, the citation reaches validation or record protection and stops there. Writing "no analogue" when
three rows in the table below carry one is the kind of claim a reader checks in ninety seconds, and the
correction costs less than the credibility does.

**PI1.5 is served by two detective controls and no preventive one, and that is deliberate rather than an
omission.** Storage completeness and accuracy is asserted by the database engine's constraints, which are
configuration and not an operating occurrence — a constraint does not "run" on a date a sampler can select.
What the library records instead are the two checks that confirm the constraints are present and that the
stored population reconciles to the ledger of runs. Recording the constraint itself as a control with a
cadence would have produced a row that cannot be sampled, and a row that cannot be sampled is worse than no
row, because it consumes coverage without providing it.

**CUEC-04, CUEC-05 and CUEC-09 sit against this family** and are not controls of CloudNimbus's. The
employer approves its own pay rules before activation, validates the export against its own records before
submitting it to its payroll provider, and maintains the accuracy of the employment data it supplies.
`CNB-C-104` records the tenant approver; it does not perform the tenant's review.

chapter — a row-level security predicate at the data layer with an automated isolation suite gating

**C1.2 is served by exactly one control, and that is a stated concentration rather than an accident.**
Disposal of confidential information at the end of a customer relationship happens in one place at
CloudNimbus — the deletion orchestrator of DF-11, which produces the certificate obligation O7 and
commitment SC-03 require. There is no second, independent path by which customer data is disposed of, so
inventing a second control to serve C1.2 would have been a documentation exercise rather than a control
design. The consequence is set out plainly in [04.03](04.03-mapping-methodology-and-its-limits.md): if
`CNB-C-118` has a deviation, **C1.2 has nothing behind it**, and there is no compensating control to argue
from. The population is whatever the period's terminations produce — it is not forecast here, because the
window has not opened and a number stated in advance would be an outcome about a period that does not
exist. 04.12 treats EC-18 the same way: the class will hold as many units as there are terminated tenants.
What can be said now is the shape of the exposure rather than its size: a small population on a single
control means every occurrence matters, and one late certificate is a visible proportion of whatever the
population turns out to be.

## 5. Privacy — `CNB-C-119` to `CNB-C-133`

Fifteen controls serve **eighteen criteria**, which is the tightest ratio in the library and reflects what
the Privacy category actually is: a set of commitments to individuals, most of which are discharged by the
same small number of mechanisms operating at different points in the data lifecycle. The programme resisted
the alternative — one control per criterion, eighteen rows, several of which would have been the same
mechanism described in different words — because a library that inflates to match a criteria count is a
library that cannot be operated.

CloudNimbus is in a **notice-and-choice position it cannot wholly delegate to the employer**: seven of the
twelve personal-data categories are collected directly from the individual, and three of those arise on
every authenticated session irrespective of any tenant configuration. That is the finding that carried
ADR-0002, and it is why `CNB-C-119` and `CNB-C-121` exist as CloudNimbus's own controls rather than as
CUEC-07 pointed at the employer.

**Four of the fifteen are SOC 2-only** — `CNB-C-119`, `CNB-C-120`, `CNB-C-121` and `CNB-C-122`. They serve
P1.1, P2.1, P3.2 and P8.1 between them: notice, choice, explicit consent and the complaint and inquiry
process. But **two of those four criteria are also served by rows that do cite Annex A**, and saying
otherwise would be wrong on the face of the table below.

**P2.1** is served by `CNB-C-119`, which cites nothing, and by `CNB-C-123`, which cites **A.5.34**: optional
collections are enabled per tenant against a recorded administrator, date and stated purpose, and A.5.34
privacy and protection of PII reaches the recording of a purpose for a collection. **P8.1** is served by
`CNB-C-122`, which cites nothing, and by `CNB-C-133`, which cites **A.5.34** and **A.5.36** — the quarterly
Data Protection Officer review that reads the disclosure register, privacy incidents and open cases
together and minutes the outcome, which is compliance with policies and standards for information security
as much as it is privacy.

**Only P1.1 and P3.2 are served exclusively by controls with no Annex A citation.** P1.1 has `CNB-C-119` and
`CNB-C-120`; P3.2 has `CNB-C-121` alone. Those two are the genuine cases, and they are where the underlying
observation holds: A.5.34 is one Annex A control facing eighteen Privacy criteria, and it treats the
protection of personal information as a security matter rather than as a set of commitments made to an
individual. Notice content and explicit consent are commitments; Annex A has no control that asks whether
one was given. This phase already made this correction for **P7.1** and stopped one criterion short of
making it for P2.1 and P8.1.

### 5.1 P3.2, and a line this programme does not cross

**P3.2 is served by exactly one control**, `CNB-C-121`, and the reason is the same as C1.2's: there is one
mechanism, and it operates in one place. Geolocation at clock-in (PD-05) is the only collection CloudNimbus
treats as requiring the individual's explicit permission before the value is captured, and the mobile
client is the only path by which it is captured.

The wording of that treatment matters. CloudNimbus treats geolocation as requiring explicit consent
**because it has committed to do so**, not because this document has determined that the data falls into
any statutorily defined class. Whether a location point, or the "Medical / sick" value on the controlled
leave-type list, constitutes a special category, sensitive personal information or any other defined class
under any named statute is a legal determination that belongs to the General Counsel and to counsel
advising the customer, and neither this control library nor the report it will support reaches it. The
control does something narrower and verifiable: it asks, it records the answer with a timestamp, and it
honours a refusal without degrading the service the individual came to use.

### 5.2 The residue that is disclosed rather than engineered away

`CNB-C-128` exists because **RT-07 and RT-08 conflict**: backups are retained 35 days rolling, deletion on
termination completes inside 30 days, and 30 + 35 = **65 days of residue** in which a deleted record still
exists in a backup that has not yet aged out. Phase 02 disclosed that rather than engineering it away, and
the control follows the disclosure: the residue window is tracked, its expiry is confirmed, and the number
appears on the certificate the customer receives. A privacy control set that claimed deletion was
instantaneous everywhere would be making a claim the backup schedule contradicts, and the first competent
question about backups would find it.

Every row names an owner who is a person. Every row cites exactly one policy. Every row has a cadence that
determines the population a sampler will draw from, and thirteen of the forty operate quarterly,
semi-annually or annually, where that population is two, one, or one-or-zero. Those three facts are the subject of
[04.11](04.11-control-ownership-and-operating-cadence.md), and they are the difference between a control
library and a list of good intentions.
