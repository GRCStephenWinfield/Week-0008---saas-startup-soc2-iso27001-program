## 1. What clause 5.2 asks for, and what it does not

Clause 5.2 requires top management to establish an information security policy that:

- is **appropriate to the purpose** of the organisation;
- **includes information security objectives**, or provides the **framework for setting** them;
- includes a **commitment to satisfy applicable requirements** related to information security;
- includes a **commitment to continual improvement** of the information security management system.

And it requires that the policy:

- is **available as documented information**;
- is **communicated within** the organisation;
- is **available to interested parties, as appropriate**.

Seven limbs. **POL-01 was approved by Elise Fontaine on 2026-06-12** under DEC-411, as the only policy
approved by the Chief Executive Officer as top management under clause 5.1. This chapter walks each limb,
quotes the POL-01 text that answers it, and then makes an argument the clause itself cannot make.

## 2. Limb by limb

### 2.1 Appropriate to the purpose of the organisation

> *"CloudNimbus operates a workforce platform on which 640 employers depend to record what their people
> worked and to calculate what they are owed. Approximately 1.24 million individuals hand us a clock-in, a
> device identifier and an authentication trail on every shift, and most of them never chose us — their
> employer did. A failure of confidentiality here exposes what people are paid. A failure of processing
> integrity puts a wrong number on a payslip at scale. A failure of availability at a payroll cut-off is a
> payroll run missed. Information security at CloudNimbus is therefore not a support function of the
> business; it is a condition of the business being permitted to exist. This policy states what is required
> of every person here, who decides when a requirement is unclear, and what to do when it conflicts with
> getting the work done."*
> — POL-01 §1

**The test of this limb is whether the paragraph could be lifted into another company's policy unchanged.**
It could not. It names the customer count, the end-user population, the fact that those end users are not
CloudNimbus's customers, and the three failure modes the business actually has. *"CloudNimbus is committed
to protecting the confidentiality, integrity and availability of its information assets"* is true, is
appropriate to the purpose of every organisation that has ever existed, and is therefore appropriate to the
purpose of none.

### 2.2 Includes objectives, or the framework for setting them

POL-01 takes the framework route rather than embedding the objectives, and says so:

> *"Information security objectives are set at least annually, proposed by the VP Security &amp; Trust,
> reviewed by the Trust Committee and approved by the Chief Executive Officer. Each objective is recorded
> as OBJ-nn with a statement, a measure that can be read from evidence rather than asserted in a status
> report, a target, and a named individual owner. Progress is reported at every Trust Committee meeting and
> quarterly to the Audit &amp; Risk Committee. An objective whose measure cannot be evaluated is given one
> or withdrawn. The objectives in force are OBJ-01 to OBJ-08, published in the programme charter and not
> reproduced here, so that a change to an objective does not require a change to this policy."*
> — POL-01 §4

The last clause is the design decision. **Embedding the objectives couples the two documents' change
cycles**: a target that moves in March forces a re-approval of the clause 5.2 policy by the Chief Executive
Officer, and the predictable consequence is that targets stop moving. The framework route keeps the
authority in POL-01 and the content in the charter; `CNB-C-144` operates it.

### 2.3 Commitment to satisfy applicable requirements

> *"CloudNimbus commits to satisfying the information security requirements that apply to it: the
> commitments made to customers and recorded as SC-01 to SC-10; the contractual and engagement obligations
> recorded as O1 to O12; the requirements of interested parties determined under clause 4.2; and the legal
> and regulatory requirements identified and maintained by the General Counsel in the applicable
> requirements list. Where an applicable
> requirement is identified and cannot presently be met, it is recorded as a gap with a named owner and a
> date, reported to the Trust Committee, and is not restated as a commitment."*
> — POL-01 §5

**The control behind this limb is `CNB-C-014`**, which maintains the O1 to O12 obligations register and the
applicable requirements list, reviewed annually and on change, and which implements **A.5.31**. A policy
that commits to satisfying applicable requirements without a control that identifies them is committing to
satisfy a set nobody maintains.

The final sentence is there because of **O1**. The enterprise master services agreement obliges CloudNimbus
to maintain a current SOC 2 Type II report and CloudNimbus has never held one, meeting the obligation with
a Security-only Type I and a customer-by-customer conversation. The programme records that as an open
contractual gap and does not characterise it as a breach, because that is a legal conclusion. **A policy that committed to satisfying all applicable requirements without
providing for the case where one is not yet satisfied would have been contradicted by its own organisation
on the day it was signed.**

### 2.4 Commitment to continual improvement of the ISMS

> *"CloudNimbus commits to the continual improvement of the information security management system itself,
> and not only of the controls inside it. Nonconformities, control failures, audit findings, incidents and
> opportunities for improvement are recorded in the improvement register, analysed for cause, and closed
> only when the effectiveness of the corrective action has been evaluated. The management review examines
> whether the management system is improving, and records that judgement in its outputs."*
> — POL-01 §6

**"The management system itself, and not only the controls inside it"** is the part that matters. A
commitment to improving controls is a commitment to doing more of what the organisation already does; a
commitment to improving the management system admits the possibility that the risk process, the audit
programme or the review cadence is what needs to change. `CNB-C-148` operates the register and `CNB-C-147`
the review.

### 2.5 Available as documented information

POL-01 is **DI-02** in the clause 7.5 inventory at
[04.10](04.10-documented-information-clause-7-5.md): the policy repository, version 1.0, approved
2026-06-12 by Elise Fontaine, owned by Karim Haddad, reviewed annually under CAL-13, retained for the life
of the ISMS plus seven years. Changes take effect through `CNB-C-145` — approved before effective, not
recorded afterwards.

### 2.6 Communicated within the organisation

> *"Every person joining CloudNimbus acknowledges this policy within 14 days of their start date as part of
> onboarding, and every person re-acknowledges it annually alongside security awareness training. The
> policy is available on the intranet without a login wall and is linked from the onboarding checklist, the
> engineering handbook and the acceptable use acknowledgement. A change of substance is announced at the
> all-hands meeting following approval, with the change summarised rather than the document re-issued
> silently."*
> — POL-01 §9

The acknowledgement record is evidence class **EC-21**; the population is 187 staff. **Communication is a
limb that fails quietly**, because a policy on an intranet is reachable, and reachability is not
communication. What distinguishes CloudNimbus's answer is that it produces a record per person per year,
and a record per person per year is a population.

### 2.7 Available to interested parties, as appropriate

This is the limb with a decision inside it, and it is where the trust centre described in Phase 01 meets
the ISMS.

> *"POL-01 is published in full on the CloudNimbus trust centre. The other eighteen policies are published
> as a summary of scope and control objectives, and are made available in full to a customer or prospective
> customer under a non-disclosure agreement on request, at the discretion of the VP Security &amp; Trust.
> Assurance deliverables are handled separately and under their own terms."*
> — POL-01 §10

Three things follow from that paragraph.

**First, "as appropriate" is a decision and not a formality.** The standard does not say which interested
parties, or how much. The answer differs by party: an enterprise customer's security team has a legitimate
need for the access control policy that a search engine crawler does not. Publishing everything is a
security decision made by default; publishing nothing fails the limb. The middle position has to be argued,
and POL-01 argues it in one sentence naming who decides.

**Second, the assurance deliverables are deliberately not covered by that paragraph.** A certificate issued
by an accredited certification body can be shown to anyone. **The SOC 2 report is restricted-use** —
intended for management, user entities during the period, and other specified parties with sufficient
knowledge and understanding — and is provided under NDA on request under O1, never posted. A paragraph
sweeping both into "available to interested parties" would create an expectation the report's own terms
forbid.

**Third, the end users are the interested party this limb serves worst.** The ~1.24 million individuals
whose data the platform holds are INT-04: the strongest interest in the register, held by parties with no
channel to CloudNimbus. What reaches them is the in-product privacy notice under `CNB-C-119`, not this
policy. That is a limitation of the arrangement rather than a solution to it, and it is recorded here
rather than left to be noticed.

## 3. The argument: clause 5.2 is a floor, and a floor is not a design

**A policy that says only what a policy is supposed to say satisfies clause 5.2 and communicates nothing.**

It is entirely possible — routine, in fact — to write a one-page policy that passes every limb above. State
a commitment to protecting information appropriate to the purpose. State that objectives are set through a
framework. State a commitment to applicable requirements and to continual improvement. Publish it,
communicate it. A certification auditor cannot raise a nonconformity against that document, because there
is nothing in it to be nonconformant with. It meets the clause.

And **not one person in the organisation is any clearer about what they may or may not do**. The engineer
wondering whether they can copy a production dataset into a notebook, the sales engineer asked by a
prospect whether data can be pinned to a region, the support agent looking at a leave record marked
"Medical / sick" — none of them is helped. The policy has satisfied a requirement and delivered nothing,
and the organisation now has an artefact it believes discharges an obligation it has not actually
discharged.

**The clause sets a floor. It does not set a standard.** That distinction is the same one this portfolio
draws about points of focus, which are considerations and not requirements, and about Annex A, which is a
reference set and not the requirements. In each case the standard supplies a boundary condition and leaves
the design to the organisation, and in each case the failure mode is to read the boundary as the
specification.

CloudNimbus's answer to the floor problem is structural rather than literary. **POL-01 is short — under
1,400 words — and is deliberately not the place where operational rules live.** The rules live in the
eighteen subordinate policies, which POL-01 names and authorises. What POL-01 adds beyond the seven limbs
is exactly three things a generic policy does not have: it states the business consequence of failure in
terms specific to this business; it states what happens when a requirement cannot be met; and it states who
decides what is disclosed to whom. Each of those is a sentence somebody can be held to. **The test of a
clause 5.2 policy is not whether it can be approved but whether any sentence in it could be breached**, and
a document with no breachable sentence has met the clause and abandoned the point.

## 4. The relationship to clause 6.2 objectives

POL-01 provides the framework; **OBJ-01 to OBJ-08 from 01.07 are the objectives**, supplemented at ISMS
level by the measures the management review considers under clause 9.3.2. Clause 6.2 imposes specific
requirements on objectives, and this is CloudNimbus's assessment against each — including the two limbs it
does not fully meet.

| Clause 6.2 requirement | Position at 2026-06-30 |
|---|---|
| **Consistent with the information security policy** | Met. POL-01 §4 states the framework and the objectives are approved by the same person who approves POL-01 |
| **Measurable, where practicable** | Met, with a qualification. All eight carry a measure that can be read from evidence. OBJ-01 and OBJ-02 measure an outcome CloudNimbus does not control — the form of the service auditor's opinion and the certification body's decision — and 01.07 §3.1 records them as tracked as forecasts rather than as deliverables. That is the honest reading of "where practicable" |
| **Take into account applicable requirements and risk assessment and treatment results** | Met. OBJ-04 derives from the Type I management letter, OBJ-06 from clause 6.1 and CC3, OBJ-01 from obligation O1 |
| **Monitored** | Met. Reported at the monthly Trust Committee under CAL-01 and quarterly to the Audit &amp; Risk Committee under CAL-03. `CNB-C-144` is the control |
| **Communicated** | Met. Objectives are published in the charter, presented at all-hands, and OBJ-05 and OBJ-08 are communicated to the functions that carry them |
| **Updated as appropriate** | Met by design, not yet demonstrated. The objectives were set at kickoff on 2026-01-19 and are re-baselined at the annual management review. `CNB-C-016` is the setting control, and it is one of the ten annual controls re-scheduled into the window, to 2026-09, so that the re-baselining and the management review coincide. No update cycle has yet completed |
| **Available as documented information** | Met. The objectives are **DI-06** in the clause 7.5 inventory at 04.10 — the programme charter 01.07, in the programme repository, approved by the Chief Executive Officer, retained for the life of the ISMS plus seven years, and changed only through the approval-gated merge in `CNB-C-145`. Clause 6.2's final limb is easy to skip because it looks administrative next to the plan limbs; an objective set in a slide deck is an objective with no controlled version |
| **Plan: what will be done** | Met. Each objective states an activity |
| **Plan: what resources will be required** | **Partially met.** Resources are stated at programme level — a $1,400,000 envelope with $1,366,000 committed, and 4.6 FTE-equivalents — and are not decomposed per objective |
| **Plan: who will be responsible** | Met. Each objective names an individual owner |
| **Plan: when it will be completed** | **Partially met.** Only OBJ-02 and OBJ-04 carry an explicit date — the certificate "by 2027-01-31" and the three management-letter matters "3 of 3 by 2026-06-30". The other six inherit dates from programme milestones MS-01 to MS-16 rather than stating their own |
| **Plan: how the results will be evaluated** | Met. The measure and target are the evaluation method, and OBJ-03's measure — the share of evidence artefacts serving both deliverables, targeted at 70% or better — is evaluated from the evidence store itself |

**Two limbs are partially met and both are recorded as gaps rather than argued away.** Per-objective
resourcing and per-objective completion dates are corrections to the charter, not to POL-01, and they are
carried into [04.13](04.13-phase-summary-and-transition.md) as known gaps with the objectives' owners.
Clause 6.2's plan limbs are a checklist of five, and an organisation that can produce three of five and
says so is in a better position than one that asserts five and is asked to show the fourth. Two of the
eight objectives carrying an explicit completion date, rather than one, does not change that position; it
changes only how far the gap runs.
