One structure for all nineteen. A suite in which every policy is shaped differently is a suite nobody can
read comparatively, and comparability is most of what a reviewer is doing.

| Section | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Identifier and title | `POL-nn` |
| Owner and approver | Named individuals. **POL-01 is approved by the Chief Executive Officer** because clause 5.2 places the information security policy with top management; the other eighteen are approved by the executive accountable for the domain |
| Purpose | What the policy is for, in two sentences |
| Scope | Who and what it applies to, **stated against the ISMS boundary** — the whole organisation — and not against the SOC 2 system |
| Requirements | What the organisation requires. Written so that a control can be built to satisfy each statement, and so that non-compliance is recognisable |
| Controls | The `CNB-C-nnn` controls that implement it. **A policy with no control under it is a wish** |
| Roles | Who does what, by name or by named role |
| Exceptions | How an exception is requested, who may grant one, for how long, and where the record lives. **A policy with no exception process is a policy that will be quietly ignored** rather than openly excepted |
| Review | Annually under CAL-13, and on any change that affects it |
| Version and approval history | Retained, not overwritten — clause 7.5.3 |

**On the exceptions section.** It is the section most often omitted and the one whose absence does the most
damage. Every organisation has situations a policy did not anticipate. Where there is a process, they
surface as recorded exceptions with owners and expiry dates; where there is none, they surface as
undocumented practice, and the first time anybody hears about it is when a control is tested against a
population that includes it.
