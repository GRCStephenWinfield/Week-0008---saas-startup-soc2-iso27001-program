## 1. What this phase is

Phase 03 ended with a determination. Ninety-one of the ninety-three Annex A controls were determined
necessary, two were not, four exclusions were argued for and refused, and thirty-one of thirty-six risks
were marked for treatment by modification. A determination is a list of things that must exist. It is not
a list of things that do exist, and it says nothing about who operates them, how often, or what they leave
behind.

**Phase 04 is where the determination becomes machinery.** It builds one control library of **148 controls**,
`CNB-C-001` to `CNB-C-148`, keyed to control identifiers rather than to framework references; it writes
**nineteen policies**, POL-01 to POL-19, and requires every control to cite exactly one of them; it declares
**twenty-four evidence classes**, EC-01 to EC-24, before the controls that produce them were built; and it
records, as an assertion CloudNimbus makes on its own authority and nobody else's, which trust services
criteria and which Annex A controls each library control serves.

The phase's centre of gravity is the direction of derivation. **Controls were designed from risks and
commitments; the mapping to criteria and to Annex A was recorded afterwards.** That order is the subject of
**DEC-402** and **ADR-0016**, and it is the difference between a control designed to work and a control
designed to be auditable. 04.03 makes the argument in full and states plainly what happens when a programme
gets it the wrong way round.

## 2. What Phase 04 establishes

It establishes the **architecture** (04.01): why a control library sits between the risk assessment and the
two deliverables, and the three properties a control must have before it can be examined at all — a **named
owner**, a **defined cadence**, and a **declared evidence artefact**. A statement missing any of the three
is a description of intent.

It publishes the **library** (04.02, 04.04 to 04.07): 148 controls across nine trust services families plus
the ISMS machinery, with **112 serving both frameworks, 21 serving SOC 2 alone and 15 serving ISO 27001
alone**. That split is never written as a column in the source tables; the generated workbook adds a
derived column so the derivation can be inspected. It is read off the last two columns of every row under
the rule in 04.02 §6 — including the special case that puts the six clause-implementing rows with both
columns empty into the ISO-only population — and the build harness re-derives it and fails if the tables
stop producing it.

It publishes the **mapping methodology and its limits** (04.03), including the coverage claim — every one of
the 61 trust services criteria served by at least one control, every one of the 91 necessary Annex A
controls implemented by at least one — and the three criteria served by **exactly one** control, named as a
concentration rather than left to be discovered.

It publishes the **policy architecture** (04.08 to 04.10): nineteen policies rather than one manual or one
document per control, **POL-01** as the clause 5.2 information security policy approved by the Chief
Executive Officer and the other eighteen approved below her, and the **37 items of documented information**
clause 7.5 requires CloudNimbus to control.

It publishes **ownership and cadence** (04.11) and the **evidence architecture** (04.12), which is the
corrective to **ML-1** from the 2025 Type I: access review evidence retained as email threads and
screenshots was a control that worked and left nothing a sampler could use.

Milestone **MS-07 — unified control library published** was met on **2026-06-05**. Milestone **MS-08 —
policy suite approved** was met on **2026-06-12**. Milestone **MS-09 — ISMS declared operational, and the
Statement of Applicability reissued the same day** was met on **2026-06-15**.

## 3. What Phase 04 deliberately does not establish

**Nothing here has been tested.** The library was published on 2026-06-05 and the ISMS was declared
operational on 2026-06-15. The observation window opens on **2026-07-01**, the day after this phase's
vantage. No control in these documents is reported as having operated effectively, because operating
effectiveness is a conclusion drawn from a period of evidence and there is no period yet.

**No opinion, certificate, audit or examination outcome appears anywhere in this phase**, and none can. The
SOC 2 examination fieldwork, the ISO Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits, the clause 9.2 internal audit and the
clause 9.3 management review of the ISMS in operation all lie beyond this horizon.

**The library is not the Statement of Applicability.** The Statement of Applicability records which Annex A
controls are necessary and whether they are implemented; the library records **how**. One Annex A control is
often satisfied by several library controls, and one library control often satisfies several Annex A
controls and several criteria at once.

## 4. The vantage this phase writes from, and R-37

**Phase 04 speaks as at 2026-06-30.** Phases 01 and 02 wrote from 2026-03-31; Phase 03 from 2026-04-30.

Five things have happened and are described in the past tense: penetration testing completed on
**2026-05-22** (MS-06); the library published on **2026-06-05**; the policy suite approved on **2026-06-12**;
the ISMS declared operational on **2026-06-15**; and the Statement of Applicability reissued the same day.

**The register has grown by one entry, and this is the phase's factual centrepiece.** Phase 03 recorded at
**DEC-306** that the register was open to additions on evidence. The first addition arrived six weeks later.
**R-37 was added on 2026-05-22** on evidence produced by the penetration test, entering at **4 × 5 = 20,
High** (**DEC-407**). It was remediated on **2026-05-29** and retested clean on **2026-06-11**, and at the
ISMS operational review it was **re-rated to 2 × 5 = 10, Moderate**: the likelihood moved because the
condition was closed and verified closed; the impact did not, because the consequence has not changed. That
is the movement rule from 03.02 applied exactly as written.

**The technical substance of the finding belongs to Phase 05**, which owns the penetration test and
describes what was found, how it was fixed and how the retest was evidenced. This document reports only
that the entry was added, at what rating, and that it has been re-rated.

**The other thirty-six entries have not been re-rated, and that is deliberate** (**DEC-408**). Ratings move
at review points, not continuously. The quarterly review under **CAL-06** fell on **2026-06-15**, alongside
the declaration that the ISMS was operational, and it is the review at which R-37 was re-rated. It
confirmed the other thirty-six **without change**, and the reason is one the programme would rather state
than be asked: a treatment implemented in June has no operating history in June. Thirty-one of the
thirty-four treatment items landed between 2026-05-15 and 2026-06-30, and a control that has run once, or
not yet at all, supplies no evidence that the likelihood it was built to reduce has actually fallen.

Re-rating on the strength of implementation rather than operation is the commonest way a register becomes
fiction, and it is comfortable precisely because the work is real — the control was built, the money was
spent, the ticket is closed. None of that is evidence about frequency. The next review under CAL-06 falls
in September, inside the observation window, and it is the first at which any of the thirty-six could move
on evidence rather than on optimism. A register that re-rates entries whenever somebody feels better about
them is a register with no audit trail between its own review dates.

**The register at this vantage is 37 entries: 7 High · 18 Moderate · 12 Low.** The arithmetic runs: the
baseline of 36 read 7 High, 17 Moderate, 12 Low; R-37 entered High, making 8 · 17 · 12 = 37; the re-rating
moved it to Moderate, making **7 · 18 · 12 = 37**. Nothing was closed and nothing was removed.

The control identifier is deliberately opaque. It carries no framework prefix, no family letter and no
sequence tied to a criterion, because **DEC-401** fixed the library's key as the control itself. A control
whose identifier encodes `CC6` cannot be re-mapped when the mapping is corrected without either renumbering
the library or leaving an identifier that lies.

Every workbook in `trackers/` is generated by parsing the narrative in these documents. The family counts,
the type and cadence distributions, the 112/21/15 split and the full criteria and Annex A coverage are all
re-derived from the nine-column tables in 04.04 to 04.07, and the build fails loudly if any of them stops
adding up. A control library maintained in a spreadsheet alongside a prose description is a library with two
versions of itself.

## 8. How to read this phase

Read **04.01** before anything else; the three-properties test in it is the criterion the whole library was
built to satisfy. Then **04.02** for the shape of the library and **04.03** for what the mapping is worth —
**04.03 is the chapter to read if you only read one**, because it is the one that says what CloudNimbus's
crosswalk is not.

**04.04 to 04.07** are the library itself and are reference material rather than argument. **04.08 to
04.10** are the policy and documented-information side. **04.11** is the sharpest chapter in the phase: it
sets out what a six-month observation window does to an annual control, derives control by control which
twelve of the twenty-two annual controls fall inside the window, and discloses the **ten** being
re-scheduled so that they have a population inside it — one of which is the only control serving CC5.3.

## 9. Standing framing

Nothing in this phase is a determination of compliance with any law. The SOC 2 deliverable is a
**restricted-use** examination report carrying a CPA firm's **opinion**; there is no such thing as a SOC 2
certification, and no document here describes CloudNimbus as certified against it. The ISO deliverable is a
**certificate issued by an accredited certification body** — Northgate Certification Services, Ltd.,
accredited by ANAB — and not by ISO, which certifies nobody. **Points of focus are not requirements**, and
**Annex A is not the requirements**: conformity is assessed against clauses 4 to 10. **The trust services
criteria contain no controls**; management designs them and the service auditor evaluates whether they meet
the criteria, which is precisely what this phase is doing. A SOC 2 finding is an **exception** or
**deviation**; an ISO finding is a **nonconformity**; this phase does not swap them, and at this vantage it
reports neither.
