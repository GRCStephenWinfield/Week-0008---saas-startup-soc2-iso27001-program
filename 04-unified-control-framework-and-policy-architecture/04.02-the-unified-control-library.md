## 1. What was published, and when

the Chief Technology Officer and recorded in `governance/GOV-13`. It contains **148 controls**, identified
`CNB-C-001` to `CNB-C-148`, allocated in a single unbroken sequence across four documents:

| **Total** | **148** | |

31 + 62 + 40 + 15 = 148.

The four documents are a reading convenience. **The library is one artefact**, and the identifier sequence
runs across the split rather than restarting in each chapter, so that no reader can mistake the chapter
boundary for a boundary in the control set.

## 2. The identifier scheme, and why it is deliberately uninformative

`CNB-C-nnn` encodes nothing. It does not carry the family, the framework, the owner or the criterion. This
was decided at **DEC-401** on 2026-05-04 and argued at **ADR-0016**, and the reasoning is the same reasoning
that produced the one-library decision in the first place.

An identifier that encodes its mapping is an identifier that is wrong as soon as the mapping is corrected.
A control numbered `SOC2-CC6-04` cannot be re-read against A.8.3 without either renumbering — which breaks
every citation in every evidence record, every policy and every prior report — or leaving an identifier that
tells the reader something untrue. The same control is frequently read against several criteria and several
Annex A controls at once, so any prefix is a choice about which of those readings is primary, made at the
moment of least information.

The cost is real: a reader cannot tell from `CNB-C-085` what it is about. That cost is paid in the
`Family` column, which is a **label for navigation** and not part of the key. `CNB-C-021` sits in the CC3
family because significant-change assessment is where a reader looks for it, and it also serves CC8.1 and
implements A.5.8 and A.8.27. The family is where it lives; the mapping columns are what it does.

## 3. The nine columns

Every row in 04.04 through 04.07 carries these nine, in this order:

| Column | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `ID` | `CNB-C-001` upward | Unique, sequential across all four documents |
| `Control` | What happens, in the present tense, naming the mechanism | Never a restatement of a criterion |
| `Family` | `CC1`…`CC9`, `A1`, `PI1`, `C1`, `P`, `ISO` | Navigation label, not part of the key |
| `Type` | Exactly one of `Preventive`, `Detective`, `Corrective` | One value, never a pair |
| `Cadence` | Exactly one of `Continuous`, `Daily`, `Weekly`, `Monthly`, `Quarterly`, `Semi-annual`, `Annual` | The declared frequency the population is derived from |
| `Owner` | A named individual | Never a team, a rota or a function (**ADR-0020**) |
| `Policy` | Exactly one `POL-nn` | Every policy cited by at least one control (**DEC-404**) |
| `Criteria` | The trust services criteria served, or `—` | Only from the 61 in scope |
| `Annex A` | The Annex A controls implemented, or `—` | Only from the 91 determined necessary |

`Type` takes one value because a control that is described as "preventive and detective" is usually two
controls that have not been separated yet, and a sampler testing it has to decide which half failed.
`Cadence` takes one value for the same reason.

**There is no evidence-class column**, and 04.01 §2.3 says why: the control-to-evidence relationship is
held in the GRC platform at control level and published per class in 04.12 §3, whose `Produced by` column
names the controls each of the twenty-four classes serves.

One reading rule governs every `Control` cell and is stated here because it is otherwise inferred
differently by different readers. **A control's population is the ISMS boundary — the whole organisation,
all 187 staff, all locations and all information assets — unless the row names the system boundary.** Where
a statement says "the 231 managed devices" or "all 96 internal SaaS applications" it is operating across
the ISMS; where it names a production component — the in-system Aurora clusters, the EKS clusters, the
seven AWS accounts, the 63 microservices — it is operating inside the SOC 2 system, which is a different
and smaller population. Phase 02 established that **neither boundary contains the other**, and a row that
does not name the system boundary should not be read as narrowing to it.

The distribution is a description of the business rather than a balanced design. **CC6 carries 28 controls,
nineteen per cent of the library**, because logical and physical access is where a multi-tenant workforce
platform holding compensation data for ~1.24M end users actually lives, and because tenant isolation is the
confidentiality control that matters. **CC3 carries 7 controls of which 5 are SOC 2-only**, because the risk

machinery that implements those clauses sits in 04.07 and is ISO-only, so **five of the seven CC3 rows cite
no Annex A control**. The other two do: `CNB-C-021` cites A.5.8 and A.8.27 and `CNB-C-022` cites A.5.9 and
A.5.22, because both are about changes to the world rather than about the risk process itself. **CC8 carries
9 for one criterion**, which is the
highest ratio in the library and is a direct answer to **ML-2**, the 2025 finding that change-management
peer review was enforced by team convention rather than by branch protection.

Two things in those figures are worth reading rather than skimming.

**Fifty-two detective controls to seventy-one preventive is a deliberate ratio, not a shortfall.** A
preventive control that fails does so silently; the population of things it should have stopped and did not
is, by construction, invisible. The library therefore pairs enforcement with observation wherever it can —
branch protection with a daily deployment-to-ticket reconciliation, automated de-provisioning with a weekly
identity-to-HR reconciliation, an entitlement model with a quarterly certification. The 25 corrective
controls are the third leg: what happens after something is found.

**Twenty-seven quarterly and twenty-two annual controls are the library's principal operating risk**, and
04.11 treats it at length. A quarterly control has a population of two inside a six-month observation
window and an annual control has a population of one or zero. These are the cadences where a single late
occurrence becomes a deviation rate of fifty or one hundred per cent, and where a control that operated
perfectly outside the window contributes nothing to it.

## 6. The dual / SOC 2-only / ISO-only split is derived, not declared

**There is no column in the source tables that says whether a control is dual.** The split is read off the
last two columns of each row, under a rule with one special case:

| `Criteria` | `Annex A` | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Populated | Populated | **Dual** — one control, two readings |
| Populated | `—` | **SOC 2-only** — serves a criterion, implements no necessary Annex A control |
| `—` | Populated | **ISO-only** — implements a necessary Annex A control, serves no criterion |
| `—` | `—` | **ISO-only** by special case — permitted only for the six ISMS machinery controls in 04.07, which implement clause requirements rather than Annex A controls |

**The special case is the whole reason the rule reaches 15 rather than 9.** Read literally, "ISO-only means
`Annex A` populated and `Criteria` empty" counts nine rows: `CNB-C-134` to `CNB-C-142`. The six
clause-implementing rows in 04.07 have both columns empty and would fall out of every category, leaving
112 + 21 + 9 = 142 and six rows in no population at all. They are ISO-only in substance — they exist
because ISO/IEC 27001 requires them and SOC 2 does not — and the derivation rule says so explicitly rather
than letting the arithmetic arrive at 15 by accident. **The split falls out of the columns given that rule**,
and a reader who applies a different rule will get a different answer, which is why the rule is printed
here and not left to be reconstructed.

Derived across all 148 rows:

| Reading | Count |
|---|---|
| **Dual** — both columns populated | **112** |
| **SOC 2-only** — `Criteria` only | **21** |
| **ISO-only** — `Annex A` only, or the six clause-implementing controls | **15** |
| **Total** | **148** |

112 + 21 + 15 = 148.

**This is the difference between a number that is true and a number that is asserted.** An organisation can
write "112 of our controls serve both frameworks" on a slide, and nothing in the slide can be checked. Here
the figure is produced by `build_soc2_p4.py` parsing every row of 04.04 to 04.07, counting the populated
cells, applying the rule above including its special case, and failing the build if the totals move. If a
control's Annex A citation is deleted because the mapping was corrected, the dual count falls by one and
the harness reports it before a reader does.

The generated workbook does carry the reading as a derived **`Serves`** column, and that is not a
contradiction of the paragraph above. The column exists **only** in the generated artefact, populated by
the harness from the two source columns, so that a reader can inspect the derivation row by row instead of
being asked to trust a total. What the library does not have is a hand-maintained `Serves` column in the
source tables, which is the thing that would drift.

The same harness re-derives the family counts, the type and cadence distributions, the criteria coverage
across all 61 criteria and the Annex A coverage across all 91 necessary controls. Phase 03 established this
discipline for the risk register's band arithmetic; Phase 04 applies it to a library four times the size,
where the drift would otherwise be undetectable by reading.

## 7. What the counts do not tell you

Three cautions belong on the face of this document.

**112 dual controls is a statement about mapping, not about efficiency.** It says CloudNimbus asserts that
112 controls can be read against both catalogues. Whether the service auditor accepts each reading against
its criterion, and whether Northgate Certification Services accepts each reading against its Annex A
control, are two separate judgements made by two separate parties, neither of whom has seen the library at
this vantage. 04.03 sets out exactly how much weight the crosswalk will bear.

**148 is not a measure of maturity.** A library of 148 controls is not safer than a library of 90 or worse
than one of 300; it is a description of how finely this organisation chose to cut its control statements.
Cutting more finely produces more rows, more evidence classes and more opportunities for a single row to
show a deviation. Cutting more coarsely produces fewer rows, each of which fails harder.

**No control in this library has been tested.** The library was published on 2026-06-05, the ISMS was
declared operational on 2026-06-15 and the observation window opens on 2026-07-01, the day after this
phase's vantage. Everything in 04.04 to 04.07 is a description of design and of controls in operation as at
2026-06-30. Suitability of design is management's assertion here and the auditor's conclusion later;
operating effectiveness is a conclusion about a period that has not yet begun.
