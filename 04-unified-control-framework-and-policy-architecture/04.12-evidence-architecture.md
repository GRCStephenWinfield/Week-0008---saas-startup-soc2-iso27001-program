## 1. The rule, and the failure it exists to prevent

**ADR-0018**, recorded by Rahul Bhargava on 2026-05-18 under **DEC-406**, states the rule this chapter is
built on:

> **A control's evidence is declared before the control is built, not discovered after it has been
> operating.**

The rule sounds procedural. It is a corrective, and the thing it corrects is **ML-1** in the 2025 Type I
management letter: *access review evidence was retained as email threads and screenshots, in a form that
would not support a Type II sample.*

Read that finding carefully, because its shape is the whole point. **The control worked.** Access was
reviewed. Entitlements were examined by people who understood them, and inappropriate access was removed.
The reviews happened on time. Pinehurst Assurance Group did not find a control failure — a Type I has no
tests of operating effectiveness and therefore cannot produce one — it found that the control had left
behind an artefact nobody could sample. A screenshot shows one moment. An email thread shows a
conversation. Neither states which system was reviewed, which population was extracted, what each line's
disposition was, who decided, or when — and those five things are what a sampler needs in order to conclude
that this occurrence of the control operated.

**The corrective is not better retention.** Retaining the same email threads for seven years in a
better-indexed archive produces seven years of unusable evidence. The corrective is **designing the
evidence at the same time as the control**, so that operating the control produces the artefact as a
by-product rather than requiring somebody to assemble one afterwards. A control whose evidence has to be
assembled will have its evidence assembled at the point somebody asks for it, which is January, about
something that happened in August, by a person reconstructing it.

That is why the mapping between controls and evidence is published rather than assumed. **Every one of the
148 controls maps to at least one of the twenty-four classes below**, and the mapping is recorded **per
evidence class rather than per control row**: the `Produced by` column of the table in §3 names the
controls each class serves, and the GRC platform holds the same relationship at control level, which is
where an operator and a sampler query it.

**It is not a tenth column of 04.04 to 04.07, and the build harness does not check it.** The harness parses
the nine columns of the control tables and re-derives what those columns contain — the family counts, the
type and cadence distributions, the 112 / 21 / 15 split and the two coverage claims. A relationship held
outside those columns is outside what it can re-derive. Completeness here therefore rests on the `Produced
by` column being read against the library, which a reader can do from this document, and that is a weaker
form of assurance than the rest of the schema enjoys. Saying so is cheaper than being asked which column
the check runs against.

## 2. Four things every evidence class must state

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| **What the artefact is** | A class whose artefact is described as "documentation" cannot be produced on request. The name has to identify a thing that exists |
| **Where it is stored** | An artefact in a person's mailbox is not in the evidence store, and the difference surfaces when that person is on leave during fieldwork |
| **How long it is kept** | The window closes 2026-12-31 and fieldwork runs to 2027-02-06. Evidence produced in July must survive at least that long, and in practice much longer |
| **What one unit of it looks like** | **This is what the auditor samples.** A class whose sampling unit is undefined cannot be sampled, and a class whose sampling unit is bigger than the control's occurrence — "the quarter's evidence" rather than "one review of one system" — collapses the population to a number too small to test |

The fourth is the one that is routinely omitted, and it is the one ML-1 was really about. **"We keep access
review evidence" is a statement about a category. "One unit is one system, for one quarter, comprising the
generated entitlement extract, a decision against each line, the tickets raised for revocations, and the
reviewer's sign-off with a timestamp" is a statement about a thing that can be selected from a list.** The
first cannot be sampled. The second defines the population.

## 4. How the classes attach to the library

**Every one of the 148 controls appears in at least one `Produced by` cell above**, which is what makes the
completeness claim checkable from this document rather than from an assertion about a column that does not
exist. Several controls appear twice — `CNB-C-118`, the offboarding deletion, appears against **EC-18** for
the certificate and **EC-17** for the job runs beneath it, because the certificate asserts a completion that
the job records substantiate.

The mapping is published in this direction on purpose. A reader who wants to know what to sample for a
control looks it up here or in the GRC platform; a reader who wants to know what one unit of an artefact
looks like reads the class. Both questions are answered from the class, which is the object that actually
has a sampling unit. A control row does not.

| Family group | Principal classes |
|---|---|
| CC1 to CC5 | EC-21, EC-23, EC-24, EC-15 |
| CC6 to CC9 | EC-01, EC-02, EC-03, EC-04, EC-05, EC-06, EC-16 |
| A1 Availability | EC-07, EC-08, EC-09, EC-10, EC-11, EC-06 |
| PI1 Processing integrity | EC-12, EC-13, EC-14, EC-15, EC-03 |
| C1 Confidentiality | EC-04, EC-05, EC-18, EC-03 |
| P Privacy | EC-17, EC-18, EC-19, EC-20, EC-22, EC-15 |
| ISO machinery and unmapped Annex A | EC-23, EC-24, EC-22, EC-16, EC-05 |

**Twenty-four classes for 148 controls is a deliberate ratio.** One class per control would have produced a
register that is the control library with different column headings and no aggregation benefit; a handful
of broad classes — "system records", "management records" — would have produced sampling units too coarse
to select from. The number falls out of the same question the policy suite answered: **how many distinct
artefact shapes are there, where a shape is defined by what one unit of it looks like?** Two controls with
the same sampling unit share a class even when they serve different criteria, and that is where the
integration dividend comes from.

## 5. One store, two propositions

**DEC-112** established a single shared evidence store rather than one per framework, and OBJ-03 measures
the result: the share of artefacts serving both deliverables, targeted at 70% or better. The store is
indexed **by control**, with each artefact tagged with the period it covers and the frameworks it serves.

The limit of that arrangement was stated in 01.06 and is restated here because the evidence architecture is
where it bites. **The same artefact can serve both audits while answering two different evidential
propositions.** A completed EC-01 access review package supports, for the service auditor, the proposition
that the control operated on each occasion across a defined period — tested by selecting occurrences from a
population. For the certification body it supports the proposition that the ISMS is operating as described
and the risk treatment is being carried out. The first is a claim about instances; the second is a claim
about a system.

The design consequence is one-directional and it is the reason every sampling unit above is defined at
occurrence level: **evidence sufficient for a Type II sample is always sufficient for a Stage 2 sample, and
the converse is not true.** An evidence architecture designed for the certification body — narrative
summaries, a representative example, a screenshot of the most recent cycle — will not survive the
examination. So the store is designed for the harder proposition and used for both, and programme risk
**PR-04** exists to keep that decision under review.

## 6. What is not solved

Three limitations, stated because a chapter about evidence should not be the least honest chapter in the
phase.

**Declaring the evidence does not mean the evidence is produced.** A class is a design; the artefact appears
only if the control operates and the export runs. Several classes above depend on a scheduled export from a
system of record into the evidence store — EC-02 monthly, EC-04 weekly, EC-16 weekly, EC-21 quarterly. **An
export that silently stops produces a gap that looks exactly like a control that stopped operating**, and
the two are indistinguishable in January. The exports are themselves monitored, and that monitoring is a
dependency this phase creates rather than resolves.

**Retention of seven years is a programme decision, not a legal determination.** It covers the period, the
fieldwork, the certification cycle and a reasonable look-back for a user entity, and POL-19 says explicitly
that it is not advice about any statutory retention obligation.

**A sampling unit can be right and the population still be small.** EC-08 will hold six units at the end of
the window; EC-11 will hold two; EC-18 will hold as many as there are terminations. Defining the unit
precisely does not make the population larger — it makes the population countable, which is the necessary
first step to noticing that it is small. That is the subject of
[04.11](04.11-control-ownership-and-operating-cadence.md), and the two chapters are two halves of the same
argument.
