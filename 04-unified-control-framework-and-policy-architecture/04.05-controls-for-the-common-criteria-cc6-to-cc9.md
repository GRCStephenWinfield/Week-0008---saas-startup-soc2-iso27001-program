## 1. The largest chapter in the phase

Sixty-two controls, `CNB-C-032` to `CNB-C-093`, against sixteen criteria. **Forty-two per cent of the whole
library sits in this chapter**, and every one of the sixty-two is dual: each carries both a `Criteria`
citation and an `Annex A` citation, because this is the part of the Common Criteria where the two
frameworks are genuinely looking at the same subject matter.

| CC6 Logical and physical access | CC6.1 – CC6.8 | 28 | 20 | 4 | 4 |

The type profile per family is the most informative thing in that table and it is not an accident. **CC6 is
overwhelmingly preventive** because access control is enforcement: the mechanism either refuses the request
or it does not. **CC7 is overwhelmingly detective and corrective** — one preventive control out of nineteen —
because system operations is the family that exists to notice and respond to what the preventive controls
did not stop. **CC8 is preventive again** because change management is a set of gates. A library whose CC7
family were mostly preventive would be a library that had misunderstood what CC7 is for.

Ownership is concentrated: **22 of the 62 are Wes Delacroix's**, 12 are Karim Haddad's and 10 are Junia
Okonkwo's. That is a real operating risk in a 4.6-FTE programme and 04.11 treats it. It is also honest.
Spreading ownership to make a distribution chart look better would have named people who do not perform the
control.

## 2. CC6 — Logical and physical access

Twenty-eight controls, the largest family in the library. Eight criteria, and it is worth restating what
each asks before the table, because CC6 is the family where a control library most often answers the
adjacent question.

**Tenant isolation is the control that matters most in this family**, and it sits at `CNB-C-035`: every
query path to the **five in-system Aurora clusters** passes through the data-access layer, which applies
the tenant scoping predicate before the statement reaches the database. The design intent behind that
wording is that the enforcement point is architectural rather than conventional — a service is not trusted
to remember to scope its own queries.

The count is five and not six on purpose. Six Aurora clusters exist; **02.03 puts five inside the SOC 2
system**, and the sixth, `staging-core`, is outside it because it holds no production customer data. A
tenant scoping predicate applied to a cluster with no tenants in it would be a control with an empty
population, and stating six would misdescribe what a sampler can select from. The same correction applies
to `CNB-C-094` in 04.06, which reviews capacity across the three in-system EKS clusters and five in-system
Aurora clusters. 04.02 §3 states the general rule: **a control's population is the ISMS boundary unless the
row names the system boundary**, and these two rows name it.

**R-37 bears on the tenant isolation control set as a whole**, and this is the one place in the phase that
says so. R-37 was added to the register on **2026-05-22** on evidence produced by the penetration test,
entering at **4 × 5 = 20, High**; it was remediated on **2026-05-29**, retested clean on **2026-06-11**, and
re-rated at the ISMS operational review on **2026-06-15** to **2 × 5 = 10, Moderate**. Those are the
disclosed facts, and they are the whole of what this phase reports. **The substance of the finding — what
was found, where, what changed and how the retest was evidenced — belongs to Phase 05**, which owns the
penetration test.

Naming a single row as "the control R-37 concerns" would be wrong twice over. It would say more about the
finding than this phase is entitled to say, and it would be inaccurate: tenant isolation at CloudNimbus is
enforced by more than one row — the data-access predicate in `CNB-C-035`, the isolation suite in
`CNB-C-115`, the account and network separation in `CNB-C-055`, the secure design review gate in
`CNB-C-086` — and an assumption disproved about isolation is an assumption about the set, not about one
member of it. The rows below and in 04.06 state the mechanisms as they stand at this vantage.

Three other design choices in CC6 are worth pointing at.

**The joiner–mover–leaver path is four controls, not one.** `CNB-C-037` provisions only from an approved
onboarding ticket with the role assignment generated from the role profile rather than typed;
`CNB-C-038` de-provisions within four hours of the HR termination event; `CNB-C-039` reconciles weekly;
`CNB-C-040` certifies quarterly. Cutting it this way means a deviation lands on one row rather than
contaminating the whole lifecycle, and it means the weekly reconciliation is a genuinely independent check
on the automated de-provisioning rather than a description of it.

**`CNB-C-040` is ML-1's direct answer.** The 2025 management letter recorded that access review evidence
was retained as email threads and screenshots. The control now requires certification of an entitlement
extract **in the GRC platform**, with revocations applied and evidenced before the review closes — which
gives a sampler a dated, attributable unit per system owner per quarter instead of a mailbox.

**The physical controls are small and deliberately unheroic.** CloudNimbus operates one leased suite of
about 4,100 square feet in Denver with no server, no network closet and no comms room. Four controls cover
it, and `CNB-C-048` includes an annual confirmation that the suite still contains no information processing
equipment — because the Statement of Applicability's two exclusions, A.7.11 and A.7.12, rest on exactly
that fact. If a rack ever appears in the Denver suite, the exclusions are wrong, and this control is how
CloudNimbus would find out.

**`CNB-C-048` also cites A.7.5, and a control statement that answers A.7.5 with "the landlord does it" is
not a control.** Protection against physical and environmental threats is a real requirement even for a
tenant with no equipment, and the honest form of it is not a disclaimer. What CloudNimbus does is three
things it can be asked to produce: it confirms and records that nothing in the suite is information
processing equipment, which is what makes the exposure small; it holds the lease provisions that oblige the
landlord to provide fire detection and suppression, power and climate control, which is what makes the
protection contractual rather than assumed; and it reviews the landlord's written confirmation that those
services were maintained and tested, which is what turns a lease clause into evidence. Where the landlord
cannot evidence a provision, the control escalates it rather than restating it. **A control that names
someone else's obligation and stops has described a dependency, not an activity** — and a dependency with
no review is exactly the shape ML-3 recorded on the vendor side.

## 3. CC7 — System operations

Nineteen controls across five criteria: detection of configuration and vulnerability conditions (CC7.1),
monitoring for anomalies (CC7.2), evaluation of security events (CC7.3), response to identified incidents
(CC7.4), and recovery (CC7.5).

**One preventive control out of nineteen.** This family is where the library assumes prevention has already
failed, and the shape of it follows the lifecycle: find the condition, see the event, decide what it is,
respond, recover.

The two hinges are `CNB-C-067` and `CNB-C-069`. The first attaches a **thirty-minute triage
service level** to every alert with automatic escalation; the second requires the analyst to record, on the
ticket, whether a triaged event **is** an incident and at what severity. Those two together are what turn a
stream of alerts into a population an auditor can sample, and the second is the one organisations usually
lack: an alert closed without a recorded decision is an alert that cannot be shown to have been evaluated.

**`CNB-C-065` carries an EU constraint that is easy to lose.** Logs are shipped to the **region-partitioned**
`cnb-security` archive, with the EU bucket in `eu-central-1` under EU-scoped keys, because 41 customers
require EU data residency and only pseudonymised or non-personal telemetry crosses regions. A single global
log archive would have been simpler, cheaper and inconsistent with obligation O8.

Two cadence notes. `CNB-C-073`, the authority and counsel contact list, and `CNB-C-076`, the tabletop
exercise, are **the only two semi-annual controls in the entire 93-control Common Criteria set** — a
semi-annual control has a population of exactly one in a six-month window, and one late occurrence is a
hundred per cent deviation rate. And `CNB-C-062`, endpoint patching, is typed **Corrective** rather than
Preventive: patching remediates a weakness that already exists, and typing it preventive would have
misdescribed what a sampler is testing.

## 4. CC8 — Change management

Nine controls against a single criterion, CC8.1: the entity authorises, designs, develops or acquires,
configures, documents, tests, approves and implements changes to infrastructure, data, software and
procedures to meet its objectives.

**Nine controls for one criterion is the highest ratio in the library, and it is a direct answer to ML-2.**
The 2025 Type I management letter recorded that change-management peer review was enforced by team
convention rather than by branch protection: *the design was adequate; the enforcement was not.* That
finding is the reason `CNB-C-079` names the enforcement point in the sentence — branch protection refuses
the merge without one approving review from a code owner who is not the author — and the reason `CNB-C-087`
exists at all. A daily reconciliation of pipeline deployments against approved change tickets is a control
whose only purpose is to catch the gap ML-2 described, and it operates daily rather than monthly because a
convention-based failure is invisible until somebody counts.

The gates run in a deliberate order: peer review (`CNB-C-079`), pipeline exclusivity (`CNB-C-080`), ticket
completeness including rollback plan (`CNB-C-081`), environment separation (`CNB-C-083`), automated test
suites including the calculation-engine regression pack (`CNB-C-084`), static analysis and composition
analysis (`CNB-C-085`), and secure design review for anything touching authentication, tenant isolation or
the calculation engine (`CNB-C-086`). `CNB-C-082` is the escape hatch — emergency change deployed ahead of
approval, retrospectively approved within five business days — and it is typed Corrective because that is
what it is.

**`CNB-C-084` is owned by Grete Lindqvist rather than by an engineering director**, and that is not a
filing error. The calculation-engine regression suite is the gate that stands between a code change and a
wrong number in somebody's pay, and the person who owns processing integrity owns the gate.

## 5. CC9 — Risk mitigation

Six controls: risk mitigation activities for business disruption (CC9.1) and management of vendor and
business partner risk (CC9.2).

CC9 is small because most of what a reader expects to find here lives elsewhere. Business continuity
mechanics — backup, restore testing, redundancy, the disaster recovery exercise — belong to the A1
availability family in 04.06, and the risk process itself belongs to the ISMS machinery in 04.07. What
remains is the mitigation posture: the continuity plan and its objectives (`CNB-C-088`), the risk transfer
review (`CNB-C-089`), and four controls on the vendor estate.

The vendor controls are shaped by the arithmetic Phase 02 established and by the error it recorded.
**Eighty-four vendors are in the register, eleven of them are sub-processors under a data processing
addendum, and both subservice organisations — Amazon Web Services and Halcyon Identity — are among those
eleven; 11 + 73 = 84.** The three classifications are overlapping sets and not a partition, and treating
them as disjoint is exactly the error **ML-3** recorded. `CNB-C-090` therefore records **the outcome and the
tier** in the vendor register rather than a single classification, and `CNB-C-092` requires the assurance
artefact for each Tier 1 vendor to be read for **what that document does and does not cover** — because a
subservice organisation's own report covers that organisation's controls, not CloudNimbus's configuration
of them.

`CNB-C-093` is the newest of the six in conception: registration and baseline review of any cloud service
before customer data is placed in it, implementing **A.5.23**, one of the eleven controls new in the 2022
revision. A company with 96 internal SaaS applications and no such control acquires its ninety-seventh
without noticing.

## 6. What this chapter does not say

Three limits, stated on the face of the chapter.

**Nothing here has operated across a period.** The library was published on 2026-06-05 and the ISMS was
declared operational on 2026-06-15. This chapter describes controls in place as at 2026-06-30 — the day
before the observation window opens. Suitability of design is management's assertion at this vantage;
operating effectiveness is a conclusion about a period that has not begun, and no control in these tables
is reported as effective.

**The subservice organisations are carved out.** AWS and Halcyon Identity perform controls that are
necessary, in combination with CloudNimbus's own, to meet the applicable criteria — and their controls are
excluded from the description, with **fourteen complementary subservice organisation controls** stated
instead: eleven for AWS and three for Halcyon Identity. Nothing in this chapter asserts anything about what
AWS does. `CNB-C-057` asserts what CloudNimbus configures.

**The `Annex A` citations are CloudNimbus's own assertion.** 04.03 sets out what that is worth and what it
is not. A citation in these tables is a navigation aid between one library and two catalogues; it is not
evidence, and no certification body or CPA firm is bound by it.
