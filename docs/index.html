The programme's worst finding was not something CloudNimbus missed. It was something it decided. The clause 9.2 gap had been in its own records for eleven months — read, agreed with, minuted at the highest governance body in the ISMS and deferred twice — before Northgate raised it as a major nonconformity. It was accepted at the closing meeting without contest.

An entity that failed to notice a gap has a detection problem. An entity that noticed it, took it to its highest governance body and decided to leave it has a different one — and the second is not the kind that monitoring evidence answers.

Exception 2 carries a dash rather than a rate, and the dash is the finding. "Clean opinion" is not a term the standards use. Test exceptions are disclosed in Section IV and do not automatically modify an opinion; the practitioner modifies only where the deviations mean the applicable criteria were not achieved. The reasoning is reproduced rather than the conclusion — a different engagement team on the same facts could have reached the other answer, and a portfolio that showed the outcome alone would teach a reader that unmodified opinions simply happen.

A rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence itself changed, and likelihood 1 is reserved for the not-reasonably-foreseeable. So an entry at 3 × 4 reaches 2 × 4 = 8 and stops. Eight is a floor.

And a floor is downward only. R-24 sat on it and then went up, from 8 to 12, when the event it describes occurred. Nothing closed. Nothing was removed. A risk that stops being likely is re-rated, not deleted.

Three up, two down, net one. 16 + 3 − 2 = 17; 22 − 3 + 2 = 21. Eleven of the thirty-eight ratings diverge in all — five crossing a band and six underneath it.

A forecast right at the band level and wrong five times underneath is not a good forecast that got lucky, and it is not a bad one. It is a forecast whose aggregate absorbed its errors. The total absorbed four of the five and showed the fifth — and the one it showed is not more important than the four it hid; it is just the one that did not have a partner. A programme that checked only the aggregate would have declared the forecast a success and learned nothing from the five.

A fifth criterion — that the programme's own account of what happened is accurate — was stated as unmeasurable at chartering and remains so.

A close-out that reports seven of eight objectives met while leaving the charter's own success test unscored has chosen the flattering instrument. Both were available on the day; only one was used, and the choice was not made deliberately, which is worse rather than better — nobody weighed the two and picked. It went unscored because nothing required it to be scored: no control in the library asks whether a charter's success criteria have been marked at close.

The better measure — 113 of 150 controls dual-serving, 75.3% — is published beside the miss and is not substituted for it, because a programme that changes a measure in the document that scores it has scored itself.

The four failures, and what each taught

RT-02 — sixty-eight consecutive nights

A control whose operating condition is that a record was written, rather than what the record says. The job ran every night, completed successfully and wrote a completion record every time — against a relation an August migration had emptied. Success and non-existence produced the same observable state.

A completion record nobody reads is a log, not a control. The library has still not been enumerated for controls with that shape, and nobody knows how many there are.

CNB-C-096 — the availability probe

A control that says how often and never says what. It fixed cadence, regions and the paging condition and was silent on what the probe must exercise. The probe performed a read.

Detection worked; measurement did not. CNB-C-068 beside it was explicit about the transactions it exercised — so the silence is a design deficiency rather than an inevitability.

The missed restore test

The August occurrence did not happen. It was not re-performed and back-dated; the deviation stands at 1 of 6. The temptation to repair a denominator arrived three times in three phases and was refused three times.

A correction that fixes the instances is not a corrective action, and clause 10.2 distinguishes them in words. An occurrence is an event on a date.

The major nonconformity

The control library got ahead of the practice it described. The audit programme document was written in January; the control describing it was written in June. Both were correct on their own face and nothing compared them.

Reading the library told nobody anything was wrong. The issue records the class rather than the instance: it can exist anywhere in 150 rows and has not been enumerated.

What none of this claims

Not that CloudNimbus is secure. Neither instrument measures it. One is a conformity assessment of a management system at the opening of a three-year cycle; the other is a practitioner's opinion on specified controls throughout a period that has ended.

Not that a clean report predicts anything. The evidence is inside the report itself — the retention job addressed an emptied relation for sixty-eight nights, eleven weeks before the period ended, in the period an unmodified opinion was expressed on, and nothing in the control library detected it.

Not that any of this is a determination of compliance with any law — not the GDPR, not the CCPA or CPRA, not any state privacy statute.

Illustrative portfolio sample. CloudNimbus, Inc. and every person, firm, figure and finding in this repository are fictional. A SOC 2 examination produces an opinion and never a certificate; the report is a restricted-use document intended for management, for user entities during the period, and for other specified parties with sufficient knowledge and understanding. ISO does not certify anyone — accredited certification bodies do. Points of focus are not requirements and Annex A is not the requirements.
