## 1. Purpose

Clause 6.1.3 f) requires two things from the risk owners and they are not the same thing: **approval of the
risk treatment plan**, and **acceptance of the residual information security risks**. 03.06 recorded the
first. This document records the second, argues the four risks CloudNimbus decided to retain, and publishes
the forecast of where the register is expected to stand when the programme closes.

The forecast is the part of this document with the most rules attached to it, and §5 explains why: a
previous engagement of this kind published a close forecast that its own movement rules made unreachable and
had to report the miss in its closing phase. **ADR-0015** is the corrective, and it is a discipline about
publication rather than about prediction.

## 2. What acceptance is not

Retention is the treatment option most often used as a filing cabinet. The failure mode is recognisable: a
register accumulates entries nobody wants, they are marked accepted, and the acceptance has no author, no
date, no reason and no expiry. Six months later the entry is quoted as evidence that the organisation
considered the risk, when what it actually evidences is that the organisation stopped considering it.

**Retention at CloudNimbus is a decision with four attributes and it is not recorded without all four**: the
**risk owner's acceptance** under clause 6.1.3 f) together with the **additional acceptance** the criteria
in 03.02 §6 require at that band, a stated reason, a review date, and a statement of what would cause the
decision to be revisited. An entry lacking any of them is not retained; it is untreated, and the difference
is the whole point.

Two consequences follow. **A High rating may not be retained on the authority of the risk owner or of the VP
Security &amp; Trust.** The acceptance criteria in 03.02 §6 are an escalation rather than a prohibition, and the
difference matters: a High rating requires treatment, and a residual that would still be High after
treatment may be retained only on the acceptance of the **Chief Executive Officer**, with the reasoning
minuted at the Audit &amp; Risk Committee. Writing that as "a High is never retainable" and then describing the
conditions under which it may be retained would have put two incompatible rules in the same document, and a
criterion that contradicts itself is applied by whoever is reading it. **No entry in this register took that
path**, and none needed to — the highest of the four retentions is a Moderate at 10. And **retention is not
permanent**. All four acceptances below are reviewed at the
management review under **CAL-15** and at each quarterly register review under **CAL-06**. An acceptance
with no review date is an abandonment with better paperwork.

**Two signatures are recorded against each of the four, and they are not interchangeable.** This is worth
setting out precisely, because getting it wrong is a conformity issue rather than a presentational one.

**Clause 6.1.3 f) names the risk owners and nobody else.** It requires the organisation to obtain *the risk
owners'* approval of the risk treatment plan **and** *their* acceptance of the residual information security
risks. The clause does not offer an executive alternative, and it does not permit the accountability to be
lifted to a level at which the person signing has never had to answer for the control. So the residual on
R-19 and R-20 is accepted by **Wes Delacroix**, who owns disaster recovery and the EU estate; the residual
on R-24 by **Tobias Lund**, who owns the privacy commitments the geolocation notice sits in; and the
residual on R-28 by **Hannah Brill**, who owns the Denver premises. Those are the clause 6.1.3 f)
acceptances.

**The acceptance criteria in 03.02 §6 then add a second signature on top of the first — they do not replace
it.** A Low retention requires the owner's decision to be endorsed by the VP Security &amp; Trust; a Moderate
retention requires the VP Security &amp; Trust's own acceptance; and the rider that overrides the band requires
the Chief Executive Officer's acceptance of any entry carrying impact 5, because the consequence limb is at
maximum and the only reason the score is not High is a judgement that the event is unlikely. Those are
CloudNimbus's internal authority thresholds. They govern *whether the organisation is willing to carry the
exposure*; clause 6.1.3 f) governs *who is accountable for it*. Both records exist for all four entries and
both are held in `governance/GOV-10`.

**An executive acceptance recorded in place of the owner's would be a clause 6.1.3 f) nonconformity waiting
to be raised**, and it is the shape of finding that is trivial to evidence: the auditor reads the retention
record, reads the register's owner column, sees two different names and one signature, and asks where the
owner's acceptance is. The programme noticed this here rather than at Stage 1, and the fix was to record
both rather than to argue that one implied the other. **R-28 is where the error nearly stuck**: the reason
for retention is a judgement about how the programme spends money, which is Karim Haddad's to make, and it
would have been easy to let the reason for the decision determine whose signature was on it. The reason and
the accountability are different things. Hannah Brill accepts the residual because she owns the risk; Karim
Haddad endorses it because the criteria require it; and the economic argument in §3.4 belongs to the second
signature, not the first.

### 3.1 R-19 and R-20 are immovable, and the register says so

**DEC-307**, decided by Elise Fontaine on 2026-04-14, records R-19 and R-20 as **immovable rather than given
an aspirational target**. This is the most consequential decision in the document.

Both sit at **2 × 5 = 10**. Apply the movement rules from 03.02 and there is nowhere for either to go. A
rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence itself has changed, and neither consequence has changed:
a disaster recovery shortfall in a real event still means the recovery objective is missed, and the loss of
`eu-central-1` still means the EU estate is gone. Likelihood is already at 2 — *foreseeable but not expected
within the cycle* — and **the next step down is 1, which is reserved for the not reasonably foreseeable**. A
disaster recovery exercise falling short of a four-hour objective in a real event is foreseeable to anyone
who has ever run one; the loss of an AWS region is foreseeable, and Phase 02's clause 4.1 determination on
climate change under **DEC-209** is one of the reasons it cannot be treated as unthinkable.

So both entries stay at 10 for the life of the programme, and the register says so at baseline rather than
carrying a target that quietly fails to arrive. The alternative — writing "target: 2 × 4 = 8" against R-19
because a target column looks better populated than an empty one — would have required either a claim that
the consequence had changed, which is false, or a use of likelihood 1, which the rules forbid. **A target
that the register's own rules make unreachable is not a target. It is a commitment to report a miss.**

Retention does not mean nothing happens. The disaster recovery exercise runs annually under **CAL-10**, the
business continuity exercise under **CAL-16**, and the recovery objectives RTO 4 hours and RPO 15 minutes
remain the design targets. What retention means is that **the rating will not move**, because the residual
after all of that work is still a foreseeable event with a maximum consequence.

### 3.2 R-20 — a commitment to a customer creating a risk to that same customer

**R-20 is the clearest case in this register of a promise made to a customer producing a risk to that same
customer, and it deserves to be said plainly rather than left to be inferred.**

Forty-one customers hold the EU-residency addendum. **Obligation O8** commits CloudNimbus to keeping their
personal data at rest within `eu-central-1`. Phase 02 established what that means architecturally:
`eu-central-1` recovers **intra-region across availability zones only**, because the cross-region
replication that would allow recovery elsewhere is precisely the thing residency forbids. The treatment that
would reduce R-20 — replicate the EU estate to a second region — is not merely expensive or difficult. **It
is the breach of the commitment that created the customer relationship.**

There is no clever resolution available and this document does not pretend to one. The options are three,
and all three are worse than retention. CloudNimbus could replicate outside the region and break O8, which
trades a foreseeable availability risk for a certain contractual one. It could seek to renegotiate the
addendum with 41 customers, which is a commercial conversation in which CloudNimbus asks for something and
offers nothing. Or it could exit the EU, which is the avoidance option 03.06 §3 records as available and not
taken.

What CloudNimbus does instead is state the position. The residual is accepted by **Wes Delacroix** as the
risk owner under clause 6.1.3 f) and, because the impact limb stands at 5, by the **Chief Executive
Officer** as well under the rider in 03.02 §6. It is disclosed as an accepted residual in the Phase 02
architecture documents, and its treatment —
in the sense of an extended recovery objective disclosed to the customers who hold the residency commitment
— is carried to **Phase 06**, where availability is designed and where the choice between a longer stated
objective and a commitment already made has to be made in front of the people it affects. **A residency
commitment and a recovery commitment are in tension, and the honest thing to do with a tension is name both
sides of it.**

### 3.3 R-24 sits on the floor

**R-24** — geolocation retained or used beyond the purpose the individual was told about — is rated **2 × 4
= 8**, and eight is where a 2 × 4 stops.

The consequence is fixed at 4: personal information used outside the notice given to the individual who
provided it, for a category collected directly from the data subject, at 387 of 640 customers who enable
geolocation at clock-in. Likelihood is already at 2 and the next step is the reserved 1. **Eight is a
floor**, as 03.02 §5.3 sets out, and R-24 is sitting on it.

The reason it is retained rather than modified is that the modification has already happened and is
recorded elsewhere. **ADR-0009** and **DEC-207** cut geolocation retention to **thirteen months**, shorter
than the contract term governing every other personal data category, as the price of refusing to withdraw
the capability. What remains is the residual of a decision already taken and already paid for. Adding a
treatment item to R-24 would have implied there was further movement available, and there is not.

### 3.4 R-28 is retained because the treatment costs more than the risk

**R-28** — an unauthorised person obtaining physical access to the Denver office — is rated **2 × 2 = 4**,
Low, and it is the one retention in this register that is a straightforward economic judgement.

The Denver suite is approximately 4,100 square feet of meeting space. It contains no server, no network
closet and no comms room; its internet service is the landlord's building service; and no production
equipment is on the premises anywhere in the organisation. The impact of an unauthorised entry is a 2 —
internal disruption, or a control failure visible to an auditor but not to a customer — because there is
nothing there to take that is not also on an encrypted laptop somewhere else.

The treatment available is a staffed reception. For a remote-first company whose Denver suite is used as a
meeting space by a fraction of 187 people across 31 states and 4 countries, that is a permanent cost against
a Low risk in a building with nothing in it. It was declined.

**This is not an argument that physical security does not apply.** The suite has controlled entry and a
visitor log, and 03.11 records that **A.7.4 physical security monitoring** was argued for exclusion from the
Statement of Applicability on the basis that a meeting space has nothing to monitor — and that the argument
was **refused**. Retaining a Low risk and excluding a control are two different decisions, and CloudNimbus
took the first and refused the second.

## 4. The residual position at baseline

There is no residual rating column in the register at baseline, and 03.04 §6 explains why: residual risk is
what remains after treatment, treatment has not been performed as at 2026-04-30, and a residual column
published at baseline would be a prediction printed in a field that readers will take for a measurement.

What the risk owners accepted at MS-05 on 2026-04-24 was therefore the residual **as it stands**: the four
retained risks at their current ratings, and — for the thirty-one modified risks — the risk that remains
while the treatment plan is being delivered. That second acceptance is easy to skip and it is the honest
one. Between 2026-04-24 and the last target date in the plan, thirty-one risks stand at their baseline
ratings with their treatments incomplete, and the organisation is carrying every one of them.

## 5. The forecast

Phase 03 publishes a forecast of the register at programme close: **0 High · 16 Moderate · 22 Low = 38.**

It is derived, not asserted, and the derivation is shown because a forecast whose arithmetic is not visible
cannot be checked by the person it is presented to.

### 5.1 The rules applied

Three rules, and no others. **Ratings move on likelihood, not impact**, unless the consequence itself has
changed — no consequence is expected to change. **Likelihood 1 is unavailable**, so no entry can be
forecast below likelihood 2. **Eight is a floor**, so no entry with impact 4 or 5 can be forecast Low.

**Twelve entries are forecast not to move at all**: **R-19** and **R-20**, the two immovables; **R-24**, the
one already on the floor; and nine Low entries already at likelihood 2 whose only remaining step is the
reserved 1 — **R-26, R-27, R-28, R-29, R-31, R-32, R-33, R-35, R-36**. Four of those twelve — **R-19,
R-20, R-24 and R-28** — are the entries **retained** under 03.06 §2, and carry no treatment item by
design. The other **eight — R-26, R-27, R-29, R-31, R-32, R-33, R-35 and R-36 — are nevertheless
*modified* risks**, and each of the eight carries a treatment item in 03.06: TP-25, TP-26, TP-27, TP-29,
TP-30, TP-31, TP-33 and TP-34. Treatment that does not move a rating is still treatment, and a plan that
only did work where the number would improve would be optimising the report rather than the environment.

**Three entries stop above the floor rather than on it**, and each stops for a reason worth stating.

**R-04** — Halcyon Identity unavailable and no end user able to authenticate — is forecast to move only
from likelihood 4 to likelihood 3, reaching 3 × 4 = 12 rather than the floor of eight. It is the one risk in
the register whose treatment option is **share**, it carries no TP item, and that combination means the
single step has to rest on something the reader can check. It rests on one thing: the **Halcyon Identity
master services agreement renewed on 2026-03-18**, which added a **service availability commitment** and a
**service credit regime** where the previous agreement had neither. **Wes Delacroix** — R-04's owner —
carried the negotiation; **Tobias Lund** settled the terms; and 03.06 §4 records the act, the date and both
owners. The renewal is stated in the past tense because it happened before this phase's vantage of
2026-04-30, and for no other reason.

**One step, and the reasoning for the ceiling is the same as the reasoning for the step.** A committed level
with a cost attached to missing it is a real influence on where a supplier spends its engineering effort,
and it is the only lever CloudNimbus has. It is also not a control. **CloudNimbus operates none of Halcyon
Identity's availability controls**, designs none, tests none and cannot inspect them; a service credit is a
remedy after an outage rather than a reduction in how often one occurs. Sharing allocates consequence — it
does not make an event less frequent. Forecasting a supplier's outage frequency all the way down to
*foreseeable but not expected within the cycle*, on the strength of a commitment CloudNimbus neither
designs, operates nor tests, would be forecasting somebody else's engineering from the outside. One step is
what a contract buys. The floor of 8 remains available only if the architecture changes, and 03.05 §6
records that it has not.

The other two are **R-13** and **R-14**, and both are forecast to move only one step, from likelihood 4 to
likelihood 3, stopping at 3 × 3 = 9. **R-13** — an employee successfully phished — cannot honestly be
forecast to *foreseeable but not expected within the cycle* across 187 people over three years, however good
the authentication becomes; phishing-resistant credentials change what a successful phish yields, which is
the consequence chain, not whether one occurs. **R-14** — a customer failing to perform CUEC-05 — is a
control CloudNimbus does not operate. Phase 02 said in terms that CUEC-05 will not be reliably performed;
the treatment available is making it easier to perform and saying plainly what happens when it is not, and
forecasting a user entity's diligence down to likelihood 2 would be forecasting somebody else's behaviour on
no evidence.

### 5.3 The two additions

**DEC-306** left the register open to additions on evidence, and the forecast provides for **two**. They
enter as **one Moderate** and **one High that is then treated to Moderate**, taking Moderate from 14 to 16
and the population from 36 to 38. They will take the next identifiers in sequence, R-37 and R-38.

Providing for additions without knowing what they are looks odd and is deliberate. The programme's standing
habit is that a register grows when evidence arrives, and the two most likely sources of evidence are the
independent testing scheduled for May 2026 and the operation of the controls themselves once they are
running. A forecast that assumed the register would find nothing would be forecasting that the programme
would not work.

### 5.4 The published forecast

**0 High · 16 Moderate · 22 Low = 38.**

Zero High follows from the derivation without being aimed at: every one of the seven High entries carries a
**forecast rating of 8, 10 or 12 — all Moderate**. Six of the seven are forecast onto the best rating
arithmetically available to them, which 03.05 §10 gives as 10, 10, 10, 8, 8, 8 and 10; the seventh is
**R-04**, forecast at **3 × 4 = 12**, one step above its own floor of 8, for the reason §5.2 sets out.

**The two figures are different things and the difference is the point.** 03.05 §10's "8 or 10" is the
**best arithmetically available** rating — what the movement rules permit if every treatment works
completely. §5.2's forecast is what the rules produce when applied to what the plan actually contains, which
for R-04 is a contractual commitment and not a control. A forecast that silently reported the
best-available figure for every entry would be reporting the ceiling of the method rather than an
expectation, and it would have shown R-04 at 8 on the strength of nothing. Where the two coincide, they
coincide because the treatment reaches the floor; where they diverge, the divergence is the honest part.

Zero High does not mean the seven have gone away, and no reader should take it that way — R-01 remains the
most serious thing in this register at a forecast 10, and R-04 at 12 is the highest forecast rating in the
population.

## 6. ADR-0015 — why the forecast was not published until it had been re-derived

**ADR-0015**, decided by Karim Haddad on 2026-04-24 and logged as **DEC-312**: *no close forecast is
published until the harness has proved it reachable.*

The decision exists because of a specific failure. In a previous engagement of this kind, a close forecast
was published that the register's **own movement rules made unreachable**. The number was plausible, it was
approved, it appeared in a board pack, and the closing phase then had to report that it had not been met —
not because the programme underperformed, but because the forecast had never been achievable. The
characteristic version of the error is a forecast showing an entry with impact 4 arriving in the Low band,
which the floor rule forbids; the next most common is a forecast that quietly uses likelihood 1 as a reward
for good treatment.

**The corrective is not better forecasting.** Better forecasting is what everyone believes they are already
doing. The corrective is a rule about publication: **the forecast is not published until the arithmetic has
been re-derived from the register, entry by entry, by applying the movement rules mechanically, and shown to
produce the number that is about to be published.** The derivation table in §5.2 is that re-derivation, and
the build harness re-runs it from the register tables in 03.04 and fails if the counts do not come out at
0 / 14 / 22 for the baseline and 0 / 16 / 22 at 38 with the two provided additions.

The discipline costs something worth naming. It removes the ability to publish an aspirational number, and
aspirational numbers are how programmes signal ambition to their boards. What it buys is that every forecast
in this portfolio can be checked by a reader with the register in front of them, which is a much better
thing to be able to say.

## 7. What the forecast is not

**It is a forecast.** Nothing in this document states that any rating has moved, because as at 2026-04-30
none has. The treatment plan's earliest target date is three weeks away, no control has been tested, no
independent testing has been performed, the ISMS is not yet operational, and the observation window has not
opened.

**Phase 09 reports the actual position**, and the two may differ. Where they differ, the difference is
attributable to one of two things and the distinction matters. It may be an **error** — a movement rule
applied wrongly, a floor missed, an entry double-counted — in which case the forecast was wrong when it was
made and should be reported as such. Or it may be an **event**: something happened during the programme that
contradicted a likelihood judgement. **A likelihood forecast contradicted by an event that actually
occurred is not a forecast that should be honoured.** If an entry that was forecast to fall experiences the
thing it describes, its likelihood is evidenced rather than estimated, and the register must record what
happened rather than what was expected. Phase 09 is required to say which of the two it is dealing with, one
entry at a time, and not to average them away.

## 8. Decision record

**DEC-307 — R-19 and R-20 recorded as immovable rather than given an aspirational target.** Decided by
Elise Fontaine on 2026-04-14. **DEC-312 — no close forecast published until the arithmetic had been
re-derived from the register.** Decided by Karim Haddad on 2026-04-24, recorded as **ADR-0015**.

Alternatives considered and rejected: giving R-19 and R-20 target ratings of 8 to populate the target
column, rejected because reaching 8 would require either a consequence change that has not occurred or the
reserved likelihood value; treating R-20's residual as resolved by the intra-region recovery design,
rejected because availability-zone recovery does not address the loss of the region; and publishing the
close forecast on the strength of the working group's judgement, rejected under ADR-0015.
