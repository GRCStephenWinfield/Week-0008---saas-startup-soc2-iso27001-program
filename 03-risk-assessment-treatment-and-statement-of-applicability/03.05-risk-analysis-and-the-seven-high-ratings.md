## 1. Purpose

Clause 6.1.2 e) requires the analysed risks to be **prioritised for treatment**. Prioritisation that stops
at sorting a column is not prioritisation; it is sorting. This document is what the sort was for: the seven
entries rated High are examined one at a time, and each is asked four questions — why is it High, what
drives the likelihood, what drives the impact, and what would have to be true for it to fall.

The fourth question is the one that makes this document useful. A risk analysis that explains a rating and
does not state the conditions under which the rating would change has produced a judgement that cannot be
audited and cannot be revisited. Every entry below therefore ends with an exit condition and with the best
score that is arithmetically available to it, because for all seven the answer is that **the entry can
never be rated Low** — every one of them carries impact 4 or 5, and the floor rule in 03.02 §5.3 applies to
each — and a reader is entitled to know that before anybody promises otherwise.

The Moderate and Low entries are not analysed individually here. Their ratings are recorded in 03.04, their
treatment in 03.06, and the four retained entries are argued in full in 03.07. Attention is a finite
resource and this phase spends it where 03.02's evaluation criteria say it should.

## 3. R-01 — cross-tenant return of compensation data · 4 × 5 = 20

**Why it is High.** This is the highest-rated entry in the register and the only one at 20. Its impact is a
5 by the definition of the scale itself: cross-tenant exposure of compensation data is the top of the impact
ladder in 03.02, and R-01 is that event described directly rather than by consequence.

**What drives the likelihood.** The rating of 4 — *expected at least once a year* — is not a claim that a
cross-tenant disclosure will occur annually. It is a claim about the class of defect: an authorisation or
query path constructed without the tenant predicate applied. CloudNimbus runs 63 microservices across four
EKS clusters and six Aurora clusters, with a reporting API that serves customer queries from an analytics
read replica, and **SR-01** requires tenant isolation to hold on *every* access path. The number of paths
grows with every feature, and a path is created by an engineer writing a query rather than by anyone
deciding to create one. Above all, **AS-02** — that row-level security is uniformly enforced across every
data access path — entered this programme as an *assumption* carried out of Phase 01, not as a tested fact.
An assumption is a risk that has been promoted without evidence, and rating this entry on the strength of
the assumption would have been assuming the conclusion.

**What drives the impact.** What is returned is another employer's compensation data. It is confidential
under the MSA, it cannot be un-disclosed, it would engage the 48-hour customer notification under **O4**,
and it reaches a population of 640 customers and 1.24 million end users. Multi-tenant isolation is the
confidentiality control that matters in this business; its failure is not a degradation of the service, it
is the failure of the promise the service is sold on.

**What would have to be true for it to fall.** Isolation enforced in one shared data-access layer rather
than in 63 independent implementations; a build-time check that no query path can be constructed which
bypasses the predicate; and independent verification of the multi-tenancy boundary by someone who is not the
team that wrote it — which is why multi-tenancy isolation testing is a named scope in the testing engagement
scheduled for May 2026. If all three hold, likelihood can reach 2. **The impact remains 5 and the best
available rating is 10.** R-01 will never be a Low risk at CloudNimbus.

## 4. R-02 — wrong derived amount reaching a payroll run · 3 × 5 = 15

**Why it is High.** Processing Integrity is not a formality at CloudNimbus; the calculation engine is the
product. It derives overtime, differentials, accrual balances and reimbursement amounts and exports the
result to customers' payroll providers. A wrong number here is a wrong paycheque, and the impact scale puts
a wrong amount reaching pay at scale at 5.

**What drives the likelihood.** The rating of 3 — *expected within the certification cycle* — reflects a
compound requirement rather than a single failure. A defect alone is very likely: the engine changes
constantly, it implements rules that differ by jurisdiction and by customer configuration, and the modal
defect in this domain is not arithmetic but interpretation, where the code faithfully implements a rule that
was understood wrongly. What R-02 requires in addition is that the defect reach a payroll run **undetected**,
which means the reconciliation and the customer's own review both had to miss it. Two things failing
together is what separates a 3 from a 4 here. It is not rated lower because the second failure is not
independent of the first: a rule misunderstood by the engineers who wrote the calculation is likely to be
misunderstood by whoever specified the reconciliation.

**What drives the impact.** Money reaching individuals, at whatever scale the affected rule is configured.
An overpayment recovered is still an employment-relations event for the person it happened to; an
underpayment is worse. It is also the failure mode least likely to be discovered by CloudNimbus first — it
is discovered by an employee reading a payslip, which means the organisation learns about its own most
serious integrity failure from the outside.

**What would have to be true for it to fall.** An independent recalculation on a separate code path rather
than a self-check by the same logic; a reconciliation exception process with a defined clearance SLA and no
silent ageing; and a pre-export variance report the customer can see before the file leaves — which is where
**CUEC-04** and **CUEC-05** sit and why **R-14** exists as its own entry. Likelihood 2 is reachable. **Impact
stays at 5 and the floor is 10.**

## 5. R-03 — privileged account compromised and used to reach production · 3 × 5 = 15

**Why it is High.** A privileged account sits underneath the control that makes this platform safe.
Row-level security is an application-layer predicate; an infrastructure or database administrator operates
below it. The consequence of a compromised privileged account is therefore not "access to some data" but
potential access across tenants, which is impact 5.

**What drives the likelihood.** The rating of 3 is a judgement about a chain rather than an event. The
initial compromise is not unlikely — **R-13**, an employee successfully phished, carries likelihood 4, and
187 people over three years makes that close to expected. What R-03 additionally requires is that the
compromised identity be one holding privileged production access, and that the access be usable at the
moment of compromise. Both of those are properties of the access model rather than of the attacker.
CloudNimbus operates 7 AWS accounts and 4 EKS clusters; the population holding standing elevated rights is
small, and the entry is rated 3 rather than 4 on that basis and on no other.

**What drives the impact.** Reach and deniability. A privileged session can read across tenants, can alter
the audit trail that would evidence what it did — which is why **R-21**, incomplete logging, is a separate
entry and not a footnote to this one — and can persist. The examination and the certification audit both
care about this: it is CC6-series territory on one side and A.5.15, A.5.18 and A.8.2 on the other.

**What would have to be true for it to fall.** No standing privileged access at all: time-bound
just-in-time elevation, granted against a recorded approval, expiring by itself. Phishing-resistant
authentication so that a stolen credential is not a usable credential. Session recording so that use is
reconstructible independently of the account being used. And, critically, the ability to **prove the absence
of standing access** rather than to assert it, because an access model that cannot be evidenced is an access
model the examination cannot rely on. With those in place, likelihood 2 is defensible. **The floor is 10.**

## 6. R-04 — Halcyon Identity unavailable and no end user can authenticate · 4 × 4 = 16

**Why it is High.** This is the entry CloudNimbus can do least about, and that is precisely why it is High.
Halcyon Identity is a subservice organisation carved out of the description: it authenticates the end users
of the platform, and CloudNimbus neither operates it nor can subject it to its own controls. Three of the
twelve data flows — DF-01, DF-05 and DF-07 — cross it.

**What drives the likelihood.** A rating of 4 — *expected at least once a year* — is the honest base rate
for a third-party platform experiencing at least one availability event in a year. Nothing about Halcyon
suggests it is worse than its peers; the rating reflects that CloudNimbus has no lever on the frequency at
all. Where a CloudNimbus-operated component would be rated on the strength of CloudNimbus's controls, this
one is rated on the strength of somebody else's, observed from outside.

**What drives the impact.** Not exposure, and not a wrong amount at scale — which is why it is 4 rather than
5. What happens is that 1.24 million people cannot clock in. Time capture is time-critical in a way that
most SaaS functionality is not: a shift that cannot be recorded at the moment it starts is a shift that has
to be reconstructed afterwards, and reconstructed time is the most reliable route there is to a wrong amount
reaching an individual. That is impact 4 by the scale's own definition, arrived at through availability
rather than through confidentiality.

**What would have to be true for it to fall.** The likelihood would fall only with an authentication path
that does not depend on Halcyon — a second identity provider, or a locally-held fallback credential. Both
were considered and both trade an availability risk for a confidentiality risk at a company with eight
security staff. **R-04 is therefore the register's single shared risk**: transferred in part through the
service availability commitment and service credit regime added when the Halcyon Identity master agreement
was renewed on **2026-03-18**, and disclosed to user entities as **CSOC-14**, so that a user entity knows the
dependency exists rather than discovering it during an outage. Sharing does not reduce the score, and a
contractual commitment does not operate a control — 03.06 §4 records what the renewal is worth and 03.07
§5.2 rests the single forecast step on it. The floor, if the architecture ever changes, is 8.

## 7. R-05 — EU-residency personal data leaving `eu-central-1` · 4 × 4 = 16

**Why it is High.** Obligation **O8** commits CloudNimbus to keeping personal data at rest inside
`eu-central-1` for 41 customers. R-05 is the failure of that commitment through the paths nobody designs.

**What drives the likelihood.** The rating of 4 comes from where the leak would occur. Nobody builds a
feature that copies EU personal data to Virginia. What happens is that a service emits a log line containing
an identifier, a metric carries a label, a trace carries a payload, an error report attaches a request body,
or a backup lands in a bucket whose replication was configured for the estate rather than for the region.
CloudNimbus runs 63 microservices and 214 S3 buckets, and every new service is a new opportunity to create a
path that does not follow the pattern. Phase 02 established the design: the `cnb-security` archive is
region-partitioned with an EU bucket under EU-scoped keys, and only pseudonymised or non-personal telemetry
crosses regions. The risk is not that the design is wrong; it is that the design is a convention and
conventions are followed unevenly — which is exactly the lesson **ML-2** taught this organisation in a
different domain.

**What drives the impact.** Personal information belonging to one tenant's employees leaving the region it
was contractually promised to stay in. It is impact 4 rather than 5 because it is one tenant at a time and
no wrong amount reaches pay. It is not lower because the commitment is explicit, the customers who hold it
chose CloudNimbus partly because of it, and the failure is discovered by inspection rather than by incident
— meaning it can persist unnoticed for as long as the log retention allows.

**What would have to be true for it to fall.** Residency enforced at the export path rather than reviewed
after it: an assertion in the pipeline that fails the build when a path is added that can carry an
identifier out of region, and pseudonymisation applied at source so that nothing crossing a region boundary
carries one in the first place. Those are two different controls addressing two different limbs, which is
why R-05 is one of the three entries carrying **two** treatment items. Likelihood 2 is reachable; **the floor
is 8.**

## 8. R-06 — scheduled retention or deletion job failing silently · 4 × 4 = 16

**Why it is High.** The word doing the work is *silently*. A job that fails loudly is an incident with an
owner and a clock. A job that fails silently is a control that has stopped operating while continuing to
appear on the calendar, and nothing in the environment says so.

**What drives the likelihood.** The rating of 4 rests on the honest status of **AS-01** — that scheduled-job
failures are alerted — which Phase 01 recorded as an *assumption* and carried forward as testable rather
than settled. Where alerting depends on a job reporting its own failure, the failure modes that matter most
are the ones where the job reports nothing at all: it did not start, it started and exited zero having
processed no rows, or it processed a partition that was empty because an upstream filter changed. Retention
and deletion jobs are particularly exposed because their correct output is *nothing visible*. There is no
customer waiting for a result to complain that it did not arrive.

**What drives the impact.** Data retained past its rule is personal information held without a basis. It
breaches the retention schedule RT-01 to RT-08 directly, and where the data belongs to a terminated customer
it reaches obligation **O7**, the 30-day deletion and certification commitment. It is impact 4: one tenant's
personal information at a time, no cross-tenant exposure, no wrong amount reaching pay.

**What would have to be true for it to fall.** Two things, and they are different in kind. First, **heartbeat
alerting** — the absence of a success signal raises the alert, so that the system's silence is itself an
event. Second, a **periodic reconciliation** comparing records that are past their rule against records
actually deleted, which is a detective control that does not depend on the job telling the truth about
itself. R-06 is the second of the three entries carrying two treatment items for that reason. With both,
likelihood 2. **The floor is 8.**

## 9. R-07 — production customer data reaching a non-production environment · 3 × 5 = 15

**Why it is High.** `cnb-staging` and `cnb-dev` are excluded from the SOC 2 system on the strength of a
single control, **SR-12** — non-production holds no production customer data — and **ADR-0007** stated in
terms that this is a claim about a control rather than a fact about the architecture. R-07 is that control
failing, and its consequence is not only a data exposure: it is that the system boundary was drawn wrongly,
retrospectively, from the moment of the copy.

**What drives the likelihood.** The mechanism is the most ordinary shortcut in software engineering. A
customer reports a defect that reproduces only on their data; an engineer restores a snapshot or runs an
export to reproduce it; the copy is made in good faith, to fix the customer's problem, by someone being
helpful. **TH-03** is *a malicious or negligent insider* and the operative word here is emphatically the
second one. The rating of 3 rather than 4 reflects that CloudNimbus's engineering culture already treats
this as prohibited and that the population able to perform it is small; it is not rated lower because
prohibition without technical prevention is a convention, and this organisation has a management letter
about what happens to conventions.

**What drives the impact.** A restore is not tenant-scoped. A production snapshot in a non-production
account contains whatever the source contained, which is every tenant in that cluster, in an environment
with weaker access control, no production monitoring, and — by construction — no place in the system
description. That is impact 5.

**What would have to be true for it to fall.** Prevention rather than prohibition: an account and network
boundary that makes the movement technically impossible, with a named, time-boxed, approved exception path
for the cases where reproduction genuinely requires it. Behind that, masked or synthetic test data so that
the need rarely arises — **A.8.11 data masking** and **A.8.33 test information** are both determined
necessary and both stand at *Planned* at Statement of Applicability v1.0, which is the honest status of a
control that does not exist yet. Likelihood 2 is reachable. **Impact stays 5; the floor is 10.**

## 10. What the seven have in common

Three observations close this analysis, and each of them shaped the treatment plan.

**Five of the seven are about a path, not a place.** A query path, an export path, a log path, a scheduled
job, a copy between environments. CloudNimbus's protections are uniform by design; the risk in almost every
case is that a path exists which does not use them. That is why the treatment items for R-01, R-05 and R-07
are enforcement-at-the-path items rather than policy items — a rule that is applied by convention is a rule
whose coverage is unknown.

**Three of the seven descend from claims that were written down rather than tested, and the three do not
have the same provenance.** **AS-02** became R-01 and **AS-01** became R-06: both were **Phase 01
assumptions**, and Phase 02 converted each into a testable system requirement — AS-02 into **SR-01**,
multi-tenant isolation enforced at the data layer on every access path, and AS-01 into **SR-08**, retention
enforced by scheduled deletion jobs whose failure is alerted. **SR-12** became R-07, and SR-12 is not an
assumption at all: it is a **Phase 02 system requirement** created under **ADR-0007**, on which four scope
exclusions rest. What the three have in common is not their category but their status — each is a claim the
programme depends on and has not yet evidenced. Had they been carried as facts, the top of this register
would be three entries shorter and materially less honest.

**None of the seven can be rated Low, ever.** The best available outcomes are 10, 10, 10, 8, 8, 8 and 10 —
all Moderate. That is the floor rule in 03.02 §5.3 doing exactly what it was written to do, and it is the
reason the forecast in 03.07 shows **zero High and no possibility of these seven disappearing from view.**
Treatment here buys a reduction in likelihood and nothing else, and any report that showed one of these
seven as Low would be reporting a change in the business rather than a change in the controls.
