## 1. Scope of this document

This document carries the **A.5 Organizational** theme of the Statement of Applicability approved at
version 1.0 on **2026-04-24** under **DEC-311**: **37 controls, all 37 determined necessary**. The
methodology behind the determination — the direction of derivation, the meaning of each justification
category, and the standing of the implementation field — is in 03.08 and is not repeated here. The A.6,
A.7 and A.8 themes are in 03.10. The exclusions and the refusals are in 03.11.

The columns are those clause 6.1.3 d) requires. `Necessary` records the determination. `Justification`
records why the control is on the list, and where it reads `Risk` it cites the entries in the register
published in 03.04 that the control addresses. `Implementation` records whether the control **is
implemented** as at the approval date, using one of `Implemented`, `Partial` or `Planned`.

**A.5 is the largest theme in Annex A and, at 37 of 93 controls, carries just under 40 per cent of the
reference set.** It is also the theme where a management system either exists or does not. The
technological controls in A.8 can be built by engineers without a management system behind them; A.5 is
where policy, role assignment, supplier governance, incident process, legal obligation and internal review
live, and an organisation with a thin A.5 has bought security tooling rather than built an ISMS.

## 3. Nothing in A.5 was determined unnecessary, and that is not a default

All 37 organizational controls are on the list. It would be easy to read that as a programme declining to
think, so the reasoning is worth stating: **A.5 is the theme where the arguments for exclusion available to
a small remote-first SaaS company are weakest.** Every one of the 37 concerns something CloudNimbus does —
it writes policy, assigns roles, holds assets, classifies information, grants access, buys services from 84
vendors, responds to incidents, holds records under legal obligation, and is about to be reviewed by an
accredited certification body.

Two candidates were considered and dismissed without reaching the formal proposal that A.7.4, A.7.8, A.7.10
and A.7.13 reached in 03.11. **A.5.32 intellectual property rights** was queried on the ground that
CloudNimbus writes its own software; it survived immediately, because the estate contains open-source
dependencies under licences with obligations and **96 third-party SaaS applications** under terms that
restrict use. **A.5.6 contact with special interest groups** was queried as a formality; 03.11 §5 sets out
why it was kept, and it is the more interesting of the two.

## 4. The seven partially implemented controls

Seven A.5 controls were `Partial` at approval. Each has a treatment item behind it in 03.06, and each is
partial for a reason worth recording rather than for want of attention.

**A.5.9 inventory of information and other associated assets.** Phase 02 baselined **1,046 assets inside
the ISMS**, of which 800 are inside the SOC 2 system. The inventory exists and is complete as a snapshot;
what is partial is the **maintenance mechanism**. R-34 — an asset missing from the inventory is outside
every control applied to its class — is a risk about drift, not about the baseline. The control reaches
full implementation when the reconciliation between the cloud asset feed, the MDM device list and the
register runs on a schedule with a named owner. R-23 is cited alongside because assisting a data subject
request within the ten business days obligation O6 requires knowing where the data is.

**A.5.12 classification of information and A.5.13 labelling of information.** The classification scheme is
approved; the labelling of information in flight is not complete. This pair is where R-35 — leave-type data
reaching a report or export seen by a party who should not see it — actually lives, and it is a good
illustration of why the two controls are separate in Annex A. A scheme that classifies leave type as
sensitive and a reporting layer that does not carry the label is a classification control implemented and a
labelling control not.

**A.5.22 monitoring, review and change management of supplier services.** Supplier assurance is collected;
the **monitoring** of the eleven sub-processors, two of which are the subservice organisations, against
agreed service levels is not yet on a defined cadence with recorded output. The phrasing matters: the two
are **inside** the eleven and not alongside them, and 02.10 §6 records that presenting the vendor
classifications as disjoint sets is the error ML-3 itself recorded. R-04 is cited because Halcyon Identity is the
one supplier whose unavailability stops every end user authenticating, and R-16 because a vendor
misclassified in the register does not receive the assurance obligations that go with its class. This
control is worth watching: it is the Annex A control that pairs with clause 9.2 when an organisation
outsources work and does not scope what it outsourced.

**A.5.23 information security for use of cloud services.** CloudNimbus runs entirely on AWS across **7
accounts, 4 EKS clusters and 3 regions**, and uses 96 SaaS applications internally. The AWS side is
governed; the **96-application SaaS estate** is where the partial status sits — acquisition, review and
exit are not yet uniformly applied to applications a team can buy with a card. One of the eleven controls
new in :2022, and discussed further in 03.10 §6.

**A.5.28 collection of evidence.** This is the Statement of Applicability's acknowledgement of **ML-1** in
the 2025 Type I management letter: access review evidence was retained as email threads and screenshots, in
a form that would not support a Type II sample. The shared evidence store established under DEC-112 is
being built out; until artefacts are captured in a defined form with a defined retention, the control is
partial. R-25 — the evidence architecture depends on one person — is cited alongside R-21, and it is the
risk that would be least visible in a control walkthrough and most damaging in fieldwork.

**A.5.35 independent review of information security.** Camberwell Risk Partners is engaged for the clause
9.2 internal audit, and the programme is scheduled for Q3 2026. Nothing has been reviewed yet, so the
control is designed and not operating. R-32 is cited because an independent review is the mechanism most
likely to catch an ISMS scope statement that has become inaccurate, and O10 gives that 30 days.

## 5. The two planned controls, and why they are the two

**A.5.7 threat intelligence** and **A.5.30 ICT readiness for business continuity** are `Planned`. Both are
new in :2022, and neither has a predecessor in the 2013 Annex that CloudNimbus could have inherited.

**A.5.7** is planned because a 187-person company with an 8-person security function has no threat
intelligence capability and cannot manufacture one by subscribing to a feed. The design that was approved
routes strategic and tactical intelligence through the special interest group memberships that **A.5.6**
provides, supplemented by vendor and cloud-provider advisories, with a named owner who reviews and acts.
That is why 03.11 §5 argues A.5.6 is the control that makes A.5.7 work here. R-12 and R-13 are cited
because dependency vulnerabilities and phishing campaigns are the two threat classes where intelligence
arriving early changes what CloudNimbus does.

**A.5.30** is planned because ICT continuity is being rebuilt around the recovery objectives rather than
documented as it stands. **RTO 4 hours and RPO 15 minutes** are committed, and the disaster recovery
exercise that will test them is one of the three treatment items in 03.06 falling after 2026-07-01. R-19
and R-20 are cited, and they are two of the four risks 03.07 retains: R-19 because a recovery shortfall in
a real event is foreseeable and its consequence has not changed, and R-20 because obligation O8 forbids the
cross-region recovery that would reduce it. **A.5.30 is therefore a control that is necessary and will not
make its risks move**, which is a perfectly ordinary situation and one a Statement of Applicability should
be able to represent without embarrassment.

## 6. The seven contractual and legal justifications

Seven A.5 controls are on the list because CloudNimbus has undertaken something, and would remain on it if
every associated risk rating fell.

| Control | The undertaking behind it |
|---|---|
| A.5.5 Contact with authorities | Regulatory notification pathways, including those a personal data incident may engage; the DPO role sits with Tobias Lund |
| A.5.20 Addressing information security within supplier agreements | O5 — a current sub-processor list and 30 days' notice with a right to object; the data processing addendum terms flowed to 11 sub-processors |
| A.5.29 Information security during disruption | SC-01, the 99.9% monthly availability commitment, and the continuity of security controls during an incident |
| A.5.31 Legal, statutory, regulatory and contractual requirements | O1 to O12 as a register with an owner; the identification obligation itself |
| A.5.32 Intellectual property rights | Open-source licence obligations in the dependency estate and the terms of 96 third-party SaaS applications |
| A.5.33 Protection of records | O7 — deletion or return of customer data within 30 days of termination, with certification; and the records the examination and certification depend on |
| A.5.34 Privacy and protection of PII | The data processing addendum, O6, O8 and the notice position that follows from collecting personal information directly from 1.24 million end users |

**A.5.34 deserves a sentence of its own.** It is one Annex A control, and it sits opposite **18 Privacy
criteria** in the trust services criteria. That disproportion is not a defect in either document; it is the
clearest single demonstration that the two catalogues are organised around different questions, and it is
where a large share of the 21 SOC 2-only controls in the Phase 04 library comes from. 03.12 quantifies it.

Neither this control nor any other in this Statement of Applicability is a determination that CloudNimbus
complies with any privacy law. Nothing in the ISO/IEC 27001 certification route and nothing in a SOC 2
examination produces such a determination, and the portfolio does not imply one.

## 7. What the A.5 rows commit CloudNimbus to

Twenty-eight controls asserted as implemented on 2026-04-24 are twenty-eight assertions Northgate may test
at Stage 2 and, where they overlap the trust services criteria, that Ashcombe &amp; Doyle may test across the
observation window. The Statement of Applicability is the document a Stage 1 auditor reads first, and the
implemented rows are the ones that turn into evidence requests.

Nine rows — seven partial, two planned — are commitments to a build. They are not softer than the other
twenty-eight; they are simply commitments with a date rather than assertions with an artefact. The
Statement of Applicability **will be reissued when the ISMS is declared operational on 2026-06-15**, and
the reissue is where those nine rows either change status or become a conversation about why they did not.
