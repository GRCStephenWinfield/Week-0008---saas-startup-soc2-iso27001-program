## 1. Purpose

This is the register. Thirty-six entries, R-01 to R-36, published at **MS-04 on 2026-04-10** and admitted to
the baseline by **DEC-306**, decided by Elise Fontaine on the same date.

Everything before this document exists to make the register defensible: 03.01 is the process clause 6.1.2
requires, 03.02 is the criteria clause 6.1.2 a) requires, and 03.03 is the taxonomy identification was run
against. Everything after it is what happens as a result: the seven High entries analysed in 03.05, the
treatment decisions in 03.06, the acceptances and the forecast in 03.07, and the control determinations in
03.08 to 03.10 that the risk-justified rows of the Statement of Applicability cite back to.

## 2. How to read a row

Each row carries eight fields and no more, because a register with twenty columns is a register nobody
maintains.

**ID** is permanent. R-14 is R-14 for the life of the programme; identifiers are never reused and never
renumbered, which is what makes a cross-reference from a Statement of Applicability row or a treatment item
mean something two years later.

**Risk** is a sentence describing an event and its consequence, not a topic. "Access management" is a topic
and cannot be rated; "access is provisioned before the manager approval that authorises it is recorded" is
an event with a consequence and can be. Every row in this register passes that test, and where a row reads
awkwardly it is usually because the awkwardness was the price of being specific.

**Threat** is the TH identifier from 03.03 judged to be the proximate source. One per row.

**Owner** is a named person: the person who can authorise the treatment and would be asked to explain the
failure, as clause 6.1.2 c) 2) requires and clause 6.1.3 f) relies on.

**L**, **I** and **Score** are likelihood, impact and their product on the 5 × 5 scale in 03.02. **Band**
follows the thresholds **High ≥ 15 · Moderate 8–12 · Low ≤ 6** and is derived, never asserted.

## 4. The arithmetic

**Score equals L × I on every one of the thirty-six rows.** There is no adjusted score, no weighting factor
and no override; a row whose score did not equal the product would be a row somebody had edited by hand, and
the register workbook build fails on exactly that condition.

**Every band follows the thresholds.** High ≥ 15, Moderate 8–12, Low ≤ 6. No row is banded by judgement. The
gap property in 03.02 §4 means no row can be near a boundary: the achievable scores are
{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 16, 20, 25} and the thresholds at 6/8 and 12/15 fall in the gaps.

**The band totals are 7 High · 17 Moderate · 12 Low = 36.**

1 + 3 + 3 = **7 High**. 7 + 2 + 6 + 2 = **17 Moderate**. 8 + 4 = **12 Low**. 7 + 17 + 12 = **36**.

Five achievable scores are unoccupied — 1, 2, 3, 5 and 25 — and the reasons are worth a sentence each. There
is no 25 because no entry combines the maximum consequence with a present occurrence. There is no 1, 2, 3 or
5 because those require either impact 1, which nothing in the register carries, or likelihood 1, which is
reserved.

Both extremes of both scales are empty and neither emptiness is an accident. **Likelihood 5** is unoccupied
because it means *occurring now, or expected more than once a year*, and nothing in this register was judged
to be occurring on 2026-04-30; the two entries with the strongest evidential support for a high frequency —
**R-08** and **R-10**, which descend directly from the 2025 management letter, from ML-2 and ML-1
respectively — sit at 4, which is what the evidence actually supports. The third management letter matter,
ML-3, became **R-16**, and it sits at 3 rather than 4 because vendor classification is an activity performed
a few times a quarter, not one performed continuously.

**R-09 carries likelihood 4 on a different basis, and the difference is worth separating out.** No
management letter matter concerns access provisioned ahead of the approval that authorises it, so there is
no finding behind its rating. The 4 is an inference from the shape of the control: the grant is made by one
person responding to a request that is usually urgent, the authorising approval is a separate record made by
a second person, and nothing in the present tooling makes the second a precondition of the first. A control
whose correct operation depends on two people performing two acts in the right order, with no gate between
them, is expected to fail at least once a year across 187 staff and ten owners. That is likelihood 4 by the
anchor in 03.02, arrived at from the design rather than from a finding — and TP-11 is written to convert the
inference into a gate, which is why it reads *no approval record, no grant*.

**Likelihood 1** is unoccupied because it is reserved. **Impact 1** is unoccupied for a
different reason: an event with negligible consequence is an issue, not a risk, and admitting one would have
diluted the register with entries that no treatment decision would ever turn on.

**Twelve entries carry impact 4 or 5**, and by the floor rule in 03.02 §5.3 none of them can ever be rated
Low while its consequence stands. That is a third of the register with a permanent Moderate minimum, and it
is the single most important structural fact about this baseline for anyone reading the forecast in 03.07.

## 6. What this register does not contain

**No residual rating column.** Residual risk is what remains after treatment, and treatment has not been
performed on 2026-04-30. Publishing a residual column at baseline would mean publishing a prediction in a
field that readers will take for a measurement. The forecast in 03.07 is presented as a forecast, in its own
document, with its derivation shown.

**No closed entries, and no removals.** Nothing has been closed and nothing has been deleted. A risk that
stops being likely is **re-rated**, not deleted, because the alternative loses the history that makes the
next assessment comparable. The register can grow; it does not shrink.

**No aggregate score.** There is no single number for "CloudNimbus's risk", because the products of an
ordinal scale do not sum to anything meaningful and a total would be quoted in a board pack within a month.

**No entry is stated to have moved.** Every rating here is the baseline rating as at MS-04. 03.07 forecasts;
Phase 09 reports.

## 7. Decision record

**DEC-306 — thirty-six risks admitted to the baseline; the register is not closed to additions on evidence.**
Decided by Elise Fontaine on 2026-04-10. The second limb is the operative one. A baseline that is closed
becomes a document rather than a register, and the two most likely sources of a new entry are the
penetration test scheduled for May 2026 and the operation of the controls themselves. The forecast in 03.07
provides for two such additions without knowing what they will be, which is the honest way to hold open a
population you expect to grow.

Related records: **DEC-301** and **ADR-0011** (one register, both frameworks), **DEC-305** (scoped to the
ISMS boundary), **DEC-302** to **DEC-304** and **ADR-0012** (the scale and the movement rules), **DEC-307**
(R-19 and R-20 recorded as immovable).
