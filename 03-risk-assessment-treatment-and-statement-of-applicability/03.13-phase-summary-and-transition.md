## 1. What Phase 03 fixed

Phase 01 established who decides and against what. Phase 02 established what is in. **Phase 03 established
what could go wrong, what CloudNimbus is doing about it, and which controls are necessary as a result.**

It baselined a risk register of **36 entries, R-01 to R-36 — 7 High, 17 Moderate, 12 Low** — scored
likelihood (1–5) × impact (1–5) with bands at **High ≥ 15, Moderate 8–12, Low ≤ 6**, and it did so once for
both frameworks: **one register serving clause 6.1.2 and the CC3 series**, scoped to the ISMS boundary and
therefore to **1,046 assets rather than 800**.

It fixed three movement rules and defended each: a rating moves on **likelihood**, not impact, unless the
consequence itself has changed; **likelihood 1 is reserved for the not reasonably foreseeable**; and **eight
is a floor**, because a 3 × 4 entry moving on likelihood alone reaches 2 × 4 and stops.

It named **fourteen threat sources, TH-01 to TH-14**, and reported that **TH-14 — an activity not
performed, performed late, or performed and not evidenced — generates fifteen of the thirty-six entries**,
more than any attacker-driven source. The modal risk in a services business is not an adversary; it is a
control that was supposed to run and did not, or ran and left nothing behind.

It recorded treatment under clause 6.1.3 a) as **31 modify, 4 retain, 1 share, 0 avoid = 36**, published
**TP-01 to TP-34** with owners and dates, and stated the zero rather than manufacturing an avoidance
CloudNimbus did not perform. **Thirty-one of the thirty-four items are due before 2026-07-01**, because a
control that is not operating on the morning the observation window opens cannot be tested across the full
period.

And it produced the **Statement of Applicability at version 1.0, approved 2026-04-24**: **93 Annex A
controls, 91 determined necessary, 2 determined not necessary**; justified **74 by the risk assessment, 11
by a contractual or legal requirement, 6 by business requirement or good practice**; with implementation
status stated as **62 implemented, 21 partially implemented, 8 planned**. 74 + 11 + 6 = 91. 62 + 21 + 8 = 91.
91 + 2 = 93.

Milestones **MS-04** (risk assessment complete, 2026-04-10) and **MS-05** (risk treatment plan and
Statement of Applicability approved, 2026-04-24) were met.

## 2. The five architectural decisions

| ADR-0013 | Ninety-one of ninety-three Annex A controls determined necessary; two excluded; four argued and refused | 2026-04-20 | Karim Haddad |
| ADR-0014 | No risk avoided, and recording that rather than manufacturing one | 2026-04-14 | Karim Haddad |
| ADR-0015 | No close forecast is published until the harness has proved it reachable | 2026-04-24 | Karim Haddad |

**ADR-0013 is the phase's centre of gravity**, and its content is the four refusals rather than the two
exclusions. A.7.4, A.7.8, A.7.10 and A.7.13 were each proposed for exclusion on the strength of a true fact
— CloudNimbus is remote-first and operates no data centre — and each proposal failed a single follow-up
question. The two exclusions that survived, A.7.11 supporting utilities and A.7.12 cabling security, rest
on the **absence of the thing the control protects** rather than on the presence of a supplier. That
distinction separates an exclusion from a carve-out, and 03.11 §2 draws it explicitly because *"our cloud
provider does it"* is the most common defective exclusion justification in this market.

**ADR-0015 is a lesson institutionalised rather than a design decision.** A close forecast published
without re-deriving it from the register's own movement rules is a forecast the closing phase may have to
report missing. The corrective is not better estimation; it is refusing to publish until the arithmetic has
been re-derived and shown to produce the figure.

## 4. Known gaps carried out of this phase

These are stated rather than left to be discovered. Each has an owner and a destination phase.

**Twenty-nine of the ninety-one necessary controls are not fully implemented.** Twenty-one partial, eight
planned. That is the correct use of the clause 6.1.3 d) status field at a v1.0 approved two months before
the ISMS is declared operational, and it is also a build backlog with a deadline. **Six of the eight
planned controls are technological**, and A.8 stands at seventeen of thirty-four implemented.

**All eleven controls new in :2022 are Partial or Planned, and none is Implemented.** CloudNimbus is
certifying for the first time and has no legacy implementation to inherit for any of them. 03.10 §6 sets
out why this clusters the way it does; it remains the most examinable single fact in the Statement of
Applicability.

**Three treatment items fall after 2026-07-01** — the disaster recovery exercise, the internal audit
programme, and an availability architecture change requiring a maintenance window. Each is a control that
cannot be tested across the full observation window, and each is disclosed rather than re-dated to look
tidy.

**R-19 and R-20 cannot move, and R-24 sits on the floor.** The immovables are recorded as immovable under
DEC-307 rather than given aspirational targets. **R-20 is the register's clearest instance of a commitment
to a customer creating a risk to that same customer**: obligation O8 forbids the cross-region recovery that
would reduce the consequence of losing `eu-central-1`.

**The mapping layer is an assertion.** 03.12 §4 states it: no authority blesses a crosswalk between Annex A
and the trust services criteria, and where the mapping is wrong both deliverables inherit the error. The
**112 dual-purpose controls** in the Phase 04 library are where that exposure concentrates.

**Two assumptions carried from Phase 01 remain assumptions.** **AS-01** — scheduled-job failures are
alerted — is what R-06 depends on, and A.8.16 monitoring activities is `Partial`. **AS-02** — row-level
security is uniformly enforced across every data access path — is what R-01 depends on, and R-01 is the
register's highest entry at 4 × 5 = 20. Neither has been tested. The penetration testing programme
scheduled from 2026-05-04 is the first occasion on which either will be.

## 5. Entry criteria for Phase 04

Phase 04 builds the **unified control framework and policy architecture**: the **148-control library** —
112 dual-purpose, 21 SOC 2 only, 15 ISO only — and the **19-policy suite**. It can begin because Phase 03
has delivered four things it needs.

| Input | What Phase 04 does with it |
|---|---|
| The Statement of Applicability — 91 necessary Annex A controls with implementation status | Determines which Annex A controls the library must implement, and which 82 of them carry a criterion as well |
| The treatment plan TP-01 to TP-34, with owners and dates | Becomes the build schedule for the 29 controls not yet fully implemented |
| The risk register with justification citations | Supplies the per-control rationale that stops the library becoming a matrix of identifiers with no reasoning |
| The coverage analysis in 03.12 | Supplies the derivation of the 21 SOC 2-only and 15 ISO-only populations |

Phase 04 also answers **DC5 and DC8** of the description criteria, which Phase 02 left to it.

The dependency runs in the order it does for a reason worth restating: **the Statement of Applicability
determines what is necessary, and the library determines how it is done.** Building the library first
produces a control set that then has to have a Statement of Applicability written to match it, which is the
reverse-engineered determination 03.08 §3 refuses.

## 6. What could still go wrong

**The forecast is a forecast.** Phase 03 publishes an expected close position of **0 High, 16 Moderate, 22
Low = 38**, derived by applying the movement rules to every entry rather than asserted. Phase 09 reports
what actually happened, and the two may differ — for reasons that are events rather than errors. A
likelihood forecast contradicted by something that actually occurs is a forecast that must not be honoured.

**Twenty-nine controls have to be built by people who also have day jobs, and the schedule has no float in
it.** The programme runs at **4.6 FTE-equivalents** across an ISMS scoped to the whole of a 187-person
company, and **all thirty-one treatment items due before the observation window opens fall inside a single
seven-week corridor** — the earliest target date on the plan is 2026-05-15 and the last before the window is
2026-06-30. Forty-six days, thirty-one control implementations, ten owners, and a fixed date behind them
that does not move. That is the phase's largest delivery exposure and 03.06 §8 sets out why the corridor is
the shape it is. The failure mode is not a wrong scope; it is a correct scope thinly covered, and it will
show first in the controls whose evidence depends on a cadence nobody owns — A.5.28 collection of evidence,
A.5.35 independent review, A.7.4's monitoring review.

**A.5.28 is ML-1 with a new label.** The 2025 Type I management letter recorded that access review evidence
was retained in a form that would not support a Type II sample. The control is `Partial`. Remediating a
control is not the same as remediating the evidence trail it leaves behind, and the two are being confused
somewhere in this programme right now.

**A.8.32 is ML-2 with a new label.** Change-management peer review enforced by convention rather than by
branch protection was adequate in design and inadequate in enforcement. Until branch protection is applied
across the whole repository estate, R-08 at 4 × 3 = 12 is a live entry and the control is `Partial`.

**The Statement of Applicability will be read line by line.** It is the document a Stage 1 auditor reads
first. Every one of the 62 implemented rows is an assertion that may be tested; every exclusion invites the
single question that decides whether the determination process was real. CloudNimbus has answered that
question twice and declined to answer it four more times, and 03.11 records both.
