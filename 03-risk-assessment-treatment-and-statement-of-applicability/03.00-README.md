## 1. What this phase is

Phase 02 finished by handing over a population: 1,046 assets inside the ISMS, 800 of them inside the SOC 2
system, twelve data flows, twelve categories of personal information, ten commitments and twelve system
requirements. Phase 03 is where that population is finally asked the only question that matters about it —
**what could go wrong here, how badly, how likely, who owns it, and what is being done about it.**

Two standards ask that question in different registers. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 asks it as **clause 6.1.2**,
which requires a defined information security risk assessment process that produces consistent, valid and
comparable results, and **clause 6.1.3**, which requires that the results be treated, compared against
Annex A, published as a Statement of Applicability, formulated into a plan, and approved by the people who
own the risks. The trust services criteria ask it as **CC3.1 to CC3.4** — objectives specified with
sufficient clarity to identify risk, risks identified and analysed, fraud considered, and significant
change assessed. CloudNimbus answers both from **one register**, and the argument for that decision is
**ADR-0011**.

The phase's centre of gravity is not the register. It is the two things the register makes possible: a
treatment plan whose dates are governed by an external constraint rather than by convenience, and a
Statement of Applicability that says *no* twice and refuses to say it four more times.

## 2. What Phase 03 establishes

Phase 03 fixes the risk position of the programme at baseline. It establishes the **methodology** under
clause 6.1.2, scoped to the ISMS boundary rather than to the SOC 2 system (**DEC-305**) — 1,046 assets, not
800 — and explains why one register serves both frameworks. It fixes the **criteria**: likelihood 1–5 by
impact 1–5, bands at **High ≥ 15 · Moderate 8–12 · Low ≤ 6**, with an impact dimension defined in the terms
this business actually fails in, and three movement rules that the rest of the portfolio is bound by
(**ADR-0012**, **DEC-302** to **DEC-304**).

It establishes a **threat taxonomy**, TH-01 to TH-14, and reports the finding that taxonomy produces:
**TH-14 — an activity not performed, performed late, or performed and not evidenced — generates fifteen of
the thirty-six register entries**, more than any attacker-driven source in the list.

It publishes the **baseline register**: **36 risks, R-01 to R-36, rated 7 High · 17 Moderate · 12 Low**,
each with a named owner drawn from the people who can actually do something about it (**DEC-306**). It
analyses the **seven High ratings** one at a time. It records the **treatment decisions** under clause
6.1.3 a) — **modify 31 · retain 4 · share 1 · avoid 0** — and the **treatment plan TP-01 to TP-34**, of
which **31 items are due before 2026-07-01**. It argues the **four retained risks** individually, names
**R-19 and R-20 as immovable** (**DEC-307**), and publishes a **forecast of the register at close** that
was not published until the arithmetic had been re-derived from the register and shown to produce it
(**ADR-0015**, **DEC-312**).

And it produces the **Statement of Applicability**: **93 Annex A controls, 91 determined necessary, 2
determined not necessary** (**DEC-309**), with **4 proposed for exclusion and refused** (**DEC-310**) —
which is the chapter this phase is really about.

Milestone **MS-04 — risk assessment complete, baseline register published** was met on 2026-04-10.
Milestone **MS-05 — risk treatment plan and Statement of Applicability approved** was met on 2026-04-24.

## 3. What Phase 03 deliberately does not establish

Phase 03 decides what is necessary. It does not build it.

**No control is designed here and no policy is written here.** The Statement of Applicability determines
that a control is necessary and states whether it is implemented; the unified control library that
implements the determination — 148 controls, 112 serving both frameworks — is Phase 04's, and the
dependency runs in that direction deliberately. A library assembled before the risk assessment would have
been assembled from a catalogue.

**No control is tested here, and no evidence is evaluated.** The penetration test is scheduled for May
2026. The ISMS is planned to be declared operational on 2026-06-15. The observation window opens on
2026-07-01. Stage 1, Stage 2, the clause 9.2 internal audit and the examination fieldwork all lie beyond
this phase's horizon, and none of their outcomes appears in these documents in any form.

**No risk is stated to have moved.** This is the constraint that shapes the phase most. Phase 03 publishes
a *forecast* of where the register will stand at close. It does not report a single re-rating as having
happened, because at this phase's vantage none has. Phase 09 reports the actual, and the two will differ
wherever an event contradicted a likelihood judgement — which is a reason, not an error, and 03.07 says so
in advance rather than in hindsight.

Two gaps are carried openly. The **completeness of the register** rests on the identification method in
03.01 and on the assets and flows Phase 02 inventoried; a risk arising from an asset nobody recorded is a
risk nobody assessed, which is why **R-34** exists. And the **Annex A to trust services criteria mapping**
in 03.12 is CloudNimbus's own assertion, blessed by nobody, which makes it the single largest exposure the
integrated operating model creates.

## 4. The vantage this phase writes from

`01.00 §8` established that the metadata date `2026-08-11` is the repository's publication date and nothing
more. Phases 01 and 02 share a vantage of **2026-03-31**.

**Phase 03 speaks as at 2026-04-30.** MS-04 on 2026-04-10 and MS-05 on 2026-04-24 have both occurred and
are described in the past tense; the workshops, the ratings, the treatment decisions and the approval of
the Statement of Applicability at v1.0 are done. Everything dated after 2026-04-30 is planned, scheduled or
targeted, and never done. Where a treatment plan item carries a target date in June, that date is a target
and not an achievement. Where the Statement of Applicability records a control as *Planned*, that is its
status on 2026-04-24 and not a prediction that it stayed there.

The register is **not closed to additions**. DEC-306 admitted thirty-six entries to the baseline and said
explicitly that evidence produced later in the programme may add more. An entry added because something was
found is a register working; a register that never grows is a register nobody is reading.

**The forecast derivation in `diagrams/` is the phase's most load-bearing artefact and is indexed as
EV-309.** It is the entry-by-entry working behind the published close position, and it exists as a
published artefact rather than as a working paper because **ADR-0015** forbids publishing a close forecast
until the arithmetic has been re-derived from the register and shown to produce it. A derivation nobody can
see is a derivation nobody can check, which is the condition ADR-0015 was written against.

Every workbook in `trackers/` is generated by parsing the narrative in these documents, with assertions on
the counts and the arithmetic. The band totals, the treatment option counts and the forecast are all
re-derived from the tables in 03.04, 03.06 and 03.07, and the build fails if any of them stops adding up.

## 8. How to read this phase

Read **03.02** before **03.04**. A register read without its scale is a column of numbers, and the three
movement rules in 03.02 are what make every later re-rating in this portfolio defensible or indefensible.
Then **03.03** for where the entries came from, **03.04** for the register itself, and **03.05** for the
seven entries that carry the programme's attention.

**03.06** and **03.07** are the treatment side: what is being done, what is not being done and why, and the
forecast. **03.08** to **03.11** are the Statement of Applicability, and **03.11** is the chapter to read if
you only read one — it is the record of four exclusions that were argued for and refused.

For a single question, the fastest routes are: *how is a rating justified* — 03.02; *what is the worst of
it* — 03.05; *what is being fixed and by when* — 03.06; *what is not being fixed* — 03.07; *which Annex A
controls apply and why* — 03.08 to 03.10.

## 9. Standing framing

Nothing in this phase is a determination of compliance with any law. The SOC 2 deliverable is a
restricted-use examination report carrying a CPA firm's opinion; there is no such thing as a SOC 2
certification, and no document here describes CloudNimbus as certified against it. The ISO deliverable is a
certificate issued by an accredited certification body — Northgate Certification Services, Ltd., accredited
by ANAB — and not by ISO, which certifies nobody. Points of focus are not requirements, and **Annex A is
not the requirements**: conformity is assessed against clauses 4 to 10, which is precisely why a control can
be excluded and the ISMS still conform. A SOC 2 finding is an **exception** or **deviation**; an ISO finding
is a **nonconformity**; this phase does not swap them.
