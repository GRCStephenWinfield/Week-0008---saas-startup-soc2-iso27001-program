| Risk | The `R-nn` it treats. One item may treat one risk; one risk may need more than one item |
| Option | Modify · retain · share · avoid |
| Treatment | What will actually change — a control, a configuration, a procedure, a contract term |
| Annex A controls | Which necessary controls this implements or strengthens |
| Criteria | Which trust services criteria the resulting control contributes to |
| Owner | A named individual |
| Target date | A date. **If the item is required to be operating before the observation window opens, that date is 2026-07-01 and the item's target must precede it** |
| Expected effect on the rating | The likelihood step claimed, and why that step and not a larger one |
| Evidence the treatment will leave | **Required.** What a sampler will find, where, and for how long |
| Completion | Date, and how completion was verified — by whom, against what |

**Two fields exist because of experience rather than theory.**

*Expected effect on the rating* forces the claim to be made before the work rather than after it, which is
what makes the close forecast derivable at all — and what makes an underperforming treatment visible as an
underperforming treatment rather than as a revised estimate.

*Evidence the treatment will leave* is the field that would have prevented **ML-1** in the 2025 Type I
management letter. A control that works and leaves nothing sampleable is a control that will produce a test
exception in a Type II, and the cheapest moment to notice that is while the control is still being designed.
