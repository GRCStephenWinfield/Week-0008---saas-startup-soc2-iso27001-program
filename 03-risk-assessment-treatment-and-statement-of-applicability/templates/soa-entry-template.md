One entry per Annex A control, all 93, whether necessary or not. Clause 6.1.3 d) requires the necessary
controls, the justification for including them, **whether they are implemented**, and the justification for
excluding any that are not necessary.

| Field | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Control | `A.5.n`, `A.6.n`, `A.7.n`, `A.8.n` |
| Title | **The Annex A title, exactly.** Not a paraphrase, not a local name. A reviewer checks these against the standard and a paraphrase reads as a control the organisation invented |
| Necessary | Yes or No |
| Justification — if necessary | One of: the **risk assessment**, citing the `R-nn` entries it treats; a **contractual or legal requirement**, citing it; or a **business requirement or good practice**, stating it |
| Justification — if not necessary | **What is absent that makes the control unnecessary.** "Our cloud provider does it" is not an exclusion justification — that is a carve-out, which is a different mechanism recorded in a different document |
| Implemented | Implemented · Partial · Planned. **Stating Partial or Planned is the correct use of the field, not an admission** |
| Owner | The named individual accountable for the control operating |
| Where implemented | The policy, procedure or technical mechanism |
| Evidence | What operating the control leaves behind |

**On exclusions.** An exclusion should be **falsifiable**: a reader must be able to say what would make the
control necessary again. A.7.11 and A.7.12 are excluded because CloudNimbus operates no facility containing
information processing equipment — and a rack in the Denver suite would make both necessary immediately, and
notifiable to the certification body within thirty days under obligation O10. An exclusion that cannot be
falsified is an opinion.

**On the direction of derivation.** Controls are determined necessary by the risk assessment and by legal
and contractual requirements. Annex A is then used to **check** that nothing necessary was missed, as clause
6.1.3 c) requires. Filling this template out by working down Annex A produces a checklist, and a checklist
will reliably implement a control the organisation does not need and miss one it does.
