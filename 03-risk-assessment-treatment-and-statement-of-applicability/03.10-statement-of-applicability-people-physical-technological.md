## 1. Scope of this document

This document carries the remaining three themes of the Statement of Applicability approved at
version 1.0 on **2026-04-24**: **A.6 People (8 controls)**, **A.7 Physical (14)** and **A.8
Technological (34)** — **56 rows**, of which **54 are determined necessary** and **2 are not**. The
A.5 Organizational theme, and the theme-by-theme reconciliation, are in 03.09. The methodology is in
03.08. The two exclusions in A.7 and the four A.7 controls proposed for exclusion and refused are
argued in full in 03.11; this document records the determinations and does not re-argue them.

The columns are those clause 6.1.3 d) requires. Where `Justification` reads `Risk` it cites the
register entries from 03.04 that the control addresses; for the two controls determined not
necessary, the cell carries the exclusion justification instead, as clause 6.1.3 d) requires.

**A.6 is the smallest theme and the one a remote-first company can least afford to treat as
human-resources paperwork.** CloudNimbus has **187 staff across 31 US states and 4 countries**, of
whom **164 are inside the people component of the SOC 2 system** because they can reach production
or customer data or operate a procedure the system depends on. There is no office through which
those people pass, no supervisor who sees their screen, and no perimeter that distinguishes a
working day from a Sunday evening. The people controls are therefore doing more work here than they
would in a company with a building.

**A.6.7 remote working is the control that would have been an afterthought in a co-located company
and is foundational in this one.** It is `Implemented`, and it is the reason the refusal of **A.7.8
equipment siting and protection** in 03.11 matters: A.6.7 governs the working arrangement and A.7.8
governs the siting and protection of the equipment used in it, and the two are not substitutes.

The row cites **R-13 and R-26**, and it was first drafted citing R-28. That was wrong and is worth
saying so: **R-28 is an intrusion into the Denver office**, which is the one place in the
organisation where nobody works remotely. Remote working is a control about the 187 people who are
not in that building — the device in the home (R-26) and the person reached by a message with no
colleague at the next desk to ask about it (R-13). Citing the office-intrusion entry against the
remote-working control was the register being read for a keyword rather than for a risk.

**A.6.3 is the single partial row.** The awareness programme exists and role-based modules for
engineering and for customer success are not yet complete. The charter sets a **95% completion
bar**, and R-31 records the risk of falling below it; R-13 is cited because phishing is the threat
the awareness programme is most directly aimed at, and because a 4 × 3 = 12 Moderate entry driven by
social engineering does not move on the strength of an annual slide deck.

**A.6.2 and A.6.6 are contractual/legal** because the employment terms and the confidentiality
undertakings are instruments CloudNimbus has executed, and they would remain necessary if every
associated risk fell to Low. **A.6.4 disciplinary process** is a business requirement: no register
entry is about the absence of a disciplinary process, and a company that asserts consequences for
policy breach without one has asserted something it cannot perform.

**Both exclusions rest on the absence of the thing the control protects, not on the presence of a
supplier.** That distinction is the whole argument of 03.11 §2 and it is stated in the justification
cells above rather than only in the narrative, because the cell is what a Stage 1 auditor reads. AWS
operates supporting utilities and cabling at extraordinary scale; that fact appears in this
programme as **CSOC-01 and CSOC-02** in the carve-out disclosed under DC7, and it is a *carve-out*,
not an exclusion justification. Had CloudNimbus written "our cloud provider does it" in those two
cells, it would have claimed an exclusion on the strength of a control existing somewhere else,
which is a different and much weaker proposition than the one it is entitled to make.

**R-28 appears in eight of the twelve necessary A.7 rows** — an unauthorised person obtains physical
access to the Denver office, rated 2 × 2 = 4 Low, and **retained** rather than modified under 03.07.
That concentration is honest rather than lazy: the physical estate consists of one meeting suite and
231 endpoints, and the register contains one entry about the suite and several about the endpoints.
It is also the reason the physical theme produced the phase's most instructive arguments. A theme
whose risks are all Low is exactly where an organisation is tempted to clear the deck, and 03.11
records that CloudNimbus was tempted four times and refused four times.

**Two A.7 rows were re-cited before approval, for the same reason as the four in §4.4.** **A.7.6
working in secure areas** was first drafted citing R-28 and R-35; R-35 is leave-type data reaching a
report or export, which is a data classification failure in a reporting layer and has no physical
dimension at all. The row now cites **R-28** alone, which is the only entry in the register about
the one area CloudNimbus has. **A.7.13 equipment maintenance** was first drafted citing R-12 and
R-26; R-12 is a vulnerability in a third-party software dependency, and 03.11 defends A.7.13 on
**MDM patching of endpoints** — a different population and a different failure. The row now cites
**R-13 and R-34**: an endpoint that is not current is the device a phished session lands on, and an
endpoint that is not enrolled is R-34's case exactly, an asset outside every control applied to its
class. The refusal argument in 03.11 §3 and the citation now say the same thing.

**A.7.4 and A.7.10 are `Partial`.** A.7.4 physical security monitoring is partial because controlled
entry and a visitor log operate, and the review of what they record is not yet on a defined cadence.
A.7.10 storage media is partial because removable media is prohibited by policy and the technical
enforcement of that prohibition on **231 managed endpoints** is not yet complete — which is
precisely the point 03.11 makes about a prohibition being an implementation that has to be enforced
and evidenced.

### 4.1 Why A.8 carries the backlog

**Seventeen of thirty-four A.8 controls are fully implemented — exactly half.** Every one of the
eight planned controls in the Statement of Applicability except A.5.7 and A.5.30 is here, and eleven
of the twenty-one partials are here. The distribution is not a comment on the engineering
organisation; it is arithmetic about what a control build costs.

An organizational control is written, approved, communicated and operated by a process. A
technological control is **configured, deployed across a live multi-tenant estate, and left
running** — 7 AWS accounts, 4 EKS clusters, 63 microservices, 6 Aurora clusters, 214 S3 buckets and
231 endpoints. The work competes with a product roadmap, and the deployment of a control that
touches data paths carries its own change risk. A Statement of Applicability that showed A.8 at the
same completion rate as A.5 in April would be describing something that did not happen.

**Four of the six planned A.8 controls address risks that the treatment plan carries anyway.**
A.8.11 data masking and A.8.33 test information both address R-07 — production customer data
reaching a non-production environment — which sits at 3 × 5 = 15 High and depends today on
**SR-12**, the requirement that non-production holds no production customer data. SR-12 is a
control-dependent boundary rather than an architectural one, as ADR-0007 records; masking and
test-information management are what turn it from a rule into an enforced property. A.8.29 security
testing in development and acceptance is planned ahead of the testing programme that runs from May.
A.8.23 web filtering is planned and is discussed at §5 below and in 03.11 §5.

### 4.2 A.8.30 outsourced development, and the reading it rests on

**A.8.30 is `Necessary` and `Implemented`, and CloudNimbus does not outsource development.** Phase
02 described an in-house engineering organisation of **78 people**, and there is no development
contract, no offshore team and no agency behind any part of the platform. A control determined
necessary with no described subject matter is exactly the shape 03.11 §4 says invites the auditor's
single question, so the reading this row rests on is stated here rather than left to be inferred.

**The reading is a supply-chain reading, and it is the one ISO/IEC 27002:2022 supports.** The
control's subject is code that CloudNimbus runs and did not produce under its own secure development
life cycle. That population is not empty at CloudNimbus; it is most of the platform by volume. The
63 microservices are built on an open-source dependency estate, on base container images, on
infrastructure modules and on vendor-supplied client libraries, and every one of those is code
produced outside CloudNimbus's SDLC that executes inside its trust boundary. A.8.30's requirements —
that the security requirements CloudNimbus applies to its own development are applied to code
produced elsewhere, that the acquisition arrangements address them, and that evidence of testing and
of provenance is obtained — apply to that population without any strain.

**A.5.21 does not reach it.** Managing information security in the ICT supply chain is a control
about the *supplier relationship*: what is required of the party, what is agreed, what is monitored.
It is answered by contract terms and vendor assurance. A.8.30 is a control about the *development
practices* that produced the code — how it was reviewed, tested and secured before it arrived. An
open-source dependency has no supplier relationship at all in A.5.21's sense, and treating A.5.21 as
covering A.8.30's subject would leave the practices unaddressed while the paperwork looked complete.

The row therefore cites **R-08** and **R-12**. R-12 — a vulnerability in a third-party dependency
exploited before it is remediated — is the direct expression of the risk in the register. R-08 — a
change deployed to production without recorded peer review — is cited because code entering the
estate from outside the SDLC is code that has not passed CloudNimbus's review gate, and A.8.30 is
where that gap is closed rather than noticed. **`Implemented` means the acquisition and review
requirements are in force**, not that the estate has been proved clean; A.8.8 management of
technical vulnerabilities is `Partial` and carries the detection limb.

### 4.3 Three rows worth explaining individually

**A.8.10 information deletion is the only A.8 control justified as contractual/legal, and it is the
control most directly opposite the register's most operational High risk.** Obligation **O7**
requires deletion or return of customer data within 30 days of termination with certification of
completion, and **O8** and the retention rules RT-01 to RT-08 govern in-life deletion. R-06 — a
scheduled retention or deletion job fails silently and data is kept past its rule, 4 × 4 = 16 High —
is addressed here in substance, and yet the justification cell reads `Contractual/legal` because the
control would be necessary on the strength of O7 alone. The consequence is visible in 03.12: **R-06
reaches only two Annex A controls by citation**, A.8.15 logging and A.8.16 monitoring activities,
and its Annex A footprint therefore reads thinner than a 16 High deserves. That is a property of the
justification taxonomy, not a gap in treatment — the treatment items are in 03.06 — and it is
recorded here rather than left for someone to find.

**A.8.15 logging and A.8.16 monitoring activities are the pair that makes silent failure loud.**
Both are partial. Logging is partial because coverage is uneven across 63 microservices and because
the `cnb-security` archive's EU partitioning has to hold for every producer, not most of them; R-05
is cited alongside R-21 for that reason. Monitoring is partial because alerting exists for
infrastructure health and not yet for the completion of scheduled jobs — which is the exact shape of
R-06 and of assumption **AS-01** carried from Phase 01, that scheduled-job failures are alerted.

**A.8.32 change management is partial and it is ML-2 in the Statement of Applicability.** The 2025
Type I management letter recorded that change-management peer review was enforced by team convention
rather than by branch protection: the design was adequate, the enforcement was not. Branch
protection is being applied across the repository estate, and until it is applied everywhere the
control is partial. R-08 sits at 4 × 3 = 12 Moderate, and it is the register's plainest instance of
TH-14 — an activity not performed, performed late, or performed and not evidenced.

### 4.4 Four rows whose citations were re-made

03.08 §3 offers the citations as the test of whether the derivation ran the right way round, which
means a citation that does not survive reading is worse than no citation at all: it invites the
auditor to ask how the other ninety were arrived at. Four A.8 rows failed that reading and were
re-cited before v1.0 was approved, and the failures had a common shape — a register entry that
mentioned an endpoint was reached for because the control also mentioned an endpoint.

**A.8.7 protection against malware** was first drafted citing R-13 and R-26. A stolen laptop is not
a malware event, and no malware control has ever prevented one. The row now cites **R-03 and R-13**:
phishing is how malicious code arrives at a person (R-13), and a compromised endpoint holding
privileged production access is what it is for (R-03).

**A.8.19 installation of software on operational systems** was first drafted citing R-08 and R-26.
Endpoint loss has nothing to do with what is installed on an operational system. The row now cites
**R-08 and R-12** — an unreviewed change reaching production, and a third-party component brought
onto a system without passing the pipeline that would have found the vulnerability in it.

**A.8.23 web filtering** was first drafted citing R-13 and R-26. Web filtering does nothing whatever
about a device that has been stolen; its subject is what a browser is allowed to reach while the
device is in the user's hands. The row now cites **R-13** alone, which is the risk 03.11 §5.1 argues
the control on and the only one in this register it addresses.

**A.8.34 protection of information systems during audit testing** was first drafted citing R-18 and
R-21. R-21 is incomplete audit logging, which is a different subject wearing a similar word: A.8.34
concerns the disruption that testing and assessment activity can cause to operational systems, not
the sufficiency of the log record. The row now cites **R-18** alone — the availability entry — which
is the exposure that a penetration test, a restore rehearsal or an auditor's query against
production actually creates.

## 5. Nothing in A.6 or A.8 was determined unnecessary

All 42 controls in the people and technological themes are on the list, and one of them was the
obvious candidate to leave off.

**A.8.23 web filtering** is `Planned` and it is `Necessary = Yes`. It is routinely excluded by SaaS
engineering organisations on the ground that filtering the web access of senior engineers is
counterproductive and that the population is trusted. CloudNimbus kept it, and 03.11 §5 sets out why
at length. Two facts belong here: it is **one of the eleven controls new in :2022**, which makes an
exclusion conspicuous rather than routine at a first certification; and R-13, an employee
successfully phished, sits at 4 × 3 = 12 Moderate with 187 remote staff and no corporate network to
filter at.

## 6. The eleven controls new in :2022

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 introduced **eleven controls with no counterpart in the 2013 Annex**. CloudNimbus
determined **all eleven necessary**.

| Control | Title | Theme | Necessary | Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A.5.7 | Threat intelligence | Organizational | Yes | Planned |
| A.5.23 | Information security for use of cloud services | Organizational | Yes | Partial |
| A.5.30 | ICT readiness for business continuity | Organizational | Yes | Planned |
| A.7.4 | Physical security monitoring | Physical | Yes | Partial |
| A.8.9 | Configuration management | Technological | Yes | Partial |
| A.8.10 | Information deletion | Technological | Yes | Partial |
| A.8.11 | Data masking | Technological | Yes | Planned |
| A.8.12 | Data leakage prevention | Technological | Yes | Planned |
| A.8.16 | Monitoring activities | Technological | Yes | Partial |
| A.8.23 | Web filtering | Technological | Yes | Planned |
| A.8.28 | Secure coding | Technological | Yes | Partial |

**Five are Planned and six are Partial. None is Implemented.** That is the most informative single
fact in this Statement of Applicability, and it has a structural cause rather than an embarrassing
one.

The **2013 to 2022 transition closed on 31 October 2025**, so every certificate issued in 2026 is
against :2022 and there is no version of this programme in which the eleven are optional. But
CloudNimbus is certifying for the **first time**. An organisation transitioning from a 2013
certificate arrives at these eleven with a functioning ISMS, an established evidence trail, and in
several cases an existing practice that merely needs to be named — its configuration baselines
become A.8.9, its SIEM use cases become A.8.16, its secure development standard becomes A.8.28.
**CloudNimbus has none of that to inherit.** Each of the eleven is a control designed and built from
nothing between January and June 2026, which is why they cluster in the two columns that are not
`Implemented`.

Two further observations follow, and both are uncomfortable in the right way.

**The new controls are disproportionately technological.** Seven of the eleven are in A.8, and A.8
is the theme with the longest build. The revision that added them was written against a decade of
cloud operations, and the controls it added — cloud service use, configuration management, deletion,
masking, leakage prevention, monitoring, web filtering, secure coding — are precisely the ones a
2013-era ISMS handled implicitly or not at all. A cloud-native company gets no relief from that; it
gets more of it.

**One of the eleven — A.7.4 physical security monitoring — is among the four controls proposed for
exclusion and refused.** It is worth sitting with. The most defensible exclusion argument available
to CloudNimbus anywhere in Annex A was about a control introduced in :2022, in a theme where the
company has almost no estate, and it still failed. 03.11 explains why: the suite has controlled
entry and a visitor log, both are monitoring in the sense the control means, and excluding a control
because it is implemented lightly is a category error. An exclusion of a control new in :2022, at a
first certification, in the theme a certification body will probe hardest at a company with no data
centre, would have been the single most examined line in the document.
