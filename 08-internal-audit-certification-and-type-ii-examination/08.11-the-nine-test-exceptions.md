## 1. What an exception is

**A test exception is a fact about a sample.**

It is not a conclusion that a criterion was not met. It is not a control failure in the sense the word
usually carries — several of the nine below are controls that operated exactly as written on every other
occasion in a hundred and eighty-four days. And it is not a conclusion about an opinion.

**Section IV of a SOC 2 report carries the criterion, the controls management states meet it, the tests the
service auditor performed, and the results — including the exception and the service auditor's evaluation
of it.** That last clause is the one that gets lost. An exception is not disclosed as a bare fact and left
for the reader to weigh; it is disclosed with what the practitioner made of it. **The service auditor
modifies the opinion only if the deviations mean the applicable trust services criteria were not met**, and
the four available opinion forms — unmodified, qualified, adverse, disclaimer — turn on that evaluation and
on nothing about the count.

**Test exceptions are disclosed in Section IV with the service auditor's evaluation, and they do not
automatically modify the opinion.** A report that carries exceptions and an unmodified opinion is an ordinary
combination, and one that carries exceptions and a qualified opinion is also an ordinary combination. **This
phase does not say which this examination produced**, because fieldwork closed on 2027-02-05 and the report
has not been issued, for the reason §6 gives.

One refinement is owed, because §1's own sentence is not quite exact for this engagement. Where a population
was tested in full — and 08.10 §4 shows that Ashcombe &amp; Doyle tested in full every population at or below
sixty — **the exception is a fact about the population, not about a sample.** That is a stronger statement
about the window and a narrower one about anything else. Two deviations in a sample of forty drawn from four
hundred would license an inference about the four hundred. Two deviations in a population of forty license
no inference at all: they are simply what happened, forty times, twice.

**A second distinction does more work than the first and is lost more often.** A deviation is recorded
against a **control**; the criterion is met or not met by **everything that serves it**. Exception 4 is a
deviation against `CNB-C-082`, and CC8.1 is served by nine controls — peer review at merge, pipeline
exclusivity, ticket completeness with a rollback plan, environment separation, automated test suites
including the calculation-engine regression pack, static and composition analysis, secure design review, the
emergency path itself, and the daily reconciliation that catches what the others miss. Two late
retrospective approvals in fifteen emergency changes is a fact about the ninth of those nine. **What it
means for CC8.1 is a question about all nine together**, and that question is answered by the service
auditor and not by this table.

The inverse is what makes exception 5 heavy and exception 1 lighter than its rate looks. **P4.2 is served by
`CNB-C-126` and `CNB-C-127` across the whole window, and by `CNB-C-149` for 65 of its nights** — 08.08 §6's
criteria table says so, and `CNB-C-149` cites P4.2 and P4.3. So a failure across the first two is the whole
of the support for that criterion in the affected region **for the 119 nights before `CNB-C-149` existed**,
and the sixty-eight are inside those hundred and nineteen. That is a slightly weaker claim than *and by
nothing else*, and it is the true one; it is stated in its weaker form deliberately, because it is the claim
the near-modification in §5 rests on and a claim carrying that weight has to be the one that survives being
checked. **A1.2 is served by five controls** — the probe, the nightly backup
job, the restore test, the Terraform module set with region-pinned recovery, and the availability
post-incident review — so one missed restore test in six sits beside four other mechanisms that operated
throughout. **The rate is a property of the control. The weight is a property of the criterion**, and a
reader who ranks the nine by percentage will rank them wrongly: 16.7% against A1.2 is a smaller matter than
37.0% against P4.2 by more than the arithmetic suggests, and 13.3% against CC8.1 is smaller than either.

## 2. The nine

| # | Criterion | Control | Window population | Deviations | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A1.2** | `CNB-C-098` | 6 | 1 | **16.7%** |
| 2 | **A1.1, A1.2** | `CNB-C-096` | 1 event | 1 | — |
| 3 | **PI1.3** | `CNB-C-108` | 58 | 2 | **3.4%** |
| 4 | **CC8.1** | `CNB-C-082` | 15 | 2 | **13.3%** |
| 5 | **P4.2** | `CNB-C-126`, `CNB-C-127` | 184 nights × 8 rules × 3 regions | 68 nights on 1 rule in 1 region | **37.0% of the nights on that rule** |
| 6 | **CC9.2** | `CNB-C-092` | 24 | 2 | **8.3%** |
| 7 | **P5.1** | `CNB-C-129` | 58 | 1 | **1.7%** |
| 8 | **CC6.2** | `CNB-C-037` | 40 | 2 | **5.0%** |
| 9 | **CC6.3** | `CNB-C-040` | 47 revocations | 3 | **6.4%** |

**Exception 2 carries a dash rather than a rate, and the dash is the finding.** `CNB-C-096`'s probe
performed a read, so no burn registered against the error budget through seventy-one minutes in which reads
succeeded and writes failed. Every word of the control was satisfied. There is no population of occurrences
in which the control sometimes worked and sometimes did not, because the control worked on all of them and
measured the wrong thing on the one that mattered. **A design deficiency has an event, not a rate**, and
expressing it as one-in-something would invite the reader to divide it into insignificance.

**Exception 5's denominator is the one to read carefully.** The population is 184 nights across 8 retention
rules in 3 regions — 4,416 job-nights — and the deviation is 68 nights on one rule in one region. Stated
against the whole population the rate would be 1.5%, which is true and useless. **Stated against the nights
of the rule that failed it is 37.0%**, and that is the number the table carries, because the failure was not
distributed across the estate: it was total, on RT-02, in `eu-central-1`, for sixty-eight consecutive
nights. Choosing the wider denominator would have been the most defensible-looking dishonesty available in
this chapter.

**Two of the nine arose in December and are new here.** Exceptions 8 and 9 were found in the window's final
weeks and had no Phase 06 or Phase 07 identifier when fieldwork opened. The other seven are `D-06-01` to
`D-06-04` and `D-07-01` to `D-07-03`, recorded and analysed at the time, with the clause 10.2 test applied
in the phase that found them.

**Where the nine fall is worth one paragraph, because the distribution is not random and is not flattering
in the way it first looks.** Four sit in the Common Criteria — change management, vendor assurance, access
provisioning and access review — and five sit outside them, in Availability, Processing Integrity and
Privacy. **The 2025 Type I examined Security alone.** So five of the nine are in categories no service
auditor had ever looked at, and four are in the one that had been examined, by a firm that opined only on
design and produced a management letter naming two of the four subject areas. **A category that has never
been examined and a category that has been examined once for design are not two degrees of the same thing**,
and the distribution says less about where CloudNimbus is weak than about where anybody had previously
looked.

**And `CNB-C-082` deserves the arithmetic spelled out, because 13.3% is the second-largest rate in the
table and the smallest matter in it.** The population is the fifteen emergency changes, not the 312 changes
deployed in the window. The emergency path is a deliberate, designed escape hatch used fifteen times in 312
— under five per cent of deployments — and every one of the fifteen reached production through the pipeline,
with a ticket, with a rollback plan. Two of the fifteen were retrospectively approved on the sixth and
seventh business day against a five-business-day requirement. **Expressing that as 2 of 312 would be
dishonest and expressing it as 13.3% is correct and invites a misreading**, which is why the population
column says fifteen and this paragraph says what fifteen is.

## 3. Where each one came from

**Every one of the nine was found by CloudNimbus before it was found by the service auditor.** That is a
fact worth stating once and not celebrating. It is what a working programme looks like rather than an
achievement, and the two exceptions that make it least comfortable are exception 1, where the finding is
that a control did not operate and nobody noticed for four weeks, and exception 5, where the programme found
the failure only because somebody was assembling a retention sample for the examination.

**Exception 3's second occurrence is the one that proves a corrective action was right about itself.**
`CA-06-02` recorded in September that `CNB-C-108` carries a two-business-day service level and a weekly
Tuesday queue, and that **for three days of every seven the second cannot deliver the first**. The second
exception was raised on **Thursday 2026-12-03**, reached business day two on Monday 2026-12-07, and was
cleared at the queue on **Tuesday 2026-12-08** — business day three. It is the worked example 06.13 §9 set
out in advance, occurring on exactly the day of the week it predicted, three months later, because the
corrective action had not yet answered which of the two figures was wrong.

**Exception 9 is the one that shows the register and the examination testing different things about the same
control.** The register moved **R-10** — *a quarterly access review is completed late, or completed and not
evidenced* — on the same day, and on the same evidence, that produced the exception the examination recorded
against the same control three weeks later: the CAL-06 review sat on **2026-12-29** and asked what had
happened to the revocations; the CC6 access family was tested in fieldwork's second week, **2027-01-18 to
01-22**. The review moved R-10 because neither limb of its own described event happened. The examination
recorded a deviation against `CNB-C-040`'s other limb, because three revocations were late. **A late
revocation is not a late review**, and a programme that could not hold both statements at once would have had
to make one of them false. **No part of the examination sat inside the observation window**, and a sentence
that put the two tests on one day would have said it did.

**And exception 9 is ML-1's and exception 4 is ML-2's subject matter, eighteen months on.** `04.05` maps ML-1
to `CNB-C-040`, which is exception 9; exception 1 is the missed restore test and has nothing to do with
either. The 2025 management letter told CloudNimbus that its access review evidence would not support a Type
II sample and that its change-management peer review was enforced by convention rather than by branch
protection. Both controls were rebuilt. Both subject matters produced an exception anyway. **Remediating a control is not the same as remediating
everything the control's failure mode can reach**, and Phase 09 reports that without softening it.

## 5. The near-modification

On **2027-01-28**, at the third fieldwork status meeting, **Meredith Vance raised that the engagement team
was considering whether exception 5 required a modification of the opinion.**

The facts on the table were these. **Sixty-eight consecutive nights.** A retention rule that was not
enforced at all for that period — not partially, not late, but not at all. **A whole region**, serving the
41 customers whose contracts oblige CloudNimbus to keep their personal data at rest inside it. And **the
most sensitive category of personal information the platform holds**: geolocation captured from the
individual at clock-in, under a thirteen-month rule that exists irrespective of contract term, on 1,847,206
capture points that should not have been there.

Management's response set out the countervailing facts and did not argue the finding.

**Seven of the eight retention rules ran correctly in all three regions throughout the window.** The
failure was confined to RT-02 in `eu-central-1` and to the object the 2026-08-17 partitioning migration left
behind.

**The failure was detected inside the period and corrected within four days of discovery** — found
2026-10-23, job re-generated 2026-10-24, catch-up deletion run 2026-10-25, completion record verified
2026-10-27 against partition counts taken independently before the run.

**A new control was admitted on its evidence.** `CNB-C-149` was added to the library on 2026-10-28, inside
the window, with its own limits recorded on the face of the decision rather than discovered afterwards: 65
nights of 184, an alerting limb that has never fired, and a blind spot on partial non-deletion that
ADR-0031 states in terms.

**And the affected records were deleted and the deletion verified**, with the backup residue window tracked
under `CNB-C-128` and the certification held open until it expired on 2026-11-29.

Management also stated what it could not offer. It could not show that any control would have found the
failure — nothing in the library could tell the difference between a rule with nothing to delete and a rule
that could no longer see what was due, and the examination's own evidence request is what found it. It could
not show that the other seven rules were verified by anything other than the class of record that was wrong
about the eighth; `AS-36` says so.

**And one thing has to be said about the shape of the five paragraphs above.** They are management's case,
set out at management's length. **The engagement team's stated concerns were not minuted.** EV-825 records
that Meredith Vance raised the question on 2027-01-28 and records management's response; it does not record
what she said in support of it, because CloudNimbus's minute of a status meeting is CloudNimbus's record of
what CloudNimbus said and the engagement team keeps its own working papers. **So this chapter carries one
side of a question it is calling open**, and the asymmetry is not evidence that the countervailing facts
outweigh anything — it is evidence about whose minute this is. A reader who weighs five bolded paragraphs
against two sentences is weighing a record, not an argument, and the missing half is not missing because it
was weak.

> **At this vantage the question is open.** It is the service auditor's to answer, it will be answered in
> the report, and **management does not know the answer on 2027-02-05.** This chapter ends there, on
> purpose. **A programme that could tell you now what the opinion will be would be describing something
> other than an independent examination.**

## 6. The boundary of this chapter

Nothing in this phase states what the opinion will be, and this chapter states that it does not.

**DEC-815**, taken by Rahul Bhargava on 2027-02-03, records the position in one line: the near-modification
is reported as **open** rather than predicted. The alternative was available and would have been easy — the
countervailing facts are strong, the programme believes them, and a sentence beginning *management expects*
would have cost nothing to write and everything to defend. **ADR-0015's rule, that nothing is published
until it has been proved reachable, is the same rule whether the thing being forecast is a register band or
a practitioner's conclusion**, and this is the fourth phase to apply it.

The assertion, the representation letter under O12, the description including DC9, the report planned for
**2027-02-26**, its restricted-use distribution under O2 and the bridge letter under O3 are all Phase 09's.
**None of them exists on this vantage.**
