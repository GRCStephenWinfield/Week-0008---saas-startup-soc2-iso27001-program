## 1. One hundred and eighty-four days, and a population that is finally complete

The Type II observation window opened on **2026-07-01** and closed on **2026-12-31**. That is **MS-16**, it
is the last milestone the programme controls, and nothing about it required anybody to do anything on the
day. What changed at midnight is not the controls and not the evidence. It is the **denominator**.

Three phases have now written operating records against this window and each of them refused the same piece
of arithmetic. 06.12 published a quarter and declined to state a deviation rate for `CNB-C-098` because
three of the control's six occurrences had not happened. 07.12 published fifty-eight days and declined
again, in sharper terms: *one in five, one in six and twenty per cent are three different numbers and only
one of them will be the honest one, in January, when the population exists.* This chapter is January's
document, and the population exists.

**That refusal, held for six months across two phases, is the most disciplined thing this portfolio has
done, and this is the chapter that gets to spend it.** Spending it means stating the rates and then not
over-reading them, because a rate computed on a complete population is an exact description of the window
and still not a conclusion about anything else.

## 2. The window's operating record

| Measure | July | August | September | October | November | December | Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Days | 31 | 31 | 30 | 31 | 30 | 31 | **184** |
| Changes deployed | 47 | 51 | 62 | 54 | 51 | 47 | **312** |
| Alerts triaged | 1,412 | 1,656 | 2,034 | 1,904 | 1,742 | 1,588 | **10,336** |
| Severity-1 or Severity-2 incidents | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **1** |
| Security incidents | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| `CNB-C-098` restore tests performed | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | **5** |
| `CNB-C-149` nights run | 0 | 0 | 0 | 4 | 30 | 31 | **65** |

Every row sums. 31 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 = 184. 47 + 51 + 62 + 54 + 51 + 47 = 312.
1,412 + 1,656 + 2,034 + 1,904 + 1,742 + 1,588 = 10,336.

**The one incident row is the September Severity-1**, `INC-2026-031`, and it is the only one in the window.
06.05 owns it and nothing here re-opens it. **The zero on the security incident row is a genuine zero and
not an absence of looking**: 10,336 alerts were acknowledged and dispositioned under `CNB-C-067`, and the
disposition on each is the record that says the analyst decided it was not one. A zero produced by a
detective control with a five-figure population is a different kind of zero from one produced by silence.

**Fifteen emergency changes were deployed across the window under `CNB-C-082`** — nine in the third
quarter, three in October and November, and three in December — against a total of 312. The two that were
retrospectively approved late are `CHG-2026-1147` and `CHG-2026-1149`, both deployed on 2026-09-08 and both
approved on the sixth and seventh business day. `CHG-2026-1248`, which 07.12 left open with its five-business-day
window expiring on **2026-12-02**, was approved inside it. The window's emergency population is
therefore **fifteen with two late**, and the monthly log review limb has a population of **six**, all
performed.

## 3. Availability: six monthly figures, one missed, and still no window average

**`us-east-1`: July 99.98% · August 99.97% · September 99.84% · October 99.96% · November 99.95% ·
December 99.94%. `eu-central-1`: 100% in all six.**

November's figure rests on **22 minutes of unavailability against a monthly allowance of 43.2 minutes** in
a thirty-day month; December's on **27 minutes against 44.64** in a thirty-one-day month. Both are inside
SC-01. September's seventy-one minutes are not, and **SC-01 was missed in September for the 599 customers
served from `us-east-1` and met for the 41 served from `eu-central-1`** — the disclosure 06.06 drafted at
its vantage and re-confirms here, on ADR-0028's standing rule that a disclosure written in the week of an
incident is re-confirmed at period end.

**There is still no window average, and this is the sixth phase to refuse the same arithmetic.** `EC-09`'s
sampling unit is one calendar month for one region, SC-01 is a monthly commitment, and a mean of six
monthly figures is a number the contract does not contain. The six-month `us-east-1` mean would be 99.94%,
which is comfortably above the commitment and would conceal the only month that matters. The refusal is not
a stylistic tic. **A commitment measured monthly and reported as a mean has been reported against the wrong
period**, and the customer who lost seventy-one minutes in September did not lose an average.

**December's rise in unavailable minutes over November — 27 against 22 — is not a trend and this chapter
will not treat it as one.** Two months is two points. Both are inside the allowance, both are an order of
magnitude below September, and the December figure includes no incident of any severity.

## 4. `CNB-C-098` and `CNB-C-149`: the two rates the window can now carry

**`CNB-C-098`'s December occurrence was performed on 2026-12-16. Five of six.**

And now — only now — the window rate is stateable: **one missed occurrence in a population of six is
16.7%.** The August occurrence did not happen, was recorded as `D-06-01`, and was deliberately not
re-performed and back-dated under **ADR-0026**. `IS-19` remains open and is irreducible: the occurrence
cannot be recovered, and the denominator carries it permanently.

The reason the number appears here and was refused twice is worth stating in one line, because it is the
whole of the discipline: **the population is complete.** A deviation rate is a proportion, a proportion
needs a denominator, and a denominator that is still accruing produces a number that will be wrong by the
time anybody reads it. Nothing else changed. The missed test is the same missed test it was in September.

**`CNB-C-149` ran on 65 of 65 nights and raised 0 alerts.** The control was admitted on **2026-10-28**
under DEC-706 and ADR-0031, and its window population is **65 nights of the window's 184**. Both halves of
that sentence are load-bearing. Sixty-five of sixty-five is a clean operating record for a daily control
across the period it existed. Sixty-five of one hundred and eighty-four is the honest denominator, and
**the 119 nights the control did not exist are the nights `D-07-01` covers** — sixty-eight of them
consecutively, on the RT-02 rule in `eu-central-1`, which is exception 5 in 08.11.

Zero alerts remains what 07.12 said it was: an empty population on the alerting limb. The control has run
sixty-five times and has never had to fire, and a detective limb that has never fired has not been shown to
fire. That is not a criticism of the control. It is a statement about what sixty-five clean nights can and
cannot support, and it belongs beside the sixty-five rather than underneath it.

## 5. Nineteen entries move at one review

The December **CAL-06** register review was held on **2026-12-29**, chaired by Karim Haddad, and it is the
largest single event in the register's life. Its record is **GOV-32**; **DEC-809** carries the review's
outcome and **DEC-810** carries the one movement that had to be argued against itself.

**Entering: 38 — 8 High · 18 Moderate · 12 Low. Leaving: 38 — 0 High · 17 Moderate · 21 Low.**
0 + 17 + 21 = 38.

| Band | Entering | Leaving |
|---|---|---|
| High | 8 | **0** |
| Moderate | 18 | **17** |
| Low | 12 | **21** |
| Total | 38 | **38** |

**Nineteen entries moved. Seven were held with a stated reason. Twelve already-Low entries did not move.**
19 + 7 + 12 = 38. **Nothing was closed and nothing was removed** — thirty-six entries have been on the
register since 2026-04-10 and two were added on evidence, which is the only growth the register has ever
experienced.

### 5.1 The seven held

| Entry | Rating | Why held |
|---|---|---|
| R-09 | 4 × 3 = 12 | Access provisioned ahead of recorded manager approval — **2 of 40 in the window, tested in full** |
| R-18 | 3 × 3 = 9 | The Severity-1 of 2026-09-08 |
| R-19 | 2 × 5 = 10 | Immovable — likelihood 1 is reserved and the consequence has not changed |
| R-20 | 2 × 5 = 10 | Immovable, and `eu-central-1` still cannot be failed over |
| R-24 | 3 × 4 = 12 | Its event occurred in October and was re-rated upward then, under DEC-715 |
| R-37 | 2 × 5 = 10 | Immovable |
| R-38 | 3 × 4 = 12 | Admitted in October on its own event |

**Every one of the seven is held because its described event occurred or because it cannot move**, and the
two reasons are not the same reason. R-19, R-20 and R-37 sit at likelihood 2 with an impact of 5 and have
nowhere to go: the next likelihood step is 1, which `03.02` reserves for the not-reasonably-foreseeable,
and no consequence has changed. R-09, R-18, R-24 and R-38 could have moved and were not allowed to, because
the window produced the event each of them describes.

**R-09 is the sharpest of the four, because the same review that held it moved nineteen others on window
evidence.** R-09 says access is provisioned before the manager approval that authorises it is recorded, and
the window's forty provisioning requests contain two that were. Holding an entry whose event occurred twice
in forty is the least interesting decision the review took and the one that makes the other nineteen
readable.

### 5.2 The six that moved two steps

**R-01** moved from 4 × 5 = 20 to **2 × 5 = 10**, on the R-37 remediation *and* the October penetration
test scoped deliberately at that remediation. Two facts sit behind it and neither would carry it alone: the
control changed in May, and in October **41 crafted access paths** including the original vector and eleven
variants of it were run against it and none returned another tenant's data. **A control that changed and
was then tested is a different proposition from a control that changed**, and 08.04 states the caveat that
goes with it.

**R-05** moved from 4 × 4 = 16 to **2 × 4 = 8**, on DEC-512's region-partitioned security log archive and a
window in which nothing left the region. Eight is the floor: impact 4 cannot be rated Low.

**R-08** moved from 5 × 3 = 15 to **3 × 3 = 9 — two steps and not three.** This is **DEC-810** and it is the
movement the review argued hardest. R-08 rose to likelihood 5 in September on nine emergency-path changes
in ninety-two days, and `CA-06-03`'s corrected path has six weeks of evidence behind it at the review date.
**Six weeks is not a window.** Three steps would have taken R-08 to 2 × 3 = 6 and to the same rating as
entries whose evidence runs the full period, on a corrected path that has been observed for a third of one.

**R-10** moved from 4 × 3 = 12 to **2 × 3 = 6**, and it is the one to get right. §5.4 does that.

**R-13** moved from 4 × 3 = 12 to **2 × 3 = 6**, on the phishing-resistant factor `CNB-C-032` now requires
on every path that reaches production, tested across the window. `03.07` §5.2 forecast R-13 stopping at
3 × 3 = 9 on the reasoning that phishing-resistant credentials change what a successful phish yields rather
than whether one occurs. The review moved it the extra step because the control changed the *path*, not the
yield: a credential phished from one of 187 people no longer reaches production at all.

**R-14** moved from 4 × 3 = 12 to **2 × 3 = 6**, on CUEC-05. This is the movement with the weakest evidence
in the nineteen and the review recorded it as such: 411 of 640 customers confirmed and 229 did not respond,
and 06.09 §6 said in terms that the 411 are unverified self-assertions carrying no assurance weight. **What
moved R-14 is not the 411.** It is `CNB-C-111` — the band check that compares each tenant's period totals
against the trailing three-period band and holds a file outside it until the tenant's named payroll contact
confirms it, which is CloudNimbus's own preventive control sitting in front of the customer step CUEC-05
describes. In the third quarter alone it operated across 5,171 export files, holding 31, with 28 released,
3 re-issued and no cut-off delayed. **The entry moved on the control CloudNimbus operates rather than on the
customer behaviour it describes**, and the minute says so.

### 5.3 R-06, and the movement that had to be defended

**R-06 — a scheduled retention or deletion job fails silently and data is kept past its rule — moved one
step, from 4 × 4 = 16 to 3 × 4 = 12, and not two.**

R-06 was tested upward on 2026-10-28 and **held** under DEC-708, on the ground that the RT-02 job did not
fail: it succeeded, on all sixty-eight nights, and reported that it had. **R-38 had already been admitted
five days earlier — 2026-10-23, under DEC-707 — on the same evidence**, and the two are separate decisions
taken on separate dates rather than a substitution made at R-06's test. DEC-306 is the standing authority
that lets a register take an addition between reviews at all; it is not the decision that took either of
them. That reasoning still stands, and it is exactly why R-06 could not move two steps in December. **A two-step move
would put a silent deletion failure at *foreseeable but not expected within the cycle* in the same quarter
the register admitted an entry for a job that deleted nothing for sixty-eight nights.** The one step it did
move rests on `CNB-C-149`'s sixty-five nights and on nothing else, and `03.07` §5.2's forecast of 2 × 4 = 8
for R-06 is not reached. That divergence is Phase 09's to reconcile against the forecast, not this
chapter's to explain away.

### 5.4 R-10 — a late revocation is not a late review

R-10 reads: *a quarterly access review is completed late, or completed and not evidenced.*

**Both quarterly certifications in the window were completed on time and evidenced** — **2026-07-27** and
**2026-10-09**. `04.12` declares EC-01's sampling unit as **one system for one quarter**, and across twelve
systems and two quarters the window's population in that unit is **24 EC-01 units**, each one an entitlement
extract, a decision against each line, the revocation tickets and the reviewer's sign-off with a timestamp.
The event R-10 describes did not occur in any of the twenty-four.

**What was late was three of the forty-seven revocations arising from those reviews**, which is
`CNB-C-040`'s other limb — *the revocations arising are applied and evidenced before the review is closed* —
and which is exception 9 in 08.11 at **3 of 47, 6.4%**. The two limbs are counted in different units and the
record states both: **24 EC-01 units on the certification limb, 47 revocations on the revocation limb.**

**So the register moved R-10 on the same day, and on the same evidence, that produced the exception the
examination recorded against the same control three weeks later.** The CAL-06 review sat on **2026-12-29**
and asked what had happened to the revocations; Ashcombe &amp; Doyle tested the CC6 access family in the
second week of fieldwork, **2027-01-18 to 01-22**. Nothing about the examination was inside the observation
window and nothing here puts it there. That is not a contradiction and it is not a convenient reading. It is
the R-06 discipline applied to a control rather than to a risk: an entry moves on whether *its own
described event* occurred, and R-10's event is a late or unevidenced review. Recording a late revocation
against R-10 would have made the register unable to distinguish the two failures, which are found by
different tests, owned at different points in the process, and remedied differently.

### 5.5 The objection

> **Nineteen entries moved at one review. Is that a review or a rubber stamp?**
>
> Every CAL-06 review since April moved at most one entry, and the September review moved none downward at
> all — it declined six reductions and raised one rating. The reviews were not being cautious for the sake
> of appearing cautious; each one said, on the record, that it was waiting for a population. **The December
> review is the first at which the population exists**, and a programme that says for nine months that it
> will move when the evidence arrives has to actually move when the evidence arrives, or the discipline was
> theatre.
>
> The review sat for four hours, its record runs to the entry level, **every movement states the population
> it moved on**, six of the nineteen needed a second step and each was argued, one moved a single step
> against its own forecast and the reason is minuted, and seven entries were held against the same evidence
> that moved the others. **A review that moves nothing for nine months and then moves nineteen entries
> looks worse than one that moved two a quarter. It is better, because the two-a-quarter version would have
> been moving on evidence that did not exist.**

The test a reader can apply is the seven held. A rubber stamp does not hold R-09 at 12 in a review that has
just taken eight entries out of the High band, and it does not stop R-08 one step short of the rating the
arithmetic offered. **ADR-0039** records the decision and the objection together, because the objection is
the more transferable half.

## 6. The window's populations, against the criteria they serve

The operating record above is a set of numbers until it is attached to the criteria the numbers are
evidence for, and a chapter that closes an observation window owes the reader that attachment once.

| 10,336 alerts, 148 of them high-severity in the third quarter | `CNB-C-067`, `CNB-C-068`, `CNB-C-070` | **CC7.2, CC7.3** · A.8.16 |

| 65 nights of `CNB-C-149` | `CNB-C-149` | **P4.2, P4.3** · A.8.10, A.8.16 |

**Two of those rows carry an exception and one carries a nonconformity, and they are not the same rows.**
`CNB-C-098` and `CNB-C-096` produced exceptions 1 and 2. `CNB-C-100`'s row is clean as a control and its
Annex A citation of **A.5.30** is where `MIN-02` lands, because two of A.5.30's four limbs are carried by no
control at all. **A population can be complete, a control can operate, and the requirement underneath can
still be short — and 08.06 is where that is worked out.**

## 7. What closing the window does not settle

**The window is closed and the examination is not.** Everything above describes a period that has ended;
none of it describes what the service auditor made of it. Fieldwork ran from 2027-01-12 and closed on this
vantage, the exceptions are in 08.11, and **nothing in this chapter is a conclusion about the opinion** —
which is not this programme's to state, on ADR-0015's rule applied for the fourth phase running.

**The register's closing position is a position, not a result.** 0 · 17 · 21 against the Phase 03 forecast
of 0 · 16 · 22 is a one-entry difference at the band level, and the comparison that matters runs underneath
the bands and in both directions. **Phase 09 takes that comparison apart**, including the entries where the
actual rating and the forecast rating differ without either band changing. Publishing the close position
here and the forecast reconciliation there is deliberate: a chapter that did both would be marking its own
homework in the same paragraph it recorded the score.

**And three of the nineteen movements rest on populations that will keep accruing.** `CNB-C-149` has
sixty-five nights and needs a year. `CA-06-03`'s corrected emergency path has six weeks and needs a window.
CUEC-05's 229 non-responses are exactly where 06.09 left them. **A rating that moved on the evidence
available in December is a rating that can move back**, and the register's next test is the March CAL-06
occurrence, which no document in this phase reports.
