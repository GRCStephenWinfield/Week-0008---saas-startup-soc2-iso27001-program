## 1. Clause 10.2, shown rather than asserted

Clause 10.2 is routinely summarised as "fix it and stop it happening again," which loses the two
distinctions the clause exists to draw. It is set out here in the order it runs, with
what CloudNimbus did against each limb, because `MAJ-01` is the one finding in the programme's life on which
every limb was exercised and evidenced to an outside party.

| Clause 10.2 requires | What was done |
|---|---|
| React to the nonconformity and, as applicable, **take action to control and correct it** | The internal audit that was missing was conducted: clauses 4 to 10 and `eu-central-1` operations, 2027-01-05 to 2027-01-09 |
| …and **deal with the consequences** | The consequence is that the ISMS had no information on conformity to clauses 4 to 10 for the 2026 cycle. The audit supplied it, and it raised a finding of its own |
| **Evaluate the need for action to eliminate the causes** so that it does not recur or occur elsewhere, by **reviewing the nonconformity** | GOV-31 §2: what the programme document said, what `CNB-C-146` said, and which was wrong |
| …**determining the causes** | **§3.** The audit programme document was written in January against the only inventory that existed; the control statement was written in June; nothing compared them |
| …and **determining whether similar nonconformities exist or could potentially occur** | The honest answer, recorded rather than avoided: **the same shape can exist anywhere in a library of 150 rows and nothing has been enumerated.** That is `IS-35` |
| **Implement any action needed** | The three-part corrective action at §4 |
| **Review the effectiveness of any corrective action taken** | Two limbs have been exercised and one has not. `CNB-C-150` has never operated — `IS-36` |
| **Make changes to the ISMS if necessary** | POL-19's annexe rewritten; `CNB-C-092`'s quarterly Tier 1 assurance refresh extended to an annual adequacy review of the scope given to the audit supplier; the library at 150 |
| **Corrective actions shall be appropriate to the effects of the nonconformities encountered** | **§4, and the answer is yes** — the reasoning is below the table rather than asserted in a cell |
| **Retain documented information on the nature of the nonconformities and any subsequent actions**, and **on the results of any corrective action** | GOV-31, EV-812 to EV-816, and the improvement register entry under `CNB-C-148`, which cannot be closed until the effectiveness check is completed |

**The distinction the table is built to show is between the third row and the fourth.** Conducting the
missing audit is a **correction**: it controls and corrects the nonconformity and deals with its
consequences, and if CloudNimbus had done only that, the 2026 cycle would have been repaired and the 2027
programme would have been set by the same process that produced the finding. **The corrective action is the
part that acts on the cause**, and clause 10.2 requires the organisation to evaluate whether it is needed
rather than to assume it is — which is why `D-06-02` produced no corrective action in Phase 06 and this
produces three limbs of one.

**The limb most often left out of a table like this one is proportionality, and it is the limb that tests
this corrective action.** Clause 10.2 requires corrective actions to be **appropriate to the effects of the
nonconformities encountered**, which is a two-directional test: an action can be disproportionate by being
too small for what the failure did, and it can be disproportionate by being larger than the failure
warrants. What the effects were is specific. The ISMS held no information on conformity to clauses 4 to 10
for a full annual cycle, and none about one of three regions; the defect was in the **definition** of the
programme rather than in an occurrence of it; and the definition is set once a year.

**Against that, a three-part action is proportionate, and the untested limb is the reason rather than the
objection.** The first part changes the definition and takes effect immediately — the 2027 programme is set
from the rewritten document and its coverage matrix, so the thing that was wrong is not wrong for any
future cycle. The second part, `CNB-C-150`, puts an annual check on that matrix, and **its first occurrence
falls when the 2028 programme is set because that is the interval at which the cause can bite**: a
divergence between a programme document and the control statement describing it becomes capable of doing
damage when a programme is next set, which is annually. A control scheduled more often would be reading a
document nobody had changed, and a control scheduled less often would let a cycle pass unchecked. The third
part attaches the scope-adequacy question to `CNB-C-092`, a control that already runs quarterly on the
supplier concerned. **Doing less —
rewriting the document alone — would have left nothing to detect a recurrence, and doing more would have
been an organisation buying reassurance rather than answering effects.** That is the proportionality limb
answered rather than omitted, and it is answered against a **major** nonconformity, which is where the
temptation to over-build the response is strongest.

## 2. Northgate's clock, and what was submitted inside it

**Northgate required a correction and a corrective action plan within twenty days of the closing meeting —
by 2026-12-24 — and evidence of implementation and effectiveness before a certification decision could be
taken.** A major nonconformity blocks certification until both are satisfied; the twenty days govern the
plan, and the decision waits on the evidence.

**The twenty days are Northgate's and not the standard's, and the distinction is worth a sentence because
the number is quoted throughout this phase.** ISO/IEC 17021-1 requires the certification body to **define**
the time within which correction and corrective action are to be addressed; it does not name a period.
Twenty days is what Northgate defined and what appears in its own report. An organisation reading twenty
days as a rule of the standard would be reading a certification body's procedure as a requirement of ISO —
which is the same error as reading Annex A as the requirements, in a smaller place.

**The plan was submitted on 2026-12-19, five days inside the limit**, and it is **GOV-31** and **EV-812**.

**And there is a second limit, which four paragraphs on the arithmetic of time had left out.** Under
ISO/IEC 17021-1, for an **initial certification** the correction and corrective action for a major
nonconformity must be verified **before the certification decision**, and where that verification cannot be
achieved within **six months of the last day of Stage 2**, a further **full Stage 2** is required. Stage 2 ended on
2026-12-04, so the six months ran to **2027-06-04**. Closure was verified on **2027-01-15** — four and a
half months inside it. **That is the deadline that actually governed the sequence, and it is the one nobody
was near.** The twenty days were tight enough to feel like the constraint and were never the one that could
have cost the programme a repeat of the audit.

**The correction was scoped as a full audit rather than a documentation review, and that is DEC-807**, taken
by Karim Haddad on 2026-12-11. The cheaper option was available and was put: rewrite the programme document,
show Northgate a corrected scope, and let the 2027 audit run against it in the ordinary course. It was
refused for a reason that is about evidence rather than about diligence. **The nonconformity is that the
ISMS had no information about conformity to clauses 4 to 10; a rewritten document does not supply
information, it promises it.** Northgate required evidence of implementation and effectiveness, and the only
thing that evidences an audit programme is an audit.

**The audit was conducted by Camberwell Risk Partners under a scope CloudNimbus rewrote**, from 2027-01-05
to 2027-01-09, against clauses 4 to 10 and `eu-central-1` operations. Retaining the same supplier was
deliberate: the finding was never about Camberwell's competence, and changing auditor would have implied it
was while losing a year of familiarity with the ISMS. What changed is the only thing that had been wrong —
**who wrote the scope, and against what.**

**It raised 1 minor nonconformity — clause 7.4, on how the ISMS communicates changes to interested parties —
and 4 opportunities for improvement.**

**The audit that was supposed to exist found something, which is the point of it existing.** A correction
audit that returned a clean sheet would have raised a question the programme could not have answered: an
audit of seven clauses and a region, conducted for the first time, by an auditor working to a scope written
in the fortnight before it started, finding nothing at all is a result that invites the question of whether
the scope was rewritten or only re-worded. **`AS-41` records exactly that**, and records that one audit
cannot establish whether a scope reaches everything clause 9.2.1 requires across a three-year cycle. The
clause 7.4 finding is also, as **08.06 §8** notes, the same subject Northgate had offered as `OFI-01` five
weeks earlier — Stage 2 closed 2026-12-04, the correction audit opened 2027-01-05 — and CloudNimbus had
declined.

## 3. The root cause, and it is not "human error"

> The audit programme document was written at chartering in **January 2026** and defined its scope by
> reference to the Statement of Applicability, because in January the SoA was **the only complete inventory
> of auditable things the organisation had.** `CNB-C-146` was written in **June**, when the control library
> was built, and it describes a programme audited across clauses 4 to 10. **Nothing required the programme
> document to be brought up to the control statement that described it, and nothing compared the two.**
>
> **The library got ahead of the practice and nothing checked that they still agreed.** That is the same
> failure mode as Phase 05's five control statements that did not say what a later phase needed, and Phase
> 06's `CNB-C-096`, which said how often and never said what — except that here the statement was right and
> the practice was wrong, which is the harder direction to notice, because reading the library tells you
> nothing is missing.

**That last clause is the whole of why this cause is worth writing down**, and it repays being spelled out.

Every previous library defect in this programme was findable **from the library**. Phase 05 read five
control statements and found that they did not say what a later phase needed them to say. Phase 06 read
`CNB-C-096` and found a control that fixed cadence, regions and a paging condition and was silent on what
the probe must exercise — every word of it satisfied on the day it mattered, and the silence visible on the
page. Phase 07 read `CNB-C-127` and found an alert condition about the arrival of a record rather than about
its content. In each case a careful reader with the library in front of them could have found the defect
before the event did.

**`CNB-C-146` cannot be found that way, because there is nothing wrong with it.** It says the programme is
risk-weighted across clauses 4 to 10 and the Annex A controls determined necessary. That is the right
statement. A reader auditing the library for gaps reads that row, ticks it, and moves on — and the row is
correct while the activity it describes is not. **The defect is not in either document. It is in the
absence of any requirement that the two be read against each other**, and no amount of reading either one
finds it.

**Two things follow, and the second is the uncomfortable one.**

**The instance is closed.** The programme document has been rewritten, the audit has been run, the
nonconformity is verified closed.

**The class is not, and nothing has been enumerated.** `IS-35` records it as the class rather than the
instance: a control statement and the procedure that carries it can drift apart silently in either
direction, anywhere in a library of 150 rows, and the library side gives no signal. `CNB-C-150` answers the
instance for the internal audit programme and answers nothing about any other control. **Naming a class of
failure without being able to say how many members it has is unsatisfying, and it is more honest than a
number nobody derived** — which is the same position `IS-33` takes about a different class, for the same
reason.

**And "human error" is not available as a cause.** Nobody forgot to update the programme document, because
nobody was ever required to. The January document was correct when written, the June statement was correct
when written, and the two were authored eight weeks apart by people who both did their jobs. A cause
analysis that landed on individual error would have produced a corrective action about attentiveness, which
is a corrective action about nothing.

## 4. The corrective action, in three parts

**The first part: the audit programme document, rewritten.** DEC-808, 2026-12-18. The audit criteria are now
**clauses 4 to 10, the Statement of Applicability, and every region and legal entity inside the ISMS scope**,
carried on a **coverage matrix that must be complete across the three-year certification cycle**, with a
named gap escalation. A row with no planned audit inside the cycle is the finding the matrix exists to
produce. The form is `templates/audit-programme-coverage-matrix-template.md`, and it exists because
CloudNimbus did not have one; `01.11` §7 recorded in January 2026 that CAL-14 fixed frequency and
prioritisation basis and fixed nothing about coverage, and the matrix is what that deferral was deferring.

**The second part: `CNB-C-150`, admitted to the library.**

> **`CNB-C-150`** — *The internal audit programme's coverage matrix is reviewed annually against the ISMS
> scope and the Statement of Applicability, and a clause, a location or a legal entity with no planned audit
> inside the certification cycle is escalated to the Audit &amp; Risk Committee before the programme is set.*
>
> Family `ISO`. Type **Detective**. Cadence **Annual**. Owner **Karim Haddad**. Policy `POL-19`.
> Criteria **—**. Annex A **—**.

**Both columns are dashes and the second one had to be argued for.** A.5.35 was available and would have
made the row look better mapped: it is an independent review of the approach to managing information
security, it is the nearest thing in Annex A to an audit, and it is already carried by **`CNB-C-024`**, an
annual review commissioned from a party that neither designed nor operates the controls. `CNB-C-150` is not
that. **It is an annual self-review of the audit programme's coverage, performed by the person who owns the
programme**, and the obligation it serves lives in clause 9.2 rather than in Annex A. **That `CNB-C-150`
cannot cite A.5.35 is the same point `CNB-C-146` makes** — `04.07` §3.1 refused the citation for the parent
control on exactly this ground — **and a corrective action that quietly claimed the citation its parent
control was denied would have been the easiest thing in the phase to get wrong**, because nobody checks the
Annex A column of a row that has just been added for the best of reasons.

**The library is 150.** It stood at 148 when Phase 04 published it in June 2026, became 149 when
`CNB-C-149` was admitted on 2026-10-28, and is 150 from 2027-01-08. **Two additions in under seven months on
the library's own clock**, both after a failure neither would have prevented.

**And the precision that matters. `CNB-C-150` was admitted on 2027-01-08, after the observation window
closed on 2026-12-31. It has no population in the window at all, it is not part of the Type II examination,
and this chapter says so rather than leaving it to be worked out.** Not a thin population and not a
population of one — none. Its cadence is annual, its first occurrence falls when the 2028 programme is set,
and it did not exist on any of the window's 184 days. **A control admitted after the period is an ISO
corrective action and nothing else.** DEC-811 and **ADR-0040** record the decision and the alternative that
was refused, which was to date it inside December where it would have counted for something.

**The third part: the A.5.22 half.** DEC-812, 2027-01-08, Tobias Lund. **`CNB-C-092`** — the quarterly
refresh of every Tier 1 vendor's assurance artefact, which carries A.5.19 and **A.5.22** — now also carries
an **annual adequacy review of the scope CloudNimbus gives its audit supplier**: not a review of
Camberwell's performance against the scope, which was never in question, but of whether the scope is
adequate to the requirement it exists to satisfy. **The failure was not the supplier's delivery. It was the
instruction**, and a review that only ever asks whether the supplier did what it was told cannot detect an
instruction that was wrong.

**The limb attaches to `CNB-C-092` and not to `CNB-C-090`, and the reason is worth stating because the
wrong attachment would have been comfortable.** `CNB-C-090` is a **pre-engagement gate**: no vendor receives
production data or a system integration until a security assessment proportionate to its tier is complete
and the General Counsel has recorded the outcome and the tier in the vendor register. It is preventive, it
is continuous, it cites A.5.19 and A.5.21, and **it contains no periodic review of anything**. Camberwell
receives no production data and no system integration, so it would never have passed through that gate at
all. The library's A.5.22 row is `CNB-C-092`.

**And attaching it there produces a finding of its own, which is the more useful half of the exercise.**
Camberwell **is** a Tier 1
vendor in the register, and `CNB-C-092` read its assurance artefact quarterly throughout the window —
operating correctly, on the right vendor, on the right cadence, for the whole period in which the scope was
inadequate. **Reading a supplier's assurance artefact is not the same activity as reviewing whether the
scope you gave that supplier was adequate.** The control was running and could not have caught this, which
is precisely why the corrective action **extends** `CNB-C-092` rather than relying on it: a limb added to a
control that was already operating is an admission that the control's existing question was the wrong one.

**One limb of the three has never operated and Northgate's acceptance did not rest on it.** What was
accepted as evidence of implementation and effectiveness was the rewritten programme document and the
January audit it produced. `CNB-C-150` is a statement in a library with no operating history until the 2028
programme is set — `IS-36` and `AS-40` — and **a corrective action whose most durable limb is untested is
not a defective corrective action; it is one whose effectiveness check is scheduled.**

**The supplementary audit was one remote auditor-day and that is a fact about what was submitted.** An
auditor arriving to verify a correction that consists of a rewritten document, an audit report, a decision
record and a library change is doing a reading exercise. An auditor arriving to verify a correction that
consists of an assurance that the 2027 programme will be different is doing something else, and it takes
longer.

**The certification decision is not the auditor's.** Halvorsen led the audits and made a recommendation;
the decision of 2027-01-20 was taken by a decision-maker at Northgate who did not conduct them, which is
what ISO/IEC 17021-1 requires and why the decision date and the audit dates are different dates.

## 6. The certificate

> The certificate is issued by **Northgate Certification Services**, a certification body accredited by
> **ANAB** under **ISO/IEC 17021-1** and **ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024**. **ISO does not issue it and has no view
> on it.** It states the standard as **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**, the scope as the ISMS scope statement reissued
> on 2026-10-28, and a validity of three years to **2030-01-21**. It is not a statement that CloudNimbus is
> secure, and it is not a statement about any control's operating effectiveness over a period — **that is
> the other document, and it has not been issued.**
>
> The **first surveillance audit** must be conducted within twelve months of the **certification decision**
> of 2027-01-20, and is scheduled for **2027-11**. Recertification falls in year three.

**Four things about that paragraph are load-bearing and each is a place the sentence usually goes wrong.**

**The issuer.** ISO writes standards. **ANAB** accredits certification bodies against ISO/IEC 17021-1 and
the sector requirements of ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024. **Northgate** audits and issues. Naming ISO as the issuer
names the wrong party, and it names a party that certifies nobody.

**The scope.** The certificate carries the scope statement reissued on 2026-10-28 — the ISMS supporting the
design, development, operation, support and delivery of the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform, **together with
all corporate functions of CloudNimbus, Inc., across all locations including home-working arrangements, in
accordance with the Statement of Applicability.** The last clause is not decoration: it points the reader at
the document recording that 91 of 93 Annex A controls were determined necessary and 2 were not, which means
**a certificate can be held with Annex A controls excluded and the reader has to look to see which.**

**The clock.** Twelve months runs from the **decision** of 2027-01-20, not from the certificate date of
2027-01-22 and not from the Stage 2 dates. Obligation **O11** carries it. The audit is scheduled for
2027-11 and **it has not happened**; nothing in this phase reports a surveillance outcome, and the four
minor nonconformities' implementation is verified there, which is what **DP-37** records.

**And what it is not.** It is not an opinion. It is not a determination of compliance with any law. It says
nothing about whether a control operated effectively throughout a period, because that is not what a
management system certificate is for — **and the document that does say something about that has not been
issued.**

## 7. What is closed, and what is not

**`MAJ-01` is verified closed.** That is Northgate's determination of 2027-01-15, taken on the correction
and the corrective action plan, and it is the only finding in this phase with a closure verified by an
external party.

**The four minors are not closed.** Their corrective action plans were accepted on 2026-12-19 and
implementation is verified at the first surveillance audit.

**`CNB-C-150` has not operated**, and cannot be tested by anybody — not Northgate at the surveillance audit,
not the Audit &amp; Risk Committee, not CloudNimbus — until the 2028 programme is set. **`IS-36`.**

**The rewritten programme has produced one audit.** A three-year coverage matrix complete on paper for a
cycle nobody has traversed is a plan, and `PR-50` says so in the terms the January 2026 programme document
also deserved.

**And none of it reaches the examination.** The correction, the corrective action, the certificate and
`CNB-C-150` are all remediation of a nonconformity against a requirement the examination does not assess.
**08.12** owns what does reach the report, which is a Section V disclosure that is not covered by the
service auditor's opinion — a section that says so on its own face.
