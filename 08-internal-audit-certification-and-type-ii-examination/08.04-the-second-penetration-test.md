## 1. The engagement, and why there was a second one

**Ironwood Security Labs tested the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform from 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-16 and
reported on 2026-11-02.** It is the second engagement of the year, five months after the first, and it
exists because of an accounting decision rather than a security one.

`CNB-C-026` commissions penetration testing annually and after significant change. Its 2026 occurrence was
**CAL-11**, 2026-05-04 to 2026-05-22, and its natural next occurrence would have fallen in May 2027 —
outside the observation window. **`CNB-C-026` is one of the ten annual controls `04.11` §4.2 re-scheduled
into the window under ADR-0019 and DEC-409**, and re-scheduling an annual test so that it has an occurrence
inside a period commits the organisation to performing a second engagement rather than to describing one.
That commitment was carried as **PR-28** in Phase 04's log and discharged as a plan in `05.11` §8, which
said at its vantage that the October engagement was scheduled and not performed and reported nothing about
it. **This is the chapter that reports it.**

**DEC-509** and **ADR-0025** are the decision and its reasoning. The argument that had to be met was that a
second test five months after the first would find little and cost a great deal; the answer was that a test
scoped at the previous test's remediation asks a question the first test could not, which is whether the fix
holds against somebody trying to break it.

**The engagement ran under a written test authorisation issued under `CNB-C-142`**, naming the systems, the
window and the accounts, agreed with the system owner before it started and revoked when the window closed.
The authorisation and its revocation record are **EV-808**. As in May, testing ran against a
production-equivalent pre-production environment seeded with synthetic tenants, which `CNB-C-083` and
**SR-12** require: production customer data is not copied into non-production.

**One thing about `CNB-C-142` has to be said rather than smoothed over.** The published statement requires
the authorisation to name systems, window, accounts **and a read-only scope**. A penetration test is not a
read-only activity — attempting what a read-only scope forbids is the whole of its method — and the
authorisation raised for this engagement recorded the scope as **write-capable against the pre-production
environment**, which is what the engagement needed and what the system owner agreed. The control's fourth
element fits an auditor sampling a live system, which is one of the four kinds of outsider `04.07` §2 says
the control exists for, and does not fit the most adversarial of them. **That is a statement about the
library and not about the engagement**, it is recorded here rather than corrected, and it is the same shape
as `IS-35`: a control statement and the practice it describes, both defensible on their own faces, that
nobody read against each other. The next issue of the library is where it belongs.

## 2. Scope — three surfaces, chosen for three different reasons

| Stream | Scope | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| **The tenant isolation remediation** | The mandatory tenant parameter at the data-access layer and the build-time check, `CNB-C-035`, and the isolation suite's documented access paths | The May Critical, `PT-01`, and the assumption `AS-02` it disproved. A remediation retested by its own author is a remediation nobody has attacked |
| **The mobile application** | The iOS and Android clients, including on-device storage, transport and the export-download path | `PT-09` and `PT-14` were the two findings deferred to the 2026-08-14 release and closed at Ironwood's retest of 2026-08-28. A closed finding is a claim about a release, not about a client |
| **The payroll export path** | The export service, the signed-URL mechanism, the storage endpoints and the delivery acknowledgements | The path the calculation engine's output leaves by, examined by nobody in May, and the one surface where a confidentiality failure reaches a customer's payroll provider |

**The corporate estate is not in scope and the perimeter was not re-tested**, because neither had changed
materially since May and neither is what this engagement was commissioned to ask about. A second test that
re-ran the first test's streams would have produced a comparison and not an answer.

## 3. The nine findings

**9 findings — 0 Critical, 1 High, 3 Medium, 5 Low. 0 + 1 + 3 + 5 = 9.**

| ID | Stream | Finding | Severity | Remediated | Retested |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PT2-03` | Export path | An export file's signed URL remained valid for its full twenty-four hours after the file had been re-issued, so a superseded URL could be replayed to retrieve a file the tenant had corrected | **High** | 2026-11-11 | **2026-11-20** |
| `PT2-01` | Mobile | The Android client retained the last export summary in an unencrypted preferences store after sign-out | Medium | 2026-11-11 | 2026-11-20 |
| `PT2-02` | Export path | The export service returned an empty file for a period outside the tenant's configured pay calendar, so a caller could not distinguish "no data" from "wrong period" | Medium | 2026-11-11 | 2026-11-20 |
| `PT2-04` | Mobile | Certificate pinning, shipped as `PT-14`'s remediation in the 2026-08-14 release, did not extend to the export-download host | Medium | 2026-11-11 | 2026-11-20 |
| `PT2-08` | Export path | A response header on the export host disclosed the storage backend | Low | 2026-11-20 | 2026-11-20 |
| `PT2-05` | Mobile | The iOS client wrote the tenant identifier and export file name to the device console at informational level | Low | 2026-12-04 | Carried |
| `PT2-06` | Export path | Response timing distinguished a valid from an invalid export file identifier | Low | 2026-12-04 | Carried |
| `PT2-07` | Mobile | The record set was not cleared from memory when the application was backgrounded | Low | 2026-12-04 | Carried |
| `PT2-09` | Export path | TLS session tickets on the export host carried a lifetime longer than the internal standard permits | Low | 2026-12-04 | Carried |

**`CNB-C-026` requires every finding to be tracked to closure with a documented retest**, and five of the
nine have one: Ironwood's retest of **2026-11-20** covered the High, the three Mediums and `PT2-08`. **The
four remaining Lows were remediated in the release of 2026-12-04 and carry a retest that has not been
performed at this vantage**; they sit in the vulnerability register against the 2027 CAL-11 engagement.
Saying so is better than reporting nine closed findings and letting a reader assume nine retests, and it is
the same discipline `05.11` applied to the three findings it left open in July.

**`PT2-04` is the row worth reading twice.** `PT-14` — the Android client did not pin the API certificate —
was found in May, deferred to the 2026-08-14 release, shipped and retested clean by Ironwood on 2026-08-28.
Five weeks after that retest the same tester found that the pinning shipped in that release **did not extend to the
export-download host**, which is a different host reached by the same client. Both statements are true: the
August retest confirmed the remediation as scoped, and the October engagement found that the scope was the
set of hosts the original finding happened to name. `06.05` §6.1 made the identical point about the eleven
services the September connection-pool fix was deployed to, and established the scope by searching all 63.
**A fix whose scope is the set of things that happened to break is a fix of unestablished scope**, and this
is the second time in one quarter that the sentence has had to be written about a different family.

## 4. `PT2-03`, the High, and what kind of finding it is

**An export file's signed URL survived the file it pointed at.**

The mechanism is ordinary and that is the point. The export service writes a payroll export file to storage
and issues a signed URL with a twenty-four-hour validity. Where a file is **re-issued** — a correction, a
held file released under `CNB-C-111` after a band check, a re-run — a new file is written and a new URL is
issued. **The old URL was not invalidated**, and for the remainder of its twenty-four hours it continued to
retrieve the superseded file. A caller who had received the first URL could replay it and obtain a version
of the export the tenant had corrected.

**It is a confidentiality finding in the export path and it is not an isolation finding**, and the
distinction matters enough to be stated on its own line. Nothing about it crosses a tenant boundary: the
URL that survives is the tenant's own URL, retrieving the tenant's own superseded file. What it defeats is
the tenant's ability to withdraw a document, not CloudNimbus's ability to keep one employer's data away
from another. Calling it an isolation finding would have been the easiest way to make the engagement look
like it was still about May, and it would have been wrong.

**It was remediated on 2026-11-11 rather than in the December mobile release, and that is DEC-805**, taken
by Junia Okonkwo on 2026-11-05. The alternative was to fold the fix into the release already scheduled for
December, which would have been cheaper and would have left a High-severity confidentiality finding open
across the Stage 2 audit and four weeks of the observation window. The decision record says what the
programme was buying: **a fix delivered on its own carries its own risk and its own rollback, and a fix
carried by a release carries the release's schedule.** Ironwood retested it clean on 2026-11-20.

## 5. Forty-one access paths, and what the result is worth

**Ironwood exercised 41 crafted access paths against the mandatory tenant parameter and the build-time check
that were R-37's remediation, including the original May vector and eleven variants of it.**

The variants are where the engagement earns its fee. `05.12` set out precisely what the May defect was — a
nested filter on the aggregate resolver built its statement through a query builder that obtained a
connection from the driver directly, bypassing the pool wrapper that carried the session-variable hook, so
the row-level policy evaluated an unset variable and did not constrain the result — and the remediation
moved the predicate out of the session variable and into the data-access layer as a mandatory parameter,
with a build-time check that fails any statically-constructible query lacking it. The eleven variants
attacked the remaining surface of that design: second connections, transaction boundaries, pool recycling
under load, the resolver's filter surface, and the composition paths a static check cannot decide.

**None of the 41 returned another tenant's data. And the build-time check rejected six
statically-constructed queries during the engagement** — six pieces of code written by a tester trying to
produce an unscoped query, refused at build rather than at run time.

**The six are the stronger half of the result, and they are the half most likely to be left out.** Forty-one
paths that returned nothing is a negative: the tester tried and did not succeed, and the reasons could
include a good design, an incomplete test, or a surface the tester did not reach. **Six rejections are a
positive**: a mechanism was observed doing the thing it exists to do, on inputs designed to make it fail,
six times. A report that led with the forty-one and buried the six would have reported the weaker evidence
first.

> **A test that fails to find something is weaker evidence than a test that finds something.** But two tests
> five months apart, the second scoped deliberately at the first's fix and designed to break it, is the best
> available evidence about a design assumption — and it is the evidence the December register review moved
> R-01 two steps on.

**R-01 moved from 4 × 5 = 20 to 2 × 5 = 10 at the CAL-06 review of 2026-12-29**, and **08.08 §5.2** owns
the movement. What belongs here is the half of it this engagement supplied. The control changed in May and
was retested clean in June, which is one fact; it was attacked in October by a tester with the finding
report in hand, which is a second. **A control that changed and was then tested is a different proposition
from a control that changed**, and neither fact would have carried two steps alone.

**Two limits go with the result and both were stated in May before anybody knew whether they would be
convenient.** The build-time check verifies that a tenant parameter is **present**, not that its value is
**right**; a call site supplying the wrong tenant compiles cleanly, and what covers that case is the
isolation suite under `CNB-C-115`. And the check is **static**, while dynamic composition is precisely what
static analysis cannot decide. Ironwood's variants were designed against both limits and found nothing,
which narrows them without removing them.

## 6. What the engagement is evidence for, and under which criteria

The governing control is **`CNB-C-026`**, and it is worth setting out what that control is mapped to,
because a penetration test is easy to file under the wrong heading.

| | What the engagement evidences |
|---|---|
| **CC4.1** — the entity selects, develops and performs **ongoing and/or separate evaluations** to ascertain whether the components of internal control are present and functioning | A penetration test is a **separate evaluation**, commissioned from a party that neither designed nor operates the controls under test. **The criterion is disjunctive**, which is what allows a separate evaluation to stand on its own rather than only as a supplement to continuous monitoring: this is the clearest example in the library of the "separate" half of CC4.1, and `CNB-C-024`'s annual independent review is the other |
| **CC7.1** — the entity uses detection and monitoring procedures to identify **(1)** changes to configurations that result in the introduction of new vulnerabilities, and **(2) susceptibilities to newly discovered vulnerabilities** | **Limb (2) is the one this engagement evidences most directly.** The 41 crafted paths and the six statically-constructed queries the build-time check rejected are a search for susceptibilities, conducted by a party using current technique against a design that had already been remediated once. Limb (1) is served in part — the configuration had changed since May — but a penetration test is commissioned to find what an estate is susceptible to rather than to watch what changes in it |
| **A.8.8** management of technical vulnerabilities | The nine findings, their severities, their remediation dates and the retests |
| **A.8.29** security testing in development and acceptance | The engagement ran against the pre-production environment under `CNB-C-083` and SR-12, on synthetic tenants, which is why a write-capable authorisation was appropriate |

**What it does not evidence.** It says nothing about **C1.1**, although tenant isolation is the
confidentiality control that matters, because `CNB-C-026` does not cite C1.1 and a test result cannot be
offered in support of a criterion its control does not carry. That is `06.10` §2's rule — an Annex A control
is answered by the rows that cite it — applied to a trust services criterion, and `08.12` §3 applies it
again to CC4.1 and CC4.2 where it costs the programme something.

## 7. What the engagement establishes, and what it does not

**It establishes that nine findings existed on the three surfaces tested, in the window tested, by the
methods used.** That is the whole of what a penetration test establishes, and `05.11` §9 said so at length
in a phase where the result was less comfortable.

**It does not establish that the platform is secure**, and the arithmetic of the two engagements makes the
point better than an assertion: sixteen findings in May from five streams, nine in October from three,
against an estate that had been remediated in between by people who knew what May had found. **A test
returning fewer findings than the last one is a fact about two tests.**

**It does not establish that the isolation model is correct.** It establishes that forty-one crafted paths
did not defeat it and that a build gate refused six attempts to write code that would have. `PT-15` —
sequential tenant identifiers, accepted rather than remediated under ADR-0022 — is still accepted, and
enumeration is still not authorisation.

**It does not reach any of the nine test exceptions.** No finding in this engagement is a control deviation:
the controls the findings bear on operated as described, and what the test found is defects in the software
the controls govern. `CNB-C-026`'s own occurrence inside the window is a population of one, tested in full,
and the control operated.

**And it is not an audit.** Ironwood is not an auditor, its severities are its own scale rather than either
framework's, and a penetration test finding is neither a nonconformity nor an exception. The finding log's
classification column says `Neither` against all nine, and that is the accurate answer rather than a gap.
