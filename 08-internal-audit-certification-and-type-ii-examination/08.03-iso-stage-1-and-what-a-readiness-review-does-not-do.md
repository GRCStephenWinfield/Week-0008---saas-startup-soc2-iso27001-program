## 1. Three days, and what they were for

**Northgate Certification Services conducted Stage 1 from 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-14** — three days, lead
auditor **Ingrid Halvorsen**, partly remote and partly at the Denver suite. It is **MS-14** and it is the
first occasion on which the certification body looked at anything. The report is dated **2026-10-14** and
is held at **EV-806** with the audit plan, the documented-information review record and the Stage 2
resourcing agreement.

**Northgate is a certification body accredited by ANAB under ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024.**
ISO writes the standard and certifies nobody; the accreditation body accredits the certification body; the
certification body audits and, if the audit supports it, issues a certificate. Three parties, three
different things, and the middle one is the one most often left out of a sentence.

**What Stage 1 is for is fixed by ISO/IEC 17021-1 rather than by the client's expectations**, and the
purposes are worth setting against what Northgate actually did, because the mismatch between the two is what
this chapter is about.

| Purpose under ISO/IEC 17021-1 | What Northgate did over the three days |
|---|---|
| Review the client's documented information | The 19 policies, the 37 items of documented information under `CNB-C-145`, the Statement of Applicability, the risk assessment and treatment plan, the clause 4.1 and 4.2 determinations and the scope statement |
| Evaluate the site and site-specific conditions | The Denver suite, which holds no information processing equipment, and the home-working arrangement that puts 187 people and 231 endpoints outside any CloudNimbus-controlled site |
| Review the client's status and understanding of the requirements, particularly the identification of key performance or significant aspects, processes and objectives | Interviews with Karim Haddad, Rahul Bhargava and four control owners; OBJ-01 to OBJ-08 as re-set on 2026-09-28; the risk criteria and the movement rules |
| Collect information on scope, processes, locations and statutory and regulatory aspects | The three AWS regions, the 63 microservices, the 84 vendors and 11 sub-processors, and the obligations register O1 to O12 |
| Review the allocation of resources for Stage 2 and agree its details | Ten auditor-days over five days with two auditors, agreed on the last afternoon |
| Provide a focus for planning Stage 2 | The Stage 2 plan's emphasis on clause 8, clause 9 and the A.5 and A.8 themes |
| **Evaluate whether internal audits and management review are being planned and performed** | **§5** |
| Evaluate whether implementation justifies proceeding to Stage 2 | Confirmed on 2026-10-14 |

## 2. The result — 0 nonconformities, 6 areas of concern

**Northgate does not raise nonconformities at Stage 1.** Its methodology raises **areas of concern**, and an
area of concern belongs to neither vocabulary this phase otherwise runs on: it is not an ISO nonconformity,
because no requirement has yet been assessed as unfulfilled in an audit conducted against the requirements;
and it is obviously not a SOC 2 test exception, because no control has been tested for operating
effectiveness by anybody. It is a statement that something, if unchanged, will be found at Stage 2.

**Six were raised, and two of the six would have become major nonconformities at Stage 2 had they not been
closed.** Northgate said so at the closing session and wrote it into the report, which is the useful thing
a Stage 1 does and the reason the grading column below is not this programme's inference.

|---|---|---|---|

## 3. The two that would have escalated, and why both are documents

**Both are documented information that fell out of step with an organisation that had moved.** Neither is a
control that failed to operate, neither is a risk that materialised, and neither would have been visible to
anybody testing the platform.

**`AOC-01`.** Clause 6.1.3 d) requires the Statement of Applicability to contain the necessary controls, the
justification for their inclusion, **whether they are implemented or not**, and the justification for
excluding any Annex A control. The status field is not decoration; it is one of the four things the clause
asks the document to say. Two rows had been `Partial` since the reissue of 2026-06-15 with no dated plan
behind them — **A.5.28 collection of evidence**, which is the Statement of Applicability's own
acknowledgement of ML-1, and **A.8.32 change management**. Two more had been overtaken by facts:
`05.13` recorded in July that **A.7.10 storage media** was `Partial` on the ground that technical
enforcement was not complete on every device, and that the enforcement had since been applied by mobile
device management across all 231 endpoints under **DEC-511** on 2026-07-13; and that **A.7.4 physical
security monitoring** was `Partial` on an undefined review cadence that **`CNB-C-047`** now defines.

**Those two were stale in the safe direction and it did not help.** `05.13` called them stale rather than
wrong and said only the Statement of Applicability could correct them, which was accurate and was also a
sentence that left the document uncorrected for thirteen weeks — Phase 05's vantage of 2026-07-31 to the
reissue of 2026-10-30. **A status field that understates is still a
status field that does not say what the clause requires it to say**, and clause 6.1.3 d) does not have a
tolerance for understatement. A.7.10 and A.7.4 are two of the four controls CloudNimbus argued for exclusion
and was refused, which makes their status fields the ones an auditor reads first.

**`AOC-02`.** Clause 4.3 requires the scope to be determined and available as documented information, and
the document is not only the sentence on the certificate. It is the sentence together with the
determinations behind it: the external and internal issues under 4.1, the interested-party requirements
under 4.2, and the **interfaces and dependencies** with other organisations. `DEC-512`'s region-partitioned
`cnb-security` archive changed how information crosses a region boundary inside the boundary, and the
sub-processor change then in notice changed the interface set. Neither had reached the document.

**It also engages an obligation.** **O10** requires Northgate to be notified of significant changes to the
ISMS, its scope or the organisation within thirty days of the change, and `01.11` §4 named O10 as *the
obligation most likely to be missed quietly* because no change arrives labelled as a certification matter.
The reissued statement went to Northgate inside the thirty days, and the notification is filed at EV-807
with its despatch date, which is the evidence O10 is discharged by rather than the assertion that it was.

**And `AOC-02` ties `R-32`, which needs the register's own discipline applied to it.** R-32 reads: *the ISMS
scope statement becomes inaccurate **and** Northgate is not notified within 30 days*, at 2 × 2 = 4, Low,
owned by Rahul Bhargava since the baseline. **The first limb occurred and the second did not.** The statement
was inaccurate, which is what `AOC-02` says; the notification went inside the thirty days, which is what
EV-807's despatch date shows. An entry joined by a conjunction describes one event, and half of one is not
it — the R-06 test applied to a Low. R-32 was accordingly not treated as realised and is among the twelve
already-Low entries that did not move at the December review. **The entry whose event this plainly is, is
`R-30`** — documented information becomes out of date and is relied on as current — which the September
review declined to reduce for the same reason.

## 4. The four that would not have escalated

**They are not minor because they are small.** `AOC-04` is two people of eight in a function of eight;
`AOC-05` is a clause 4.2 determination seven months old in a company that had grown, changed its log
architecture and acquired a Severity-1 incident in the interval; `AOC-06` is a correct determination in the
wrong record; and `AOC-03` is a clause 10.2 record with no dated closure against a control occurrence that
did not happen.

What separates them from the two above is what an auditor at Stage 2 would have been able to conclude from
each. An incomplete competence record for two people is a nonconformity against clause 7.2 and it is
bounded: the requirement, the population and the remedy are all visible. **A Statement of Applicability
whose status fields cannot be relied on is a nonconformity against the document the whole certification
scope rests on**, because clause 6.1.3 d) makes it the record of what is necessary and what is implemented,
and an auditor who cannot trust it has to test every one of 91 rows from first principles.

**`AOC-03` is the one to watch across the phase.** It is the same missed `CNB-C-136` occurrence that Phase
06 recorded as `D-06-05`, and Northgate's complaint at Stage 1 was not that the occurrence was missed — it
was that **the clause 10.2 record carried no dated closure**, which is `CNB-C-148`'s condition. The
occurrence was performed on 2026-12-08, four months late, and **that is a correction and not a recovery**:
an annual control's occurrence is an event on a date, and performing it in December does not make it have
happened in August. `D-06-05` stands.

## 5. What Stage 1 did not find, and why that is Stage 1 working correctly

**Northgate looked at the internal audit and the management review, because ISO/IEC 17021-1 requires it.**
It reviewed Camberwell's report of 2026-09-25, the audit plan, the recorded independence check and the
management review minute of 2026-09-30. It found a programme that **existed, was resourced, ran to plan,
reported, and whose results reached a management review** chaired by the Chief Executive Officer with all
seven clause 9.3.2 inputs presented.

It raised nothing about it, and **it was right not to**.

**Stage 1 asks whether the machinery exists. Stage 2 asks what it covered.** The Stage 1 purpose is to
evaluate whether internal audits and management review are being **planned and performed** — whether the
organisation has the mechanisms clause 9 requires and is operating them — and CloudNimbus did and was. The
question of whether the audit programme's coverage discharged clause 9.2.1's requirement to report on
conformity **to the requirements of this document** is a Stage 2 question, asked in a Stage 2 audit
conducted against clauses 4 to 10, by auditors with ten auditor-days rather than three, sampling the
programme's outputs rather than confirming its existence.

> **CloudNimbus read Stage 1's silence as assurance.** A clean Stage 1 was taken, without anybody writing it
> down, as confirmation that deferring the scope question was safe. It was not. **A Stage 1 that raises
> nothing about your internal audit is not evidence that your internal audit is adequate; it is evidence
> that you have one.** ADR-0036 records the misreading rather than the decision, because the misreading is
> the transferable lesson and the decision is already at DEC-801.

**Three properties of that misreading make it worth a decision record rather than a rueful sentence.**

**It was never written down, which is why it is a misreading and not a decision.** DEC-801 and DEC-802
recorded the deferral and its reasoning in September. Nothing recorded, in October, that Stage 1's silence
had been taken as support for it. There is no minute to point at, no line in the Trust Committee record and
no note against `PR-06`, and the inference was nonetheless operative: between 2026-10-14 and 2026-11-30
nobody re-opened the scope question, and the reason nobody re-opened it is that an accredited certification
body had just looked at the internal audit programme and said nothing. **An inference that changes behaviour
and appears in no record is the hardest kind to audit, including for the organisation making it.**

**It is a category error about what a clean result means, and the same error is available elsewhere in this
programme.** The 2025 Type I was clean and told CloudNimbus exactly where it would fail a Type II. The
disaster recovery exercise of 2026-08-19 achieved its objectives and could not have found the connection
pool assumption that produced the September incident twenty days later. In each case the absence of a
finding was a fact about the scope of the thing that found nothing, and in each case reading it as a fact
about the organisation was available and wrong. **The transferable form is: ask what the exercise was
capable of finding before deciding what its silence is worth.**

**And it is the reason the finding, when it came, was major rather than minor.** Not because Stage 1 caused
it — Stage 1 evaluated correctly what it was there to evaluate — but because the seven weeks between Stage 1
and Stage 2 were the last interval in which a second internal audit could have been commissioned, and they
were spent on the strength of an inference nobody had tested. **08.05 §4** argues the grade on its own
terms; this is the paragraph that says what was lost in between.

## 6. What Stage 1 decided, and what it did not

**It decided that implementation justified proceeding to Stage 2**, and it agreed the Stage 2 details: five
days, two auditors, ten auditor-days, criteria clauses 4 to 10 and the Statement of Applicability.

**It did not decide anything about certification.** No certification decision can be taken on a Stage 1, no
recommendation for certification arises from one, and an organisation that leaves Stage 1 with zero
nonconformities has been told that its documented information supports proceeding and nothing else.

**It did not audit operating effectiveness.** That is Stage 2's, and the distinction is the ISO analogue of
the one this portfolio has been making about Type I and Type II since Phase 01 — a review of what is
designed and documented is not a test of what has been operating, and the gap between the two is exactly
where both frameworks put their most expensive findings.

**And it has nothing to do with the examination.** Stage 1 is a certification body's activity under
ISO/IEC 17021-1, its areas of concern are not deviations, and none of the six appears in any Section IV.
