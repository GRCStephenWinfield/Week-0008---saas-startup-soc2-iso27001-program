## Context

**`MAJ-01` was raised on 2026-12-02**, the third day of Stage 2, against **clause 9.2.1**, **clause 9.2.2 a)**
and **A.5.22**: the internal audit conducted 2026-09-14 to 2026-09-25 covered the 93 Annex A controls in the
Statement of Applicability — the 91 determined necessary against their implementation status and the 2
excluded against their justification — did not cover clauses 4 to 10, and did not cover
`eu-central-1` operations. CloudNimbus's own control `CNB-C-146` states a programme risk-weighted across
clauses 4 to 10 and the Annex A controls determined necessary.

**A grade is contestable at a closing meeting.** Under ISO/IEC 17021-1 a major nonconformity is one that
affects the capability of the management system to achieve intended results, or a number of minors against
one requirement demonstrating a systemic failure. That is a judgement, the auditee is entitled to put its
view, and the certification body is obliged to consider it.

**The materials for a contest existed and were assembled before the closing meeting.** The Annex A coverage
was genuine and complete across all 93 rows — the 91 determined necessary and the 2 excluded. The programme was resourced, ran ten working days, and produced
two minor nonconformities that Northgate itself later re-raised as `MIN-01` and `MIN-03` because they were
correct. The auditor was independent, with the check recorded under `CNB-C-146` before the plan was agreed.
The scope had been read, agreed and minuted rather than overlooked. **A version of `MAJ-01` could have been
put as a minor**, and the argument would not have been frivolous.

The closing meeting was held on **2026-12-04**, with Ingrid Halvorsen and Tomás Ferreira presenting one
major, four minors and seven opportunities.

## Decision

**Karim Haddad accepted `MAJ-01` at the closing meeting on 2026-12-04 without contest. DEC-806.** The
twenty-day clock for a correction and a corrective action plan started that day, and no part of it was spent
on the grade.

> A major nonconformity is arguable in the way most findings are arguable — the Annex A coverage was
> genuine, the programme was resourced, the auditor was independent, and a version of this could have been
> put as a minor. **The organisation did not put it.** An auditee that argues a major down spends the only
> currency it has with a certification body, and spends it on the finding its own control statement, its own
> auditor's report and its own management review minutes already support. **The correct response to a
> finding you cannot honestly dispute is to accept it on the day and start the clock.**

## Consequences

**The twenty days went to the remedy rather than to correspondence.** Northgate required a correction and a
corrective action plan by 2026-12-24. What CloudNimbus submitted on 2026-12-19 was not a document review: it
was a full internal audit of clauses 4 to 10 and of `eu-central-1`, which had to be scoped under DEC-807 on
2026-12-11, given a rewritten programme document under DEC-808 on 2026-12-18, and commissioned for January.
**Days spent arguing a grade are days taken from a remedy that has a fixed deadline and a real delivery
behind it.**

**The supplementary audit took one remote auditor-day.** An auditor verifying a correction that consists of
a rewritten programme document, an audit report from a scope the client wrote, a decision record and a
library change is performing a reading exercise. An auditee that had contested the grade in December would
have arrived in January asking the same auditor to accept a corrective action for a nonconformity it had
argued did not exist at that grade.

**The acceptance is one line in GOV-30, and the record is thin on purpose.** There is no reasoned response,
no reservation of position, and no note that the organisation considered the grade harsh. A minute that
accepted the finding and then explained at length why it was severe would be a contest conducted in the
record rather than in the room.

**No dissent is recorded in this phase, and the absence is itself the record.** Phase 05 carries a minuted
dissent on voluntary disclosure of the isolation finding and Phase 07 carries Ana-Sofia Cruz's on notifying
forty-one customers where no commitment required it, because in both cases reasonable people disagreed.
**Nobody disagreed about `MAJ-01`.** A reader who notices that a phase built around one severe finding
contains no dissent has noticed something true about it, and manufacturing one to match a pattern would
have been the dishonest move.

**The cost is that the record contains no test of the grade.** Nothing anywhere establishes that a minor was
unavailable; what is established is that CloudNimbus did not seek one. A later reader who thinks the finding
was over-graded will find no counter-argument in these documents, and that is the price of the decision
rather than an oversight in recording it.

**And the acceptance is not an admission about anything beyond clause 9.2.** It says the internal audit
programme did not report on conformity to the requirements. It does not say that a control failed, that
data was exposed, that the Annex A work was unsound, or that Camberwell performed badly. **08.01 §6** and
**08.05 §5** hold those lines, and the acceptance does not move them.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

**Put the case for a minor at the closing meeting** — rejected. The three strongest facts available for it —
that the Annex A coverage was genuine, that the programme was resourced and that the auditor was independent
— are all true and none of them answers the finding, which is that the programme could not report on
conformity to clauses 4 to 10 and did not reach one of three regions. **An argument built entirely from
facts the finding does not dispute is an argument that concedes the finding at length.**

**Accept the finding and reserve the position in writing** — rejected. A reservation is a contest with a
delay attached: it preserves the option to argue later, which means the certification body reads the
corrective action as submitted under protest and the auditee has to decide, at the supplementary audit,
whether to exercise it. **The option has a cost every day it is open and is worth nothing if it is never
used.**

**Argue the A.5.22 limb separately, as a second and lesser finding** — rejected, and it was the most
tempting of the three. Splitting `MAJ-01` into a clause 9.2 finding and an A.5.22 finding would have
invited a grade on each and the second would plausibly have been a minor. But it is **one failure**: the
scope was inadequate, the scope was a supplier instruction, and the instruction was never reviewed for
adequacy. **Recording one thing as two doubles a count and halves a meaning**, and Northgate's own statement
says why it is one — outsourcing the work did not outsource the accountability.

**Delay acceptance to take advice** — rejected. Nothing about the finding turned on a question anybody
outside the room could answer, the facts were the organisation's own, and the closing meeting is where an
auditee is expected to respond. Deferring would have consumed part of twenty days to reach the same answer.
