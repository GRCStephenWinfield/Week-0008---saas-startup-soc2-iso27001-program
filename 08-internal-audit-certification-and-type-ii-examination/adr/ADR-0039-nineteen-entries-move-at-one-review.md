## Context

The risk register has been reviewed quarterly under **CAL-06** since 2026-03. **Three of those occurrences
reviewed a register** — 2026-06-15, 2026-09-29 and 2026-12-29 — because no register existed until the
baseline of 2026-04-10. The movement history to 2026-12-29 is short and, read alone, unimpressive.

| Review | Movement |
|---|---|
| 2026-03 | The risk assessment **method**, not a register — the baseline did not exist until 2026-04-10 |
| 2026-06-15 | **First review of a register.** One entry moved, downward: **R-37**, re-rated from 4 × 5 = 20 to 2 × 5 = 10 on the retested isolation remediation |
| 2026-09-29 | **Second review. Six reductions proposed, none accepted.** One entry moved, upward and unproposed: R-08 from 4 × 3 = 12 to 5 × 3 = 15 |
| Between reviews | **R-38** admitted 2026-10-23 under **DEC-707**; **R-24** re-rated upward 2026-10-28 under **DEC-715**. DEC-306 is the standing authority for admitting and re-rating between reviews; it is not either decision |
| 2026-12-29 | **Third review, and the fourth CAL-06 occurrence.** Nineteen entries moved |

**So the first two reviews of a register produced two movements between them — one down in June and one up
in September — and the third produced nineteen.** Every one of those earlier reviews stated on the record why
it was not reducing: **it was waiting for a
population.** 06.13 §4 put it in terms — *a quarterly review at which nothing was reduced is not a review
that found nothing; it is a review whose evidence was three months old in a six-month window, at an
organisation that had just missed a commitment.*

The December review of **2026-12-29** is the first held after the observation window's evidence was
complete. The proposals before it covered nineteen entries, six of them for two steps.

**The objection was raised inside the review before it was raised anywhere else**, by Rahul Bhargava as
secretary, and it is the reason this record exists: *nineteen entries moving at one meeting is what a
rubber stamp looks like from outside, and a register that moves once a year in a single block has less
credibility than one that moves steadily, whatever the underlying evidence.*

## Decision

**The nineteen movements are accepted, the six two-step movements are argued individually, seven entries
are held, and the objection is recorded in the review minute and in this record rather than answered
informally. DEC-809; the R-08 movement separately at DEC-810.**

**Entering: 38 — 8 High · 18 Moderate · 12 Low. Leaving: 38 — 0 High · 17 Moderate · 21 Low.**
19 moved + 7 held + 12 unchanged Low = 38.

Four conditions were attached to the acceptance and all four are visible in **GOV-32**.

**Every movement states the population it moved on.** Not the treatment that was delivered — the population
of occurrences the window produced against it. A movement whose supporting line reads "TP-14 complete" was
sent back; a movement whose line reads "312 changes, 40 sampled, 2 deviations" was taken.

**Every two-step movement is argued separately.** R-01, R-05, R-08, R-10, R-13 and R-14. A second step is
not the first step repeated; it is a claim that an event *expected at least once a year* has become one
*foreseeable but not expected within the certification cycle*, and the anchors in `03.02` §2 are three
years apart.

**Seven entries are held against the same evidence that moved the others**, each with a stated reason, and
four of the seven are held because their described event occurred inside the window.

**R-06 moved one step and not two, against its own Phase 03 forecast of 2 × 4 = 8**, and the divergence is
recorded rather than reconciled away.

## Consequences

**The register's credibility now rests on the entry-level record rather than on the shape of the
movement history.** A reader who looks only at the three band counts across the register's three reviews sees
nothing for nine months and then everything at once, and that reader is entitled to be suspicious. The answer available
is GOV-32's thirty-eight rows, each carrying a rating, a population and a reason. **A decision that can only
be defended by producing the working is a decision that had better have produced the working**, and it did.

**Three of the nineteen moved on evidence that will keep accruing and can move back.** R-08's corrected
emergency change path had six weeks behind it at the review — which is why it stopped two steps short of the
arithmetic; `CNB-C-149`, which carries R-06's single step, had sixty-five nights; and R-14 rests partly on
CUEC-05's 229 non-responses, which are neither failures nor successes and will still be neither in March.
**A rating that moved on the evidence available in December is a rating the March review has to test again**,
and the movement rule cuts in both directions: R-24 proved that in October when a floor stopped an entry
going down and nothing stopped it going up.

**R-14 is the weakest of the nineteen and is recorded as such.** It moved on CloudNimbus's own preventive
export controls rather than on the customer behaviour the entry describes, because 411 confirmations out of
640 are unverified self-assertions carrying no assurance weight and 229 non-responses are neither failures
nor successes. `03.07` §5.2 forecast R-14 stopping at 3 × 3 = 9 precisely because forecasting a user
entity's diligence down to likelihood 2 would be forecasting somebody else's behaviour. **The review moved
it anyway, on a different basis from the one the forecast contemplated**, and a reader who thinks that
basis is insufficient has the minute to argue with.

**The forecast comparison is not performed here.** Publishing the close position and marking it against the
Phase 03 forecast in the same document would be the register grading its own movement. Phase 09 takes it,
entry by entry, in both directions.

**And nothing was closed or removed.** Thirty-eight entries entered the review and thirty-eight left it. A
risk that stops being likely is re-rated, not deleted, and the population has changed exactly twice in the
register's life, both times by addition on evidence.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

**Move a subset now and the rest at the March review** — rejected, and this was the closest call. Spreading
nineteen movements across two reviews would have produced a movement history that looks more like
continuous risk management, and it was refused because the second half would then have been held back for a
reason that had nothing to do with the evidence. **The evidence for all nineteen arrived on the same day,
because the window closed on the same day for all of them.** Deferring movements whose support is complete,
in order to make a chart look better, is the presentational management of a register rather than the
management of risk — and the deferred entries would have sat for a quarter at ratings the programme had
already concluded were wrong.

**Move the six two-step entries by one step each and revisit in March** — rejected on the same reasoning,
with one exception: it is exactly what was done to **R-08**, and DEC-810 records why. R-08's corrected path
had six weeks of evidence and six weeks is not a window. The other five have the full period behind them,
and applying R-08's caution to entries that do not share R-08's thinness would be caution as a habit rather
than as a judgement.

**Decline the reductions again and let the register close at 8 High** — rejected. A programme that says for
nine months that it will move when the evidence arrives, and then does not move when the evidence arrives,
has not been disciplined. It has been unfalsifiable. **The September review's refusals are only defensible
if the December review's acceptances happened**, and the two records have to be read together or neither
means anything.

**Answer the objection informally and minute only the outcome** — rejected without much discussion. The
objection is the more transferable half of this record: the movement is specific to one register in one
quarter, and *a review that moves nineteen entries looks worse than one that moved two a quarter, and is
better* is a sentence another programme can use.
