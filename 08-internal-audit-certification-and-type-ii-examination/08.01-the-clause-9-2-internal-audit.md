## 1. The audit, and the control that governs it

**Camberwell Risk Partners audited the CloudNimbus information security management system from 2026-09-14 to
2026-09-25** — ten working days, lead auditor **Priya Raghunathan**, with a second auditor for the
fieldwork weeks. It is **CAL-14** in the assurance calendar and **MS-12** in the programme milestones, and
it is the first internal audit the ISMS has ever had, the management system having been declared operational
on 2026-06-15.

The engagement is outsourced, which was **DEC-110** at chartering, and the independence position it rests on
is the four-way separation **ADR-0004** fixed in January: Solstice Trust Advisors advised on control design
and may not audit its own work; Camberwell audits and may not have advised; Northgate Certification Services
certifies and may not consult; Ashcombe &amp; Doyle examines and may not design, implement or operate what it
examines. Camberwell's independence check for this engagement was recorded before the audit plan was agreed.

The governing control is **`CNB-C-146`**, and it is quoted here in full because everything in this chapter
turns on the gap between what it says and what happened.

> *The internal audit programme required by clause 9.2 is risk-weighted **across clauses 4 to 10 and the
> Annex A controls determined necessary**, is set annually with a recorded independence check on the
> auditor, and each audit runs to an agreed plan with findings entered in the improvement register.*
>
> Family `ISO`. Type **Detective**. Cadence **Annual**. Owner **Karim Haddad**. Policy `POL-19`.
> Criteria **—**. Annex A **—**.

Two properties of that row are worth naming before the findings. **It cites no trust services criterion**,
which is why nothing in this chapter appears in Section IV of a SOC 2 report and why the finding that
eventually arrives against it is a nonconformity and not a test exception. And **it cites no Annex A control
either**, because Annex A contains no internal audit control: A.5.35 is an independent review of the
approach to managing information security, which is a different activity performed by a different party for
a different purpose. Clause 9.2's obligation lives in clause 9.2.

## 2. What the audit covered, and what it did not

**The audit covered the 93 Annex A controls in the Statement of Applicability** — the 91 determined
necessary, tested against the implementation status the SoA records for each, and the 2 determined not
necessary, tested against the justification for exclusion that clause 6.1.3 d) requires. Ten working days
across four themes is a real audit and it was performed as one: sampling, interviews with named control
owners, evidence drawn from the shared store rather than assembled for the auditor, and a documented plan
agreed before it started.

**It did not cover clauses 4 to 10.** Not partially, not at a lower depth, not by sampling — the audit
criteria as stated in the plan were the Statement of Applicability, and no clause of the requirements had a
row in the programme. **It did not cover `eu-central-1`**, which is one of the three AWS regions inside an
ISMS scope that is the whole organisation.

The reason is not a lapse and is worth setting out exactly, because a reader who assumes carelessness will
draw the wrong lesson from the rest of the phase.

**The audit programme document is POL-19's annexe, and it was written at chartering in January 2026.** At
that date the programme had a charter, a governance cadence, an obligations register and an assurance
calendar. It had no control library, no risk register and no ISMS. What it had, by April, was a **Statement
of Applicability** — 93 rows, 91 necessary, each with a justification and an implementation status — and
that was the only complete inventory of auditable things the organisation possessed. A programme document
that scoped the audit by reference to it was, in January and again in April, the only programme document
anybody could have written.

**`01.11` §7 recorded the consequence in January, before any of this happened.** CAL-14 reads *"Annual
programme, risk-weighted,"* which fixes frequency and prioritisation basis and fixes nothing about coverage:
not whether the programme audits the requirements of clauses 4 to 10 themselves, not whether coverage
extends to all locations inside the ISMS scope, not whether the whole ISMS is covered in one cycle or across
several, and not who defines the audit criteria and scope for each individual audit. Defining coverage was
**deferred to an internal audit charter**, and that charter is a Phase 08 artefact — which is to say it did
not exist when the audit ran. The exposure was carried as programme risk **PR-06**: *where the client has
not defined coverage, the supplier will define it.*

**`CNB-C-146` was written in June**, when the control library was built, and it describes a programme
risk-weighted across clauses 4 to 10 and the Annex A controls determined necessary. **Nobody brought the
programme document up to it, and nothing required anybody to.** That divergence — its shape, its class and
why it is the harder direction to notice — is **08.07 §3**'s and is not argued here.

## 3. What the audit found

**Two minor nonconformities and nine opportunities for improvement**, all entered in the improvement
register under `CNB-C-148` on the day the report was accepted.

| ID | Requirement | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NC-INT-01` | Clause **7.5.3** | Three items of documented information carried a review date that had passed by more than thirty days | **Closed 2026-10-09** |
| `NC-INT-02` | Clause **9.1** | The ISMS determines **what** is monitored and measured, and for six of the sixteen assurance calendar items it does not document **the methods for evaluating the results** | **Partially closed** |

**`NC-INT-02` is the better of the two findings and it was nearly mis-stated in the response.** Clause 9.1
requires the organisation to determine what needs to be monitored and measured, **the methods for
monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation** needed to ensure valid results, when the monitoring and
measuring shall be performed, who shall perform it, when the results shall be analysed and evaluated, and
who shall analyse and evaluate them. CloudNimbus had the *what*, the *when* and the *who* for all sixteen
calendar items — that is what an assurance calendar with a named owner per line is — and for six of them it
had nothing written down about **how a result would be judged**. A control that produces a number every
quarter and no recorded rule for reading the number is a control that will be read differently by whoever
reads it, and the first draft of the management response treated the finding as a documentation gap. It is
not: it is the difference between measuring and evaluating, and clause 9.1 asks for both.

**Closure and correction are not the same thing, and `NC-INT-01` is where this phase's clearest illustration
of that starts.** Three stale items were brought current on 2026-10-09 and the record was closed. Nothing
was done to the mechanism that let them go stale. Eight weeks later Northgate found two further items in the
same condition and raised **`MIN-01`**. That sequence belongs to **08.06**, and it is named here only so
that the closure date above is not read as an ending.

**The nine opportunities** carry the 2026 programme's own identifiers rather than this phase's, because they
are the internal auditor's and not the certification body's, and they group into four themes.

| Theme | Count | What they concerned |
|---|---|---|
| Documented information | 3 | Review cadence, version history on two procedures, and the retention rule applied to superseded issues |
| Monitoring and reporting | 2 | Trend presentation to the Trust Committee, and the granularity at which control-owner attestations are recorded |
| Supplier and third-party | 2 | Tier definitions in the vendor register, and the reading recorded against a subservice organisation's report |
| Competence and awareness | 2 | Role-based content for the eight security staff, and evidence of understanding rather than of attendance |

**An opportunity for improvement is not a nonconformity and carries no obligation.** Six of the nine were
adopted, two were scheduled into the 2027 cycle and one was declined with the reason recorded — which is
what the category is for, and what **08.06 §5** says again about Northgate's seven.

## 4. What the audit did well, and it is not a formality to say so

The programme's instinct in this phase is to read every finding backwards from `MAJ-01`, and that produces a
false account of the September audit. **Inside the scope it was given, the audit was good.** It found a
clause 7.5.3 breach and a clause 9.1 gap that had survived two internal reviews and a readiness advisor. It
sampled from the evidence store rather than from what control owners offered it, which is the ML-1
remediation being tested by somebody who did not design it. It reported to Karim Haddad and, through him, to
the management review, which is clause 9.2.2 c) discharged.

And it did one thing more, which is §5's subject.

## 5. The scope limitation was written down by the auditor, and read

> Camberwell's report §1.3 states the scope on its face and adds a note: the programme excludes clauses 4 to
> 10 and one of the three regions in the ISMS scope, and Camberwell **recommends the exclusion be
> reconsidered before the certification audit.** Karim Haddad read it on 2026-09-25, agreed with it, and
> scheduled clause coverage into the 2027 programme — **DEC-801**, 2026-09-28. It was then presented to the
> clause 9.3 management review of 2026-09-30 under input d) 3) and **deferred there too** — DEC-802.
>
> **The scope limitation was foreseen in writing by the party later blamed for not finding the gap, read by
> the person who set the scope, minuted at the highest governance body in the ISMS, and deferred twice.**
> Nothing about it was missed. It was decided.

Three things follow from that paragraph and each is uncomfortable in a different way.

**The first is that the record is complete.** There is no missing artefact anywhere in this sequence. EV-801
holds Camberwell's report exactly as delivered, including §1.3. EV-802 holds the audit plan, the recorded
independence check and the programme document as it stood. EV-804 holds the management review minute with
input d) 3) carrying the report and its note. A programme with a gap in its records could at least argue
that it did not know. **This one knew, in writing, three times, and can prove it.**

**The second is that the deferral had a reason, and the reason was not a bad one at the time.** Clause
coverage was scheduled into the 2027 programme rather than bolted onto 2026 because the 2026 audit had
already run, because a second audit inside the same annual cycle would have had to be commissioned,
resourced and completed in the nine weeks before Stage 2, and because — this is the part that reads worst
in hindsight — **Stage 1 was three weeks away and nobody expected it to be silent about the internal audit
programme.** What a clean Stage 1 was then taken to mean is **08.03 §5**'s, and it is the misreading rather
than the deferral that ADR-0036 records.

**The third is that being right early is worth nothing on its own.** `01.11` §7 named this exposure in
January 2026 and gave it a risk number. Camberwell named it in September and recommended reconsideration.
Both were correct, both were recorded, and the gap was still open on 2026-12-02. **A programme that writes
down the right thing and then does not act on it has not demonstrated foresight; it has demonstrated that
its records are better than its decisions**, and the two are not the same asset.

## 6. Camberwell audited what it was asked to audit

**That is what an outsourced auditor does, and the outsourcing must not be allowed to carry blame the scope
decision earns.**

The internal audit is a supplier service. CloudNimbus wrote the scope, approved the plan, received the
report, read the note on its face, and had every opportunity to widen the engagement. Camberwell had no
authority to audit outside the criteria it was given and would have been criticised had it tried. It went
further than the engagement required by writing the limitation into the report and recommending that it be
reconsidered — which is precisely what an auditor with no mandate to widen the scope can do about a scope it
thinks is wrong.

The corollary is the half CloudNimbus owns. **A.5.22 — monitoring, review and change management of supplier
services — requires the organisation to regularly monitor, review, evaluate and manage change in supplier
information security practices and service delivery**, and the internal audit supplier is a supplier like
any other. **The words about information security practices are the ones that make an audit supplier's
scope an A.5.22 matter at all**: a review confined to service delivery asks whether the work arrived, and
the question here is about the security work the scope did and did not require. CloudNimbus defined the
scope it gave Camberwell in January and **never reviewed whether that scope was adequate to the requirement
it existed to satisfy.** That is one failure with two limbs and
not two failures, and how Northgate recorded it is **08.05**'s.

## 7. What this chapter does not claim

**It does not claim the audit was inadequate.** Within its criteria it was competent, evidenced and useful,
and two of its findings survived into December because they were right, not because they were weak.

**It does not claim that a wider scope would have produced a different December.** An internal audit of
clauses 4 to 10 conducted in September might have found the same things Northgate found, or fewer, or
others; nobody knows, and the correction audit of January 2027 is the nearest available evidence rather
than a controlled comparison. What a wider scope would certainly have produced is a programme able to
report on conformity to the requirements conformity is assessed against, which is the finding.

**And it does not state anything about the examination.** Clause 9.2 has no trust services criterion,
`CNB-C-146` appears in no Section IV, and nothing in this chapter is a deviation, an exception or a
conclusion about an opinion.
