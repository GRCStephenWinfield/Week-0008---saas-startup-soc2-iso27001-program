## 1. The review, and the control that governs it

**The clause 9.3 management review was held on 2026-09-30, chaired by Elise Fontaine.** It is **CAL-15**,
**MS-13**, and the first management review the ISMS has had. It ran three hours with Marisol Vega, Nathan
Oyelaran, Karim Haddad, Rahul Bhargava, Tobias Lund and Wes Delacroix present, and Lorraine Kessler
attending as chair of the Audit &amp; Risk Committee. The minute is **GOV-29** and the evidence unit is
**EV-804**.

The governing control is **`CNB-C-147`**:

> *The management review required by clause 9.3 is held at least annually, chaired by the Chief Executive
> Officer, with the agenda built from the clause 9.3.2 inputs and the minutes recording the clause 9.3.3
> outputs, and an input line marked not presented cannot be closed.*
>
> Family `ISO`. Type **Detective**. Cadence **Annual**. Owner **Elise Fontaine**. Policy `POL-01`.
> Criteria **—**. Annex A **—**.

**The control is owned by a named person and not by a committee**, which is ADR-0020 applied to the row
where it matters most: clause 9.3 places the obligation on top management, and assigning it to "the
executive team" would assign it to nobody. `04.11` §2.1 says so about this row specifically.

## 2. The seven inputs, in the standard's own order

Clause 9.3.2 lists seven inputs and the order is the standard's, not a convenience. Getting it wrong — and
in particular losing input c), which the 2022 edition separates from b) — is the tell that an agenda was
assembled from a template rather than from the clause. The agenda was built from the clause.

**All seven were presented.** None was marked "not presented", so `CNB-C-147`'s gate never had anything to
refuse. That fact is the chapter's subject and §5 says what it is worth.

## 3. Input b) and the climate determination

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022/Amd 1:2024 adds to clause 4.1 the requirement to determine whether climate change is a
relevant issue, and a note to clause 4.2 that interested parties can have climate-related requirements.** It
is in force and it is audited, and a management system whose clause 4.1 record is silent on it has an
incomplete determination rather than a negative one.

**CloudNimbus determined that climate change is a relevant issue for the ISMS**, and the determination is
narrow on purpose. The company owns no facility containing information processing equipment: the Denver
suite is 4,100 square feet of meeting space with no server, no network closet and no comms room, and the
231 managed endpoints live wherever their users do. What the determination reaches is therefore **physical
infrastructure CloudNimbus does not own** — the availability of the three AWS regions the platform runs in
under conditions the operator manages and CloudNimbus does not — and **a supplier concentration**, because
all seven accounts, four clusters and six database clusters sit with one infrastructure provider, and
`eu-central-1` cannot be failed over at all under obligation **O8**.

That is a real determination and it connects to entries the register already holds: **R-19**, disaster
recovery failing to meet the four-hour objective in a real event, and **R-20**, loss of `eu-central-1`. Both
sit at 2 × 5 = 10 and cannot move.

**The determination was correct and it was in the wrong place.** At the review it sat in a decision log. It
belongs in the **clause 4.1 documented information**, because that is where clause 4.1's determination
lives and a decision log is a record of decisions rather than a record of context. Northgate raised the
placement as **`AOC-06`** at Stage 1 on 2026-10-14 and it was moved into the clause 4.1 record on
2026-10-22.

## 4. Input d) 3), and what the review did with it

**The scope note was presented complete, correct and on the day.** Camberwell's report went to the review
whole, with §1.3 on its face and its recommendation intact, three business days after Karim Haddad had read
it and five days after DEC-801 had scheduled clause coverage into the 2027 programme rather than 2026.

**The review confirmed the deferral. That is DEC-802, taken by Elise Fontaine on 2026-09-30.**

The reasoning minuted is short and was not unreasonable in the room. A second internal audit inside the same
annual cycle would have to be commissioned, scoped, resourced and completed in the nine weeks before Stage
2 opened, against a programme already carrying a certification audit, a penetration test and an observation
window. The 2027 programme was the next scheduled opportunity and it was six months away. Nobody in the room
argued that the exclusion was acceptable in principle; the question put and answered was **when**, not
**whether**.

**The full account of what the deferral cost belongs to 08.01 §5**, which owns the sequence, and to **08.05**,
which owns the finding it became. What belongs here is the single fact this chapter is accountable for:
**the highest governance body in the ISMS, chaired by the Chief Executive Officer, received the gap in
writing and decided to leave it.**

## 5. The clause 9.3.3 outputs

Clause 9.3.3 requires the outputs of the management review to include **decisions related to continual
improvement opportunities and any need for changes to the information security management system**, and
`CNB-C-147` requires the minutes to record them. They are minuted as decisions with owners rather than as
themes, because a theme has no due date and cannot be closed.

| Output | Owner | Where it went |
|---|---|---|
| The clause 9.2 audit programme is extended to clauses 4 to 10 and to all regions **in the 2027 programme** | Karim Haddad | **DEC-802**, confirming DEC-801 |
| `NC-INT-01`'s three stale items are brought current, and the review date discipline is re-stated to document owners | Rahul Bhargava | Closed 2026-10-09; the mechanism was not changed, which became `MIN-01` |
| `NC-INT-02`'s evaluation methods are documented for the six calendar items that lack them | Rahul Bhargava | Partially closed; four remained open and became `MIN-03` |
| The corrective actions `CA-06-01` to `CA-06-04` are carried with dated closures at the Trust Committee monthly | Karim Haddad | `CA-06-04`'s undated closure became `AOC-03` |
| The per-region availability reporting basis stands, and no blended platform figure is produced | Wes Delacroix | Held across the window; **there is still no window average** |
| The objectives re-set on 2026-09-28 stand, and the two measures that cannot yet be evaluated are carried to the quarterly report under `CNB-C-144` rather than re-set | Marisol Vega | **OBJ-03** is one of the two; `PR-48` carries it |
| No change to the ISMS scope, the risk criteria or the Statement of Applicability arises from this review | Karim Haddad | The SoA was nonetheless reissued five weeks later on `AOC-01`'s evidence — **DEC-803** |

**The last row is the one to read against the two below it.** The review determined that no change to the
ISMS was needed. Twelve working days later Northgate's Stage 1 raised six areas of concern, two of which
would have escalated to major nonconformities at Stage 2, and both of those were **documented information
that had fallen out of step with the organisation**: the Statement of Applicability's status fields and the
scope statement's determinations. Neither had been looked at during the review, because neither was an input
the clause names and nobody went looking beyond the agenda.

## 6. A control that guarantees the agenda cannot guarantee the decision

**`CNB-C-147` says an input line marked "not presented" cannot be closed. All seven inputs were presented.**

The control worked. Every limb of it operated: the review was held, it was chaired by the Chief Executive
Officer, the agenda was built from the clause 9.3.2 inputs in the clause's own order, and the clause 9.3.3
outputs were minuted as decisions with owners. An auditor testing `CNB-C-147` against this occurrence finds
a control that did what it says.

**The review's failure was not an absent input. It was what it did with input d) 3) when the input was
present, complete and correct.**

That distinction is worth holding onto because it is the second time this programme has met it in four
months, in a different family. `CNB-C-127` wrote a truthful, complete, timestamped record of a deletion job
that deleted nothing, sixty-eight times, and nothing in the library required anybody to read what the record
said. `CNB-C-147` requires an input to be presented and is silent about what the review must conclude —
necessarily silent, because a control that specified the decision would not be a management review, it
would be a rule. **The gate is on the agenda. The judgement is not gateable**, and a programme that reads a
complete agenda as evidence of a sound review has confused the two.

**There is a version of this chapter that treats the review as a failure, and it would be wrong.** The
inputs were right, the order was right, the outputs were minuted with owners, the escalation rule fired for
R-08 and reached the Chief Executive Officer and the Audit &amp; Risk Committee as `03.02` §6 requires, and
the record is complete enough that everything in **08.05** can be traced through it. **What the review did
badly was make one decision, in the open, with the right information in front of it.** That is a different
kind of failure from a broken process and it is not fixed by a better agenda.

## 7. What this chapter does not claim

**It does not claim the review would have decided differently with more time.** Nobody in the room proposed
commissioning a second audit and being turned down; the deferral was unanimous and unremarkable, which is
worse and is the point.

**It does not restate why the finding that followed is major**, which is **08.05 §4**'s, or the root cause,
which is **08.07 §3**'s.

**And it says nothing about the examination.** Clause 9.3 has no trust services criterion and `CNB-C-147`
appears in no Section IV. The evidence this review produced is of interest to a service auditor examining
CC4.1 and CC4.2, as `04.07` §3.3 said in June, and **evidence of interest is not a mapping**: citing CC4.1
against `CNB-C-147` would assert that the criterion requires an ISO management review, which it does not.
