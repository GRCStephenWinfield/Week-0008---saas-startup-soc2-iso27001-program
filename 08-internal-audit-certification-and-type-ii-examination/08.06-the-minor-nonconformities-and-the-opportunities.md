## 1. Four minors, and where each of them had been before

Stage 2 raised **four minor nonconformities** alongside the major. A minor nonconformity is a failure to
fulfil a requirement that does not affect the capability of the management system to achieve intended
results; it does not block certification, and it is closed on evidence of implementation verified at a
subsequent audit rather than before a decision is taken. **The distinction turns on capability and not on
how many occurrences a finding has** — `MIN-03` is four calendar items and `MIN-04` is two consecutive
refreshes, and both are minors.

| ID | Requirement | Finding | Where it had been before |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MIN-01` | Clause **7.5.3** | Two further items of documented information past their review date | **Camberwell raised this in September as `NC-INT-01` and it was recorded closed on 2026-10-09** |
| `MIN-02` | **A.5.30**, clause **8.1** | ICT readiness for business continuity: two of the four limbs — implementation and maintenance — are carried by no control in the library | **Phase 06's `IS-24`**, referred and not closed |
| `MIN-03` | **A.8.16**, clause **9.1** | Monitoring activities: the evaluation methods gap Camberwell raised as `NC-INT-02` remained open for four of the sixteen assurance calendar items | **Camberwell's `NC-INT-02`, partially closed** |
| `MIN-04` | **A.5.19**, **A.5.22** | One Tier 1 vendor held no current assurance artefact at two consecutive quarterly refreshes | **Phase 07's `D-07-02`**, found by CloudNimbus in July and again in October |

**Three of the four were already in CloudNimbus's own records before Northgate arrived**, and §6 says what
that is worth. Each of the four is taken in turn first, because each fails in a different way and treating
them as one paragraph would lose all four lessons.

## 2. `MIN-01` — a correction is not a corrective action, with a date on it

**Camberwell found three items of documented information more than thirty days past their review date on
2026-09-25. They were brought current, and the record was closed on 2026-10-09.** Eight weeks later Northgate
found two more in the same condition.

**Clause 10.2 distinguishes the two responses and the September closure took only the first.** The clause
requires the organisation to react to a nonconformity and, as applicable, take action to control and correct
it and deal with the consequences — **that is the correction** — and then to evaluate the need for action to
eliminate the causes so that it does not recur or occur elsewhere, by reviewing the nonconformity,
determining its causes and determining whether similar nonconformities exist or could potentially occur —
**that is the corrective action**. Three stale items were brought current. **Nothing determined why they had
gone stale, whether others were in the same condition, or what would stop the next ones.**

**And the mechanism was available and was not asked about.** `CNB-C-145` records an item's review date at
the approval-gated merge that puts it in the controlled repository, so the review date exists as a field on
every one of the thirty-seven items of documented information. What nothing in the library does is **read
the field against the calendar and tell somebody**. `CNB-C-023` reports controls overdue against their
cadence; documented information overdue against its review date is a different population and has no
equivalent report. That is the cause, it was findable in October, and it took an external auditor in
December to make anybody look for it.

**This is the phase's clearest illustration of the distinction because it has dates on both halves.**
Correction: 2026-10-09, three items, effective, complete, closed. Corrective action: not taken.
Consequence: `MIN-01`, 2026-12-03, two items. **A closure record that says "closed" and a cause analysis
that says nothing are not in tension with each other; the first is simply answering a smaller question**,
and an improvement register that does not force the second question will accumulate closures that predict
nothing.

## 3. `MIN-02` — a referral is a finding you have written down, not one you have answered

**A.5.30 requires ICT readiness for business continuity to be planned, implemented, maintained and tested.**
`CNB-C-136` plans it and `CNB-C-100` tests it. **Nothing in the library cites A.5.30 for the other two
limbs.** `06.10` §3.2 established that in September, widened the finding from one limb to two when it
applied its own rule to itself, and referred it as **`IS-24`** rather than editing a citation onto a nearby
row — on the reasoning that a library which corrects itself silently cannot demonstrate that it was ever
wrong.

**The referral was the right call and it did not make the finding go away.** Northgate raised the same gap
at Stage 2 against **A.5.30 and clause 8.1**, and it is now a minor nonconformity with a corrective action
plan accepted on 2026-12-19 rather than an internal referral with an owner and no due date. `IS-24` is
**closed by `MIN-02`** — the only closure in the RAID log this phase records, and it closes because somebody
else answered it.

**Both halves of that are worth keeping.** Refusing to add a citation to `CNB-C-088` or `CNB-C-101` — which
cite A.5.29 and would have made the table look complete — preserved the evidence that the gap existed. And
three months of referral produced no internal treatment. **A referral with an owner is a record of a
problem; it is not a treatment of one**, and a programme that refers a great deal is a programme that will
eventually meet its referrals coming the other way with somebody else's letterhead on them.

**The Statement of Applicability's own entry is the uncomfortable part.** A.5.30 reads `Implemented`, moved
there at the reissue of 2026-06-15 before `CNB-C-100` had operated, and the status became supportable in
August only because an exercise scheduled in January happened to fall inside the gap. `IS-22` carries that
as the clause 7.5 lesson. The status field is not what `MIN-02` is about — the missing limbs are — but a
reader who arrives at A.5.30 through the SoA will see `Implemented` against a control two of whose four
limbs nothing in the library carries.

## 4. `MIN-03` — a partially closed nonconformity, found again

**`NC-INT-02` was partially closed and four of the sixteen calendar items still had no documented evaluation
method when Northgate looked.**

Northgate raised it against **A.8.16 monitoring activities and clause 9.1**, which is the better pairing:
clause 9.1 requires the methods for monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation to be determined, and
A.8.16 requires networks, systems and applications to be monitored for anomalous behaviour with appropriate
actions taken to evaluate potential incidents. A calendar item that produces a number every quarter with no
recorded rule for reading it satisfies neither.

**"Partially closed" is an honest status and it is also a status that stops being read.** The September
finding said six items; the October response fixed two and left four, and the improvement register recorded
partial closure with the remaining four named. Nothing was hidden. What happened instead is that a partially
closed record sits in a register alongside closed ones and attracts the attention a closed one does, which
is none. **The difference between "open" and "partially closed" is invisible in a status column and total in
substance**, and the corrective action accepted in December addresses the four items and the register
convention together.

## 5. `MIN-04` — finding it three times and not closing it is the finding

**One Tier 1 vendor held no current assurance artefact at two consecutive quarterly refreshes.**
`CNB-C-092` requires the current assurance artefact for every Tier 1 vendor — its service auditor's report
or its certificate, with a reading of what that document does and does not cover — to be refreshed each
quarter and the conclusion recorded in the vendor register. The vendor's last report lapsed on 2026-06-30.
**The negative reading form was completed at both refreshes**, on 2026-07-30 and on 2026-10-07, which means
the control operated correctly on both occasions and recorded that there was nothing to read.

**CloudNimbus found it in July. CloudNimbus found it again in October. Northgate found it in December.**
Phase 07 recorded it as **`D-07-02`** with `CA-07-03` attached, and the same fact is **test exception 6** at
2 of 24 readings, 8.3%.

**A lapse detected and left standing is a different finding from a lapse undetected**, and Northgate's
statement is against **A.5.19** — information security in supplier relationships — and **A.5.22**, not
against the detection. The control saw it twice. **What no control does is escalate on the second
consecutive negative reading**, which is why the corrective action addresses the cadence rather than the
vendor: a quarterly refresh that produces the same negative answer twice has told the organisation
something the refresh itself is not equipped to act on.

**A.5.22 appears here and in `MAJ-01`, and the two are not the same failure.** In `MAJ-01` the supplier's
service was adequate and the **scope CloudNimbus set** was not reviewed. Here the supplier's **assurance
position** lapsed and the review that detected it had nowhere to send the result. Same control, two
different limbs, and `08.05` owns the first.

## 6. The sentence this chapter exists for

**Three of the four minors were already in CloudNimbus's own records before Northgate arrived, and that is
what a working programme looks like — the certification body confirming what the programme found rather
than discovering it.**

That is a real result and it should be read as one. A monitoring apparatus that has independently written
down three of the four minor findings an accredited certification body raises, with dates, owners and
corrective actions attached, is an apparatus that is functioning. **08.12 §3** records that the engagement
team examining CC4.1 and CC4.2 read the same fact and gave it weight.

**The exception is `MAJ-01`, which the programme also had in its records and decided about, which is
worse.** An entity that failed to notice a gap has a detection problem, and detection problems are
tractable: build a report, set a cadence, name an owner. An entity that noticed a gap, wrote it down,
carried it to its highest governance body and decided to leave it has a problem no additional monitoring
answers, because the monitoring already worked.

**And the three that were found are not free of that shape either.** `MIN-01` was found and closed without a
cause. `MIN-03` was found and closed partially. `MIN-04` was found twice by the same control and escalated
by nothing. **Finding a thing is the cheap half.** The four minors, taken together, say that CloudNimbus's
detection is good and its treatment discipline is where the next findings will come from — which is a more
useful conclusion than four separate observations about four separate clauses.

## 7. Seven opportunities for improvement

**An opportunity for improvement is not a nonconformity and carries no obligation.** No requirement has been
found unfulfilled; nothing has to be corrected; nothing is verified at a surveillance audit; declining one
breaches nothing. It is an auditor's observation that something could be better, offered because the auditor
saw it and the organisation may not have.

| ID | Requirement area | Opportunity | What CloudNimbus did |
|---|---|---|---|
| `OFI-01` | Clause 7.4 | Communication about the ISMS is performed but is not planned as a set — on what, when, with whom, by whom and how | **Not adopted at the time.** §8 |
| `OFI-02` | Clause 6.2 | Two of the eight objectives carry a measure read from a status report rather than from the evidence store, which `CNB-C-144` requires | Adopted; both measures re-sourced 2027-01-15 |
| `OFI-03` | A.5.7 | Threat intelligence dispositions are recorded, but the source set itself is not periodically re-evaluated | Adopted; added to `CNB-C-134`'s semi-annual membership review |
| `OFI-04` | Clause 7.2 | Competence for the two people who operate the evidence store is held informally, against the concentration **R-25** describes | Adopted; scheduled into the 2027 competence cycle |
| `OFI-05` | A.8.16 | Alert-tuning decisions from `CNB-C-070`'s weekly review are not linked to the register entries they bear on | **Declined, with the reason recorded.** The link would be created by hand and would decay |
| `OFI-06` | Clause 9.1 | Monitoring results are presented to the management review annually where the underlying data is monthly | Adopted; the monthly series is carried to the 2027 review |
| `OFI-07` | A.5.36 | `CNB-C-023` reports controls overdue against their cadence and does not report a control whose evidence class produced **no unit** | **Declined for now, and referred.** It is `IS-33`'s question asked from the other end |

**Five adopted, two declined with reasons recorded**, and the two declinations are the rows worth reading.

**`OFI-05` was declined on maintenance grounds and the reasoning is the ordinary one.** A link created by
hand between an alert-tuning decision and a register entry is correct on the day it is made and decays from
the next one; a stale link is worse than none, because it asserts a relationship somebody would have to
check before trusting. Nothing about declining it is a judgement that the observation was wrong.

**`OFI-07` is the same question `IS-33` asks, arriving from outside.** `07.03` §4 raised it after the RT-02
failure: the library holds controls whose operating condition is *that a record exists* rather than *what
the record says*, and nobody has enumerated them. Northgate arrived at the same place from the reporting
end — `CNB-C-023` reports a control overdue and cannot report a control whose evidence class produced
nothing at all. It was declined for now and referred, **and declining it does not reduce what `IS-33`
owes**, which is a number the programme still cannot state.

## 8. `OFI-01`, and what declining an opportunity does not protect you from

**Northgate raised clause 7.4 communication planning as an opportunity in December. CloudNimbus did not
adopt it. Five weeks later — Stage 2 closed 2026-12-04 and the correction audit opened 2027-01-05 — the same
subject arrived as a minor nonconformity** in the correction audit CloudNimbus itself commissioned: clause
7.4, on how the ISMS communicates changes to interested parties, raised by Camberwell between 2027-01-05 and
2027-01-09.

**No obligation was breached by declining it.** That is not a hedge; it is the whole content of the category.
An opportunity for improvement carries no requirement, the declination was recorded with its reason, and
Northgate is owed nothing on it.

**The finding still arrived**, and it arrived from a different auditor against a different framework element,
because the underlying condition was the same in January as it had been in December and neither auditor
invented it. **An opportunity is an auditor telling you something without the authority to make you do it**,
and the useful reading is not that declining one is risky — sometimes it is right, as `OFI-05` is — but that
the observation and its grade are independent facts. **The grade tells you what you owe. It tells you
nothing about whether the observation is true.**
