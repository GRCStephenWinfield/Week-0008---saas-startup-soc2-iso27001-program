## 1. Two request lists and one store

**Ashcombe &amp; Doyle LLP's request list ran to 427 items. Northgate Certification Services' Stage 2 and
supplementary requests came to 118.** Both were answered from the single shared evidence store **DEC-112**
established in January 2026, indexed by control, with each artefact tagged with the period it covers and
the frameworks it serves.

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Artefacts produced from the shared evidence store | **2,103** |
| — requested by Ashcombe &amp; Doyle | **1,847** |
| — requested by Northgate | **612** |
| — **requested by both, produced once** | **356** |
| 1,847 + 612 − 356 = 2,103 | |

**Turnaround: median 1.4 days from request to delivery. Eleven items took more than five days. The longest
took twelve.**

The twelve-day item is worth naming because it is the store's architecture producing a cost the
architecture was designed to accept. It is the **`eu-central-1` log residency evidence** — the demonstration
that the `cnb-security` archive is region-partitioned, that the EU bucket sits in `eu-central-1` under
EU-scoped keys, and that only pseudonymised or non-personal telemetry crosses regions. It took twelve days
because the evidence store **deliberately does not aggregate across that boundary**. A store that could
have produced the artefact in a day would have been a store that had already moved the data it exists to
prove was not moved. `CNB-C-065` and obligation **O8** are the reason, and the twelve days are what they
cost.

## 2. What a request actually is, and why the median matters more than the mean

A request in either list is a sentence naming a control or a criterion and a period, and it resolves — if
the evidence architecture worked — to a **class**, a **sampling unit** and a **selection**. 04.12 published
twenty-four classes and defined one unit for each, and the whole point of defining the unit in advance was
that a request would then be a lookup rather than an investigation.

The median of 1.4 days is the number that says whether that held. **A median of 1.4 days across 545
requests means the ordinary request was answered from the store by the person who received it**, without a
control owner being interrupted, without a system being queried live, and without anybody reconstructing
what had happened in August from a calendar and a memory. That is ADR-0018's rule working: *a control's
evidence is declared before the control is built, not discovered after it has been operating.*

The mean would have been higher and less informative, because eleven long items drag it. The eleven are
worth characterising as a group rather than listed, and they divide into three kinds. **Four crossed a
region boundary** the store does not cross, of which the log residency item is the longest. **Five required
a named individual rather than a stored artefact** — an interview note, a signature, a confirmation of what
a record meant — which is not an evidence-store failure but is a reminder that a store holds artefacts and
not explanations. **Two were requests for something that had never been declared as a class at all**, and
both were resolved by producing the underlying records and saying so. Nothing in the eleven was a control
whose evidence had been lost.

**ML-1 is the finding all of this answers**, and it is worth measuring against rather than referring to.
The 2025 management letter recorded that access review evidence was retained as email threads and
screenshots, in a form that would not support a Type II sample. Eighteen months later the same activity
produced 24 units of **EC-01** across two quarterly certifications, each one a system for a quarter with an
entitlement extract, a per-line decision, the revocation tickets and a timestamped sign-off. The unit is
selectable. **That is the fix, and it is worth noticing that it did not stop ML-1's subject matter from
producing a test exception anyway** — 3 of 47 revocations were applied late, which is a fact about the
control and not about the evidence, and which a mailbox would have concealed.

## 3. What could not be produced

**One request could not be answered: item `AD-118`, the August 2026 restore test record.**

There is no artefact. `CNB-C-098`'s August occurrence did not take place, `D-06-01` records it, **ADR-0026**
records the decision not to re-perform it in September and date it August, and `IS-19` records that the
occurrence is unrecoverable.

The response to AD-118 was sent on the second day and contained four things: **there is no artefact; here is
the deficiency record; here is the clause 10.2 corrective action `CA-06-01`; and here is the reasoning for
not back-dating it.** Nothing was searched for, because 06.12 had already established that nothing existed.

**The evidence store's job in that exchange was to say "no" quickly, completely, and with the reason
attached.** A store that cannot answer "no" is a store that will eventually answer "yes" wrongly — because
the request does not go away when the artefact is absent, and the alternatives to a fast, complete negative
are all worse. A slow negative reads as an attempt to find something. A partial negative — the September
test, offered without comment — reads as an answer until somebody compares a timestamp against a calendar.
And an artefact assembled in January describing an activity that did not happen in August is the failure
mode that ends programmes.

The store was extended twice during the window to hold this shape of record. Phase 06 asked it for a unit
recording **a control that did not operate**. Phase 07 asked it for a unit recording **a control that
operated and had nothing to say** — `CNB-C-149`'s nightly eligible-population computation on the sixty-five
nights it raised no alert. Both extensions were made because a class that holds only successes is a class
that is empty at exactly the moment it is needed.

## 4. The dividend, and it is smaller than the rhetoric

**356 artefacts of 2,103 served two audits without being produced twice. That is 16.9%.**

**OBJ-03 targets 70%.** 01.07 set it at the share of artefacts serving both deliverables, 04.12 §5 restated
it, and the window produced 16.9%. That is not a near miss and this chapter is not going to present it as
one. It is the objective missing its target by a factor of four, on the measure the objective itself
names.

ADR-0001's case for a single control framework was argued in January 2026 in terms that implied more. The
argument was correct and the measure attached to it was wrong, and those are different failures. Saying the
real number first, and then saying why it is the wrong number to look at, is the only order in which the
second statement is worth anything.

**Most evidence is framework-specific and always will be.** A clause 9.2 audit plan is not a SOC 2 artefact.
A Section IV test population is not an ISO one. Camberwell's independence check, Northgate's Stage 1
documented-information review, the Statement of Applicability's status fields, the clause 9.3 minutes
against the seven named inputs — none of these has a counterpart in an examination, and none of them
*should*. Running in the other direction: the calculation-engine reconciliation records, the export delivery
acknowledgements, the CUEC disposition register and the description of the system itself have no
counterpart in a certification audit. **Two frameworks assessing different objects will ask for different
things, and an integration design that produced a high artefact-overlap number would be a design that had
flattened one of the two.**

**The dividend was never in the artefacts. It was in the 112 controls that serve both frameworks**, which
is where the design cost and the operating cost sit, and which 04.03 quantified: 112 dual, 21 SOC 2-only,
15 ISO-only, out of a library that stood at 148 when it was published and at 150 today. The saving is that
CloudNimbus designed, owned, scheduled, trained and operated **one** access provisioning control rather
than two, one change peer review rather than two, one retention job set rather than two — and produced
evidence from each of them once, into one store, in one unit, whether or not that unit was ever requested
twice.

**An integration argument defended on artefact counts is an integration argument that will lose**, because
the artefact count is the one number in the design that a sceptic can compute and that goes the sceptic's
way. The number that goes the other way is 112 of 148, and it is a number about controls, which is what the
programme actually unified.

Two consequences follow and both were recorded rather than absorbed.

**OBJ-03's measure is being taken to the quarterly objectives report.** `CNB-C-144` requires each
objective's measure to be read from the evidence store rather than from a status report, and requires an
objective whose measure cannot be evaluated from evidence to be **given a measure that can be, or
withdrawn**, with the decision minuted. OBJ-03's measure *can* be evaluated from evidence — that is how
16.9% was produced — so withdrawal is not the available answer. What is available is re-measuring the
objective against the thing it was always about, and that decision is not this phase's to take: the
objectives are re-set annually under `CNB-C-016`, the window's occurrence was 2026-09-28, and the next
falls outside this vantage. **`PR-48` carries it.**

**And the 356 is a floor rather than a ceiling for a reason nobody controls.** An artefact counts as shared
only if both parties asked for it. Several hundred more units in the store would have satisfied both had
both requested them, and neither party's request list is CloudNimbus's to write. **A measure of overlap
computed from two independent selections measures the selections at least as much as it measures the
store**, and reporting 16.9% without that sentence would be reporting a property of Ashcombe &amp; Doyle's
and Northgate's sampling as though it were a property of CloudNimbus's architecture.

## 5. What this chapter does not claim

**It does not claim the evidence was adequate.** Adequacy is a conclusion about whether the artefacts
support the propositions each assurance provider was testing, and neither provider's conclusion is
CloudNimbus's to report. What is reported is that the artefacts existed, were located, and were delivered —
which is a necessary condition and not a sufficient one.

**It does not claim the store is complete.** 04.12 §6 named three limitations and all three survive the
window intact. A class is a design and the artefact appears only if the control operates and the export
runs; several classes depend on a scheduled export, and **an export that silently stops produces a gap that
looks exactly like a control that stopped operating.** No such gap was found in this window. That is not the
same as there having been none.

**And it does not claim that a fast median is an assurance outcome.** Producing 2,103 artefacts in
nineteen working days for one engagement and six audit days for the other — Stage 2's five plus the
supplementary audit of 2027-01-13, which is the span Northgate's 118 requests actually covered — is an
operational achievement of a 4.6-FTE programme, and it says nothing whatever about what any of them showed. **Nine test exceptions
were found in this evidence**, every one of them by CloudNimbus before it was found by anybody else, and
the store's contribution to that was to make them findable.
