## 1. The proposition this chapter defends

**There is no standing production access at CloudNimbus, and that single fact is what makes the rest of the
control estate testable.**

The claim is not that removing standing access prevents more attacks than the alternatives. It is narrower
and more useful than that. A privileged action that requires elevation **leaves an elevation record** — a
request, an approver, a window, a session. A privileged action available to somebody who already holds the
access **leaves nothing but its effect**: a changed row, a restarted service, a deleted object, discovered
later by whoever notices the consequence. The first can be sampled. The second can only be reconstructed,
and reconstruction from effects is an investigation rather than a test.

Phase 08 will sample this estate. The population it samples from is created by the design decision described
here, and every other privileged control in this chapter inherits its evidentiary value from it.

## 2. What "no standing access" means concretely

Nobody at CloudNimbus holds, at rest, a credential that reaches a production console, a production host or a
production database. The tier model in 02.05 names infrastructure administration and application
administration as tiers people hold; what they hold is **eligibility**, not access.

Access is issued by the just-in-time broker under `CNB-C-034`, which grants a **time-boxed role of at most
four hours against an approved ticket** and records every elevation, including the invocation of privileged
utility programs. Three parameters make the mechanism what it is.

**Four hours is a ceiling, not a default.** An elevation may be shorter and usually is; what the ceiling
forbids is the quiet drift by which a temporary grant becomes a standing one. The expiry is enforced by the
broker rather than by a reminder.

**Approval is by a second engineer.** The requester cannot approve their own elevation. The approver is a
peer with the competence to know whether the stated reason justifies the requested scope, which is a
deliberate choice against routing approval to a manager who would approve it on trust. Segregation of duties
here is about knowledge, not seniority — CC6.3 asks for access authorisation that considers segregation of
duties, and a rubber stamp from someone who cannot evaluate the request satisfies the form and not the
criterion.

**Every session is recorded.** The recording is the artefact that distinguishes "an engineer had four hours
of database access" from "an engineer ran these statements". Recordings are reviewed by exception rather
than in full — a full review of every session would be a control nobody performs by month three — and the
exceptions that trigger review are set by the detection rules described in 05.10, which alert on privileged
role assumption among other conditions (`CNB-C-066`).

## 3. The path into production

There is exactly one interactive path, and it is short.

| Step | Mechanism | Control |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Authenticate | Workforce identity provider, phishing-resistant WebAuthn factor, no time-based code accepted on this path | `CNB-C-032`, `CNB-C-045` |
| 2. Prove the device | Mobile device management compliance signal read at the moment of the request; a non-compliant device is refused | `CNB-C-032`, `CNB-C-059`, `CNB-C-062` |
| 3. Reach the proxy | The zero-trust access proxy authenticates the user, checks the device and authorises per application; there is no VPN concentrator and no administrative interface exposed to the public internet | `CNB-C-053` |
| 4. Request elevation | Just-in-time broker, approved ticket, second-engineer approval, ceiling of four hours | `CNB-C-034` |
| 5. Act | Session recorded; privileged utility program invocation recorded | `CNB-C-034` |
| 6. Expire | The role expires at the ceiling without human action | `CNB-C-034` |

Two structural facts sit underneath the table. **The accounts are the blast-radius boundary**: production,
staging and corporate workloads run in separate AWS accounts with no transitive routing between them,
cross-account access is by assumed role only, and the trust policies are reviewed quarterly (`CNB-C-055`).
And **the pipeline is the only way software reaches production**: permission boundaries deny direct console
mutation of production infrastructure and deny installation of software on production hosts outside the
pipeline (`CNB-C-080`). Elevation buys the ability to look, to diagnose and to operate — not the ability to
deploy. A change still has to be a change, with a peer review and a ticket, and 05.09 owns that.

## 4. Who is eligible, and how that list stays honest

Eligibility is itself a register rather than a habit. Membership of the AWS administrator, Kubernetes
cluster-admin and database superuser roles is limited to the individuals named in the **privileged access
register**; no addition takes effect without the VP Security &amp; Trust's recorded approval, and the register
is reissued each quarter (`CNB-C-041`). Repository administrator rights are held by **three named
engineers**, recorded in the same register and reissued quarterly (`CNB-C-036`).

There is a third population that is easy to forget and that `CNB-C-142` exists for: **people who touch
production because they are assessing it**. Audit and assessment activity runs under a written test
authorisation naming systems, window, accounts and read-only scope, agreed with the system owner before it
starts, and the open authorisations and the accounts issued under them are reviewed quarterly and revoked
once the window has closed. This is the control under which Ironwood Security Labs held access in May 2026,
and it is the reason a penetration tester's account is not still live in August.

## 5. Break-glass

Two accounts. No more, and not zero.

| Property | Position |
|---|---|
| Count | **Two** break-glass accounts |
| Binding | Each bound to a hardware token |
| Storage | Credentials held in a sealed record |
| Alerting | Use raises an alert to the VP Security &amp; Trust **within 60 seconds** |
| Review | Every use is reviewed and closed, with the reason and the actions recorded |
| Use in the window to date | **Once**, on **2026-07-14**, during a database failover rehearsal; reviewed and closed within 48 hours |

The reason for two rather than one is availability: a single break-glass account is a single point of
failure in precisely the circumstance where the ordinary path has already failed. The reason for two rather
than several is that the population must be small enough to review every use without judgement about which
uses are worth reviewing.

The reason for having them at all is worth stating, because a programme that wanted a cleaner story would
delete them. An access model with no emergency path is an access model that will be bypassed under pressure
by whatever means are available, and the bypass will not be recorded. **A break-glass account is the
recorded version of the thing that would otherwise happen unrecorded.** The sixty-second alert is what makes
it a control instead of a back door: the use is known to the VP Security &amp; Trust before the session has
finished, not at the quarterly review.

The July use is exactly the shape the control was designed for and is reported without embellishment. It was
a rehearsal, not an incident; a failover rehearsal is a planned exercise of a path that must work in a real
event; it was alerted, reviewed and closed inside 48 hours. It is also **one use**, and one use tells a
sampler that the review procedure has been exercised once.

## 6. What each control leaves behind — because Phase 08 will sample it

This is the section that justifies the chapter's opening proposition. Each row states the artefact, the unit
a sampler can select, and — the column that carries the argument — **what would exist instead if the same
privileged action were performed by somebody holding standing access**.

| Control | Artefact | Sampling unit | Evidence class | Without elevation, what remains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CNB-C-034` just-in-time elevation | Elevation record: requester, approver, ticket, scope, granted and expiry timestamps, session recording reference | One elevation | EC-02 | The effect of the action, in an application or infrastructure log, with no request, no approver and no stated reason |
| `CNB-C-053` zero-trust proxy | Per-application authorisation decision with user, device posture and outcome | One access decision | EC-04 | A network connection, if flow logs happened to capture it |
| `CNB-C-041` privileged access register | Quarterly reissued register with the VP Security &amp; Trust's approval against each addition | One quarterly reissue | EC-01 | A cloud IAM policy document, which states who *can* act and never who *did* |
| `CNB-C-036` repository administration | Named administrators, reissued quarterly | One quarterly reissue | EC-01 | The same |
| `CNB-C-055` account separation | Quarterly trust policy review | One quarterly review | EC-01 | Nothing; a trust policy that is never reviewed produces no record of having been correct |
| `CNB-C-080` pipeline exclusivity | Permission boundary denial events and the pipeline's own deployment record | One deployment; one denial | EC-03 | A changed production host with no approved change behind it — the ML-2 failure mode |
| `CNB-C-142` test authorisation | Written authorisation, its window, the accounts issued under it, the revocation | One authorisation | EC-02 | An unexplained account belonging to a third party |
| Break-glass | Sealed-record opening, the 60-second alert, the session recording, the review closing the use | One use | EC-02, EC-05, EC-06 | An administrator session indistinguishable from any other |

Read the last column as a whole and the argument is complete. **Almost every entry in it is a record of an
effect rather than a record of a decision.** An examination tests whether a control operated, which means it
needs the decision: who asked, who approved, on what basis, within what window. A design in which people
already hold the access they need cannot produce that evidence at all, however carefully it logs what they
did — and a control library built on top of such a design would be describing intentions.

This is also why the elevation record is the highest-value artefact in the phase. It is one of the few that
is created **before** the privileged action rather than as a by-product of it, which means it cannot be
reconstructed after the fact by somebody with an interest in how it reads.

## 7. Separation of the record from the actor

The evidentiary value of everything above depends on one more property: **a principal capable of acting in
production is not capable of altering the record of what it did.**

`cnb-security` is a separate trust domain. It holds the CloudTrail organisation trail, the region-partitioned
append-only log archive and the delegated administration of the security tooling. Production roles can write
to the log pipeline and cannot read, modify, delete or expire what has landed in the archive; the archive's
object lock configuration is administered from `cnb-security` by a distinct set of principals; and the
CloudTrail trail is configured at the organisation level, so that an individual member account cannot
switch off its own logging.

**That last property is an operating fact and not yet a control row, and the distinction is worth being
exact about.** `CNB-C-065` requires application, infrastructure, database and identity-provider logs to
reach the `cnb-security` archive within five minutes of generation, written under an immutability policy
that prevents alteration or early deletion. That is a statement about the **destination**. The
organisation-level trail configuration is a statement about the **origin**, and no row in the library
carries it. It is real, it is verifiable by inspecting the organisation's trail configuration, and until a
control row states it there is no owner, no cadence and no artefact attached to it — which means a sampler
cannot select it and a reader should not be told that a control guarantees it. 05.10 develops the detection
side; the access-control point is that the separation is between **principals**, not between folders.

**R-03** — a privileged account compromised and used to reach production — sits at 3 × 5 = 15 in the
register, and Phase 03 rated its likelihood at 3 rather than 4 specifically because the population holding
usable elevated rights at any moment is small. That rating is a claim about this design. If standing access
returned, the rating would be wrong, and the register would have to say so.

**And it still sits at 15, which this chapter has to reconcile rather than pass over.** 03.05 sets R-03's
exit conditions — no standing privileged access, time-bound elevation against a recorded approval expiring
by itself, phishing-resistant authentication, session recording, and the ability to prove the absence of
use — and this chapter has just demonstrated every one of them. The register did not move, for the reason
**DEC-408** gives and Phase 04 applied to thirty-five other entries: **a rating is not re-rated on the
strength of a treatment having been implemented.** Likelihood is a claim about recurrence over time, and a
broker configured in May supplies no history. **R-37** is not a counter-example: it moved because a
specific defect on a specific path was closed and independently verified closed — a condition demonstrated
closed, not a control demonstrated built.

**When R-03 could move.** At a **CAL-06** quarterly review supported by operating history rather than
configuration: elevation records across the window showing the ceiling enforced by the broker rather than
by intention, break-glass uses reviewed inside the stated period, and the detection rules in 05.10 firing
on privileged role assumption. One break-glass use and one month of elevations is not that. As with R-01,
**impact stays at 5**, so the floor is 2 × 5 = 10.

## 8. Limits, stated

**A ceiling is not a measurement.** Four hours is the maximum an elevation can last; this chapter does not
assert an average, because an average across one month would be a number with no useful meaning.

**Session recording is retained, not watched.** Review is by exception and the exceptions are set by
detection rules. A reader should not take "every session is recorded" to mean "every session is reviewed",
and this document does not say it.

**Second-engineer approval is peer approval.** It is strong against carelessness and weaker against
collusion, and no access model of this shape claims otherwise. What limits the collusion case here is that
the elevation record survives the collusion: two people agreeing to a bad elevation still produce a dated
record naming both of them.

**One month is one month.** July 2026 contains one break-glass use, and elevation and access decisions
across four weeks. The criterion the service auditor tests is CC6.1 and CC6.3 across six months. Nothing
here is a statement about that period.
