## 1. Why the endpoint carries more weight here than in most programmes

CloudNimbus has one leased suite of about 4,100 square feet in Denver, with no server, no network closet and
no comms room, and **187 staff working from 31 US states and 4 countries**. The office is a meeting space.
For every practical purpose the workstation **is** the workplace: it is where customer data is looked at,
where production is reached from, and where the physical, human and technical control families meet in one
object a person carries home.

That is why this chapter is owned by the Head of People rather than by infrastructure. The controls are
technical, the estate is administered by mobile device management, and the behaviour being governed belongs
to a person under **POL-17**, the Remote Working and Endpoint Policy, and **POL-02**, the Acceptable Use
Policy.

## 2. The population, and why the number is 231

**231 managed devices**: 187 staff laptops and 44 test and lab devices. Every one of them is enrolled in
mobile device management, full-disk encrypted, and running an endpoint detection and response agent with
tamper protection.

**208 of the 231 are inside the SOC 2 system** — 164 laptops belonging to the people component plus the 44
test and lab devices; 164 + 44 = 208, and 208 + 23 = 231. The 23 outside belong to quota-carrying sales and
marketing staff who reach neither production nor customer data nor an in-scope procedure.

The control rows name **231 and not 208**, and that is the correct number for them. Phase 04 fixed the rule:
**a control's population is the ISMS boundary unless the row names the system boundary**. The ISMS scope is
the whole organisation, a laptop belonging to a salesperson is still an information asset holding customer
correspondence, and an endpoint standard that covered only the devices inside the SOC 2 system would leave
twenty-three encrypted-in-principle laptops governed by nothing. `CNB-C-059`, `CNB-C-062`, `CNB-C-138` and
`CNB-C-140` all state 231 deliberately.

## 4. The compliance signal is the enforcement point

A managed-device standard that produces a report is a report. What makes this estate a control is that **the
compliance signal is consumed by the access decision**.

Conditional access refuses any sign-in from a device the mobile device management platform does not report
as compliant (`CNB-C-032`). A device whose endpoint agent is unhealthy loses its compliance signal
(`CNB-C-059`). **A device past the 14-day Critical patch target loses its compliance signal and with it
access to production and customer systems until it is brought current** (`CNB-C-062`). The zero-trust access
proxy checks device posture at the moment of the request rather than at enrolment (`CNB-C-053`).

The consequence is that endpoint hygiene is self-enforcing in the only way that reliably works: the person
whose device is out of date cannot work until it is. No ticket is raised against them, no manager chases
them, and no exception queue accumulates. It also means the endpoint estate and the identity estate cannot
drift apart, because they are joined at the point of use rather than reconciled at a review.

`CNB-C-062` is typed **Corrective** rather than Preventive in the library, and the typing is worth
understanding rather than skipping. Patching remediates a weakness that already exists; it does not prevent
one. Typing it preventive would have misdescribed what a sampler is testing when they select a device and a
month.

## 5. Removable media: prohibited **and** blocked

This is the argument this chapter exists to make.

**POL-02 prohibits removable media.** `CNB-C-051` blocks it at the endpoint by mobile device management
policy; an exception requires the VP Security &amp; Trust's approval, is time-limited, and is recorded in the
asset register. **DEC-511**, recorded on **2026-07-13**, states the position in terms: removable media is
blocked on the endpoint, not prohibited by policy alone.

Phase 03 was asked to exclude **A.7.10 storage media** from the Statement of Applicability on the ground
that CloudNimbus prohibits removable media and therefore has no storage media to manage. It refused, and it
was right to, for two reasons that must not be run together.

**First: a prohibition is an implementation of the control, not a reason to exclude it.** Clause 6.1.3 c)
requires comparison against Annex A to verify that no necessary control has been omitted, and clause 6.1.3
d) requires the Statement of Applicability to state the necessary controls, the justification for including
each, whether each **is implemented**, and the justification for excluding any. A control that an
organisation satisfies by forbidding the activity is a control it has answered, and the field it belongs in
is a status on the implementation axis rather than the necessity axis. The argument for exclusion inverts
the logic of the standard: it treats the absence of the risk, achieved by the control, as evidence that the
control was never needed. By that reasoning every effective control could be excluded on the strength of
its own success. It is the same error as excluding fire suppression because there has been no fire.

**The status the issued Statement of Applicability actually records for A.7.10 is `Partial`, not
`Implemented`, and this chapter states that rather than rounding it up.** 03.11 gives the reason in terms:
the policy exists and the technical enforcement was not complete on every device. That is a statement about
231 endpoints, not about the argument above — the control was determined **necessary** and the prohibition
is its implementation; what was incomplete was the enforcement that makes the implementation evidenced.

**The enforcement is now applied across all 231 managed devices** by mobile device management policy under
`CNB-C-051`, with the exception path described below, and **DEC-511** records the position on **2026-07-13**.
That date is after the SoA was reissued on **2026-06-15** when the ISMS was declared operational, so the
`Partial` determination stands on the record and this phase has no authority to revise it: the
implementation field is the SoA's to state, as clause 6.1.3 d) requires, and it will be revised at the next
issue or not at all. **The residue is therefore a documented-information gap rather than a security one**,
and 05.13 §6 carries it as such alongside `A.5.28`. An ISMS whose SoA understates its own position is a
smaller problem than one that overstates it, and it is still a problem.

**Second: a prohibition with no enforcement is a preference.** This is the harder half, and it is why the
distinction between POL-02 and `CNB-C-051` matters more than it looks. A policy sentence forbidding USB
storage is a statement about what CloudNimbus would like its 187 staff to do. It creates an expectation, it
supports a disciplinary process, and it is worth having. What it does not do is stop a person in a hurry
from copying an export to a memory stick on a Friday afternoon — and it produces no record when they do, so
nobody knows it happened. Enforcement at the endpoint changes both properties at once: the write is refused,
and the attempt is a recorded event.

Put the two together and the position is complete. **The prohibition makes A.7.10 implemented; the
enforcement makes it testable.** Had CloudNimbus held only the first, the Statement of Applicability would
have been defensible and the control would have been unevidenced — and an ISMS that cannot evidence a
control it has declared implemented has a documented-information problem as well as a security one. Had it
held only the second, staff would have encountered a block with no stated rule behind it, which is how
exceptions get granted informally.

The exception path is what keeps this honest. There is one, it requires the VP Security &amp; Trust's
approval, it is time-limited and it is recorded in the asset register. A blanket technical block with no
approved way to do the occasional legitimate thing is a control that will be worked around, and the
work-around will not be recorded either.

The same reasoning carries the other endpoint-facing exclusions Phase 03 refused. **A.7.8 equipment siting
and protection** applies because laptops are equipment and home-working siting is exactly what the control
is for — which is why the remote working standard in `CNB-C-049` addresses overlooking and unattended
devices. **A.7.13 equipment maintenance** applies because endpoint maintenance is performed under mobile
device management, which is `CNB-C-138`. **A.7.9 security of assets off-premises** is served by `CNB-C-052`,
and in a company where every asset is permanently off-premises it could hardly be otherwise.

## 6. Data leaving the endpoint

Data loss prevention rules on email and the managed endpoints block transfer of files matching customer
compensation and personal-data patterns to unmanaged destinations, and blocked events are **triaged daily**
by the Data Platform owner (`CNB-C-058`), serving **A.8.12 data leakage prevention**, one of the eleven
controls new in the 2022 revision.

Daily triage is the property that distinguishes this from a block list. A blocked event is either an attempt
that should not have been made — which is a people matter — or a legitimate workflow that has no sanctioned
route, which is a product or process matter. Both need somebody to look. A blocking rule whose events are
never read produces a queue that grows until somebody disables the rule.

## 7. What this leaves behind

| Control | Artefact | Evidence class | Sampling unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CNB-C-032`, `CNB-C-049`, `CNB-C-059`, `CNB-C-062` | Mobile device management state and the access decision applied | EC-16 | **One device on one date**: device identifier, operating system and firmware version, delta from baseline, compliance state, access decision |
| `CNB-C-051` | Blocked removable-media events; the approved, time-limited exceptions in the asset register | EC-16 | One blocked event; one approved exception |
| `CNB-C-138` | Monthly hardware and firmware health read | EC-16 | One device for one month |
| `CNB-C-140` | Quarterly re-approval of the category set and standing unblocks | EC-16 | One quarterly re-approval |
| `CNB-C-058` | Daily data loss prevention triage | EC-05 | One blocked event with its disposition |
| `CNB-C-078` | Isolation, eradication confirmation and clean rescan | EC-06 | One malware detection |
| `CNB-C-052`, `CNB-C-050` | Offboarding asset record; certificate of erasure against the asset record | EC-16 | One leaver's asset return or wipe; one erased device |

**EC-16's unit — one device on one date — is the most sampleable unit in the phase.** A tester can select
any of 231 devices on any of 184 days in the window and ask what the platform reported and what access was
granted. That density is unusual and it is a consequence of the estate being managed centrally rather than
inspected periodically.

## 8. Limits, stated

**A managed estate is not every device a person owns.** CloudNimbus's position is that only managed devices
reach production and customer systems, and the mechanism that makes it true is the compliance signal, not a
policy sentence. A personal laptop is not blocked by prohibition; it simply has no compliance signal and is
refused.

**The home network is not administered and is not claimed to be.** The controls compensate at the device and
at the session — full-disk encryption, endpoint filtering, TLS, the zero-trust proxy — and the design
premise in 05.06 is that no network is trusted anyway.

**Physical protections at the Denver suite are small and deliberately unheroic**: individually issued badges,
escorted and recorded visitors, a clear desk and clear screen standard, and an annual confirmation that the
suite contains no information processing equipment (`CNB-C-046`, `CNB-C-047`, `CNB-C-048`). That annual
confirmation is load-bearing for the Statement of Applicability's physical determinations, and it is how
CloudNimbus would discover that a determination had stopped being true.

**`A.7.4` physical security monitoring is also recorded `Partial` in the issued Statement of
Applicability**, and for a different reason from A.7.10's. It is one of the four controls argued for
exclusion and refused, and 03.11's ground for the `Partial` status is that the badge records and the signed
visitor log are monitoring in the sense the control means, while the **review cadence was not yet defined**.
`CNB-C-047` now defines one — the badge holder list and the preceding quarter's visitor log reviewed each
quarter against the active worker list, with badges lacking a matching active worker revoked at the review.
As with A.7.10, the operating position has moved and the SoA field has not, and the status is the SoA's to
revise at its next issue rather than this chapter's to assert.

**One month is one month.** July 2026 produced four weeks of daily compliance evaluations and one monthly
patch cycle. Whether the 14-day Critical target was met on every device across the observation window is a
question about six months, and this chapter does not answer it.
