## 3. What the committee declined to conclude

That the controls are operating effectively.

**One month is not a period.** The observation window is six months; the population the service auditor
samples is the whole of it; and a control with a clean July has one month of history and five to go. The
committee recorded that the temptation to read this table as reassurance is exactly the temptation Phase 04
refused when it declined to re-rate the register on the strength of implementation rather than operation.

## 4. What it did conclude

That the controls **exist, are configured, and produced evidence in July**. Those are three separate
statements and all three are worth having a month in, because the alternative — discovering in January that
a control never produced the artefact its evidence class described — is the failure this programme spent
Phase 04 trying to design out.

## 5. Two figures the committee asked about

**Three access provisioning requests in a month** is a small population, and small populations are where a
single deviation becomes a large percentage. At three a month the six-month population is around eighteen,
and one late approval is more than five per cent.

**Forty-seven changes** includes remediation work for **four** penetration test findings — PT-06, PT-08,
PT-12 and PT-16. Nine findings were remediated *or retested* inside July, but the other five were
remediated in June and only retested on 2026-07-24, and a retest performed by Ironwood is not a CloudNimbus
change. The July change population and the remediation timeline therefore overlap by four, not by nine. The committee asked that this be
stated wherever both are given, rather than left for a reader to reconcile.
