## 1. What this phase is

Phase 04 published a library. One hundred and forty-eight controls, `CNB-C-001` to `CNB-C-148`, each with a
named owner, a defined cadence and a declared evidence artefact, and each citing exactly one of nineteen
policies. Sixty-two of them — `CNB-C-032` to `CNB-C-093` — sit against the sixteen Common Criteria that make
up CC6 to CC9, which is forty-two per cent of the whole library. A library is a set of statements about what
must happen. It is not a description of a running system.

**Phase 05 is where the statements meet the machinery.** It describes how identity, privileged access,
tenant isolation, cryptography, network boundaries, endpoints, vulnerability management, change management
and detection are actually configured at CloudNimbus: the mechanism, the parameter, the enforcement point,
and — the part that matters most to what comes next — **the record each control leaves behind**. Phase 08
will sample those records. A control that works and leaves nothing is the shape of **ML-1** from the 2025
Type I management letter, and this phase is written so that shape cannot recur unnoticed.

It also carries the programme's first genuine security finding. The May 2026 penetration test found a tenant
isolation defect in the reporting API and **R-37** was added to the register on its evidence. Phase 04
reported that R-37 exists, at what rating, and that it was re-rated. **Phase 05 reports what was found, what
changed, and what the search of the logs could and could not establish.**

## 2. What Phase 05 establishes

It establishes the **implementation position** (05.01): what a phase written one month into an observation
window is entitled to assert, and the three-way distinction between a control that exists, a control that
did something in July, and a control that operated effectively across a period. Only the first two are
available here.

It describes the **technical estate** control by control across 05.02 to 05.10, from identity through
privileged access, tenant isolation, cryptography, network boundaries, endpoints, vulnerability management
and change management to detection and response. Each chapter names the Phase 04 control identifiers it
implements, the evidence class the control feeds, and the parameters a tester would check.

It publishes the **penetration test** (05.11): five test streams, sixteen findings **PT-01 to PT-16**, the
severity split of 1 Critical, 3 High, 6 Medium and 6 Low, and the status arithmetic of thirteen closed and
retested, two open and scheduled for the mobile release of 2026-08-14, and one open and accepted.

It publishes **R-37 in full** (05.12): the defect, the environment it was proved in, the forensic search of
the retained query logs, what that search could not reach, the remediation and retest, and the notification
judgement together with the dissent recorded against it.

It closes with the **first month of the observation window** (05.13) and the argument that one month is not
a period.

## 3. The vantage — 2026-07-31

**Phase 05 speaks as at 2026-07-31**, one month into the six-month observation window. Phases 01 and 02
wrote from 2026-03-31, Phase 03 from 2026-04-30 and Phase 04 from 2026-06-30.

These have happened and are described in the past tense: the penetration test, which ran **2026-05-04 to
2026-05-22**; the remediation of the Critical finding on **2026-05-29** and its clean retest on
**2026-06-11**; the admission, remediation, retest and re-rating of **R-37**; the publication of the control
library on **2026-06-05**, the approval of the policy suite on **2026-06-12**, and the declaration that the
ISMS was operational on **2026-06-15**; and the whole of **July 2026**, the first month of the window.

**Nothing after 2026-07-31 is stated as an outcome anywhere in this phase**, and nothing can be. There has
been no internal audit, no management review of the ISMS in operation, no Stage 1 and no Stage 2, no
examination fieldwork, no test exception, no nonconformity, no opinion and no certificate. The second
penetration test is **scheduled** for October 2026 under **ADR-0025**; it has not been performed. Two mobile
findings are scheduled for the **2026-08-14** release; that release has not shipped.

## 4. R-37 and the penetration test as this phase's subject

The programme's most load-bearing assumption was disproved by evidence in May. **AS-02** — that row-level
security is uniformly enforced across every data access path — was carried out of Phase 01 as an assumption
rather than a fact, became **SR-01** in Phase 02, drove **R-01** to the top of the risk register in Phase 03,
and was tested in May 2026 by a firm that does not report to engineering. It did not hold on one path.

Two of this phase's chapters exist because that assumption failed: **05.04**, which describes the
authorisation model as it stands after remediation, and **05.12**, which describes how it failed, what was
and was not established about production, and what was decided about telling customers.

## 8. How to read this phase

Read **05.01** first. It states what this phase's sentences mean, and a reader who takes an implementation
description for an effectiveness conclusion will misread every chapter after it.

**05.02 to 05.10** are the technical estate and can be read in any order, though 05.03 and 05.04 carry
arguments the rest depend on: no standing production access is the control that makes the others testable,
and tenant isolation is a property of a predicate rather than of an identifier.

**05.11 and 05.12** are the chapters to read if you only read two. 05.12 is the most careful chapter in the
portfolio to date, and it is written for the reader who is looking for a reason not to trust the rest.

## 9. Standing framing

Nothing in this phase is a determination of compliance with any law, and 05.12 says so explicitly about the
one question where it matters most. The SOC 2 deliverable is a **restricted-use** examination report
carrying a CPA firm's **opinion**; there is no such thing as a SOC 2 certification, and no document here
describes CloudNimbus as certified against it. The ISO deliverable is a **certificate issued by an
accredited certification body** — Northgate Certification Services, Ltd., accredited by ANAB — and not by
ISO, which certifies nobody. **Points of focus are not requirements**, and **Annex A is not the
requirements**: conformity is assessed against clauses 4 to 10. A SOC 2 finding is an **exception** or
**deviation**; an ISO finding is a **nonconformity**; at this vantage there are none of either, because
neither engagement has tested anything. All content is fictional and illustrative.
