## 1. Purpose

This document is the reading instruction for the nine technical chapters that follow it. Those chapters
describe mechanisms — an identity provider configuration, an access broker with a four-hour ceiling, a
predicate at the data-access layer, a key policy, a subnet without a route to an internet gateway, a device
compliance signal. Each description is a claim, and a reader is entitled to know precisely what kind of
claim it is before deciding what weight to give it.

There are exactly three kinds available at this vantage, and only two of them are asserted.

## 2. What Phase 04 handed over

The unified control library contains **148 controls**. Sixty-two of them, `CNB-C-032` to `CNB-C-093`, answer
the sixteen Common Criteria in CC6 to CC9, and they are the backbone of this phase.

| CC7 System operations | CC7.1 – CC7.5 | 19 | 1 | 11 | 7 | 05.07, 05.08, 05.10 |

Three sets of rows outside that range are also implemented here, because the subject matter is technical
even though the criteria are not Common Criteria. `CNB-C-114` to `CNB-C-118` serve **C1.1 and C1.2**, and two
of them — `CNB-C-115`, the isolation suite, and `CNB-C-116`, region-scoped keys and annual rotation — are
where confidentiality is actually enforced rather than described. `CNB-C-134` to `CNB-C-142` are nine of the
fifteen **ISO-only** rows, and calling them the ISO-only *technological* rows would be wrong: `CNB-C-134`
maps to **A.5.6** and `CNB-C-135` to **A.5.7**, both in the Organizational theme, and `CNB-C-138` maps to
**A.7.13**, which is Physical. **Only `CNB-C-139` to `CNB-C-142` sit in A.8 Technological.** Four of the
nine are implemented in this phase because their subject matter is technical whatever theme the reference
set files them under: `CNB-C-138` device health, `CNB-C-139` time synchronisation, `CNB-C-140` DNS-layer and
endpoint filtering, and `CNB-C-141` the curated internal registry for third-party code. The first three are
endpoint and infrastructure mechanisms. **`CNB-C-141` is neither**: it governs what may enter the build, it
is a supply-chain control, and it is implemented in 05.09 alongside the merge gates rather than in 05.07
alongside the endpoint estate. And `CNB-C-026`, the annual penetration test, sits in CC4 rather than CC6 to
CC9 — because independent testing is a monitoring activity, not an access control — but its product is 05.11
and 05.12.

**Phase 05 creates no new control identifiers.** Where a mechanism changed during the implementation period,
the change is recorded against the existing row and in an architectural decision record. The most important
instance is `CNB-C-035`: its wording in Phase 04 is the wording of the mechanism **after** the May
remediation, and 05.04 and 05.12 both say so, because a reader who assumed the library described the
pre-remediation design would misread the finding.

## 3. The three sentences

### 3.1 This phase can say that a control exists and is configured

The strongest form of this claim is a parameter a third party could read off a system without being told
what to expect: phishing-resistant MFA required on every path into production; a maximum elevation of four
hours; two break-glass accounts and no more; one customer-managed key per region; thirty-one managed rule
groups and nine custom rules at the web application firewall; two hundred and thirty-one managed devices
under mobile device management. These are facts about a configuration at a moment, and they are verifiable
by inspection.

This is the same class of claim a **Type I** report addresses: suitability of **design**, as of a specified
date. The 2025 Type I that Pinehurst Assurance Group issued was exactly this and nothing more, and its
unmodified opinion carried no test of operating effectiveness because a Type I has none to carry.

### 3.2 This phase can say what a control did in July 2026

The observation window opened on **2026-07-01**. One month of it had elapsed at this vantage, and the events
of that month are facts: three access provisioning requests, each with a manager approval recorded before
provisioning; one entitlement certification, completed **2026-07-27**; forty-seven changes deployed, each
with a recorded peer review; two emergency changes, both retrospectively approved within five business days;
one break-glass use, on **2026-07-14**, reviewed and closed within forty-eight hours; 1,412 alerts triaged,
11 escalated, 0 incidents.

Each of those is a count of instances in a defined interval. Stating them is not a conclusion; it is a
record, and 05.13 presents the whole of it in one table.

### 3.3 This phase cannot say that any control operated effectively

**Operating effectiveness is a conclusion about a period, and there is one month of it.**

That is not a stylistic caution. A **Type II** examination opines on suitability of design **and** operating
effectiveness **throughout a specified period**, and the specified period here is 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31.
The service auditor's tests will be designed against the whole six months, the samples will be drawn from
the whole six months, and a control that operated perfectly in July has one-sixth of a history. Nobody at
CloudNimbus — not the control owner, not the programme owner, not management in its assertion — is in a
position to reach the conclusion the examination exists to reach, and management could not reach it in
advance even if the evidence were complete, because the conclusion is the service auditor's to draw and the
assertion is management's to make about the period once it has run.

The same asymmetry applies on the ISO side in different words. Clause 8.1 requires the organisation to plan,
implement and control the processes needed to meet its information security requirements, and to keep
documented information to the extent necessary to have confidence the processes were carried out as planned.
"Were carried out" is past tense over an interval. **Annex A is not the requirements**, and an Annex A
control marked implemented in the Statement of Applicability is a statement about implementation status,
which is what clause 6.1.3 d) asks for — not a statement about how it behaved over a year. The Statement of
Applicability was reissued on **2026-06-15** when the ISMS was declared operational, and its implementation
field remains the SoA's to state and not this phase's to reinterpret.

## 4. Why the distinction is the whole point

A phase that blurred these three would be doing exactly what Phase 04 refused to do.

At the quarterly risk review under **CAL-06** on 2026-06-15, thirty-six of the thirty-seven register entries
were confirmed **without change**, and the reason recorded was that a treatment implemented in June has no
operating history in June. Thirty-one of the thirty-four treatment items had landed between 2026-05-15 and
2026-06-30. Every one of them was real work — controls were built, budget was spent, tickets were closed —
and none of it was evidence about how often the thing it was built to prevent would now occur. Only **R-37**
moved, and it moved because a condition was closed and independently verified closed by a retest, not
because somebody felt better about it.

That decision is the precedent this phase inherits. If a register may not be re-rated on the strength of
implementation, then an implementation chapter may not be written as though implementation were performance.
The two errors are the same error, and the second is easier to commit because the prose is technical and
sounds like measurement. "Multi-factor authentication is enforced on every path into production" is a
configuration fact. "Multi-factor authentication has been enforced on every path into production throughout
the period" is a conclusion, and this phase does not contain the second sentence anywhere.

There is a commercial temptation in the other direction too. **O1** — the standing obligation to hold a
current Type II report — is the programme's commercial mandate, and every month of the window is a month
closer to discharging it. A phase that presented a strong July as a strong period would be telling the
customer-facing organisation what it wants to hear five months early. The honest position is that July went
well and that this says nothing whatever about November.

## 5. What an implementation description has to contain to be worth writing

Phase 04 fixed three properties an examinable control must have: a named owner, a defined cadence and a
declared evidence artefact. This phase adds a fourth for the technical chapters, and it is the one that
distinguishes a description from a brochure.

**A description must name the enforcement point.** Not what the organisation intends, and not what an
engineer is expected to remember, but the specific place where a request is refused, a merge is blocked, a
device is denied or a query fails to compile. `CNB-C-035` is architectural rather than conventional for
exactly this reason: a service is not trusted to remember to scope its own queries. The removable-media
position in 05.07 is the same argument in a different medium — a prohibition in **POL-02** is a preference
until `CNB-C-051` blocks the port at the endpoint.

**A description must name what the control leaves behind.** Every chapter here names the evidence class from
04.12 that its controls feed, because that is the population Phase 08 samples from. The mapping is not
decorative:

**That column is complete against each chapter's own artefact table and not against the shortest defensible
summary of it**, because the column is what tells Phase 08 which populations to sample. A chapter that fed
six classes and declared three would have hidden three populations from the sampler — and the classes most
easily lost are the ones a chapter produces incidentally rather than as its subject: 05.03's incident
records from break-glass, 05.05's key-policy change records, 05.10's single continuity exercise.

A control that cannot be placed in that second column is a control whose operation cannot be tested, however
well it works. **ML-1** was precisely that: an access review that happened, evidenced as email threads and
screenshots, in a form no sampler could use.

## 6. What a reader should not take from this phase

Three things, stated plainly.

**Nothing here asserts that AWS or Halcyon Identity does anything.** Both are subservice organisations and
both are **carved out**, with fourteen complementary subservice organisation controls stated instead —
eleven for AWS and three for Halcyon Identity. When 05.05 says customer data at rest is encrypted under
region-scoped customer-managed keys, that is a claim about **CloudNimbus's configuration** of AWS KMS. It is
not a claim about how AWS operates KMS, and AWS's own assurance reports cover AWS's controls rather than
CloudNimbus's use of them.

**Nothing here is a legal conclusion.** The tightest instance is in 05.12, where a notification obligation
was assessed against a contractual commitment and the question of any statutory regime was left where it
belongs, with counsel.

**Nothing here is an audit finding.** No fieldwork has been performed by anybody. Where this phase reports a
control not yet complete — two mobile findings scheduled for the 2026-08-14 release, one finding accepted
with reasoning — those are the entity's own records of its own position, disclosed because concealing them
would be worth less than the disclosure costs.
