## 1. What Phase 05 established

Phase 04 published 148 control statements. **Phase 05 showed the mechanisms behind the security half of
them** — identity, privileged access, tenant isolation, cryptography, network, endpoints, vulnerability
management, change management and detection — and then did the harder thing, which was to describe the one
occasion on which a mechanism did not work. Three of its outputs will outlast the rest.

**The penetration test is documented in full.** Ironwood Security Labs tested five streams from 2026-05-04
to 2026-05-22 and returned **sixteen findings — 1 Critical, 3 High, 6 Medium, 6 Low**. Thirteen are closed
and independently retested, two are open and scheduled for the 2026-08-14 mobile release, and one is open
and accepted with its reasoning recorded. 05.11 argues `PT-15` rather than filing it: the isolation control
is the predicate rather than the opacity of the identifier, and the argument is credible only because
`PT-01` proved that the predicate is what fails and the predicate is what was fixed.

**R-37 has its own document, and it is the most careful in the portfolio to date.** 05.12 states that no
real customer data was exposed at any point in the test; that the same code path existed in production;
that thirteen months of retained query logs hold **117 invocations of that path from 9 customers, every one
of them returning a response cardinality consistent with a single-tenant result**; that the defect existed
for twenty-two months; and that **9 months are therefore unexamined and cannot now be examined** — a period
that, at the observed rate, holds on the order of eighty invocations nobody can check. It records
the notification assessment against SC-02 and O4, the Audit &amp; Risk Committee's refusal of voluntary
disclosure on 2026-06-03, and Lorraine Kessler's dissent in substance rather than by reference. It reaches
no legal conclusion, and says so.

**And the first month of the observation window is on the record** — which is where this document has to be
careful.

## 3. One month is not a period

That table is the most persuasive thing in this phase and the most dangerous, so the phase says the
following on the face of the summary rather than in a footnote.

**One month is not a period.** The Type II observation window runs 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31. **Five months
remain**, the population the service auditor samples is the whole six, and **a control that operated
correctly in July has one month of history and no more.** Nothing in the table above supports a statement
about operating effectiveness, because operating effectiveness is a conclusion about a period and this is a
sixth of one.

The arithmetic underneath makes it worse rather than better. **Three access provisioning requests** is a
population of three. **One access review** is the first of **three** occurrences the window will hold —
01.11 schedules CAL-07 at 2026-03, 2026-06, 2026-09 and 2026-12, of which September and December fall
inside the window, and the certification of 2026-07-27 is an additional occurrence run when the control
moved into the GRC platform rather than a re-based cycle. Three is still a small denominator: one
certification completed after its due date is a deviation rate of a third. **Two emergency changes** is a
population of two, and the five-business-day retrospective approval has no volume to absorb a slip. The controls with comfortable populations — 47 changes, 1,412 alerts — are the ones where a small
proportion of deviations is arithmetically survivable, and they are not the controls where the risk sits.

**A phase presenting this table as reassurance would be making exactly the error Phase 04 refused to
make.** DEC-408 declined to re-rate the 36 baseline risks on the strength of the control library being
implemented, on the ground that a register moved by intention is a register scored on intention. The same
discipline applies to a month of clean operation: it is evidence about July, it is welcome, and it is not a
result.

**And the two zeros are the weakest numbers in the table.** Zero incidents in July is either a month in
which nothing happened or a month in which something happened and was not detected, and 05.10 declines to
distinguish them from the inside. Zero Critical or High vulnerabilities open past target is a photograph
taken on 31 July, not a statement about the thirty days before it.

willing to publish that is making a harder claim about its governance than any control statement can.

Gaps 4 and 5 are the two a certification auditor will reach first, and they fail in the same direction for
different reasons. `A.5.28` is `Partial` because the practice has had nothing to demonstrate itself
against: zero incidents in July means a population of zero, and a control with no occurrences cannot be
shown to work. `A.7.10` is `Partial` because a document has not caught up with an operating position that
has already moved. Neither is a security failure. Both are documented-information problems of the kind
clause 7.5 exists for, and they are the family this programme has been least good at.

## 8. What could still go wrong

**Five months of controls have to operate.** Everything in §2 is one month old. The quarterly controls have
two further occurrences each inside the window — the September and December points of the calendar cycle —
the two semi-annual controls have one occurrence in the whole window, and 04.11's arithmetic holds: a
population of one or two turns a single late occurrence into a deviation rate of fifty or a hundred per
cent.

**The August release carries two open findings and no slack.** If it slips, `PT-09` reaches its target date
with nothing in hand, and DEC-510 stops being the decision that was taken on the facts of 6 July.

**The October test could reopen more than a finding.** It re-runs TS-05 against the remediated isolation
model. A second defect on a different path would falsify the premise the `PT-15` acceptance rests on, and it
would also reach the Audit &amp; Risk Committee, which agreed on 2026-06-03 to revisit the non-disclosure
decision if the second test or any subsequent evidence changed the picture.

**And the evidence has to survive the window.** ML-1 was not a failure of design but of what a design turns
into once it meets a busy quarter. Every claim in this phase resolves, later, to whether the change records,
triage dispositions, closure timestamps and retest attestations are in the evidence store in the unit 04.12
defined — not to whether the mechanisms worked, but to whether anyone can show that they did.
