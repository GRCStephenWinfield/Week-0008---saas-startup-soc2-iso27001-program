## 1. What collection control looks like when it works

**P3.1** concerns the collection of personal information consistent with the entity's objectives — that what
is collected is what was said would be collected, and no more. **P3.2** concerns explicit consent for
information the entity treats as requiring it. **P4.1** concerns limiting use to the purposes identified.
Three criteria, and between them they ask a single practical question: **does the set of personal data
fields actually in production match the set the inventory and the notice describe?**

CloudNimbus answers that question with a preventive control and a detective one, and the interesting thing
about this chapter is that both of them returned zero.

| Control | Type | What it requires, as published | Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CNB-C-124` | **Preventive** | The API schema for personal data is generated from the personal-information inventory, and a pull request introducing a personal-data field absent from the inventory **fails the schema check and cannot merge** without a recorded privacy review | P3.1, P4.1 |
| `CNB-C-125` | **Detective** | Each quarter the data platform owner reconciles the fields actually populated in production against PD-01 to PD-12 and the notice, re-confirms the stated source and accuracy expectation for each category, and a field in use but absent from the inventory is removed or added with the notice reissued | P3.1, P7.1 |

| Measure | 2026-10-01 to 2026-11-27 |
|---|---|
| Pull requests blocked by `CNB-C-124` for introducing an uninventoried personal-data field | **0** |
| Recorded privacy reviews required to unblock one | **0** |
| `CNB-C-125` Q4 field reconciliation | Performed **2026-10-16** |
| Divergences found between production fields and PD-01 to PD-12 | **0** |
| Fields removed, or added with the notice reissued | **0** |

**Two zeros, and they are one result rather than two.** The temptation is to report them as independent
confirmations — a gate that blocked nothing and a reconciliation that found nothing, therefore twice as much
assurance. That reading is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that matters for how a sampler should read the
evidence.

**The detective control is quiet because the preventive one is loud.** `CNB-C-124` operates on every pull
request that touches the personal-data schema, which at 63 microservices and a continuously deployed
platform is a large and unglamorous population. A field that is not in the inventory cannot reach production
by the ordinary path, because the schema the API is built from is **generated** from the inventory and the
check fails the merge. So when `CNB-C-125` looks at production three months later and compares it against
the inventory, the two agree — not because nothing was attempted, but because the gate is upstream of the
place the reconciliation looks.

The right statement of the result is therefore: **the reconciliation confirms that the gate held, and the
gate is the reason there was nothing to find.**

**That is the rule this phase applies to every zero it reports, and it applies in both directions.** A zero
is reassuring where a **preventive** control explains it, and unreassuring where nothing does. `CNB-C-125`'s
zero divergences are the first case: `CNB-C-124` is upstream, it operates on every merge, and a zero is what
a working gate produces. An annual re-approval that returned zero non-conforming resources with no
preventive control sitting in front of it would be the second case, and 07.12 §4.3 reads it that way. The
same number means opposite things depending on whether something in the design accounts for it, and a phase
that graded zeros consistently as good news would be reporting the shape of its arithmetic rather than the
state of its controls.

A programme that reported the two zeros as two clean numbers would be
double-counting one control's work, and would have no answer if asked what `CNB-C-125` would ever detect.
What it would detect is a field arriving by a path `CNB-C-124` does not sit on — a manual migration, a
vendor-supplied component writing its own columns, a debug field left in an internal service that later
starts being persisted. Those paths exist, the reconciliation is the only thing looking at them, and its
population is one occurrence a quarter.

`CNB-C-124`'s zero carries a smaller caveat that should be said and not laboured. **Zero blocked pull
requests is not evidence that anybody tried.** It is consistent with a gate that refuses everything and with
a gate nobody tested, and the control record cannot distinguish them. What can be said is that the check ran
on every merge to the affected paths and passed, which is what EC-03 records.

## 2. Leave type, and the inference the schema cannot see

Everything above is a story about fields. Here is the finding, and it is not about a field. It is about a
**value**.

CloudNimbus collects **PD-07**, absence and leave records including leave type, from the individual and the
employer together. The leave type is recorded against a **controlled list** rather than as free text: values
are configured per tenant from a fixed set and cannot be extended into a description. Phase 02 recorded the
exposure and bounded it in three ways, and the bounds hold at this vantage.

**CloudNimbus stores the type only.** There is no reason field, no diagnosis field, no condition code, no
free-text note on the leave record, and no facility to attach a medical certificate, a fit note or any other
document to it. Customers who need to hold such material hold it in their own human-resources system, where
the employment relationship lives. **CloudNimbus never asks for a reason and holds no diagnosis**, and that
is a design decision recorded at DEC-206 rather than an accident of the schema.

And here is the number this chapter adds.

**327 of 640 tenants have configured at least one leave type whose label names a medical or health
circumstance.**

Not a diagnosis. A label — the kind of label an employer writes when it wants its own absence categories to
be legible to its own managers. The controlled list is configured by the employer, from a fixed set, and the
employer chooses which values its workforce sees.

**Information from which health may be inferred is still information about health to the person it
concerns.** A record showing that a named individual took leave of a type so labelled, on given dates,
carries an inference that a record showing "Annual leave" does not. The person whose record it is does not
experience the distinction between a diagnosis and a category name that implies one. That sentence is the
whole of the finding and the chapter will not soften it.

**What this document does not do is characterise the value in the language of any legal regime.** Whether a
leave-type label constitutes a special category, sensitive personal information, or any other statutorily
defined class under any named statute is a determination that belongs to counsel on the facts of a given
jurisdiction and a given customer, and neither a restricted-use examination report nor a certificate issued
by an accredited certification body is a determination of compliance with any law. 02.07 §5 declined to
reach it, 04.06 §5.1 named it as a line the programme does not cross, and this chapter — the one that has
the number in front of it — declines it too. **The finding is about coverage, not about classification.**

## 3. P3.2 is served by one control and that control is about something else

Here is the coverage problem stated exactly.

**P3.2 is served by `CNB-C-121` alone.** It is one of the three single-control criteria in the library, with
C1.2 and CC5.3, and 04.06 §5.1 explained the concentration in terms that were true when written: geolocation
at clock-in is the only collection CloudNimbus treats as requiring the individual's explicit permission
before the value is captured, the mobile client is the only path by which it is captured, and one mechanism
in one place gets one control.

`CNB-C-121` asks the operating system for the location permission at first clock-in, records the grant or
refusal with a timestamp, and honours a refusal without blocking the clock-in. It is a good control. **It is
about the operating-system location permission and it does not reach leave-type inference at all.** There is
no permission prompt on a leave request, no recorded grant or refusal against PD-07, and no mechanism
anywhere in the library that treats a leave-type value as requiring the individual's explicit permission
before it is stored.

That is not a criticism of `CNB-C-121`, which was never written to do it. It is a statement about **P3.2's
coverage**: the criterion is served by a control whose subject matter is one collection, and the library
contains a second collection with a plausible claim on the same criterion and nothing pointed at it. **The
programme discovered this by reading its own numbers rather than by being told**, which is the only reason
it is in a document at this vantage rather than in somebody's finding next year.

It is recorded as **`IS-29`**, referred, owner Tobias Lund, and it is a candidate for **the next Statement of
Applicability issue and the next control library issue**. It is deliberately **not** recorded as a deviation,
for the same reason `IS-30` is not: **no control in the library required anybody to treat leave type this
way, so no control failed.** Recording it as a deviation would attach it to a control that never made the
promise, and 07.10 makes the same argument about a different absence.

Nor is the library amended in this phase. The programme's standing habit since Phase 06 is that **a library
which corrects itself silently cannot demonstrate that it was ever wrong**: `IS-24`, `IS-25`, `IS-26` and
`IS-27` were all referred rather than quietly resolved, and this one joins them. The one control that *was*
admitted mid-window, `CNB-C-149`, was admitted because a control had a design gap on a path where a failure
had actually occurred; a referral is what happens where the gap is in the mapping rather than in a
mechanism.

## 4. R-35, and the handling that does exist

The register has an entry here and it is worth reading against the finding rather than instead of it.

**R-35 — *leave-type data reaches a report or export seen by a party who should not see it*** — sits at **2 ×
3 = 6, Low**, owned by Devon Ashby, and it has not moved since the Phase 03 baseline. Likelihood 2:
foreseeable but not expected within the cycle. Impact 3: a commitment breached with contractual consequence.
**Nothing in this period moved it, no CAL-06 review falls in this period, and the Q4 review of 2026-12-29 is
where any proposal would be heard.**

What R-35 describes is a **disclosure** risk, and the handling that exists is aimed at it. PD-07 carries
elevated handling under DEC-206: **narrower access within the support console under DF-12**, **restricted
inclusion in exports and reports**, and separate treatment in the access model Phase 05 designed. That
handling is real, it operates, and it is the reason the entry sits at likelihood 2 rather than higher.

**`IS-29` is not R-35 and the two must not be allowed to absorb each other.** R-35 is about the value
reaching the wrong reader. `IS-29` is about the individual never having been asked, and about a criterion
whose only control looks somewhere else. Handling controls address the first and are silent on the second: a
leave type that no unauthorised party ever sees is still a leave type collected without the mechanism P3.2
contemplates. Treating the existence of R-35 and its handling as an answer to `IS-29` would be the
comfortable move and it is the wrong one, which is why the referral is recorded separately and against a
different owner.

Whether `IS-29` produces a register entry of its own is a question for the December review with the whole
period's evidence in front of it. This phase does not pre-empt it, and it does not admit a risk on the
strength of a coverage gap the way it admitted **R-38** on the strength of a disproved assumption — the
distinction ADR-0029 drew and DEC-306 permits.

## 5. What collection and use rest on at this vantage

| Proposition | Position at 2026-11-27 | What supports it |
|---|---|---|
| The personal-data API schema is generated from the inventory rather than maintained beside it | **Evidenced continuously** | `CNB-C-124`, EC-03 |
| A pull request introducing an uninventoried personal-data field cannot merge | **Evidenced by design; 0 attempts blocked in the period** | `CNB-C-124`. Zero blocked is not evidence anybody tried |
| Fields populated in production reconcile to PD-01 to PD-12 and the notice | **Evidenced 2026-10-16, 0 divergences** | `CNB-C-125`, EC-15 |
| The two results above are independent | **No. The reconciliation confirms the gate held** | The gate is upstream of where the reconciliation looks |
| CloudNimbus holds a diagnosis, a reason or a medical document | **No** | PD-07 holds the type only; DEC-206 |
| The leave-type list is bounded and configured per tenant from a fixed set | **Evidenced** | The controlled list; no free text |
| 327 of 640 tenants have configured at least one leave type naming a medical or health circumstance | **Stated as a fact about configuration** | Tenant configuration, at this vantage |
| A control treats a leave-type value as requiring the individual's explicit permission | **No. P3.2's only control is about the operating-system location permission** | **`IS-29`**, referred |
| Leave-type data is handled more narrowly than other categories | **Evidenced** | DEC-206, DF-12, restricted inclusion in exports and reports; R-35 at 2 × 3 = 6 |
| Any of this constitutes a determination about the status of the data under any law | **No, at any point in this phase** | — |

Everything above the eighth row is a control set doing what it was designed to do and producing zeros that
mean something. **The eighth row is a criterion with one control, a second collection with a claim on it,
and nothing in between** — found by counting tenants rather than by an auditor, referred rather than papered
over, and carried into the next issue of the two documents that would fix it.
