## 1. Two criteria, five controls, and a very uneven distribution

The Confidentiality category has two criteria. **C1.1** concerns the identification and maintenance of
information designated confidential to meet the entity's objectives; **C1.2** concerns its disposal to meet
those objectives. Five controls in the library serve them, and the split is **four to one**.

| ID | Control, as published | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| `CNB-C-114` | Every data store is provisioned with a classification tag drawn from the 02.06 scheme, and the Terraform policy gate fails any plan that creates an S3 bucket, Aurora cluster or Kafka topic without one | C1.1 |
| `CNB-C-115` | Tenant isolation is enforced by the scoping predicate the data-access layer applies, and an automated isolation suite exercising every documented access path runs on every deployment and blocks promotion on a single failure | C1.1 |
| `CNB-C-116` | Customer data is encrypted at rest under region-scoped customer-managed keys and in transit under TLS 1.2 or above, and the keys are rotated annually per region with the rotation and the re-encryption completion recorded | C1.1 |
| `CNB-C-117` | Each quarter the export and bulk-download events above the 5,000-row threshold are reviewed against the requesting role and the tenant's support case history, and an event with no matching case is investigated as a potential confidentiality event | C1.1 |
| `CNB-C-118` | On termination the deletion orchestrator runs across every store holding that tenant's data and issues a machine-verifiable completion certificate inside 30 days, and the monthly offboarding review lists any tenant whose certificate is outstanding | C1.2 |

**Confidentiality has never been examined.** The 2025 Type I covered Security alone, so **no service auditor
has opined on the design of any of these five controls**, and — a separate and narrower point — **none of
the five existed at the Type I's as-of date**. What is *not* a fact about Confidentiality is the absence of
an operating-effectiveness conclusion: a Type I opines on design at a point in time and tests no control's
operating effectiveness in any category, so that is true of all 149 controls in the library and says nothing
about this category in particular. The Type II examination now running is the first to reach operating
effectiveness for anything, and this category starts it from nothing. That is the starting position for this
chapter as it was for Phase 04's, and it is why the chapter is written control by control.

What is designated confidential here is not a matter of judgement. **Customer compensation data is
confidential under the master services agreement**, and the classification scheme at 02.06 puts it at
**Restricted** — the top of the four-tier scheme, alongside calculation output that becomes pay,
authentication secrets and keys. Below it sit Confidential (tenant configuration, pay rules, commercial
terms, security documentation), Internal and Public. `CNB-C-114` is the control that makes the scheme
operative rather than aspirational: a store with no tag does not get created, because the Terraform policy
gate fails the plan. **The rule that a container inherits the highest classification of anything inside it**
is what stops a "general" bucket quietly accumulating Restricted data under an Internal label.

**Multi-tenant isolation is the confidentiality control that matters**, and it is `CNB-C-115`. In a
single-tenant world confidentiality is mostly encryption and access control; in a platform where 640
employers' compensation data sits in five shared Aurora clusters, the property that keeps one customer's
payroll away from another's is a scoping predicate applied at the data-access layer on every path, and an
automated suite that blocks a deployment if any documented path fails. R-37 is the entry that describes
what happens when that assumption is wrong, and **Phase 05 owns it**: the finding, the remediation of
2026-05-29 and the clean retest of 2026-06-11, all before the observation window opened. Nothing in this
chapter re-opens it.

**`CNB-C-116`'s annual occurrence falls inside this period and is reported here rather than assumed.**
`04.11` §4.2 re-scheduled the annual rotation of region-scoped customer-managed keys under **DEC-409** from
its natural 2026-05 anniversary to **2026-11**, which puts its whole window population inside these
fifty-eight days. **It was performed on 2026-11-10**, across **all three regions** — `us-east-1`,
`us-west-2` and `eu-central-1` — with the rotation and the re-encryption completion recorded under **EC-04**
for each. **Its window population is one**, and one occurrence performed is the whole of the evidence there
will ever be for it inside this examination.

Saying so is not housekeeping. **This is the failure mode `06.10` recorded against A.5.30 and called the
clause 7.5 lesson**: a status was moved to `Implemented` before the control that supports it had operated,
and the status ran ahead of the evidence. A row reading "Evidenced" for encryption with no rotation
occurrence anywhere in the phase would have been the same mistake in the same month, and §5's row is written
against the date rather than against the control statement.

**No deviation was recorded against `CNB-C-114`, `CNB-C-115` or `CNB-C-116` in the period.** None of this
phase's deviations is here; 07.12 §5 carries the table.

## 2. `CNB-C-117` — the quarterly export review, and the two events that did not match

`CNB-C-117`'s Q4 occurrence was performed on **2026-10-14**.

| Measure | Q4 occurrence, 2026-10-14 |
|---|---|
| Export and bulk-download events above the 5,000-row threshold | **41** |
| Matched to a support case | **39** |
| With no matching case, investigated | **2** |
| Confidentiality events arising | **0** |
| | **39 + 2 = 41** |

**The two unmatched events are the output worth reading, and they say something about the control rather
than about the exports.** Both were **bulk exports run on 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-02 by a Customer Success
engineer, at two tenants' written instruction, in response to those customers' own auditors** — the same two
requests the disclosure register `CNB-C-132` maintains carries, in the auditor-request class 07.07 §2
describes. Each entry carries a named requester, a scope, a stated basis and a date. Both events were traced
on 2026-10-14 and both were closed the same day.

**They were lawful, instructed and recorded, and they still presented as unmatched, because the control
compared against the wrong register.** `CNB-C-117` compares export events against **the tenant's support
case history**. An export that arrives through the **disclosure** path and not the **support** path is
invisible to that comparison, has no case to match, and will present as an unmatched event every single
time. Two of the forty-one were exactly that.

**This is not a deviation.** `CNB-C-117` did what its statement says, on the population its statement names,
and what it says is the problem: a comparison that only knows about support cases cannot account for a
disclosure that was properly recorded somewhere else. It is recorded as **`IS-32`**, referred, owner Karim
Haddad, and a candidate for the next library issue — where the question is whether the control should
compare against the disclosure register as well as the case register, or whether a disclosure-register match
should be a recognised disposition rather than an investigation.

The population is worth stating plainly for the sampler: **one occurrence per quarter**, so **two
occurrences in the observation window and both are behind** — Q3 on **2026-07-15** and Q4 on **2026-10-14**.
The next occurrence is Q1 2027, outside the window. Nothing here is a window rate.

## 3. C1.2 is served by exactly one control, and this is where that is tested

**C1.2 is one of the three criteria Phase 04 identified as served by a single control** — with **CC5.3**,
served by `CNB-C-031`, and **P3.2**, served by `CNB-C-121`. Phase 04 recorded the concentration and stated
its consequence in advance: *if `CNB-C-118` has a deviation, C1.2 has nothing behind it, and there is no
compensating control to argue from.* This chapter is where that prediction meets a population.

The concentration is deliberate rather than accidental. **Disposal of confidential information at the end of
a customer relationship happens in one place at CloudNimbus** — the deletion orchestrator of DF-11, which
produces the certificate obligation O7 and commitment SC-03 require. There is no second, independent path by
which a terminated customer's data is disposed of. Inventing a second control to serve C1.2 would have been
a documentation exercise, and 04.03 refused it.

### 3.1 Nine terminations, nine certificates

| Measure | 2026-10-01 to 2026-11-27 |
|---|---|
| Tenant terminations completing in the period | **9** |
| Completion certificates issued inside 30 days | **9** |
| Mean days from termination to certificate | **11** |
| Longest | **19** |
| Certificates outstanding at the monthly offboarding review | **0** |

**`CNB-C-118` operated on every occurrence in the period.** Nine of nine, none late, the longest at nineteen
days against a thirty-day commitment. Each certificate is one unit of **EC-18** and carries what Phase 02
committed it to carry: the stores covered, records deleted per store, the **backup residue window**, the
completion date and the machine-verifiable attestation.

**Nine departures and a customer register that does not move is not an inconsistency.** The phase states
**640 customers** at 2026-10-01 and 640 at 2026-11-27, and nine tenants terminated in between. **The nine
were offset by nine onboardings**, so the register stood at 640 at both ends of the period and the
tenant-configuration arithmetic elsewhere in the phase — 147 disabled and 493 enabled — reconciles at both.
Stating it is the only way the two figures close; a period that reports nine terminations against an
unchanged denominator and does not say why has published a sum that does not add.

Nine is a small population and the chapter will not dress it up. On a control with a thirty-day commitment
and a single-control criterion, **a small population is not a comfort — it is a magnifier.** One late
certificate in nine is eleven per cent of the period's population, and Phase 04 said as much before the
window opened: *a small population on a single control means every occurrence matters, and one late
certificate is a visible proportion of whatever the population turns out to be.* The window's population is
whatever the six months produce, and December is still ahead. **No window rate is stated here**, for the
same reason 06.03 refused one for `CNB-C-098`.

### 3.2 The half of C1.2 that CloudNimbus does not operate

There is a second fact about C1.2 and it is the one worth knowing.

**The disposal of the media underneath is carved out.** `CSOC-03` — *secure decommissioning and sanitisation
of storage media* — is one of the fourteen complementary subservice organisation controls disclosed at
02.10, and it cites **CC6.5 and C1.2**. It is **AWS's control, not CloudNimbus's**. When the deletion
orchestrator removes a tenant's rows, what happens to the physical media those rows were written on is
performed by the subservice organisation, excluded from the description under the carve-out method, and
disclosed rather than claimed.

So the criterion with the **thinnest coverage in the whole examination** — one control, no compensating
control, a population of nine in this period — is also a criterion **whose lower half CloudNimbus does not
operate and cannot test.** CloudNimbus can evidence that the rows are gone from every store the orchestrator
enumerates. It cannot evidence that the drive was sanitised, because it does not own the drive, does not
know which drive it was, and has no path to the facility.

**That is not a defect; it is the carve-out working as described.** The carve-out method excludes the
subservice organisation's controls from the description and states the complementary controls instead, which
is exactly what 02.10 did. A reader of the description learns that C1.2 depends on AWS performing CSOC-03,
and can go and read AWS's own report about it — subject to what 07.11 says about which months of the window
that report covers.

What is worth saying, and what nobody usually says, is **which criterion the carve-out lands hardest on.**
It lands hardest here. Across sixty-one criteria in scope, the one where CloudNimbus's own coverage is a
single control is also the one where a material part of the underlying activity belongs to somebody else. If
`CNB-C-118` deviates, the CloudNimbus half of C1.2 is unevidenced; the AWS half was never CloudNimbus's to
evidence in the first place. Those two facts are independent, they compound, and 07.11 is where the second
is examined.

## 4. Why the RT-02 failure is not a C1.2 matter, although it looks like one

A retention rule that failed to delete records, in a phase that also reports a deletion criterion served by
one control, invites an obvious and wrong connection. This chapter breaks it here so that nothing later has
to.

**They are different rules, different controls and different criteria.**

| | The RT-02 failure | C1.2 |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | **RT-02** — geolocation held 13 months, then deleted, irrespective of contract term | **RT-08** — all customer data deleted within 30 days of termination |
| Trigger | The passage of thirteen months. The contract is **live** | **Termination** of the customer relationship |
| Control | `CNB-C-126` and `CNB-C-127` | `CNB-C-118` |
| Criterion | **P4.2, P4.3** — retention and disposal of personal information | **C1.2** — disposal of confidential information |
| Commitment | None directly; the promise is in the **privacy notice** | **SC-03**, from obligation **O7**, with a certificate |
| Position in the period | **`D-07-01`**, 68 nights on 1 of 8 rules in 1 of 3 regions | **9 of 9 certificates issued inside 30 days** |

The two share a verb and nothing else. One is an in-life privacy rule expiring a category while the customer
is still a customer; the other is a confidentiality commitment discharged once, at the end, with a document
issued to a counterparty. **`CNB-C-118` operated correctly throughout the period, and C1.2 is not evidenced
by the RT-02 failure** — neither favourably nor unfavourably. 07.03 §5 states the same boundary from the
other direction.

The distinction is worth the table because the alternative is a reader concluding that CloudNimbus's
deletion machinery failed in the period. **One of two independent deletion mechanisms failed, on one rule,
in one region.** The other issued nine certificates on time.

## 5. What confidentiality rests on at this vantage

| Proposition | Position at 2026-11-27 | What supports it |
|---|---|---|
| Every in-scope data store carries a classification tag | **Evidenced continuously** | `CNB-C-114`, the Terraform policy gate, EC-04 |
| Tenant isolation is enforced on every documented access path and gates promotion | **Evidenced continuously** | `CNB-C-115`, the isolation suite on every deployment, EC-03 |
| Customer data is encrypted at rest under region-scoped customer-managed keys and in transit | **Evidenced continuously** | `CNB-C-116`'s encryption limb, SR-03, EC-04 |
| Those keys were rotated and the data re-encrypted on the annual cadence | **Evidenced once, on 2026-11-10, in all three regions. Window population 1** | `CNB-C-116`'s rotation limb, EC-04, DEC-409's re-scheduling to 2026-11 |
| Threshold-crossing exports are reviewed against the case record each quarter | **Evidenced for Q3 and Q4 — 2026-07-15 and 2026-10-14** | `CNB-C-117`, 41 events at Q4, 39 + 2 |
| An export that is lawful, instructed and recorded in the disclosure register can be matched by that review | **No. The review compares against support cases only** | **`IS-32`**, referred; the two unmatched Q4 events |
| A terminated tenant's data is removed from every store and certified inside 30 days | **Evidenced for 9 of 9 in the period** | `CNB-C-118`, EC-18, mean 11 days, longest 19 |
| The media beneath those stores are sanitised on decommissioning | **Carved out. Not evidenced by CloudNimbus and not CloudNimbus's to evidence** | `CSOC-03`, disclosed at 02.10 |
| C1.2 has a compensating control | **False, and stated as such since Phase 04** | `CNB-C-118` is the whole of it on CloudNimbus's side |

Everything above the last two rows is a control library doing ordinary work. **The last two rows are where
the criterion is not whole.** C1.2 is a criterion carried by one control on one side of a boundary and by a subservice
organisation on the other, and the honest description of it says both halves out loud rather than reporting
nine clean certificates and stopping.

**Nothing in this chapter is a conclusion about the service auditor's opinion**, and none of it forecasts
what a sample of nine, or of however many the window finally holds, will produce.
