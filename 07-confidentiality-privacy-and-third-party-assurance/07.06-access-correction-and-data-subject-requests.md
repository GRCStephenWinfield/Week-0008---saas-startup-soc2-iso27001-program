## 1. CloudNimbus does not answer a data subject request. It assists with one.

That sentence is the shape of this whole chapter and it is a contractual fact rather than a modest one.

**O6** in the obligations register, and **SC-09** in the principal service commitments, commit CloudNimbus to
**assistance with a data subject's request within 10 business days**. The commitment is owed to the
*customer*, under the data processing addendum. **CUEC-08** discloses the other side of the same
arrangement: the employer responds to its employees' data subject requests, with CloudNimbus's assistance
under O6, and 02.11 names **P5.1** and **P5.2** as the criteria that cannot be met without it.

So the machinery has three parties and two of them are not CloudNimbus. An individual asks; their employer
is the party that must decide what to give them and on what terms; and CloudNimbus assembles, from the
platform, the record set the employer needs in order to decide. **CloudNimbus produces the material and does
not produce the answer.** Where a request reaches CloudNimbus directly — through the in-product channel or
the Data Protection Officer's mailbox — it is logged under `CNB-C-122` and the assembly runs the same way,
because the employment relationship the request arises from is still the employer's.

Two controls carry the period's work.

| Control | What it requires, as published | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| `CNB-C-129` | A data subject request arriving directly or through the employer is logged and the individual's record set is assembled from the platform **inside 10 business days**, with the categories held and any disclosures listed on the response | P5.1, P5.2, P6.7 |
| `CNB-C-130` | Every end user can retrieve their own time, expense, leave and approval records in the application and submit a correction request, which is **routed to their manager for decision** and retained with its outcome and timestamps | P5.1, P5.2, P6.7, P7.1 |

`CNB-C-129` is the assisted path and `CNB-C-130` is the self-service one, and the difference between them is
not convenience. `CNB-C-130` is the reason most individuals never need to make a request at all: the records
are visible in the application to the person they concern, continuously, without anybody being asked. **The
best data subject access mechanism is one nobody has to invoke**, and the thirty-one requests reported below
should be read against 1.24 million end users who can already see their own timesheets.

## 2. Thirty-one requests, thirty inside the window

| Measure | 2026-10-01 to 2026-11-27 |
|---|---|
| Data subject request assistance requests | **31** |
| Record sets assembled inside 10 business days | **30** |
| Assembled outside 10 business days | **1** |
| Record sets delivered incomplete | **0** |
| Requests refused | **0** |

Each is one unit of **EC-20**: type, received date, owner, actions taken, response issued, and closure date
against the due date. The response lists **the categories held and any disclosures**, which is what carries
**P6.7** — an individual asking what CloudNimbus holds is also asking who else has seen it, and answering
the first without the second would be answering a narrower question than the one that was put.

**Thirty of thirty-one is the figure, and §3 is about the one.**

## 3. The one that took twelve business days

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Received | **Thursday 2026-10-29** |
| Due under `CNB-C-129` and SC-09 | **Thursday 2026-11-12** — ten business days |
| Delivered | **Monday 2026-11-16** — **twelve business days** |
| Overrun | **Two business days** |
| Record set delivered | **Complete** |
| Deviation | **`D-07-03`**, 1 of 31, clause 10.2 corrective action **`CA-07-05`** |

**The business-day convention is stated, because the whole case turns on two days.** A business day is
**Monday to Friday excluding the holidays on CloudNimbus's published US calendar**, the calendar maintained
for the Delaware entity headquartered in Denver; the clock starts on the **first business day after
receipt**, not on the date of receipt. Two dates in this window make the convention load-bearing rather than
decorative. **2026-11-11 falls inside the ten-day window, and CloudNimbus's calendar does not observe it**,
so it counts and day ten is Thursday 2026-11-12; had it been a company holiday the due date would have been
Friday 2026-11-13 and the overrun one business day rather than two. **2026-11-26 is on the calendar and is
not a working day**, but it falls after delivery and does not touch this case — it touches the phase's other
business-day counts, and **2026-11-27 is a working day on the same calendar**, which is why this vantage is
described as the last working day before Stage 2 opens. `CNB-C-129`, SC-09, O6 and the CUEC-08 routing all
run on this one calendar and none of them runs on a different one.

**The cause is structural.**

**The individual had changed employer, and both employers use CloudNimbus.** Their record set therefore
spanned two tenants: time records, leave, expenses and approvals accumulated under one employer up to a
leaving date, and a second set accumulated under another from a starting date. From the individual's point
of view that is one working life and one request.

`CNB-C-129` assembles a record set from the platform, and **the assembly is tenant-scoped by design.** It has
to be. The scoping predicate the assembly runs under is the one `CNB-C-115` applies at the data-access
layer — the predicate that keeps one employer's compensation data away from another employer, on every path,
enforced by an isolation suite that blocks a deployment if any documented path fails. It is the single most
important confidentiality control in the platform and it does not have an exception for good intentions.

So the assembly ran twice, in two tenants, produced two record sets, and then somebody had to combine them
into one response for one person without either employer seeing the other's data and without the combined
artefact existing anywhere the isolation model does not reach. That work is manual, it was done by an
engineer working outside the ordinary support path, and it is where the two business days went.

**Nothing about this assembly appears in `CNB-C-117`'s export review.** The Q4 export review was performed
on **2026-10-14, fifteen days before this request was received**, and the two unmatched events it found have
nothing to do with it: they were bulk exports of 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-02 at two tenants' written
instruction, and 07.01 §2 carries them. The assembly for this request produced no threshold-crossing export
that any review in this period has looked at; the next occurrence of `CNB-C-117` is Q1 2027, outside the
window.

**The control that protects the individual's data from the other tenant is the control that made assembling
the individual's data slow.** That is not a bug and it is not a trade-off anybody chose in the abstract. It
is what tenant isolation means when the subject of the data is a person and the unit of isolation is an
employer, and it will recur every time somebody moves between two customers of the same platform — which, at
640 mid-market employers in overlapping labour markets, is not a rare event and will become less rare.

### 3.1 `CA-07-05`, and why it may not have a good answer

**`CA-07-05` asks for a cross-tenant assembly path that does not depend on either tenant seeing the other's
data.** Owner Tobias Lund, clause 10.2, open at this vantage.

The chapter should say plainly that **this is harder than it sounds and may not have a good answer.**

The obvious designs all fail on inspection. A privileged assembly role that can read across tenants is a
standing exception to `CNB-C-115` — the exact shape of the finding R-37 described, created deliberately, for
a use case that arises a handful of times a quarter. A per-request temporary grant is the same thing with a
timer on it, and the audit trail it produces is a record of the isolation model being suspended rather than
of it holding. An identity-linking table that knows one individual is the same person across two employers
creates a new cross-tenant personal-data structure, which is a collection nobody has been told about, in a
platform whose whole confidentiality argument is that no such structure exists. And asking the individual to
make two requests, one per employer, is not an answer to the individual — it is CloudNimbus's architecture
becoming the individual's administrative problem.

What is more likely to work is duller: **assembly stays tenant-scoped and the combination becomes a
procedure rather than an improvisation** — a documented two-tenant assembly path with a named owner, a
checklist, a place to put the intermediate artefacts and a rule for destroying them, and the ten-business-day
clock started at receipt with the second tenant identified on day one rather than on day four. That does not
solve the architectural problem. It converts an unplanned two-day overrun into a planned procedure with the
same isolation properties, which is what a corrective action is allowed to be when the cause is a genuine
tension between two things the programme is right to want.

**The corrective action is recorded as open and no completion date is claimed.** ISO Stage 2 opens in three
days and nothing in this phase forecasts what happens to it there or anywhere else.

## 4. `CNB-C-130` — four hundred and twelve corrections, and the boundary

| Measure | 2026-10-01 to 2026-11-27 |
|---|---|
| Correction requests submitted by end users | **412** |
| Approved by a manager | **389** |
| Declined by a manager | **23** |
| | **389 + 23 = 412** |
| Decided by CloudNimbus | **0** |

Four hundred and twelve is a much larger number than thirty-one, and the two are measuring different
things. A correction request is an individual saying *this clock-out is wrong, I left at six*. It is
routine, it is high-volume, and at CloudNimbus it is the ordinary way a timesheet gets fixed.

**And CloudNimbus routes the request and retains the outcome. It does not decide it.**

The decision belongs to the individual's manager, and it must, because the question a correction request
asks is a question about employment. Whether a worker was on shift until six is a fact about the working
relationship between that worker and that employer. CloudNimbus holds the record; it did not observe the
shift, it does not employ either party, and it has no basis on which to prefer one account of the evening to
another. If it decided, it would be adjudicating an employment matter between two people who have a
relationship with each other and not with it — and the decision would land in somebody's pay.

**That is CUEC-08's boundary and it is P5.2's.** P5.2 concerns correction, amendment or deletion of personal
information at the individual's request; CloudNimbus's control provides the channel, the routing, the
decision record and the timestamps, and the employer supplies the decision. The twenty-three declines are as
much a result of the control operating as the three hundred and eighty-nine approvals: a mechanism in which
nothing is ever declined is a mechanism in which nobody is deciding.

What CloudNimbus does own is the **completeness of the record**. Every one of the 412 is logged with its
outcome and timestamps, retained as a unit of **EC-20**, and visible to the individual in the application.
**No correction request in the period is unaccounted for**, and that — not the approval rate — is what the
control evidences.

## 5. SC-09, listed separately, and the determination that follows

**SC-09 is a commitment and it was missed once.** It is listed **separately from the three deviations**, in
this chapter and in 07.12, for the same reason Phase 06 listed the September SC-01 failure separately from
its five: **a service commitment failure is not a control deviation.** They arise from different instruments,
they are disclosed in different parts of a report, and netting one into the other loses the distinction that
makes both readable.

`D-07-03` is the deviation — a control that requires assembly inside ten business days assembled one in
twelve. **The SC-09 miss is the commitment consequence of the same event**, and it is one event counted
against two different things rather than two events.

Then the question that had to be answered rather than assumed. **DC4** requires disclosure of identified
system incidents that resulted from controls not suitably designed or not operating effectively, **or that
resulted in a significant failure in the achievement of one or more service commitments and system
requirements**. The second limb turns on the word *significant*, and somebody has to decide.

> **Management determined that a single assistance request delivered two business days late, on a record set
> spanning two employers, was not a significant failure in the achievement of SC-09 for the purposes of
> DC4** — one instance in thirty-one, the employer's own response window unaffected, and the individual's
> record set delivered complete. **The determination is management's, it is written down here rather than
> left implicit, and the service auditor may reach a different view.**

The determination is **DEC-711**, taken by Tobias Lund on **2026-11-17**, and argued at **ADR-0034**. Three
things about it are worth stating.

**It is a determination and not an absence of one.** The easy path was to say nothing: a two-day overrun on
one request looks self-evidently minor, and a description that simply did not mention it would attract no
questions until somebody sampled the case register. Phase 06 established the opposite habit when it
disclosed under DC4's second limb rather than arguing the September incident down, and **an unrecorded
judgement that something was insignificant is indistinguishable, a year later, from nobody having
considered it.**

**It is bounded to SC-09 and to DC4's second limb.** It says nothing about `D-07-03`, which stands as a
deviation and is disclosed as one; nothing about whether the criterion P5.1 or P5.2 was met, which is not a
management determination to make; and nothing about the opinion.

**And it is contestable, which is why it is written down in the form it is.** The service auditor evaluates
the significance of a commitment failure independently, on the whole period's evidence rather than on
fifty-eight days of it, and may weigh one in thirty-one differently. Recording the reasoning gives the
auditor something to disagree with, which is more useful than a silence they would have to reconstruct.

## 6. What access and correction rest on at this vantage

| Proposition | Position at 2026-11-27 | What supports it |
|---|---|---|
| A data subject request is logged and the record set assembled inside ten business days | **Evidenced for 30 of 31** | `CNB-C-129`, EC-20 |
| One was not | **Twelve business days, 2026-10-29 to 2026-11-16** | **`D-07-03`**, `CA-07-05` |
| Any record set was delivered incomplete | **No** | All 31 complete |
| The overrun was caused by a control failing | **No — by tenant isolation operating** | `CNB-C-115`'s scoping predicate; §3 |
| An individual can see their own time, expense, leave and approval records without asking | **Evidenced continuously** | `CNB-C-130` |
| Correction requests are routed, decided by a manager and retained with the outcome | **Evidenced 412 times** | `CNB-C-130`, EC-20, 389 + 23 = 412 |
| CloudNimbus decides a correction | **No, and it should not** | CUEC-08; P5.2's boundary |
| The response states the categories held and any disclosures | **Evidenced** | `CNB-C-129`, P6.7 |
| **P7.1** — personal information is accurate, complete, relevant and kept up to date — has an operating record in this period | **Yes, in two places, and neither of them is a CloudNimbus judgement about accuracy** | `CNB-C-130`'s 412 correction requests and 389 approvals; `CNB-C-125`'s Q4 re-confirmation of the stated source and accuracy expectation for each field, 2026-10-16 |
| CloudNimbus determines whether a record is accurate | **No.** The manager decides a correction; the employer maintains the accuracy of the data it supplies | CUEC-09, and P7.1's boundary |
| SC-09 was met | **No — once in thirty-one** | Listed separately from the deviations |
| The SC-09 miss is a significant failure for DC4 purposes | **Management determined not.** The determination is recorded and the service auditor may differ | **DEC-711**, **ADR-0034** |
| Anything here is a conclusion about the opinion | **No** | — |

**P7.1's two rows are worth reading together, because the criterion had no operating record anywhere in this
phase until they were written down.** P7.1 asks whether personal information is accurate, complete, relevant
and up to date for the purposes it is used for. CloudNimbus's answer has two limbs and neither is an opinion
about a fact. The **correction channel** is the mechanism by which an individual disputes a record and a
decision is taken and retained — 412 requests, 389 approvals, 23 declines, none decided by CloudNimbus. The
**field reconciliation** is the mechanism by which each category's **stated source and accuracy expectation**
is re-confirmed, quarterly, against what is actually populated in production; `CNB-C-125` performed it on
2026-10-16 and found no divergence, for the reason 07.05 §1 gives. **CUEC-09 draws the boundary**: the
employer maintains the accuracy of the employment data it supplies, and CloudNimbus maintains the channel,
the record and the reconciliation. A phase that had answered P7.1 with silence would have implied there was
nothing to answer with.

**The fourth row is the one to take away.** The single deviation in this chapter was produced by the
platform's strongest confidentiality control doing exactly what it is designed to do, to an individual whose
data it was protecting, in a case where the individual and the isolation boundary pointed in opposite
directions. That is a real tension, it is disclosed rather than resolved, and `CA-07-05` is an attempt at it
rather than an answer to it.
