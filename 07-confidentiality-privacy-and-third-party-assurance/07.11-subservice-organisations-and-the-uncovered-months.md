## 1. What the carve-out promised the reader, and what has to be delivered against it

Phase 02 identified two subservice organisations — **SUB-01 Amazon Web Services** and **SUB-02 Halcyon
Identity, Inc.** — and presented both under the **carve-out method** at **DEC-108**. Under that method their
controls are excluded from the description of the system and are **not tested by the service auditor**, and
the description states instead the **complementary subservice organisation controls** CloudNimbus assumes
they perform: **eleven for AWS and three for Halcyon Identity, 11 + 3 = 14**.

02.10 §7 stated the cost of that method in one sentence: **the report will contain no evidence about whether
AWS or Halcyon Identity performed the controls attributed to them**, and a reader who needs that assurance
obtains it from those organisations' own reports. This chapter is where that promise is tested against what
those reports actually cover, and the answer is less comfortable than the promise sounds.

The fourteen assumptions are set out in full at 02.10 §8 and are not restated here. What is worth carrying
into a chapter about coverage is the shape of what they claim, because it determines what a gap in coverage
touches.

| The fourteen, grouped | Party | Criteria they are stated against |
|---|---|---|
| Physical and environmental protection, physical access restriction — CSOC-01, CSOC-02 | AWS | CC6.4, A1.2 |
| Media sanitisation on decommissioning — CSOC-03 | AWS | CC6.5, **C1.2** |
| Logical isolation of the virtualisation and managed service planes, key material protection, AWS personnel access, control-plane integrity — CSOC-04, CSOC-06, CSOC-09, CSOC-10 | AWS | CC6.1, CC6.3, C1.1 |
| Redundancy of power, cooling and network; durability and integrity of stored objects and snapshots — CSOC-05, CSOC-08 | AWS | A1.1, A1.2, PI1.5 |
| Infrastructure patching beneath the managed services, and incident notification — CSOC-07, CSOC-11 | AWS | CC6.6, CC7.1, CC7.2, CC7.3 |
| Authentication of end users, protection of credential and identity records, availability and continuity of the identity service — CSOC-12, CSOC-13, CSOC-14 | Halcyon Identity | CC6.1, CC6.6, C1.1, CC7.2, A1.1, A1.2, A1.3 |

**One of these lands on the criterion with the thinnest coverage in the whole examination.** CSOC-03 — secure
decommissioning and sanitisation of storage media — is stated against **C1.2**, and C1.2 is served on
CloudNimbus's side by exactly one control, `CNB-C-118`.
[07.01 §3](07.01-the-confidentiality-criteria-and-the-single-control.md) owns that argument and this chapter
adds only the coverage consequence: the half of C1.2 that CloudNimbus does not operate is also the half
about which no evidence will appear anywhere in the report.

## 2. The two artefacts, and what each of them is

**AWS** provides a service auditor's report covering **2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30**. **Halcyon Identity**
provides one covering **2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30**. Both were read at the CAL-08 review of **2026-10-07**
under `CNB-C-092`, and the reading of what each does and does not cover produced two separate outputs: the
complementary user entity control enumeration that
[07.10](07.10-reading-the-other-sides-complementary-controls.md) carries, and the period arithmetic below.

Both organisations were also asked for a **bridge letter**, and the chapter has to be precise about what
that instrument is.

**A bridge letter — or gap letter — is issued by the subservice organisation's own management. It is
unaudited. No CPA firm signs it, reviews it, or opines on it.** It says, in substance, that management is
not aware of material changes to the control environment between the end of the report period and the date
of the letter. 01.11 §4 records CloudNimbus's own position on the instrument from the other side, where
**O3** obliges CloudNimbus to provide one on request: a customer that treats a bridge letter as an extension
of the examination has misread it.

**Requesting one is nonetheless the right thing to do**, and the reason is worth stating so that the rest of
this chapter is not read as dismissing them. A bridge letter puts a dated, signed statement by a named
management on the record for a period that would otherwise hold nothing at all, and it creates an occasion
on which somebody at the subservice organisation has to consider the question. That is worth having. It is
not assurance, and the difference between "worth having" and "assurance" is the whole of §3.

## 3. The coverage arithmetic

The observation window is **2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31 — six months.**

| | AWS | Halcyon Identity |
|---|---|---|
| Report period | **2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30** | **2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30** |
| Months of CloudNimbus's window covered by a practitioner's opinion | **3** — July, August, September | **0** |
| Bridge letter | **Requested 2026-10-09, received 2026-11-13** — thirty-five days — covering **2026-10-01 to 2026-10-31** | **Requested 2026-10-09, received 2026-11-06** — twenty-eight days — covering to **2026-10-31**, July to October |
| Months covered by an unaudited bridge letter | **1** | **4** |
| **Months covered by nothing at this vantage** † | **2** — November and December | **2** — November and December |
| | 3 + 1 + 2 = 6 | 0 + 4 + 2 = 6 |

† **The two are not two elapsed months.** At **2026-11-27** November is **27 of its 30 days elapsed and
uncovered**, and **December has not happened**. This chapter refuses to publish a November availability
figure on the ground that a partial month is not a month, and it must not then count an unelapsed month in a
deficit. The honest statement is: **one month less three days is uncovered and elapsed; one month is
uncovered and has not begun.** The figure of two is the number of window months the artefacts in hand do not
reach, which is what the row is for and is not the same claim.

Three readings of that table matter, in ascending order of discomfort.

**Two months of the window are not reached by any artefact in hand, for both parties.** November and
December sit beyond the
end of both bridge letters. That is a known and ordinary consequence of a window that closes on 31 December
and of subservice organisations whose reporting calendars are their own, and it is addressed by the plan in
§4 rather than by alarm.

**Both letters were requested on the same day and arrived a week apart.** Requested **2026-10-09**;
Halcyon's received **2026-11-06** at twenty-eight days, AWS's **2026-11-13** at thirty-five. That is a fact
about the mechanism rather than about either party: a bridge letter is a
management statement produced on request by a party with no obligation to produce it quickly, and the
interval between asking and receiving is not under CloudNimbus's control. It matters because the final
letters will be requested on the same terms, against a fieldwork start date that does
not move — which is what §4 sets the request date against.

**And Halcyon Identity's report period ends before CloudNimbus's window begins.** 2026-06-30 is the day
before 2026-07-01. **Not one day of the observation window is covered by a practitioner's opinion on
Halcyon Identity's controls**, and four of the six months rest entirely on Halcyon management's own
statement.

**This was knowable in March, and the chapter must not record it as a discovery.** Halcyon Identity's
report period was on file when `02.10` was written and the observation window's dates were fixed at the same
time; the arithmetic that produces a zero in that column is a subtraction anybody could have done eight
months before the CAL-08 review did it. What was missing was not the information but a control that compares
the two. **`CA-07-06`** is opened for that under clause 10.2, owner **Rahul Bhargava**: at each CAL-08
occurrence, every Tier 1 vendor's assurance artefact has its **period compared against CloudNimbus's own
observation window**, and the months covered, bridged and uncovered are recorded on the reading form before
the review closes. It has no deviation above it, for the reason
[07.10 §4](07.10-reading-the-other-sides-complementary-controls.md) gives: no control required the
comparison, so no control failed.

The position itself is recorded as an issue:

> **`IS-31` — No month of the six-month observation window is covered by a practitioner's opinion on Halcyon
> Identity's controls; four are covered by an unaudited bridge letter and two by nothing at this vantage.
> For AWS, three months are covered by an opinion, one by a bridge letter and two by nothing.** Owner Rahul
> Bhargava. Referred.

**A bridge letter covers a gap; it does not close one.** Those two verbs are the whole of the distinction. A
statement that management is not aware of material changes is evidence of an absence of awareness, made by
the party whose controls are in question, unexamined by anybody. It is better than silence and it is not the
same kind of thing as an opinion on operating effectiveness throughout a period, supported by tests and
results the reader can see.

**DEC-710**, taken by Rahul Bhargava on **2026-11-06**, accepts Halcyon's bridge letter **and records the
two uncovered months rather than waiving them.** Acceptance and waiver are different acts. Accepting the
letter means the letter is on the record and the gap it does not reach is on the record beside it; waiving
the gap would mean deciding it does not need to be recorded, which is a decision nobody at CloudNimbus is in
a position to take on the service auditor's behalf.

## 4. The plan, and the forecast this chapter refuses to make

**Final bridge letters covering to 2026-12-31 will be requested from both organisations on 2026-12-15**,
owner **Rahul Bhargava**, with escalation to Karim Haddad if either has not arrived by **2027-01-08**.

**The date is not January, and the change of date is the correction.** A request made in January against
fieldwork that opens on **2027-01-12** leaves no room at all: AWS's October letter took **thirty-five days**
and Halcyon's twenty-eight, and thirty-five days from a January request lands well after fieldwork has
started. Requesting on 2026-12-15 — two weeks before the period the letters have to cover has even closed,
which the covering language has to accommodate — is the earliest date CloudNimbus can usefully ask.

**It does not close the arithmetic, and the chapter says so rather than implying that it does.**
Thirty-five days from 2026-12-15 is **2027-01-19**, seven days after fieldwork opens. **On AWS's own
precedent the final letter is expected after fieldwork opens** — which is exactly why the position is stated
to Ashcombe &amp; Doyle in advance rather than discovered in fieldwork. The request date is the only
variable CloudNimbus controls, and it is now set rather than described.

**And the plan states what happens if they do not arrive**, because a plan whose failure branch is unwritten
is a plan that fails silently. If either letter is outstanding at 2027-01-08, the position is **stated to
Ashcombe &amp; Doyle in writing before fieldwork opens** rather than discovered inside it: which months of
the window are covered by an opinion, which by a letter, which by nothing, and what CloudNimbus did to ask.
That is the whole of the escalation. **It changes nothing about the coverage and it changes who finds out
about it first**, which for an examination is not a small difference — and it is `IS-31`'s carrier into
Phase 08 rather than a promise that the letters will come.

**What this chapter does not do is predict what the service auditor will make of any of it.** Whether the
absence of a practitioner's opinion on a carved-out subservice organisation's controls for part or all of
the period affects the examination, and if so how, is the service auditor's evaluation to perform on
evidence that does not yet exist. The programme has a standing rule about this — **ADR-0015**: no forecast
is published until it has been proved reachable — and 06.12 §7 applied it to the opinion rather than to the
register. It applies here in the same form. **A programme willing to guess at its own result is a programme
whose other numbers are worth less.**

What can be said is what the position is, in the two sentences a reader needs: the carve-out means the
report contains no evidence about these controls in any case, and the artefacts CloudNimbus holds to satisfy
itself about them cover the window unevenly, with the unevenness stated above rather than averaged away.

## 5. The carve-out point, stated once

**The report covers the subservice organisation's controls. It does not cover CloudNimbus's configuration of
them.**

CSOC-01 to CSOC-14 state what CloudNimbus depends on: that the facilities are protected, that the
virtualisation plane isolates tenants, that key material cannot be extracted, that snapshots are durable,
that end users are authenticated as configured. Every one of those is a statement about what the other
organisation does.

**A clean AWS report says nothing whatever about whether CloudNimbus turned the right things on.** A bucket
policy that permits more than it should, an IAM role scoped too widely, a snapshot created without
encryption, a security group left open — each is **CloudNimbus's control failure in an environment the
provider operated correctly**, and no amount of assurance about the provider touches it. That is what
`CNB-C-093` exists for on the way in, what the CUEC mapping in 07.10 exists for on the way through, and what
02.10 §9 states directly: a service organisation that answers questions about its own
configuration by citing its cloud provider's certifications is trading on a confusion rather than
correcting it.

The two facts sit together and neither cancels the other. **The months the artefacts cover are the months
somebody examined the provider. None of those months is a month anybody examined CloudNimbus's use of the
provider**, and the second question is answered by CloudNimbus's own controls in the description, tested by
the service auditor, in Section IV.

## 6. The same instrument, from the other side

CloudNimbus is obliged under **O3** and **SC-10** to provide a bridge letter, on request, covering the gap
between its own report period end and a customer's reporting date, and 01.11 §8 records that preparing the
standard form and the basis on which management can sign it is work belonging inside the window rather than
in the queue behind the distribution exercise.

**This phase is the first time the programme has been on the receiving end of the instrument it is
preparing to issue**, and the experience should change the drafting. Two things stand out from reading the
two letters received. The first is that **the covering language is the letter**: a bridge letter whose text
does not say plainly that it is management-issued, unaudited and carries no auditor opinion will be read as
though it were an extension of the report, by exactly the procurement reader 02.11 §6 describes. The second
is that **the date it arrives is part of what it is worth**. A letter that arrives thirty-five days after it
is asked for has left the requester holding an uncovered period for thirty-five days, whatever the letter
eventually says about it.

CloudNimbus will be asked for its own letters in the same weeks it is chasing the final two. The programme
that finds that irritating from one side should recognise it from the other, and the standard form should be
drafted so that a customer never has to work out what the instrument is from the fact that it does not say.
