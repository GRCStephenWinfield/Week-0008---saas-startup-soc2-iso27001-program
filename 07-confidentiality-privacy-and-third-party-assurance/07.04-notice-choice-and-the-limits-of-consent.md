## 1. The two criteria a processor is usually told it does not have

The orthodox position for a business-to-business platform is that notice and choice belong to the employer.
The employer decides what to collect, tells its workforce about it, and the platform processes on
instruction. On that view **P1.1** and **P2.1** are somebody else's criteria and a SaaS vendor takes
Confidentiality and stops.

Phase 01 rejected that position and Phase 02 evidenced the rejection with a count: **seven of the twelve
personal-data categories are collected directly from the individual**, with no intermediary in the moment of
collection, and three of those seven arise on every authenticated session irrespective of any tenant
setting. A worker looking at a CloudNimbus screen on a device CloudNimbus's application runs on, handing
CloudNimbus a value in that instant, is in a relationship with CloudNimbus whatever the contract between
CloudNimbus and their employer says about it. That is ADR-0002, and this chapter is where it acquires an
operating record.

Two controls carry notice and two carry choice, and the criteria they answer are asymmetric in a way that
matters:

| Control | Criteria served | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `CNB-C-119` | **P1.1, P2.1** | Versions the in-product notice, refuses a change without a version increment, displays the current version at first session after a change, and records the display against the user record |
| `CNB-C-120` | **P1.1** | The Data Protection Officer's annual reconciliation of the notice against PD-01 to PD-12, RT-01 to RT-08 and the published sub-processor list, signed |
| `CNB-C-121` | **P3.2** | Requests the operating-system location permission at first clock-in where a tenant has enabled capture, records grant or refusal with a timestamp, and leaves the field unpopulated on a refusal **without blocking the clock-in** |
| `CNB-C-123` | **P2.1, P4.1** | Optional collections are enabled per tenant against a recorded administrator, date and stated purpose, and a collection that is not enabled is not made by the client at all |

**P3.2 is served by `CNB-C-121` alone**, one of the three single-control criteria in the library, and 07.05
carries what that costs. This chapter is about P1.1 and P2.1.

## 2. The notice is displayed. It is not read.

`CNB-C-119` is a good control and its evidence is unusually clean. The notice is a versioned artefact inside
the application; a change cannot ship without an increment; the clients display the current version at first
session after a change; and the display is recorded against the user record as one unit of **EC-19**.

| Measure | Position at 2026-11-27 |
|---|---|
| End users of the platform | **~1.24 million** |
| Users to whom the current notice version was displayed at first session | **1,109,442** |
| Users to whom it has not been displayed | The remainder — **roughly 130,000** — who have not opened the app since the last increment |
| Notice versions shipped without a version increment | **0** |

**The exact numerator against an approximate denominator is deliberate and is worth one sentence.**
CloudNimbus knows precisely how many display events it recorded; it knows the size of its end-user
population only to the nearest ten thousand, because that population changes every day as employers hire and
terminate and as tenants onboard. A percentage computed from those two numbers would carry the precision of
the first and the accuracy of the second. **The count is the evidence. The proportion is an illustration.**

Then the harder point, which the chapter states and deliberately does not resolve.

**The notice is published in the product and it is not read.** A little over 1.1 million people were shown
it at first session, which means the application put it in front of them and recorded that it did. Nothing
in that record establishes that anybody read a word of it, understood the thirteen-month geolocation rule,
or could name a sub-processor afterwards. And the roughly 130,000 who have not opened the app since the last
increment have not been shown the current version at all — they are shown it the next time they open it,
which for a seasonal or intermittent worker may be months.

**P1.1 asks whether notice is provided. It does not ask whether it landed.** The criterion concerns the
communication of the entity's commitments and system requirements relating to the collection, use,
retention, disclosure and disposal of personal information to the data subjects. Providing it is a testable
act with a timestamp; landing is not a property any control in this library can evidence and not one the
criterion asks for. **CloudNimbus can answer the criterion completely and still not know whether anybody
knows anything**, and a chapter that reported 1,109,442 as though it were a comprehension figure would be
overstating what the control produced. The gap is stated here rather than closed, because closing it would
require a claim nothing supports.

## 3. `CNB-C-120` — the annual reconciliation, and the one divergence it found

`CNB-C-120` is the control that keeps the notice true. Once a year the Data Protection Officer reconciles
what the notice says against three registers — **PD-01 to PD-12**, **RT-01 to RT-08**, and the **published
sub-processor list** — and signs the reconciliation. It is a unit of **EC-15**, and the occurrence in this
period was performed on **2026-11-19** and is recorded at **GOV-28**.

| Reconciled against | Result |
|---|---|
| PD-01 to PD-12, the personal-information inventory | **No divergence.** Every category the notice describes is in the inventory and every category in the inventory is described |
| RT-01 to RT-08, the retention schedule | **No divergence.** The notice's thirteen-month geolocation statement matches RT-02; the contract-term statements match RT-01, RT-03 and RT-04 |
| The published sub-processor list | **One divergence** |

**The divergence: the published list still named the outgoing email delivery provider fourteen days after
the replacement took effect on 2026-11-05.** It was corrected on **2026-11-20**, the working day after the
reconciliation. That is **`IS-34`** — a referred issue rather than a deviation, because
`CNB-C-120` operated on time and **found** it, and no control in the library required the published list to
be updated at the change. Clause 10.2 corrective action **`CA-07-04`** creates that requirement. 07.07 §5
sets out why the doctrine that put `CA-07-01` outside the deviation set puts this one outside it too. 07.07 §4 owns the sub-processor change itself — the notice period, the two
objections and what the master services agreement says about them — and this chapter reports only what the
reconciliation found.

Two things about that finding are worth more than the finding.

**A population of one is the whole of an annual control's exposure in this window.** `CNB-C-120` operates
once a year. It operated once in the observation window, it found something, and the deviation rate is one
of one. There is no second occurrence to average against and none will exist before the period closes.
Phase 04 named exactly this exposure for the thirteen controls in the library that operate quarterly,
semi-annually or annually, and this is what it looks like when one of them fires.

**And the divergence was found by the annual control rather than by the change.** A sub-processor change is
a discrete, dated, planned event with an owner; the currency of the published list is a consequence of it,
and nothing required the person making the change to update the list as part of making it. It fell instead
to a once-a-year reconciliation, which found it fourteen days later and would equally have found it eleven
months later. **DEC-712** moves the currency check to the change itself, which is the corrective action
rather than the correction, and 07.07 records why that is the right place for it.

## 4. Choice, at two layers, and only one of them is CloudNimbus's

Choice at CloudNimbus operates at two layers, and confusing them is the commonest error available in this
subject.

**The first layer is the tenant.** `CNB-C-123` requires an optional collection to be enabled per tenant in
the admin console against a recorded administrator, a date and a stated purpose, and a collection that is
not enabled **is not made by the client at all** — the mobile application does not request the permission,
the field is not populated, and there is nothing to retain.

| Tenant position, geolocation capture | Count |
|---|---|
| Tenants with capture **disabled** | **147** |
| Tenants with capture **enabled** | **493** |
| **Total** | **147 + 493 = 640** |

**640 is the register at both ends of the period.** Nine tenants terminated between 2026-10-01 and
2026-11-27 — the nine `CNB-C-118` certified at 07.01 §3.1 — and nine onboarded, so the denominator above is
the same number on both dates rather than a figure that has been left where it was found.

Phase 02 recorded **387 of 640** enabled at its vantage of 2026-03-31. At this vantage the figure is
**493**. The two are the same measure taken eight months apart, both are stated with their dates, and this
phase does not restate the earlier one as though it were current. What has not changed is the argument
underneath it: Phase 02 tested whether the Privacy category rested on a configuration flag and found that it
does not, because six further categories are collected directly from the individual irrespective of any
tenant setting and three of those arise on every authenticated session. **A rising number of enabled tenants
strengthens the operational case for the geolocation controls and changes nothing about the categorical
one.**

**The second layer is the individual.** Where a tenant has enabled capture, `CNB-C-121` asks the operating
system for the location permission at first clock-in and records what the individual answered.

| Individual position, in the 493 enabled tenants | Count |
|---|---|
| End users in enabled tenants | **approximately 1,060,000** |
| **Of those, end users who have reached a first clock-in and have a recorded response** | **961,000** |
| Operating-system permission **granted** | **918,204** |
| Operating-system permission **refused** | **42,796** |
| | **918,204 + 42,796 = 961,000** |
| End users in enabled tenants with **no recorded response** | **roughly 99,000** — they have not clocked in on the mobile client |

**961,000 is not the population of the enabled tenants and the table says which population it is.**
`CNB-C-121` asks for the permission **at first clock-in**, so a person who has never clocked in on the
mobile client has never been asked and has no recorded answer. The enabled-tenant population is
approximately **1,060,000**; the population with a recorded response is **961,000**; and the roughly 99,000
in between are neither grants nor refusals. Reading 961,000 as the enabled-tenant total would contradict §2,
which reports the same shape from the other side — the platform knows exactly what it recorded and knows its
end-user population only to the nearest ten thousand.

Forty-two thousand seven hundred and ninety-six people said no, and the platform recorded it, honoured it,
and let them clock in. That is the control working, and it is a larger number than most notice-and-choice
programmes ever produce because most of them never offer a real refusal.

And here is the paragraph this chapter owes the reader, which must not be allowed to become a legal
conclusion:

> **A refusal is recorded, the capture is skipped, and the worker still clocks in.** At the operating-system
> layer the choice is real. At the employment layer it is not CloudNimbus's to offer: the employer decides
> whether location is captured at all, and the individual whose location it is was not asked. **P2.1 asks
> whether choice and consent are communicated and obtained where required. CloudNimbus can answer the first
> half completely and can only describe the second.** Where the requirement sits, and on whom, is a question
> for the employer and its own advisers; this programme does not answer it and 02.11's CUEC-07 is why.

The two layers together are the honest description of what a refusal means. The individual can refuse the
value. They cannot refuse the collection, because the collection was configured by their employer under
`CNB-C-123` with an administrator's name, a date and a stated purpose against it, and the stated purpose
belongs to a business relationship the worker is not party to. **A refusal at the operating-system layer is
a real refusal of a real thing, and it is not the same as having been asked.**

## 5. The promise was made to a person CloudNimbus cannot reach

This is the structural fact under the whole privacy category at CloudNimbus, and it is stated here in full
because it is older than any event in this phase and does not go away when the events close.

**The data subjects are the approximately 1.24 million employees of CloudNimbus's 640 customers.** They are
not CloudNimbus's customers. They did not choose CloudNimbus, cannot switch away from it, have no contract
with it, and in most cases could not name it as anything other than the thing they clock in on. Phase 02
registered them as INT-04 in the interested-party register and SH-22 in the stakeholder register, and
recorded the same uncomfortable fact in both: **the strongest interest in the table, held by the parties with
no channel.**

CloudNimbus nonetheless makes promises to them directly, because it collects from them directly. The
in-product notice tells an individual what is collected, what it is used for, how long it is kept, and who
it is disclosed to. **The thirteen-month geolocation statement is a promise to a worker, not to their
employer**, and no employer is the beneficiary of it. RT-02 exists because of that promise.

The problem is what happens when the promise needs maintaining. **CloudNimbus's only channel to an
individual is the application.** There is no address, no mailing list, no email relationship — PD-02 is the
work email address, supplied by the employer for notification and account recovery, and using it as a
privacy communication channel would be CloudNimbus writing to a worker's employer-administered mailbox about
their employer's processing. The in-product notice reaches only a worker who opens the app, which is why
roughly 130,000 people have not seen the current version. **CUEC-07 puts notice of the employer's own
processing in the employer's hands**, and 02.11 §5.3 recorded CUEC-07 as the entry on the list closest to
the line for precisely this reason: P1.1 is CloudNimbus's criterion, and CloudNimbus's notice can be accurate
and still incomplete about the combined processing an individual is actually subject to.

**The RT-02 failure is what this looks like when something goes wrong.** For sixty-eight nights CloudNimbus
kept some individuals' clock-in locations longer than it had told them it would, and when it wanted to say
so, it found it had nobody to say it to. It notified the forty-one employers instead, on 2026-10-26, under
**DEC-704** — **the nearest available thing to keeping the promise, and nearest by some distance rather than
close.** 07.03 §6 owns that decision and the dissent recorded against it.

The chapter states the position rather than proposing a solution, because the plausible solutions are all
worse. A direct channel to 1.24 million people who did not ask for one, opened by their employer's supplier,
would be a new collection and a new intrusion justified by the desire to be able to apologise. **The
condition is structural, it is disclosed, and it is the reason the privacy design leans on the employer
through CUEC-07 and CUEC-08 while refusing to pretend that the leaning discharges P1.1.**

## 6. What notice and choice rest on at this vantage

| Proposition | Position at 2026-11-27 | What supports it |
|---|---|---|
| The in-product notice is versioned and cannot ship without an increment | **Evidenced continuously** | `CNB-C-119`, EC-19 |
| The current version was displayed at first session and the display recorded | **Evidenced for 1,109,442 users** | `CNB-C-119`, EC-19 |
| Every end user has seen the current version | **False, and stated as such** | Roughly 130,000 have not opened the app since the last increment |
| Anybody read it | **Not evidenced, not evidenceable, and not what P1.1 asks** | — |
| The notice reconciles to the inventory and the retention schedule | **Evidenced 2026-11-19** | `CNB-C-120`, GOV-28 |
| The notice reconciled to the published sub-processor list | **No — one divergence, fourteen days, corrected 2026-11-20** | **`IS-34`**, `CA-07-04` |
| An optional collection is not made unless a tenant enabled it with a stated purpose | **Evidenced continuously** | `CNB-C-123`, 147 disabled and 493 enabled |
| An individual can refuse geolocation and still use the service | **Evidenced 42,796 times** | `CNB-C-121`, EC-19 |
| Every end user in an enabled tenant has answered the permission prompt | **False. 961,000 of approximately 1,060,000 have a recorded response** | `CNB-C-121` asks at first clock-in; roughly 99,000 have not clocked in on the mobile client |
| The individual was asked whether the collection should happen at all | **No. That decision is the employer's** | `CNB-C-123`, CUEC-07 |

**The last row is not a deficiency in the control set.** It is a description of where CloudNimbus sits, and
the programme's position is that describing it precisely is worth more than a control that appeared to close
it. Nothing in this chapter states that any processing had or lacked any particular status under any law,
that any consent was or was not valid, or that any requirement applied to anybody. It states what the notice
says, what the control records, what the employer decides, and where the boundary between them falls.
