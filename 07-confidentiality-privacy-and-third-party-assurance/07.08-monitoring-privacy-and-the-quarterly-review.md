## 1. One criterion, two controls, and the question neither of them asks

**P8.1** is the last of the eighteen Privacy criteria and the only one that is about the programme rather
than about the data. It asks whether the entity monitors compliance with its privacy commitments and system
requirements, whether it has a process for receiving, addressing, resolving and communicating the resolution
of inquiries, complaints and disputes, and whether it corrects what it finds.

Two controls serve it. **`CNB-C-122`** — Corrective, Monthly, Tobias Lund — requires every privacy inquiry
or complaint arriving through the in-product channel or the Data Protection Officer's mailbox to be logged
in the privacy case register with a received date, an owner and a due date, and requires the register to be
worked and reviewed monthly. **`CNB-C-133`** — Detective, Quarterly, Tobias Lund — requires the Data
Protection Officer to review the sub-processor assurance evidence, the disclosure register, privacy
incidents and open privacy cases **together** each quarter, and to minute the outcome and any required
action to the Trust Committee.

The pairing is intake and review, and 04.06 §5 records that `CNB-C-122` is one of the four Privacy controls
with nothing to cite in Annex A while `CNB-C-133` cites **A.5.34** and **A.5.36**. That asymmetry is
accurate rather than untidy: a complaint channel is a commitment made to an individual, and the annex has no
control that asks whether one exists.

**Neither control asks whether anybody knows the channel is there**, and §3 is about that gap.

## 2. Fourteen enquiries, no complaints

| Measure | 2026-10-01 to 2026-11-27 |
|---|---|
| Privacy enquiries received | **14** |
| Complaints received | **0** |
| Routed to the employer within two business days, on the same boundary CUEC-08 draws | **12** |
| Answered directly by CloudNimbus | **2** |
| | 12 + 2 = **14** |

**The register distinguishes an enquiry from a complaint and the distinction is not cosmetic.** An enquiry
asks what CloudNimbus does with something; a complaint asserts that what CloudNimbus did was wrong. They
carry different due dates, different owners and different escalation, and a register that collapses them
into "cases" cannot report either. Fourteen enquiries and no complaints is what the register says; it is not
a claim that nobody was dissatisfied, only that nothing arrived through the two channels `CNB-C-122` names
in the form a complaint takes.

**Twelve of the fourteen were routed to the employer within two business days, on the same boundary CUEC-08
draws, and that routing is not CloudNimbus declining to answer.** CUEC-08 itself is about **data subject
requests**, and a general privacy enquiry is not one; calling this routing "CUEC-08 operating" would credit
a complementary user entity control with an occurrence it did not have. What is true is narrower: the line
between what CloudNimbus can answer and what only the employer can answer is the line CUEC-07 and CUEC-08
were drawn along, and these twelve fall on the employer's side of it. The employer is the party with the employment
relationship and the party that decides what is collected in its tenant and why. Where an individual asks
why their location is captured at clock-in, or who inside their organisation can see their leave records,
the answer is the employer's configuration and the employer's decision, and CloudNimbus routing the question
to the party that can answer it is the design 02.11 §5.3 set out when it disclosed CUEC-07 and CUEC-08.
Routing is not deflection provided two things are true, and both are recorded against each of the twelve:
the individual is told where their question has gone, and the routing is timed. **Two business days is the
timed part**, and it is the only part of the twelve CloudNimbus can be examined on — what the employer did
next is outside the system boundary and outside this record.

**The two answered directly are the two that were about CloudNimbus.** Both concerned **CloudNimbus's own
notice** — its content and its version — which is the one subject on which routing to the employer would
have been an evasion, because the employer did not write it and cannot explain it.
[07.04](07.04-notice-choice-and-the-limits-of-consent.md) carries the notice itself and the
1,109,442 end users who have seen the current version at first session.

**The monthly review limb has a population of one inside this period.** `CNB-C-122`'s cadence is Monthly and
the period runs to 2026-11-27, so the October occurrence falls inside it and November's does not — **November
is not a month until it closes**, which is the same discipline
[07.12 §3](07.12-quarter-four-to-date-operating-record.md) applies to the availability figure and 06.12
applied to the restore test population. One occurrence of a monthly control is the whole of this period's
evidence for that limb, and the observation window will hold six.

## 3. Fourteen, from 1.24 million people, in fifty-eight days

> **Fourteen enquiries from 1.24 million end users in fifty-eight days is either a programme working or a
> channel nobody knows about, and the register cannot tell you which.** P8.1 asks whether the entity
> monitors compliance and addresses what it finds. It does not ask whether anybody found the door.

That is the chapter's uncomfortable paragraph and it is not rhetorical. Work the arithmetic in either
direction and both readings survive. Fourteen in fifty-eight days is roughly one enquiry per **88,000 end
users across the fifty-eight days**, which is **one per about 171,000 per month** — and the two figures are
not interchangeable, which is why both are stated. On either normalisation, a workforce application with a
published in-product privacy notice and a mailbox on the notice could plausibly produce that rate from a
population that is largely content and largely uninterested. It is equally plausible as the figure produced by a channel that exists, is documented, is
reachable in three taps from a screen nobody visits, and is found only by the small number of people
already determined to find it.

**The register cannot distinguish them because a register records what arrived.** Nothing in the case
register is evidence about the population that did not write in, and no volume of clean case handling
changes that. This is the same structural limit 06.09 §6 recorded for CUEC-05's 229 non-responses and 06.12
§7 recorded for zero security incidents: **an absence of signal is not a signal, and a programme that reads
it as one has stopped measuring and started reassuring itself.**

What can honestly be said is what the fourteen show about the mechanism. Every one was logged with a
received date, an owner and a due date. Twelve were routed inside two business days. Two were answered.
None was left open past its due date at this vantage. That is a working intake process demonstrated on a
population of fourteen, and it is not a statement about awareness. **This phase does not resolve the
question and does not propose a measurement of awareness**, because the obvious measurement — asking end
users whether they know the channel exists — would require CloudNimbus to reach 1.24 million individuals
through their employers, which is the same channel problem
[07.04 §5](07.04-notice-choice-and-the-limits-of-consent.md) describes from the other end.

## 4. The quarterly review of 2026-10-28

`CNB-C-133`'s Q4 occurrence was held on **2026-10-28** and is recorded at **GOV-27**. The observation window
holds **two** occurrences of this control; this period holds one of them, and 04.11 §3's arithmetic applies
without softening — **a single missed occurrence on a quarterly control is a deviation rate of fifty per
cent in a six-month window.**

The control requires four things to be read **together**, and the review's minute follows that structure
because the conjunction is the control. Reading the disclosure register alone tells you what left; reading
open cases alone tells you who asked; reading them in the same hour is what allows a pattern that spans two
registers to be seen by one person.

| What `CNB-C-133` requires to be reviewed | Position at 2026-10-28 |
|---|---|
| Sub-processor assurance evidence | The CAL-08 Q4 review of 2026-10-07 and its Tier 1 refresh, including the one vendor holding no current artefact — 07.09 and GOV-25 |
| The disclosure register | Six entries in the period, four customer-directed exports and two responses to a customer's own auditors — 07.07 §2 |
| Privacy incidents | The RT-02 over-retention, its correction and the notification of all 41 EU-residency customers on 2026-10-26 — 07.03 and GOV-26 |
| Open privacy cases | The privacy case register, worked and current |

**The review's output was a new control, which is not the output a quarterly review usually produces.**
`CNB-C-149` was admitted to the library at this occurrence under **DEC-706**, taken by Karim Haddad, and
the library is **149** controls from 2026-10-28 rather than 148.
[07.02 §5](07.02-the-retention-schedule-and-the-deletion-machinery.md) owns what that growth is and what it
costs — the eligible-population problem, the two implementations of one rule, and the sixty-five-night
population a control admitted in October can have inside a window that opened in July — and **ADR-0031**
records the decision and the alternative. This chapter records only that the admission happened here, at the
control the criteria give the Data Protection Officer for exactly this purpose, and that it was minuted to
the Trust Committee as `CNB-C-133` requires.

## 5. P8.1 and the failure the monitoring control could not have found

The chapter has to sit with one fact rather than route around it. **The RT-02 over-retention was not found
by either of the controls serving P8.1.** It was found on 2026-10-23 by the Compliance Manager assembling a
Q4 retention evidence sample for Ashcombe &amp; Doyle, five days before the quarterly review met.
[07.03 §4](07.03-the-rt-02-retention-failure.md) states what that means and this chapter does not restate
it. What belongs here is the narrower consequence for P8.1.

**`CNB-C-133` reviews registers, and a register only contains what somebody entered into it.** The
disclosure register held nothing about RT-02 because nothing was disclosed. The privacy case register held
nothing because nobody complained — the individuals concerned had no way to know, and
[07.03 §6](07.03-the-rt-02-retention-failure.md) is about precisely that. The privacy incident record
existed by 2026-10-28 only because the matter had already been found five days earlier by somebody doing
something else. **Had it not been found on 2026-10-23, the quarterly review of 2026-10-28 would have read
four clean registers and minuted a clean outcome**, and every word of `CNB-C-133` would have been satisfied.

That is not an argument that the control is defective, and this chapter is careful not to make it into one.
A review of registers is a review of registers; the criterion asks whether the entity monitors compliance
and addresses what it finds, and it addressed what it found within four days of finding it. But it is the
reason the review's answer was a **detective control at the job level** rather than a case disposition. The
monitoring layer could not see a nightly job reporting success, because nothing about a successful job
reaches a register a Data Protection Officer reads. **Monitoring that only reads the registers of things
that went wrong cannot see the class of failure whose signature is that nothing went wrong.**

## 6. What this chapter does not say

**It offers no view on whether the 12 routed enquiries were answered well.** They were routed to the
employer, timed, and recorded. What the employer did with each is outside the system boundary, on the side
of it CUEC-07 and CUEC-08 describe, and is not evidence available to CloudNimbus or to the service auditor.

**It draws no conclusion about the adequacy of the channel from a clean register**, for the reason §3 gives
at length.

**And it states no rate over the observation window.** Fourteen enquiries in fifty-eight days of a
184-day window is a count. At **2026-11-27** `CNB-C-122` has **two** monthly occurrences still to come —
November's, which falls after this vantage, and December's — and **`CNB-C-133` has none**: Q4's was held on
2026-10-28 and the next quarterly review falls in Q1 2027, outside the window. The observation window's
population is six for the first and two for the second, and both of `CNB-C-133`'s are behind.
The service auditor will compute rates in January over populations that do not exist yet, and this chapter
declines to compute them early in the direction that flatters — the discipline ADR-0015 fixed for forecasts
and 06.12 §7 applied to the quarter.
