## 1. Purpose, and why this document is not a formality

Phase 01 selected the Privacy category on a single factual claim: that CloudNimbus collects personal
information directly from the data subject through its own interface, and therefore cannot delegate the
whole of a notice-and-choice position to the employer. That claim was made in 01.05 and recorded in
ADR-0002. It was a claim about the data, and until the data had been inventoried it was an assertion.

This document is where the assertion is either evidenced or withdrawn. It enumerates every category of
personal information the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform holds, states for each where the information comes
from, and produces the arithmetic that either supports the Privacy decision or does not. It then sets the
retention period for each category, and states — before anyone else has to find it — the one place where
two retention rules in this programme genuinely conflict.

It is written from the programme's position as at 2026-03-31, and anticipates no risk rating, control
determination or examination outcome. The full privacy control design — notice architecture, lawful-basis
analysis, sub-processor disclosure mechanics — is Phase 07's subject. What Phase 02 owes Phase 07 is an
accurate inventory and honest retention positions to design against.

## 2. The twelve categories

Personal information is inventoried by **category**, not by field. A field-level register for 63
microservices would be obsolete within a sprint and would tell a reader nothing about the programme's
obligations. A category-level register is stable across schema changes, maps to a retention rule, and
reconciles to the data flows in 02.04.

| ID | Category | Collected from | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| PD-01 | Name and employee identifier | Employer | Identity within the tenant |
| PD-02 | Work email address and telephone number | Employer | Notification and account recovery |
| PD-03 | Employment terms — role, department, FTE, pay rate, cost centre | Employer | Inputs to the calculation engine |
| PD-04 | Time records — clock-in, clock-out, breaks, shift assignment | **The individual** | The core service |
| PD-05 | **Geolocation captured at clock-in** | **The individual** | Clock-in verification, where the employer enables it |
| PD-06 | Device identifier and application telemetry | **The individual** | Session integrity and anomaly signals |
| PD-07 | Absence and leave records, including leave type | **The individual and the employer** | Accrual and entitlement |
| PD-08 | Expense records and receipt images | **The individual** | Reimbursement |
| PD-09 | Bank account reference — last four digits only, held as a token issued by the payroll provider | Employer's payroll provider | Routing reference on the export |
| PD-10 | Manager approval and comment records | **The individual** (as manager) | Authorisation trail |
| PD-11 | Support interaction records | **The individual** | Support |
| PD-12 | Authentication metadata — IP address, timestamps, MFA events | **The individual** | Security and audit |

The data subjects behind these twelve categories are the approximately **1.24 million employees** of
CloudNimbus's 640 customers. They are not CloudNimbus's customers, they did not select CloudNimbus as a
supplier, and they have no contract with it. They are INT-04 in the interested-party register at 02.09 and
SH-22 in the stakeholder register at 01.10, and both entries record the same uncomfortable fact: the
strongest interest in the table, held by the parties with no channel.

## 3. The arithmetic that decides the Privacy category

Sorting the twelve categories by source produces the number this chapter exists to produce.

**Seven categories are collected directly from the individual** — PD-04, PD-05, PD-06, PD-08, PD-10,
PD-11 and PD-12. **Four come from the employer** — PD-01, PD-02, PD-03 and PD-09, the last of these
arriving through the employer's payroll provider acting on the employer's instruction rather than
CloudNimbus's. **One comes from both** — PD-07, where the individual requests leave and the employer
approves it and maintains the entitlement balance behind it. **7 + 4 + 1 = 12.**

That is the evidential basis for ADR-0002, and it is worth being precise about what it does and does not
establish. It does not establish that CloudNimbus is a controller; the data processing addendum says what
it says and the roles it assigns are a legal matter for counsel, not for this document. What it establishes
is narrower and sufficient: that for seven of twelve categories there is **no intermediary in the moment of
collection**. The individual is looking at a CloudNimbus screen on a device CloudNimbus's application runs
on, and CloudNimbus is reading a value from that device in that instant. An entity in that position has
privacy practices of its own — retention periods, sub-processors, regions, deletion behaviour — that the
employer cannot describe because they are not the employer's. That is what P1.1 addresses and it is why the
category was kept.

Had the sorting come out the other way — had all twelve arrived from the employer's HR system through an
integration — the orthodox processor position set out in 01.05 would have been the right one, and this
programme would have taken Confidentiality and stopped. The count is the reason it did not.

## 4. Geolocation (PD-05): the trigger that turned out not to be universal

Geolocation at clock-in is the collection that carries the Privacy argument in Phase 01, so the first thing
the team did with the inventory was test whether it was universal. It is not.

**Geolocation is enabled by 387 of the 640 customers. For the remaining 253, no location value is captured
at all** — the mobile application does not request the permission, the field is not populated, and there is
nothing to retain. Roughly two in five tenants therefore run the platform with the collection that decided
the category switched off.

That finding was material enough to reopen the question, and it was reopened. If the direct-collection
trigger applied to only 387 tenants, was the Privacy category resting on a configuration flag? The answer
is no, and the reason is the rest of the count. **Six further categories are collected directly from the
individual through CloudNimbus's own interface irrespective of any customer configuration** — PD-04,
PD-06, PD-08, PD-10, PD-11 and PD-12. Three of those six arise on every single authenticated session
without exception and without any tenant setting: the time record itself (PD-04), the device identifier and
telemetry (PD-06), and the authentication metadata (PD-12). A worker at one of the 253 tenants that never
sees a location value still hands CloudNimbus a clock-in, a device fingerprint and an authentication trail
directly, every shift. The direct-collection position survives the removal of geolocation entirely, which
is precisely why the team was willing to test it.

**Removing geolocation from the platform was argued for and refused** (DEC-205, ADR-0009). The argument for
removal was clean: it is the most intrusive category in the inventory, it is the one an end user would
object to first, and deleting the feature would have removed the objection permanently. The argument that
defeated it is that 387 customers turned it on deliberately, and they did so to evidence compliance with
their own labour, premises and safety rules — obligations that belong to the employer and that CloudNimbus
cannot discharge on their behalf by deleting the evidence. Removing the capability would not have removed
the employers' need for it; it would have moved that need to a supplier who documents it less carefully.

What the refusal bought had to be paid for somewhere, and it was paid for in retention. **PD-05 is retained
for 13 months and then deleted, irrespective of contract term** (RT-02, DEC-207) — the only category in the
inventory whose retention is shorter than the commercial relationship that generates it. Every other
customer-data category runs to contract term plus 90 days. Geolocation does not, because a location point
from three years ago serves no purpose CloudNimbus can articulate and is a liability CloudNimbus would then
have to defend.

## 5. Leave type (PD-07): stating the exposure without reaching a conclusion

Absence and leave records are recorded against a **controlled list** of leave types rather than as free
text, and one value on that controlled list is **"Medical / sick"**.

The exposure is plain and this document will state it plainly: a leave type of that kind is **capable of
implying something about a person's health**. A record showing that a named individual took leave of that
type on given dates carries an inference that a record showing "Annual leave" does not.

Three facts bound the exposure, and the first is a design decision rather than an accident of the schema.
**CloudNimbus stores the type only.** There is no reason field, no diagnosis field, no condition code, no
free-text note on the leave record, and no facility to attach a medical certificate, fit-note or any other
document to it. Customers who need to hold such material hold it in their own HR system, where the
employment relationship lives. Second, the list is controlled: values are configured per tenant from a fixed
set and cannot be extended into a description. Third, the field carries **elevated handling** — narrower
access within the support console under DF-12, restricted inclusion in exports and reports, and separate
treatment in the access model Phase 05 will design. Recorded as **DEC-206**, taken 2026-03-06.

What this document deliberately does **not** do is characterise the field in the language of any particular
legal regime. Whether a leave-type value constitutes a special category, sensitive personal information, or
any other statutorily defined class under any named statute is a legal determination that belongs to
counsel on the facts of a given jurisdiction and a given customer. Neither a SOC 2 examination report nor an
ISO/IEC 27001 certificate is a determination of compliance with any law, and a scoping document that
reached such a conclusion would be asserting something no assurance product behind it supports. The
inventory records the field, the exposure and the handling. **The full treatment — notice, lawful basis,
data subject request handling and the disclosure position — is deferred to Phase 07.**

## 6. Retention

Eight rules govern retention across the estate. What they do **not** do is give each of the twelve
categories a rule of its own, and the honest statement of the position is made here rather than left for a
reader to derive by mapping the two tables against each other.

| ID | Data | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| RT-01 | Time and attendance records | Contract term plus 90 days |
| RT-02 | **Geolocation at clock-in (PD-05)** | **13 months**, then deleted, irrespective of contract term |
| RT-03 | Expense records and receipt images | Contract term plus 90 days |
| RT-04 | Absence and leave records | Contract term plus 90 days |
| RT-05 | Authentication and audit logs | 13 months in the queryable store, then 7 years in the `cnb-security` archive in pseudonymised form |
| RT-06 | Support interaction records | 24 months |
| RT-07 | Backups and snapshots | 35 days, rolling |
| RT-08 | All customer data after termination | Deleted within 30 days of termination, with a certificate issued (obligation O7) |

Enforcement is by scheduled deletion job, which is **SR-01's sibling requirement SR-08** in 02.12:
retention enforced by scheduled deletion jobs *whose failure is alerted*. The alerting half of that sentence
is assumption **AS-01** carried out of Phase 01, and it is carried as an assumption rather than a fact for
the reason Phase 01 gave: a programme that writes its assumptions down can be shown to have been wrong
about them.

### 6.1 What the eight rules actually cover, and what they do not

Mapping the rules back onto the twelve categories gives a less tidy answer than "eight rules cover twelve
categories", and the less tidy answer is the true one.

**Six rules govern a specific category.** RT-01 governs **PD-04**, time records. RT-02 governs **PD-05**,
geolocation. RT-03 governs **PD-08**, expense records and receipt images. RT-04 governs **PD-07**, absence
and leave records. RT-05 governs **PD-12**, authentication metadata. RT-06 governs **PD-11**, support
interaction records. **PD-10**, manager approval and comment records, is held with the records it approves
and expires with them under RT-01 and RT-04, which is why it is not counted as uncovered.

**Two rules govern the estate rather than a category.** RT-07 is a property of the backup cycle and applies
to whatever the snapshot held. RT-08 is the termination rule and applies to everything.

**Five categories have no in-life rule at all: PD-01, PD-02, PD-03, PD-06 and PD-09.** Name and employee
identifier, work email and telephone number, employment terms including pay rate, device identifier and
application telemetry, and the payroll routing token. Each of them is governed by the contract-term default
and by RT-08 alone — which is to say it is held for as long as the customer relationship lasts and is
deleted within 30 days of termination, and nothing shorter than that ever applies to it. There is no rule
that expires a leaver's record while their employer remains a customer, and no rule that ages out telemetry
that has stopped being useful.

**PD-03 is the one that most deserves a rule of its own**, and this document is not going to bury that in a
list. It is the most sensitive category of the five by a distance: role, department, FTE, cost centre and
**pay rate**, for every employee of every customer, held for the life of a commercial relationship that may
run for many years and covering individuals who left those employers long ago. The other four are
identifiers, contact details, device telemetry and a routing token. PD-03 is what somebody earns. A
retention position that treats an individual's pay rate the same way it treats their work telephone number
has not thought about the category; it has inherited a default. The obvious candidates — expiry of
historical rate records a stated period after they cease to be current, and expiry of the whole record a
stated period after an individual ceases to be active in the tenant — are both designable, and neither can
be designed here, because retention shortened below the contract term has to be reconciled against the
customers' own record-keeping obligations and against what the calculation engine needs to reproduce a
prior period under SR-06.

**This is recorded as an open gap and carried to Phase 07**, where the privacy control design is done and
where the retention schedule is reconciled against the notice and the lawful-basis analysis. Phase 02's job
is to make it visible rather than to close it, and the visible version is: **six of the eight rules govern a
named category, two govern the estate, and five of the twelve categories are governed by the contract-term
default and RT-08 alone.**

### 6.2 RT-07 and RT-08 conflict, and here is the number

Read them together. RT-08 commits CloudNimbus to delete all customer data **within 30 days of
termination**, and to certify that it has done so. RT-07 holds backups and snapshots on a **35-day rolling
cycle**. A record deleted from the live stores on day 30 therefore still exists inside every encrypted
backup taken up to and including that day, and the last of those is the backup taken **on day 30 itself**,
which captured the record moments before the deletion ran. That backup is not expired until 35 days after
it was taken.

**30 + 35 = 65 days.** Between the certificate and day 65 there is a residue of terminated-customer data
inside CloudNimbus's backup estate, and the deletion certificate is issued in the middle of that window.
The worked example has to use the day-30 backup rather than any earlier one, because it is the last
surviving copy that determines when the residue clears, and an illustration built on the day-29 backup
would give 64 and understate the window by a day. A commitment stated to the day is worth stating
correctly.

This is not a defect that better engineering removes. It is the unavoidable arithmetic of holding backups
at all: a backup that could be surgically edited to remove one tenant's rows would not be a backup but a
mutable copy, and the recovery-point objective in SR-10 depends on it being neither. Every service
organisation offering both a deletion commitment and a recovery objective has this window. Most do not
mention it.

The honest treatment has four parts, all stated on the record rather than held for a question:

1. **The deletion certificate states what it covers and what it does not.** It certifies deletion from the
   live production stores enumerated by the deletion orchestrator (DF-11, SR-07). It states the backup
   residue, states the maximum period, and states the date by which the residue expires. A certificate that
   implies more than has happened is worse than no certificate, because it converts an engineering fact
   into a false representation.
2. **The residue is protected as production data is protected.** Backups are encrypted at rest under
   region-scoped customer-managed keys (SR-03), held in the same residency region as the source data
   (SR-02), and access to restore them is restricted to the infrastructure function.
3. **The residue expires without intervention.** The 35-day cycle is self-clearing. No person has to
   remember to complete anything, which matters because the failure mode of manual deletion follow-ups is
   that they are forgotten by whoever owned them once the customer has gone.
4. **Restoration from a snapshot predating a deletion triggers a documented re-deletion procedure.** If a
   snapshot from before day 30 is restored for any reason, the deletion is re-executed against the restored
   estate and the re-execution is recorded. This is the part that actually requires discipline, and it is
   named here so that Phase 06 designs it rather than discovers the need for it.

A programme that conceals this window has a deletion certificate worth nothing, because the first
sophisticated reader to ask "and your backups?" discovers both the residue and the concealment at the same
moment. The second discovery is the expensive one.

## 7. What Phase 02 fixes here, and what it does not

Phase 02 fixes the inventory, the sources, the retention rules and the two positions that required an
argument — geolocation and leave type. It designs no privacy control, states no lawful basis, drafts no
notice and rates no risk. Those are Phase 03, Phase 05 and Phase 07 respectively.

One structural limitation is recorded rather than left implicit. This inventory covers personal information
within the **SOC 2 system** — the platform. Personal information CloudNimbus holds as the employer of 187
people, and personal information in its corporate systems, sits inside the **ISMS** boundary and outside
this table, for the reason 02.01 gives at length: the two boundaries are deliberately different and neither
contains the other. A reader who takes this document as CloudNimbus's complete personal-data map has taken
it for something it does not claim to be.
