## 1. DC2, and the distinction that makes it useful

**DC2** of DC section 200 requires the description of the system to disclose the **principal service
commitments** and the **system requirements**. It names two things because they are two things, and a
description that treats them as one produces prose that reads persuasively and cannot be tested.

**A commitment is what the entity promised somebody else.** It exists in an executed contract, is owed to a
named counterparty, and has a remedy behind it. It is a fact about the commercial relationship.

**A system requirement is what the system must do for the entity to keep that promise.** It is a fact about
the system. It is stated in terms specific enough that an engineer could build against it and an auditor
could test whether it holds.

The distinction matters because of where the criteria bite. A trust services criterion is evaluated against
**what the system must do** — against controls that address the requirement — not against what the sales
contract says. "We are committed to protecting our customers' data" is a sentence with no testable content;
"encryption at rest under region-scoped customer-managed keys, TLS 1.2 or above in transit" is a
requirement that either holds on a given day or does not. When the two are conflated, what results is a
description whose commitments are eloquent and whose requirements are absent, and an examination that has
nothing to attach itself to.

The ownership split reflects the distinction. Commitments are owned by the General Counsel because they
come out of contracts; the requirements are approved by the Chief Technology Officer because they are
statements about a system he is accountable for building.

## 2. Principal service commitments

Every commitment below is drawn from an **executed agreement** — the customer master services agreement,
the data processing addendum, or the EU-residency addendum — and each traces to an obligation already
registered as O1 to O12 in 01.11 or to the MSA directly. Nothing on this list is an aspiration, a roadmap
item or a marketing statement.

The split between those two sources is worth stating rather than leaving to be counted. **Six of the ten
trace to a registered obligation** — SC-02 to O4, SC-03 to O7, SC-04 to O8, SC-08 to O5, SC-09 to O6, and
SC-10 to O1, O2 and O3 together. **The other four — SC-01, SC-05, SC-06 and SC-07 — are sourced from the
customer MSA directly**, and are none the weaker for it: an obligation register is a register of the
obligations somebody thought to register, and the availability, encryption, authentication and calculation
commitments are the four a customer is most likely to raise. That they were not in the O-series is a fact
about the register's history, not about the commitments, and Phase 07 should reconcile the two rather than
leave a reader to notice that four principal commitments have no O-number.

| ID | Service commitment | Source |
|---|---|---|
| SC-01 | 99.9% monthly availability of the platform, **excluding maintenance windows announced at least fourteen days in advance** | Customer MSA |
| SC-02 | Notification of a security incident affecting customer data within 48 hours of determination | O4 |
| SC-03 | Deletion or return of customer data within 30 days of termination, with certification | O7 |
| SC-04 | Personal data of EU-residency customers held at rest in `eu-central-1` | O8 |
| SC-05 | Encryption of customer data in transit and at rest | Customer MSA |
| SC-06 | Availability of multi-factor authentication and single sign-on federation for tenant administrators | Customer MSA |
| SC-07 | Calculation output that is complete, accurate and reproducible against the configured rules | Customer MSA |
| SC-08 | Thirty days' notice of a new sub-processor, with a right to object | O5 |
| SC-09 | Assistance with a data subject request within 10 business days | O6 |
| SC-10 | Provision of the assurance report under NDA on request, and a bridge letter where asked | O1, O2, O3 |

**Four** of these are worth a note on how carefully they are worded, because the wording is the commitment.

**SC-01** carries an **exclusion**, and the exclusion is part of the commitment rather than a footnote to
it. The master services agreement measures the 99.9% against elapsed minutes in the calendar month less any
maintenance window announced at least fourteen days ahead. A commitment stated without its exclusion is a
commitment a reader will measure differently from the party who wrote it, and a description of the system
that carried the headline figure and omitted the carve-out would be incomplete under **DC2**, which asks
for the principal service commitments as they are. Version 1.1 of this document adds the exclusion, which
version 1.0 omitted; the correction was made on **2026-09-24** under **DEC-613**, when the first announced
maintenance window of the observation window was measured against it.

**SC-02** runs from **determination**, not from occurrence. The 48-hour clock starts when CloudNimbus
determines that an incident affecting customer data has occurred, and the determination is itself an act
with a control behind it. A commitment worded from occurrence would be one CloudNimbus could breach before
it knew anything had happened, which is not a commitment a competent counterparty should want either,
because it incentivises slow determination.

**SC-07** commits to output that is complete, accurate and reproducible **against the configured rules**.
That qualification is load-bearing and is the same boundary CUEC-04 and CUEC-05 sit on at 02.11:
CloudNimbus commits that the engine faithfully applies the rules the employer configured. It does not, and
could not, commit that the employer configured the rules it intended.

**SC-10** concerns a **restricted-use** report. A SOC 2 report is intended for management of the service
organisation, user entities during the period, and other specified parties with sufficient knowledge and
understanding of the matters it covers. It is not a general-use document, is not published, and the
commitment is to provide it under non-disclosure on request. A bridge letter, where one is asked for, is a
management-issued and unaudited statement covering the gap between the report period end and the customer's
own reporting date; it carries no auditor opinion, and Phase 09 will say so on its face.

## 3. System requirements

| ID | System requirement | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| SR-01 | Multi-tenant isolation enforced at the data layer on every access path | SC-05, SC-07 |
| SR-02 | Region pinning of personal data at rest by tenant residency configuration | SC-04 |
| SR-03 | Encryption at rest under region-scoped customer-managed keys; TLS 1.2 or above in transit | SC-05 |
| SR-04 | Authentication of every end user through the CIAM platform; MFA enforced for tenant administrators | SC-06 |
| SR-05 | Authorisation evaluated per request against tenant-scoped roles | SC-05 |
| SR-06 | Calculation runs reproducible from inputs and rule version, with a reconciliation record retained | SC-07 |
| SR-07 | Deletion orchestrated across every store holding tenant data, with a machine-verifiable completion record | SC-03 |
| SR-08 | Retention enforced by scheduled deletion jobs whose failure is alerted | SC-03 |
| SR-09 | Audit logging of security-relevant events shipped to an append-only archive in a separate account | SC-02 |
| SR-10 | Availability architecture supporting a 4-hour recovery time objective and a 15-minute recovery point objective | SC-01 |
| SR-11 | Change promotion through an automated pipeline with recorded peer review | SC-01, SC-07 |
| SR-12 | Non-production environments hold no production customer data | SC-05 |

The mapping is deliberately not one-to-one in either direction, and both irregularities carry information.

**Some commitments are served by several requirements.** SC-05, encryption of customer data, is served by
SR-01, SR-03, SR-05 and SR-12, because "protect the data" decomposes into isolating it, encrypting it,
authorising each access to it, and not copying it into places where none of that applies. A description
that answered SC-05 with SR-03 alone would have described the cryptography and left the isolation
unstated — and in a multi-tenant platform, isolation is the control that actually protects confidentiality.

**Some requirements serve no commitment directly, and they are still requirements.** SR-09, audit logging
to an append-only archive in a separate account, is mapped to SC-02 because incident notification depends
on being able to determine what happened. But its real function is evidential: it is the requirement that
makes every other requirement demonstrable. A system requirement whose purpose is to make the system
auditable is a legitimate requirement, and one that gets dropped first when a description is written by
working backwards from the contract.

**SR-04 spans a boundary CloudNimbus does not control.** Authentication of every end user is performed by
Halcyon Identity, carved out at 02.10, and the requirement is met by CloudNimbus's controls in combination
with CSOC-12 to CSOC-14. That is stated here rather than left to be inferred, because a requirement that
depends on another organisation is exactly the kind of thing a description is supposed to disclose.

## 4. Three requirements that were assumptions first

Phase 01 closed with two load-bearing assumptions recorded rather than asserted, and Phase 02's scoping
work rests one boundary decision on a third statement of the same kind. All three now appear in the table
above as system requirements, and that transformation is the point of this section.

**AS-02 → SR-01.** The assumption was that row-level security is uniformly enforced across every data
access path. As a requirement it reads: multi-tenant isolation enforced at the data layer on **every**
access path. The word "every" is doing the work, and the access path where it is hardest to satisfy is
DF-06, the reporting query from the reporting API to the analytics replica, because that is the one place
in the platform where a query composes across tenants at the query layer and relies on a predicate to
constrain the result. Phase 02 states the requirement and states where it is hardest. It states no result;
penetration testing is scheduled for May 2026 and this phase does not know its outcome.

**AS-01 → SR-08.** The assumption was that scheduled-job failures are alerted. As a requirement it reads:
retention enforced by scheduled deletion jobs **whose failure is alerted**. The second clause is the whole
requirement. A deletion job that runs is worth nothing if a job that silently stops running produces the
same observable state — an absence of errors — as a job that succeeded. SC-03 and the retention rules at
02.07 §6 depend entirely on the failure being noisy.

**ADR-0007 → SR-12.** The exclusion of `cnb-staging` and `cnb-dev` from the SOC 2 system rests on SR-12:
non-production environments hold no production customer data. 02.03 makes the point in its own terms and it
bears repeating here in requirement language — **the exclusion is not a fact about the architecture, it is
a claim about a control.** Nothing about the shape of an AWS account makes it incapable of holding
production data. If SR-12 fails, the boundary was wrong from the day it was drawn, and it was wrong
retrospectively rather than from the moment of failure.

An assumption that becomes a system requirement is an assumption that has been made **testable**, which is
the only useful thing to do with one. An assumption left in an assumption log is a note about something
somebody once believed. A system requirement is a statement the examination will attach controls to, and
Phase 04 will design those controls against these twelve statements rather than against the ten
commitments.

## 5. What is not on either list

Two exclusions are deliberate.

**Marketing statements are not commitments.** Claims made on the website, in a security overview, or in a
sales conversation do not appear here unless they are also terms of an executed agreement. This is not
pedantry about paperwork: a description that elevates a marketing claim to a principal service commitment
creates an obligation the company did not undertake and then has to be examined against it. Where a
marketing statement is materially stronger than the corresponding contractual commitment, that is a matter
for the General Counsel to correct at source, not for this document to ratify.

**Internal targets are not commitments.** The programme carries objectives — response times, review
cadences, remediation windows — and none of them is owed to a customer. They are managed as objectives and
appear in Phase 09's reporting, not in DC2.

The commitments and requirements are reviewed whenever the MSA template or an addendum changes, at each
management review under clause 9.3, and before the description is finalised for the examination. A new
commitment that arrives through a negotiated customer amendment — the most likely source — must be
reconciled against the requirements here before it is signed, because a commitment with no requirement
behind it is a promise nobody has worked out how to keep.
