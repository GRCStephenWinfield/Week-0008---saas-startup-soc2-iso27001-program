## 1. What clause 4.2 requires, and what it does not

Clause 4.2 of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requires the organisation to determine three things: **the interested
parties that are relevant to the information security management system**, **the relevant requirements of
those interested parties**, and — a limb the 2022 edition made explicit — **which of those requirements
will be addressed through the ISMS**.

The third limb is the one that changes the character of the clause. A register that lists parties and
their wishes is a stakeholder map. Clause 4.2 asks the organisation to state which of those requirements
the management system is actually going to carry, which means some requirements are recorded and not
addressed, and the organisation has to be willing to say so. A register in which every party's every
requirement is addressed through the ISMS is a register that has not been thought about.

Clause 4.2 is also not the same instrument as the stakeholder register at 01.10. That register was built
for **engagement** — who is told what, how often, by whom — and it rates parties on interest and influence.
This register is built for **conformity**: it feeds the scope determination at 02.08 under clause 4.3 b),
and it feeds the risk assessment in Phase 03. The two overlap heavily and answer different questions, and
the entries that appear in both — most obviously the end users, SH-22 there and INT-04 here — appear for
different reasons.

Three of these need a word about why they are in the register at all.

**INT-09, ANAB**, has no requirement of CloudNimbus and never will. It accredits Northgate; its rules
constrain how Northgate conducts the audit, what it may not do alongside it, and what its certificate is
worth. It is recorded because a reader who finds Northgate in the register and not ANAB may conclude
CloudNimbus does not know why accreditation matters. The requirement column says what the relationship is:
indirect.

**INT-11, the subservice organisations**, are in the register as parties with requirements *of CloudNimbus*
rather than only as suppliers to it. AWS and Halcyon Identity require correct use of their services under
the shared responsibility model — acceptable use, correct configuration, appropriate credential handling.
That direction of obligation is easy to forget and is exactly the direction in which CloudNimbus can fail.
It is also one of the two entries section 6 records as **not addressed through the ISMS**, for the reason
given there: a requirement running from a supplier to CloudNimbus is discharged through contract and
conduct, not through a management system process.

**INT-14, the cyber liability insurer**, requires that the control representations made at renewal remain
true. It is in the register because those representations are made by CloudNimbus about CloudNimbus's own
controls, and a control that quietly stops operating is a representation that has quietly become inaccurate.

## 3. Clause 4.1, and the climate determination the amendment requires

Clause 4.1 requires the organisation to determine external and internal issues relevant to its purpose and
affecting its ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the ISMS. **ISO/IEC 27001:2022/Amd 1:2024** adds
to 4.1 the requirement to **determine whether climate change is a relevant issue**, and adds a note to 4.2
that relevant interested parties can have requirements related to climate change. The amendment is in force
and is audited.

What the amendment requires is a **determination**, not a conclusion in a particular direction. An
organisation may properly determine that climate change is not a relevant issue for its ISMS, provided it
has actually determined it and can say on what basis. What it may not do is leave the question unasked, and
the failure mode in the market is not a wrong answer — it is a single unreasoned sentence copied from a
template, asserting relevance or irrelevance with no visible thought behind it. A Stage 1 auditor reads
clause 4 documentation before anything else, and an unreasoned sentence in the first clause examined sets
the tone for everything after it.

CloudNimbus determined on 2026-03-17 (**DEC-209**) that climate change **is a relevant issue, in two
specific and limited senses**, and that it is **not** relevant in a third.

**It is relevant to availability.** The platform runs entirely in three AWS regions. Those regions are
physical facilities in physical places, and their availability is subject to physical conditions —
including extreme weather events and the grid and cooling behaviour that accompanies them. CloudNimbus does
not operate those facilities and cannot control that exposure; what it can do is treat regional
unavailability as a continuity input rather than as an unthinkable event. That is an input to the business
continuity and disaster recovery work in Phase 06, where the 4-hour recovery time objective and the
15-minute recovery point objective in SR-10 are designed, and to the region strategy in 02.03. It has one
immediate consequence this phase records rather than defers: because the `eu-central-1` estate is
single-region by force of obligation O8, the loss of that region is an **accepted residual** with no
recovery path on SR-10's objectives, stated as such at 02.03 §4 and carried to Phase 06 for treatment. A
determination that regional disruption is a real event and an architecture described as recovering from it
in four hours cannot both stand, and this phase declines to write both. The honest
statement of the issue is therefore narrow and specific: climate change is a factor in the likelihood of
regional-scale disruption to a dependency CloudNimbus has no substitute for.

**It is relevant as an interested-party requirement.** **Nine enterprise-tier customers** ask about the
environmental profile of their vendors in procurement questionnaires — energy sourcing, regional footprint,
supplier environmental commitments. Those questions arrive through the same channel as the security
questionnaire and are answered by the same people. That is precisely the situation the amendment's note to
4.2 contemplates: a requirement of a relevant interested party that happens to be climate-related. Nine of
118 is a minority and the register says so; it does not follow that the requirement is irrelevant, only
that it is bounded.

**It is not relevant to the confidentiality or integrity of information.** No pathway was identified by
which climate change affects whether tenant data stays segregated or whether a calculation produces the
right number. Determining that a thing is not relevant, and saying why, is a conforming answer to 4.1, and
it is a better answer than an expansive one that cannot be evidenced.

The reasoning is recorded here rather than the answer alone, because the answer alone is what the clause
does not ask for.

## 4. INT-04 — the party with the strongest interest and no channel

Approximately **1.24 million people** use the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform. They clock in on it, submit
expenses through it, request leave in it, and are paid on the basis of what its calculation engine derives.
They are the parties whose interest in CloudNimbus's information security is by some distance the strongest
in the register: a cross-tenant disclosure exposes their compensation, a calculation defect changes what
lands in their bank account, and a location record captured at clock-in is a record of where they
physically were.

**They have no contractual channel to CloudNimbus at all.** They did not select CloudNimbus, cannot
negotiate with it, cannot terminate it, and in most cases could not name it. Their employer signed the
master services agreement; their employer configures the tenant; their employer decides whether geolocation
is captured. Every route from an end user to CloudNimbus runs through the employer, which is the same party
whose interests may not coincide with theirs on the questions that matter most. 01.10 rates the same
population, as SH-22, at high interest and **no** influence, and this document reaches the same place from
the conformity side.

This programme does not pretend to fix that, and no document in it will claim to give 1.24 million people a
voice they do not have. Three things it does instead are worth stating precisely, because they are what is
actually available.

It **kept the Privacy category**. The argument in 01.05 and ADR-0002 turned on direct collection — seven of
twelve personal data categories are collected from the individual through CloudNimbus's own interface, as
02.07 evidences — and the practical consequence is that CloudNimbus's own retention, deletion and
disclosure practices are examined by an independent CPA firm rather than argued about contractually. The
examination does not report to the end user; a restricted-use report reaches management, user entities
during the period and other specified parties with sufficient knowledge and understanding, and the end
users are none of those. But the practices are examined, and an examined practice is a different thing from
an asserted one.

It **cut geolocation retention to 13 months** (RT-02, DEC-207), shorter than the contract term that governs
every other category, because the party that benefits from that reduction has nobody to ask for it.

And it **records the asymmetry in both registers** rather than in neither. INT-04's requirement column
carries the phrase "no contractual channel to CloudNimbus" because a register that recorded the requirement
and omitted the absence of a channel would flatter the position. The requirement is addressed through the
ISMS — clause 4.2 c) — but it is addressed on the end users' behalf and without their participation, and
that distinction should survive into every phase that touches it.

## 5. INT-13 — recording the party without reaching a legal conclusion

Supervisory authorities and regulators are a relevant interested party. CloudNimbus processes personal data
belonging to individuals in multiple US states and in four countries, holds EU-resident data in
`eu-central-1` for 41 customers, and operates in a sector where employment and wage rules apply to its
customers and are evidenced partly by its outputs.

The requirement is recorded at the level of category: **compliance with applicable law**. Which laws apply,
in which jurisdictions, on which facts, and whether CloudNimbus meets them, is determined by CloudNimbus's
General Counsel on legal advice. It is not determined by this programme, and neither deliverable this
programme produces is a determination of compliance with any law. A SOC 2 examination reports on whether
controls were suitably designed and operated to meet criteria stated by management. An ISO/IEC 27001
certificate attests conformity of a management system with the requirements of clauses 4 to 10. Neither is
an opinion on statutory compliance, and a scoping document that asserted CloudNimbus was or was not
compliant with a named statute would be making a claim that nothing behind it supports.

What the ISMS does carry is the mechanism: legal and regulatory requirements are identified and maintained
as an input to the ISMS, Tobias Lund owns that identification, and where a requirement produces a control
obligation it enters the control library in Phase 04 like any other. Where a requirement produces a legal
question, it goes to counsel. The register records the party and the category; it does not record a verdict.

## 6. How the register is used, and how it is maintained

Clause 4.2 c) requires the organisation to state **which** requirements will be addressed through the ISMS,
and section 1 of this document says that a register in which every party's every requirement is addressed
through the ISMS is a register that has not been thought about. This section has to be consistent with
that, so it names the ones that are not.

**Seven of the fourteen resolve into obligations already registered as O1 to O12 in 01.11, into service
commitments SC-01 to SC-10 at 02.12, or into both.** They are **INT-01** (O1, and SC-10), **INT-02** (O2 and
O3, and SC-10), **INT-03** (O8, and SC-04), **INT-06** (O9), **INT-07** (O12), **INT-08** (O10 and O11) and
**INT-10** (O5, and SC-08). For those seven the ISMS carries the requirement in the strongest available
sense: it is written down as an obligation with a counterparty and a due period, it will acquire controls
in Phase 04, and its non-performance is observable.

**Two are recorded and are not addressed through the ISMS, and saying so is the point of limb c).**

**INT-13**, supervisory authorities and regulators, has its requirement recorded at the level of category —
compliance with applicable law — and the ISMS does not address it. It cannot. Whether CloudNimbus complies
with a named statute in a named jurisdiction is a legal determination made by the General Counsel on legal
advice, and neither deliverable this programme produces is a determination of compliance with any law. What
the ISMS carries is the **mechanism** described in section 5: legal and regulatory requirements are
identified and maintained as an input, Tobias Lund owns that identification, and where a requirement
produces a control obligation the control enters the library like any other. Carrying a mechanism for
identifying a requirement is not the same as addressing the requirement, and a register that recorded
INT-13 as "addressed" would be claiming the ISMS delivers an outcome no management system can deliver.

**INT-11**, the two subservice organisations, hold a requirement *of* CloudNimbus — correct use of their
services under the shared responsibility model and their acceptable use terms — and that requirement runs
in the opposite direction to every other entry in the table. It is discharged through CloudNimbus's
contracts and its own operational conduct, and it is enforced, if it is ever enforced, by the suppliers'
remedies rather than by anything inside the ISMS. Some of CloudNimbus's configuration controls will happen
to satisfy it, but coincidence is not carriage: no ISMS process exists whose purpose is to meet AWS's
requirements of CloudNimbus, and the register should not imply one. It is recorded because it is a real
requirement of a relevant interested party, and because the direction of obligation it runs in is exactly
the direction a supplier register tends to forget.

**The remaining five sit between those two positions** and are described as they are rather than forced
into one of them. **INT-09**, ANAB, has no requirement of CloudNimbus at all; there is nothing to address,
and the accreditation constrains Northgate's conduct rather than CloudNimbus's. **INT-04**'s requirement is
addressed on the parties' behalf and without their participation, for the reasons in section 4. **INT-05**,
**INT-12** and **INT-14** resolve into no obligation and no service commitment — workable security
expectations for employees, well-formed exports for payroll providers, and continued accuracy of the
control representations made to the insurer — and are addressed through the ISMS's ordinary operation: the
policy suite, the interfaces the integration tier maintains, and the control set the representations were
made about. They are the entries most likely to be forgotten precisely because nothing contractual falls
over when they are.

**7 + 2 + 5 = 14.**

The register is maintained by the Compliance Manager, reviewed at the monthly Trust Committee when a party
or a requirement changes, and reviewed formally at each management review under clause 9.3. It is an input
to the Phase 03 risk assessment, where a requirement that the ISMS is committed to addressing and does not
have a control behind it becomes a risk rather than an omission. The additions most likely to arise are a
new regulator following geographic expansion, and a new subservice organisation — the second of which has
already happened once during this phase, and is the subject of 02.10.
