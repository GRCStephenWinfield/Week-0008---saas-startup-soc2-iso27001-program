## 1. What clause 4.3 actually asks for

Clause 4.3 of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requires the organisation to determine the boundaries and applicability of
the information security management system to establish its scope, and in doing so to consider three
things: **a)** the external and internal issues referred to in 4.1, **b)** the requirements referred to in
4.2, and **c)** the interfaces and dependencies between activities performed by the organisation and those
performed by other organisations. It then requires that the scope be **available as documented
information**.

Three features of that requirement are routinely misread and are worth stating before the statement itself.

First, the scope is **determined by the organisation**, not proposed by the certification body and not
recommended by an advisor. Northgate Certification Services will audit whether the ISMS CloudNimbus has
scoped conforms to the standard; it will not tell CloudNimbus what to scope, and under ISO/IEC 17021-1 it
could not, because that would be management system consultancy on the system it certifies. The scope is
CloudNimbus's assertion and CloudNimbus's exposure.

Second, limb **c)** is the one that carries the intellectual weight, and it is the limb most often reduced
to a sentence about suppliers. Interfaces and dependencies with other organisations are precisely where an
ISMS boundary becomes difficult, because they are the points at which the organisation's control ends and
somebody else's begins. For CloudNimbus these points have names: Amazon Web Services and Halcyon Identity,
Inc.

Third, conformity is assessed against **clauses 4 to 10**. Annex A is a reference set of controls, not the
requirements, and no amount of Annex A implementation cures a scope determined without regard to 4.1, 4.2
and the interfaces in 4.3 c). This document is a clause 4 document, and its adequacy is judged as one.

The scope was fixed in Phase 01 as an architectural decision — **ADR-0003**, the ISMS scope is the whole
organisation, not platform engineering — and its **wording** was fixed here on 2026-03-20 as **DEC-210**,
at milestone MS-03. The gap between those two dates is the work recorded in the three sections that follow.

## 2. Limb a) — the external and internal issues determined under 4.1

The 4.1 determination is set out in full at 02.09 §3, including the climate-change determination the 2024
amendment requires. What limb a) demands here is the connection: which of those issues bear on where the
boundary is drawn.

Three did. **CloudNimbus is remote-first**, with 187 staff across 31 US states and four countries and a
single leased Denver suite of roughly 4,100 square feet that contains no production equipment. An ISMS
scoped by location would have been scoped around a meeting room. **The commercial purpose of the
certificate** is answering enterprise security questionnaires — questions that arrive from procurement
functions asking about the whole supplier, not about one engineering department. And **the work itself is
not confined to engineering**: Customer Success staff open tenant-scoped support sessions under DF-12,
Sales staff handle customer contact data, and Finance handles customer billing information. In a company of
this size and shape the population that touches customer information is not a subset that can be drawn
neatly around a product team.

The internal issue that pushed hardest in the other direction was capacity. A 4.6-FTE programme scoping an
ISMS across 187 people, all locations and all information assets is taking on more than a narrow
certificate would require. That was argued, and it lost, for the reasons in section 5.

## 3. Limb b) — the requirements of interested parties determined under 4.2

The fourteen interested parties and their requirements are recorded at 02.09 as INT-01 to INT-14. Four of
them constrain the boundary directly.

**INT-01 and INT-02**, the 640 customers and the 118 on the enterprise tier, want an answer to a supplier
question, and a supplier question is about the supplier. A certificate scoped to "platform engineering"
answers a question nobody asked.

**INT-03**, the 41 EU-residency customers, generate obligation O8 and therefore a residency requirement
that reaches into infrastructure CloudNimbus does not own. That requirement is met inside `eu-central-1`,
which is AWS's estate, and it is therefore a limb b) requirement discharged across a limb c) interface.

**INT-05**, the 187 employees, are inside the boundary as subjects of it rather than as customers of it,
and the scope wording has to be true of them: home-working arrangements are named explicitly in the
statement because an ISMS that excluded the place most of its people work would be a fiction with a
certificate attached.

**INT-08**, the certification body, has a requirement of its own that bears on wording rather than on
extent: documented information sufficient for a Stage 1 readiness review. Stage 1 examines whether the
documented ISMS is capable of being audited at Stage 2, and the scope statement is among the first
documents read.

## 4. Limb c) — interfaces and dependencies with other organisations

This is where the ISMS boundary meets the boundary of the SOC 2 system, and where the two refuse to
coincide.

CloudNimbus runs entirely on **AWS** — seven accounts, four EKS clusters, six Aurora PostgreSQL clusters
and 214 S3 buckets across three regions — and **Halcyon Identity** authenticates every end user of the
platform. Both perform activities on which CloudNimbus's information security outcomes depend. Neither is
inside the ISMS, and neither can be.

The reason is definitional rather than convenient. A management system is a set of processes an
organisation **operates**: it sets policy, assigns roles, plans, resources, audits and improves. CloudNimbus
cannot audit AWS's data centre access controls under clause 9.2, cannot correct an AWS nonconformity under
clause 10, and cannot subject AWS's management to clause 5.1 leadership requirements. Declaring AWS inside
the ISMS boundary would assert a control relationship that does not exist, and it would be discovered by
the first Stage 2 auditor who asked to see the internal audit plan covering it.

What clause 4.3 c) requires instead is that the **interface** be determined, and it has been. The interface
with AWS is the shared responsibility model: AWS operates the infrastructure and its own controls, and
CloudNimbus operates everything it configures on top of them. The interface with Halcyon Identity is the
authentication of end users and the identity records behind it. Both interfaces are managed as supplier
relationships inside the ISMS — the parties sit in the vendor register, under a data processing addendum,
inside the quarterly sub-processor review CAL-08 — which is the correct place for them. **Annex A control
A.5.22**, monitoring, review and change management of supplier services, is the Annex A reference the
interface points at; whether it is determined necessary and how it is implemented is Phase 03's
Statement of Applicability work and Phase 04's control design, not this document's.

The same two organisations sit **inside** the SOC 2 system, carved out, with complementary subservice
organisation controls stated at 02.10. That is not an inconsistency between the two frameworks; it is the
frameworks measuring different things. A system description reaches through a supplier because the service
does. A management system stops where the organisation's control stops. 02.01 argues this at length and
ADR-0006 records it.

## 5. The scope statement

The following wording was fixed on 2026-03-20 (DEC-210) and is the wording that will be submitted for
Stage 1 and printed on the certificate.

> The information security management system supporting the design, development, operation, support and
> delivery of the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform — a multi-tenant software-as-a-service platform providing
> workforce time capture, scheduling, absence and leave management, expense capture and earnings
> calculation — together with all corporate functions of CloudNimbus, Inc., across all locations including
> home-working arrangements. In accordance with the Statement of Applicability.

Every clause of it is doing work.

**"Design, development, operation, support and delivery"** covers the lifecycle rather than a state. A
scope that says only "operation" invites the question of whether the code was developed inside the ISMS,
and secure development is exactly the kind of activity a reader assumes is covered until they read the
sentence carefully.

**The description of the platform** is there for the commercial reader, not the auditor. A procurement
officer reading a certificate should be able to tell that the certified organisation is the one that sells
the product they are buying, without cross-referencing a product catalogue.

**"Together with all corporate functions of CloudNimbus, Inc."** is the sentence that makes this a
whole-organisation scope. It is short, and it is the most expensive phrase in the document: it puts
Finance, Sales, Marketing, People and Legal inside the boundary, along with the corporate systems those
functions run on and the procedures that govern them.

It is worth being exact about which people that phrase adds, because the arithmetic is easy to get
backwards. It does **not** add the People, Legal and Finance staff to the certified population as though
they were otherwise outside the programme — under the rule at 02.05 §3 those staff are already inside the
SOC 2 system's people component, on the strength of the in-scope procedures they operate. The 23 personnel
who sit outside that component are the quota-carrying sales and marketing personnel, and this phrase is
what brings them, their endpoints and their corporate estate inside the ISMS. The distinction matters for
the same reason the whole two-boundary argument matters: a certification body reading this sentence is
being told the boundary is the organisation, and a service auditor reading 02.05 is being told the people
component is 164. Neither statement is a qualification of the other.

**"Across all locations including home-working arrangements"** forecloses the reading that a remote-first
company has certified its office. It is also an accurate statement about where the work happens, which is
the test.

**"In accordance with the Statement of Applicability"** is the conventional closing formula and it is a
pointer, not a claim. The Statement of Applicability required by clause 6.1.3 d) is Phase 03's deliverable;
as at this phase's vantage it has not been produced, and no determination about any Annex A control has
been made or is implied by this sentence. What the formula does is tell the reader of the certificate that
the controls behind it are enumerated somewhere specific and can be asked for.

**No organisational unit, location, function or asset class is excluded from the ISMS boundary.** There is
no exclusions clause because there are no exclusions.

## 6. What a narrowed scope statement costs

The alternative was available and would have been easier: scope the ISMS to platform engineering and the
production environment, certify perhaps 90 people instead of 187, and reduce the internal audit programme,
the awareness training population, the asset register and the supplier review accordingly. Plenty of
certificates read that way.

It was refused in Phase 01 (ADR-0003) for two reasons that this phase's work confirmed rather than
discovered.

The first is honesty about the work. Sales and Customer Success staff routinely handle customer data;
excluding them would have drawn a boundary that the company's own data flows cross daily. A boundary drawn
around a subset of the people who touch the information is not a scoping decision, it is a scoping claim,
and claims are audited.

The second is that **the scope statement is a commercial document as well as a conformity one**. It is
printed on the certificate, and the certificate is the artefact a prospective customer actually reads —
often the only one they read. A certificate whose scope statement says "the information security management
system supporting platform engineering operations" tells an enterprise buyer that the supplier's Sales,
Support and Finance functions, all of which will hold that buyer's data, are outside the certified
management system. Sophisticated buyers read the scope line first for exactly this reason, and the answer
to the follow-up question — "what about the rest of you?" — is a conversation that costs more than the
narrow scope saved.

That is the asymmetry worth naming: a narrowed scope reduces cost in the certification year and is visible
to everybody who matters for three years afterwards. Nobody sees the internal audit hours. Everybody sees
the sentence.

## 7. Availability, maintenance and change

The scope is available as documented information in this document, which is the record clause 4.3 requires,
and is reproduced in the ISMS manual and in the Stage 1 documentation pack. It is reviewed at each
management review under clause 9.3 and whenever a change would alter its truth — a new region, an
acquisition, a material change in the service, or the addition of an organisational unit.

One consequence carries a contractual obligation. **O10** requires CloudNimbus to notify Northgate of
significant changes to the ISMS, its scope or the organisation within 30 days of the change. A scope
statement is not a document that can be quietly revised; changing it is an event with an external
notification attached, which is a reasonable discipline for a sentence that appears on a certificate.
