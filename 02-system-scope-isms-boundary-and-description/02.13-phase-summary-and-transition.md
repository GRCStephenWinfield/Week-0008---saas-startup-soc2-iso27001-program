## 1. What Phase 02 fixed

Phase 01 established who decides and against what. Phase 02 established **what is in**, and it did so twice,
because the two deliverables measure different things.

It fixed the **SOC 2 system**: the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform, the multi-tenant production service
across three AWS regions, with its infrastructure, software, people, procedures and data. It fixed the
**ISMS boundary**: the whole of CloudNimbus, Inc. — 187 staff, all locations including home-working, all
information assets, nothing excluded. And it fixed the claim that connects them: **neither boundary
contains the other**, and forcing them to coincide would have required either excluding organisational
units from the ISMS or claiming AWS inside a management system CloudNimbus does not operate. ADR-0006
records why both alternatives are worse.

It baselined the asset inventory at **1,046 assets inside the ISMS, 800 of them inside the SOC 2 system**,
and named the difference — **246** — as the measure of how far the certificate reaches beyond the
examination report. It refused to give a number for the reverse, because an asset register that counted a
supplier's racks would be a fiction.

It mapped **twelve data flows**, ten of which carry personal data — only DF-02 and DF-08 do not — and three
of which cross a subservice organisation, the other nine having no third party performing a step inside
them. It inventoried **twelve categories of personal information** and produced the count that
evidences the Privacy decision taken in Phase 01: **7 collected directly from the individual, 4 from the
employer, 1 from both**. It set **eight retention rules** — six governing a named category, two governing
the estate — and stated two things in the document rather than in an answer to a question: that a record
deleted from the live stores on day 30 survives in the backup taken that day until **day 65**, and that
**five of the twelve categories have no in-life rule at all** and are governed by the contract-term default
and RT-08 alone.

It classified the vendor estate correctly for the first time: **84 vendors, 11 sub-processors under a data
processing addendum with both subservice organisations among them, 73 ordinary vendors, 11 + 73 = 84**. It
carved out **two** subservice organisations with **14** complementary subservice organisation controls,
**11 for AWS and 3 for Halcyon Identity**; disclosed **11** complementary user entity controls, having
proposed **15** and refused **4**; and stated **10 principal service commitments** with the **12 system
requirements** that have to hold for those commitments to be kept.

Milestones **MS-02** (SOC 2 system scope and ISMS boundary defined, 2026-02-27) and **MS-03** (asset
inventory and data flows baselined, 2026-03-20) were met.

ADR-0008 is a record of a failure: the vendor register still did not distinguish subservice organisations
from ordinary vendors six weeks after the programme was chartered to fix exactly that, which is **ML-3 from
the 2025 Type I management letter repeating in real time**. Its sequence is set out at 02.10 §4 and is
worth restating here because it is easy to misread. **DEC-108** on 2026-02-25 settled the *method* — the
carve-out method rather than the inclusive method — at a point when AWS was the only identified subservice
organisation. The classification test applied to the register on 2026-03-03 identified Halcyon Identity as
a second, and the method already decided applied to it without being retaken. Phase 01's documents name
both parties because they describe the position as at the shared vantage of 2026-03-31, which falls after
that identification. ADR-0009 records that removing geolocation altogether was argued for and refused, and
that the refusal was paid for in a shorter retention period. ADR-0010 records four proposed complementary
user entity controls whose disclosure would have narrowed what the report could say on the strength of
tested controls, while appearing to describe more of the system.

## 4. What Phase 02 deliberately did not do

Phase 02 answers **five of the nine description criteria** — DC1, DC2, DC3, DC6 and DC7 — and names where
the other four are answered rather than leaving them to be assembled later from whatever documents happen
to exist, which is how descriptions come to omit DC4 and DC9.

It assessed no risk, rated nothing, and produced no Statement of Applicability. Those are Phase 03's, and
the reasoning is sequential rather than administrative: a risk assessment scoped before the boundary is
settled assesses the wrong population, and a Statement of Applicability produced before the risk assessment
has no basis on which to justify anything. It designed no control and wrote no policy — Phase 04. It states
no test result, and says nothing about tenant isolation beyond the requirement SR-01 and the flow DF-06
where that requirement is hardest to satisfy; penetration testing is scheduled for May 2026 and this phase
does not know its outcome.

## 5. Entry criteria for Phase 03

Phase 03 opens with everything it needs and nothing it does not.

It inherits a **defined population to assess** — 800 assets in the SOC 2 system, 1,046 in the ISMS, and the
246 that sit inside one and outside the other. It inherits **twelve data flows** with their personal-data
and subservice-organisation flags, which is what makes a threat analysis specific rather than generic;
**twelve personal data categories with retention rules**, including the one conflict those rules contain
and the five categories they do not reach in life;
**fourteen interested parties and their requirements**, with the clause 4.1 determination including the
climate-change determination that ISO/IEC 27001:2022/Amd 1:2024 requires — a determination, recorded with
its reasoning, and not a conclusion in a prescribed direction; and **ten commitments and twelve system
requirements**, which are the statements against
which a failure becomes a consequence rather than an event. And it inherits **three requirements that were
assumptions first** — SR-01 from AS-02, SR-08 from AS-01, and SR-12 under ADR-0007 — each now testable and
each a candidate risk in its own right.

The risk register baseline is due at **MS-04, 2026-04-10**, and the Statement of Applicability at
**MS-05, 2026-04-24**.

## 6. What could still go wrong with the choices made here

Seven exposures follow from Phase 02's own decisions rather than from anything outside them.

**The `cnb-staging` and `cnb-dev` exclusion rests on a control, and controls fail.** SR-12 says
non-production environments hold no production customer data. If a single production extract is ever copied
into staging for debugging — the most ordinary engineering shortcut there is — the SOC 2 system boundary
was drawn wrong, and it was drawn wrong retrospectively rather than from the moment of the copy. ADR-0007
states this in terms; Phase 03 should treat it as a risk and not as a settled fact.

**The classification error behind ADR-0008 was structural.** A vendor register with independent flags is
the remediation, but the flags are only as good as the test applied at each quarterly review, and the party
most likely to be misclassified next is the one that arrives during a busy month and looks like a tool.

**The 65-day backup residue is disclosed and is not solved.** The part of the treatment that requires human
discipline — re-executing a deletion after a restore from a snapshot that predates it — is the part that
will be forgotten first, because it occurs during an incident, when nobody is thinking about a customer who
left six weeks ago.

**Five personal data categories have no in-life retention rule.** PD-01, PD-02, PD-03, PD-06 and PD-09 are
governed by the contract-term default and RT-08 alone, and the eight RT rules give six of them a named
category apiece and two an estate-wide scope. **PD-03 — employment terms including pay rate — is the one
that most deserves a rule of its own**, and it does not have one. 02.07 §6.1 names the gap rather than
letting a reader find it by mapping two tables against each other, and it is carried to **Phase 07**, where
the retention schedule is reconciled against the notice and the lawful-basis analysis.

**The loss of `eu-central-1` has no recovery path, and that is a choice rather than an oversight.** The EU
estate is single-region because obligation O8 forbids replicating EU-residency customers' personal data
outside it, so the `eu-central-1` architecture meets SR-10 for availability-zone failure and not for the
loss of the region. 02.03 §4 states it as an **accepted residual** and 02.09 §3's climate determination is
the reason it cannot be treated as unthinkable. Its treatment belongs to **Phase 06**, which will have to
choose between an extended recovery objective disclosed to 41 customers and a residency commitment that has
already been made.

**CUEC-05 will not be reliably performed**, and 02.11 says so rather than assuming otherwise. It is not the
control that makes the calculation right — CloudNimbus's own processing-integrity controls are not
conditional on it — but it is the most consequential entry on the list when it is not performed, because
the class of defect it catches, a faithful calculation of a wrongly configured rule, is invisible to every
CloudNimbus control. The necessary user-entity control for that defect is CUEC-04, approval of the rule
before activation; the programme's response on CUEC-05 is limited to making it easier to perform and saying
plainly what happens when it is not.

**The ISMS scope statement is the widest one available**, so every clause 9.2 audit, awareness obligation
and asset review now covers 187 people rather than 90. That was right for the reasons at 02.08 §6, and it
is a standing capacity exposure for a 4.6-FTE programme. The failure mode is not a wrong scope; it is a
correct scope thinly covered.
