## 1. What this phase is

Phase 02 draws two lines and then refuses to pretend they are one line.

The first is the **boundary of the system** that the SOC 2 Type II examination will describe: the
infrastructure, software, people, procedures and data that together deliver the CloudNimbus Workforce
Platform. The second is the **boundary of the information security management system** that the
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification audit will assess under clause 4.3. Phase 01 asserted that these two
boundaries would not coincide and that making them coincide would be an error. Phase 02 is where that
assertion is either made good or found to be a slogan.

Everything else in this phase is the work of making those two lines concrete enough to be tested by
somebody else. A boundary that exists only as a sentence in a charter cannot be audited. A boundary
expressed as an inventory of accounts, clusters, services, endpoints, procedures, people, data flows and
personal data categories — each with an owner, each countable, each capable of being wrong in a way a
reader can check — can be. That is the difference between a scope statement and a system description, and
it is the whole of Phase 02's job.

## 2. What Phase 02 establishes

Phase 02 fixes the descriptive foundation that Phases 03 through 09 all consume. It establishes the
methodology by which each boundary was derived and the argument that neither contains the other
(**ADR-0006**, **DEC-201**). It sets out the service description and the five components of the system
under DC3 of the 2018 Description Criteria. It records the infrastructure — the seven AWS accounts of
which five are in the system, the six Aurora PostgreSQL clusters of which five are, the EKS estate, the
region strategy and the `cnb-security` account as a separate trust domain — together with the reasoning
that excludes `cnb-staging` and `cnb-dev` (**ADR-0007**, **DEC-202**). It documents the software estate
and the twelve principal data flows DF-01 to DF-12. It sets the people component at 164 of 187 personnel
and the procedures component at 78 of 130 documented procedures, and explains why the ISMS nonetheless
covers all 187. It baselines the information asset inventory AC-01 to AC-12 at 1,046 assets inside the
ISMS and 800 inside the SOC 2 system.

It then establishes the material the two auditors will read before they read anything else: the personal
information inventory PD-01 to PD-12 and the retention schedule RT-01 to RT-08; the clause 4.3 ISMS scope
statement in the exact words that will be printed on a certificate (**DEC-210**); the interested parties
INT-01 to INT-14 and the clause 4.1 determinations including the climate determination required by
ISO/IEC 27001:2022/Amd 1:2024 (**DEC-209**); the two subservice organisations and the reclassification of
Halcyon Identity (**ADR-0008**, **DEC-203**); the eleven complementary user entity controls and the four
that were proposed and refused (**ADR-0010**, **DEC-208**); and the principal service commitments SC-01 to
SC-10 with the system requirements SR-01 to SR-12 that serve them.

Milestone **MS-02 — SOC 2 system scope and ISMS boundary defined** was met on 2026-02-27. Milestone
**MS-03 — asset inventory and data flows baselined** was met on 2026-03-20.

## 3. What Phase 02 deliberately does not establish

Phase 02 describes a system. It does not assess it.

No risk is rated here. The asset inventory, the data flows and the personal data categories are the inputs
to the Phase 03 risk assessment, and a phase that scored its own assets while inventorying them would have
prejudged the assessment it exists to feed. No Statement of Applicability determination appears here: which
Annex A controls are necessary, and the justification for including or excluding any of them, is a Phase 03
output under clause 6.1.3 d). No control library contents appear here — Phase 02 names system requirements,
which are what the system must do, not controls, which are what CloudNimbus does about it; Phase 04 builds
the controls. No test result of any kind appears here. The penetration test is scheduled for May 2026 and
this phase does not know its findings; the observation window has not opened; neither audit has begun.

Two known gaps are recorded rather than papered over. The completeness of the asset inventory rests on
discovery tooling plus attestation by asset owners, and 02.06 says what that does and does not prove. The
exclusion of the non-production AWS accounts rests entirely on a control, **SR-12**, and 02.03 says so in
terms that would be uncomfortable to read if that control ever failed.

## 4. The vantage this phase writes from

`01.00 §8` established that the metadata date `2026-08-11` is the repository's publication date and nothing
more, and that each phase speaks from its own position in the programme rather than from the date on the
cover.

**Phases 01 and 02 share a vantage of 2026-03-31.** Foundation and scoping ran concurrently through the
first quarter, because a real programme does not finish chartering before it starts scoping — the charter
needs to know roughly what it is chartering, and the scope needs to know who has authority to fix it. That
overlap is why **DEC-108**, the decision to use the carve-out method rather than the inclusive method for
subservice organisations, is recorded as a Phase 01 decision on 2026-02-25 and argued at length as a Phase
02 subject in 02.10: the decision was taken while the two workstreams were running side by side, and the
material that justifies it is descriptive material this phase produces. DEC-108 settled a **method**, not a
population. On 2026-02-25 AWS was the only identified subservice organisation; Halcyon Identity was
identified as a second on 2026-03-03, when the classification test was applied to the vendor register, and
the method already decided applied to it without having to be taken again. Phase 01's documents name both
parties because they describe the position as at the shared vantage of 2026-03-31, which is after that
identification. 02.10 §4 sets the sequence out in full. From Phase 03 onward each phase has its own later
vantage.

The consequence is the same as Phase 01's and equally deliberate. Nothing in these fourteen documents
states an outcome that occurs after 2026-03-31. Events after that date appear as planned, scheduled or
targeted, and never as done.

## 6. The description criteria, in brief

A SOC 2 description is measured against **DC section 200, the 2018 Description Criteria for a Description
of a Service Organization's System in a SOC 2 Report**, criteria DC1 to DC9. **Phase 02 answers five of
them and names where the other four are answered.** DC1, the types of services, and DC3, the components of
the system, are answered here across 02.02 to 02.05. DC2, the principal service commitments and system
requirements, is answered in 02.12. DC6, complementary user entity controls, is answered in 02.11. DC7,
subservice organisations and the method used, is answered in 02.10. DC5 and DC8 — the applicable criteria
with their related controls, and any criteria not relevant with the reasons — belong to Phase 04. DC4,
disclosures about incidents, belongs to Phase 06, and no reportable incident has occurred as at this
phase's vantage. DC9, relevant changes during the period, belongs to Phase 09, because the period has not
started. The full map is in 02.02 §2.

The reason for mapping the criteria to documents this early is that descriptions assembled at the end of a
period, out of whatever documents happen to exist by then, are exactly the descriptions that omit DC4 and
DC9. Those two are the criteria nobody writes in advance, because the first requires admitting something
went wrong and the second requires having noticed that something changed.

## 8. How to read this phase

Read **02.01** first and do not skip it. It carries the argument the rest of the phase depends on, and a
reader who takes the two boundaries as an administrative detail will misread every count that follows.
Then **02.02** for what the service is and how the description criteria are covered, and **02.03** to
**02.05** for the five DC3 components in detail — infrastructure, software and data, people and procedures.
**02.06** and **02.07** are the two inventories that Phase 03 consumes directly.

**02.08** and **02.09** are the ISO side: the scope statement in the words that reach the certificate, and
the clause 4.1 and 4.2 context work behind it. **02.10** to **02.12** are the parts of the description that
say what CloudNimbus does not do itself — the carved-out subservice organisations, the controls customers
must operate, and the commitments against which everything is measured. **02.13** closes the phase.

For a single question, the fastest routes are: *what is in scope and why is it not one boundary* — 02.01;
*what does the platform actually do* — 02.02; *where does the data go* — 02.04; *what does CloudNimbus
promise* — 02.12; *what must the customer do* — 02.11.

## 9. Standing framing

Nothing in this phase is a determination of compliance with any law. The SOC 2 deliverable is a
restricted-use examination report carrying a CPA firm's opinion; there is no such thing as a SOC 2
certification, and no document in this repository describes CloudNimbus as certified against it. The ISO
deliverable is a certificate issued by an accredited certification body — Northgate Certification Services,
Ltd., accredited by ANAB — and not by ISO, which certifies nobody. Points of focus are not requirements,
and Annex A is not the requirements: conformity is assessed against clauses 4 to 10. A SOC 2 finding is an
**exception** or **deviation**; an ISO finding is a **nonconformity**; the two words are not
interchangeable and this phase does not swap them.
