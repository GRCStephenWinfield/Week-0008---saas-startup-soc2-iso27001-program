## 1. Purpose

Two of the five DC3 components are not technical, and they are the two most likely to be described badly.
**People** and **procedures** are components of the system in exactly the sense that infrastructure and
software are: the service is not delivered without them, and a description that lists servers and omits the
people who operate them has described a machine rather than a system.

This document sets out both. It fixes the people component at 164 of 187 personnel and says who the other
23 are; it describes the access tiers by which the boundary is drawn person by person rather than
department by department; it fixes the procedures component at 78 of 130 documented procedures; and it
answers the question a careful reader asks immediately, which is how the ISMS can cover all 187 while the
system component covers 164 without one of those two numbers being wrong.

## 2. The organisation

CloudNimbus employs **187 people** across 31 US states and 4 countries. The company is **remote-first**: the
Denver suite is a meeting space of approximately 4,100 square feet with no production equipment, and the
normal working location of the overwhelming majority of staff is their home.

| Function | Headcount |
|---|---|
| Engineering | 78 |
| Sales | 34 |
| Customer Success | 31 |
| G&amp;A | 22 |
| Product | 14 |
| Security | 8 |
| **Total** | **187** |

Two features of this shape matter to the boundary. The first is that **Customer Success is 31 people who
routinely reach customer data**, through the support console described as DF-12, in the ordinary course of
their work. Any instinct to treat "the platform" as an engineering concern collides with that immediately.
The second is that **Security is 8 people**, which is why the programme runs at 4.6 FTE-equivalents and why
the design of every control in it has to survive contact with a small team. A programme designed for a
thirty-person security function would not be describing this company.

There is **no CISO title** at CloudNimbus. Karim Haddad, VP Security &amp; Trust, holds the programme, and
because the title does not exist the ISO/IEC 27001 clause 5.3 assignment of roles, responsibilities and
authorities had to be made explicitly rather than inferred from an org chart — a point 01.08 records and
this phase inherits.

## 3. The people component — 164 of 187

**The people component of the system comprises 164 of the 187 personnel. 187 − 164 = 23.**

One rule produces that number, it is stated here once, and every other statement in this phase about who is
inside the people component is an application of it.

> **A person is in the people component if they either (a) can reach the production environment or customer
> data, or (b) operate a documented procedure the system depends on.**

The rule has two limbs because DC3 has two kinds of person in it. Limb (a) is the obvious one: somebody who
can touch the system or the data it holds is part of the system that delivers the service. Limb (b) is the
one a scoping exercise built out of access lists alone will miss, and it is the limb the CC1 series depends
on. Somebody who performs background screening, grants and revokes access, or administers the data
processing addendum a sub-processor operates under is operating one of the 78 procedures inside the system
(§7), and is inside the component whether or not they hold a production credential. A description that put
the people who *administer* access outside the system that *depends* on access administration would have
described a machine and omitted the mechanism deciding who may work it.

Applying the rule produces the 164 in two parts.

**Four functions are wholly inside, on limb (a) — 131 people.** Engineering 78, Customer Success 31,
Product 14 and Security 8. **78 + 31 + 14 + 8 = 131.** Every member of these four either holds an access
tier that reaches the platform or holds one that reaches the pipeline and telemetry that build it; Customer
Success is inside because support sessions under DF-12 reach customer personal data in the ordinary course
of the work.

**Thirty-three more come from Sales and G&amp;A, most of them on limb (b) — 33 people.** They are the
solutions-engineering part of the Sales organisation, who can open scoped sessions and configure
demonstration and migration tenants, and the People, Legal, Finance and business-operations staff who
operate in-scope procedures: screening, onboarding and offboarding; access administration for the 22
in-scope internal SaaS applications and for the platform tiers; MDM enrolment and endpoint issue;
administration of the data processing addenda, the sub-processor disclosure behind O5 and the residency
addendum behind O8; and the procurement and expense feed that the asset inventory in 02.06 §7 reconciles
its SaaS population against. **131 + 33 = 164.**

**The 23 outside are the quota-carrying sales and marketing personnel** — account executives, sales
development representatives, and the marketing function — who neither reach the platform nor operate a
procedure inside the system. They fail both limbs, which is the only way to be outside. They sell the
service, they do not operate it, and no control that any trust services criterion depends on is performed
by them.

A note on where marketing sits, because the function table in §2 has no marketing row. Marketing is part of
the **34-person Sales and marketing organisation** counted there as "Sales"; that is the only place it
appears in the headcount and it is not counted anywhere else. The 23 who fall outside the people component
are drawn entirely from that organisation, which leaves the remaining 11 of the 34 — the
solutions-engineering side — inside, and the whole of the 22-person G&amp;A function inside on limb (b).
**23 + 11 = 34.**

That the whole of G&amp;A falls inside is the statement most likely to be challenged, so it is defended
rather than asserted. Each of the four groups within those 22 people operates at least one of the 78
procedures the system depends on: **People** owns hiring, screening, onboarding, training and offboarding,
on which the CC1 series rests; **Legal** owns the data processing addenda, the sub-processor register and
the disclosure and residency commitments; **business operations** performs access administration and
endpoint issue; and **Finance** operates the procurement and expense feed the SaaS population is reconciled
against. In a company of this size those functions are individually small enough that no member of them
works more than one step from a procedure inside the system. Where a future G&amp;A hire operates none, the
rule puts them outside and the register will say so.

**The boundary is therefore drawn person by person, not department by department.** Drawing it at the
department would have been faster and wrong in both directions: it would have swept in the twenty-odd
people who can neither reach the system nor operate a procedure it depends on, and excluded the solutions
engineers and the People, Legal and Finance staff who do.

**All 23 remain inside the ISMS**, and this is the point of the phase in miniature. They carry managed
endpoints under MDM. They complete the same security awareness training. They are subject to the same
acceptable use policy, the same joiner-mover-leaver process, the same screening at hire, and the same
incident reporting obligation. They handle customer information — procurement questionnaires, security
documentation, commercial terms — that is confidential even though it is not tenant data. An ISMS that
excluded them would be an ISMS excluding the function most likely to email a customer document to the wrong
address.

So the same twenty-three people are **outside a component of a system description and inside a management
system**, simultaneously, and both statements are exact. That is not a contradiction; it is what having two
boundaries means. 02.01 makes the general argument, and this is the clearest single instance of it in the
phase.

## 4. Access tiers

Limb (a) of the rule in §3 is applied against a tier model. A person's tier is a property of the access
they hold, reviewed when they join, when they move and when they leave. Limb (b) is not an access
property, so it appears in the model as its own row rather than as a tier that reaches further than the
one above it.

| Tier | Who typically holds it | What it reaches | In the people component |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate only | Quota-carrying sales and marketing personnel | Internal SaaS applications, corporate document stores, no platform access | No — fails both limbs of the rule in §3 |
| Corporate, with an in-scope procedure | People, Legal, Finance and business operations | The same corporate estate and no platform access, but the holder operates one or more of the 78 procedures inside the system | Yes — under limb (b), not because of the access |
| Platform build | Product, parts of Engineering, parts of solutions engineering | The change promotion pipeline, non-production environments, dashboards and telemetry that carry no customer records | Yes — the pipeline is in the system even though the environments are not (ADR-0007) |
| Tenant-scoped support | Customer Success, solutions engineering, parts of Product | The support console under a session scoped to one named tenant, time-bounded and logged as DF-12 | Yes |
| Application administration | Platform engineering, on-call engineers | Service configuration, feature flags, tenant configuration in `platform-metadata` | Yes |
| Infrastructure administration | Infrastructure and SRE, delegated security administration | AWS accounts, EKS clusters, data stores, network and key configuration | Yes |
| Break-glass | A named subset of infrastructure administration and security | Elevated, time-boxed access invoked under an incident, alerted on invocation and reviewed afterwards | Yes |

Three properties of this model do the work. **Tiers are not cumulative by seniority.** Nobody holds
infrastructure administration because they are senior; they hold it because their role requires it, and a
director who does not operate infrastructure does not have it. **The support tier is scoped to one tenant
at a time**, which is the difference between a support capability and a standing route to 640 customers'
data. And **only one row is decided by something other than access**: the second, where the determination
turns on a procedure the person operates rather than on a credential they hold. That row is the reason the
Corporate-only tier can sit outside the component while the People, Legal and Finance staff who hold
identical access sit inside it, and stating it as a separate row is what stops the model from being read as
"corporate access means outside the system", which is the reading that would put the joiner-mover-leaver
process outside a description whose access controls assume it.

The tier model is also the mechanism by which the component stays accurate. A person moving from a
quota-carrying sales role into Customer Success crosses the boundary, and so does a person moving into a
G&amp;A role that operates an in-scope procedure; the joiner-mover-leaver process treats a move as a change
of access rather than a change of manager. Reviews of who holds which tier are a Phase 04 and Phase 05
subject; what Phase 02 fixes is the model against which those reviews will be performed.

## 6. Joiners, movers, leavers — and the physical dimension of a component with no premises

The people component is a population that changes, and a description of it is only as good as the process
that keeps it current. Three transitions govern it.

**Joining.** Background screening proportionate to the role and jurisdiction, acceptable use and
confidentiality undertakings, role assignment to an access tier by the hiring manager with security review
where the tier reaches production, and security awareness training on the cadence Phase 01 fixed.

That cadence is **CAL-12** in the assurance calendar at 01.11: **annual, plus within 14 days of hire.**
This document states it in Phase 01's terms because Phase 01 is issued, and a scoping document does not get
to move a control's frequency by describing it differently. An earlier draft said training happened
*before* access was granted rather than within a period afterwards — a materially tighter position that
nobody had decided.

The tighter position was argued for during this phase and is recorded rather than discarded, because the
argument is a good one: fourteen days is a fortnight in which a new joiner may hold a tier reaching
production without having been trained on how to use it, and the joiners most likely to be granted such a
tier on day one are the engineering and infrastructure hires for whom the exposure is largest. The proposal
is that training precede the *access grant* for tiers reaching production or customer data, leaving
CAL-12's 14-day rule to govern the general population. **Adopting it would require CAL-12 to be amended**,
which is a change to an issued record and to the calendar the examination samples against. This phase
therefore proposes it and does not implement it: it goes to **Phase 04**, where the control is designed and
where a calendar entry can be changed as a change rather than left as a discrepancy between two documents.
Hannah Brill owns the process; the access grant itself is performed by the tier's owning function.

**Moving.** Treated as a change of access, not a change of reporting line. The failure mode this guards
against is accumulation: a person who has held three roles and retains the access of all three is a
standing exception waiting to be sampled, and it is one of the reasons a mover is the hardest of the three
transitions to evidence well.

**Leaving.** Revocation across the identity provider, the platform, the in-scope SaaS applications and the
endpoint, with device return or remote wipe. In a remote-first company the endpoint is somewhere else,
which is precisely why every endpoint is enrolled in MDM from the day it is issued.

Contractors and temporary staff are handled under the same process and the same tier model, and hold access
for a stated engagement period rather than indefinitely.

The physical dimension is unusual enough to state. CloudNimbus operates **no facility containing
information processing equipment**: the Denver suite has no server, no network closet and no comms room,
and its internet service is the landlord's building service. The consequence for the people component is
that "the workplace" is 187 homes and whatever else people work from, and that the physical controls which
matter here are equipment-centred — device encryption, MDM enforcement, screen locking, a prohibition on
removable media, and siting expectations for home working — rather than premises-centred. Whether and how
each corresponding Annex A reference control is necessary is a **Statement of Applicability** determination
and belongs to Phase 03; this document records the fact pattern the determination will be made against, not
the determination.

## 7. Procedures — 78 of 130

CloudNimbus maintains **130 documented procedures**. **78 are inside the system.** The remaining 52 are
corporate procedures that support the company rather than the service: the finance close, procurement of
non-platform goods and services, sales operations and commission administration, marketing operations,
travel and expense administration for CloudNimbus's own staff, and the corporate insurance and contract
administration routines. They are inside the ISMS, they have owners, and they are subject to the same
document control. They serve no trust services criterion.

The 78 inside the system are the procedures somebody would have to follow for the service to work as
described: change management and deployment, incident response and escalation, access provisioning and
revocation, backup and restoration, disaster recovery invocation, key management, vulnerability management,
monitoring and alert triage, calculation rule change and reconciliation handling, tenant onboarding,
customer offboarding and deletion, support session handling, log retention, and the onboarding, screening,
training and offboarding routines that determine who is allowed near any of it.

That last group is worth naming explicitly, because it is the one most often mis-sorted. Hiring, background
screening, onboarding, training and offboarding are **People procedures that are inside the system**, since
they are how the people component is populated and depopulated, and the CC1 series depends on them. The
same is true of access administration, of the data processing addendum and sub-processor administration
that O5 and O8 rest on, and of endpoint issue and MDM enrolment. A scoping exercise that filed everything
owned by the People, Legal or Finance functions under "corporate" would have removed the
joiner-mover-leaver process from a description whose access controls assume it.

This is also the other half of limb (b) in §3, and the two halves have to agree or neither is true. A
procedure inside the system is operated by somebody, and that somebody is inside the people component. The
78 in-scope procedures and the 164 in-scope people are two views of the same determination, and the
quarterly reconciliation Rahul Bhargava performs checks them against each other rather than separately.

Procedures are documented in one place under one document-control regime, whichever side of the boundary
they sit on, and each carries a flag recording whether it is in the system. That single-register approach
is the procedural equivalent of the two-flag asset model in 02.06, and it exists for the same reason: two
registers drift, one register with two flags cannot.

## 8. Why the two figures do not conflict

The question is worth answering directly, because it is the question a Stage 1 auditor and a service
auditor will each ask from opposite directions.

The ISMS covers **187 people** because clause 4.3 asks the organisation to determine the boundaries of a
**management system**, and CloudNimbus determined that boundary to be the whole organisation, recorded in
Phase 01 as DEC-103 and ADR-0003. Every person is in scope of the policies, the training, the screening and
the incident obligations. There is no organisational unit outside it.

The system component covers **164 people** because DC3 asks management to describe the components used to
**provide the service**, and 23 people neither reach it nor operate a procedure it depends on. Including them would not make the description more
complete; it would make it less accurate, by asserting that people who cannot reach the platform are part
of the system that delivers it.

The two answers are responses to two different questions. The error would be answering one question with
the other's number — telling the certification body that the ISMS covers 164 people, which would be an
untrue statement about the management system, or telling the service auditor that 187 people operate the
platform, which would be an untrue statement about the system. Both numbers are stated here, on the same
page, with the reason for each, so that neither can be quoted without the other.
