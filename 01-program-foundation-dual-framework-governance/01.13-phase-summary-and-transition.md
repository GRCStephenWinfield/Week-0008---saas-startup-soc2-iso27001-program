## 1. What Phase 01 fixed

Phase 01 established the authority under which the Trust &amp; Assurance Programme operates and settled the
questions that every later phase would otherwise have to reopen.

It fixed the two deliverables and refused to blur them: a SOC 2 Type II examination report carrying an
independent CPA firm's opinion, and an ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate granted by an ANAB-accredited
certification body. There is no combined report and no such thing as SOC 2 certification, and the programme
will correct any communication that implies otherwise. It fixed the operating model behind them — one
control library, one risk assessment, one evidence store, two deliverables — and the four-way separation of
readiness advisor, internal auditor, certification body and service auditor that makes each deliverable
worth having.

It fixed governance: a Trust Committee monthly under the CFO, a Trust Working Group weekly under the
Compliance Manager, and a quarterly Audit &amp; Risk Committee chaired by an independent non-executive
director, with accountability for the charter and for the clause 9.2 internal audit programme placed
outside the management line. The claim attached to that last arrangement is deliberately modest, and 01.07
§4.1 states its limits: the ARC has one voting member, so this is not the structural independence a listed
company would mean by the words. What it is, and all it is, is that **internal audit does not report to the
person whose management system it audits.** Camberwell Risk Partners' findings reach Lorraine Kessler
directly, under engagement terms that let her meet the auditor without management present. That reporting
line exists even though the committee behind it is thin, and Phase 01 would rather record both halves of
that than the flattering half. It also made the clause 5.3 assignment explicitly, in writing, because
CloudNimbus has no CISO title and inference is not assignment.

It fixed the money: a $1,400,000 envelope, $1,366,000 committed across ten lines, $34,000 of headroom, and
4.6 FTE-equivalents. It fixed eight objectives, and said plainly that one of them — OBJ-01 — states an
outcome the company does not control, because the opinion belongs to the auditor and a programme that
believes otherwise will argue with its auditor instead of fixing its controls.

And it fixed the obligations the programme exists to serve, twelve of them, together with the recurring
calendar that discharges them, and a roadmap of eighteen milestones on a critical path whose hardest
constraint is a single date: 2026-07-01, the morning the observation window opens, after which no control
can be made to have been operating for the full period.

## 4. What Phase 01 deliberately left open

A foundation phase that answered everything would be a foundation phase that had guessed. Five things were
left open, four of them on purpose, and each is carried forward as a known gap rather than as an omission.

| Gap | Position at close of Phase 01 | Resolves in |
|---|---|---|
| CAL-14 internal audit coverage | The calendar fixes frequency — annual, risk-weighted — and leaves coverage undefined: whether clauses 4 to 10 are audited as well as Annex A controls, whether all locations and regions are covered, and whether the ISMS is covered in one cycle or across a rotation. Deferred to the internal audit charter. Accountability for the programme sits with the Audit &amp; Risk Committee at A-16 | Phase 08 |
| The 148-control library | Asserted as a design intent — 112 controls serving both frameworks, 21 SOC 2 only, 15 ISO only — and not yet built. The counts are a claim Phase 04 must earn, and the mapping behind them is an assertion by CloudNimbus that no authority blesses | Phase 04 |
| CUEC and CSOC counts | Complementary user entity controls and complementary subservice organisation controls are known to be required by the carve-out method chosen at DEC-108. How many there are, and what they say, is determined with the system description | Phase 02 |
| The risk register | No risk has been assessed. The baseline register, the scoring scale and the movement rules are Phase 03 deliverables. Phase 01's OBJ-06 commits to the process, not to a result. No close position and no forecast of one is published here, and none will be published in Phase 03 until it has been proved arithmetically reachable | Phase 03 |
| The thinness of the Audit &amp; Risk Committee | Not left open on purpose — left open because it cannot be closed at this headcount. The ARC has **one voting member**, and a committee with one voting member is not a committee in the sense the word usually carries. What exists is an independent non-executive chair with a direct line to the outsourced internal auditor, engagement terms giving that auditor access to her without management present, and her right to convene out of cycle. That is real and it is thinner than a mature company's structure, and it depends materially on one person. Set out in 01.07 §4.1 | Revisited when the board expands |

The Audit &amp; Risk Committee entry deserves a further sentence, because it is the one gap on this list
that no later phase resolves. Phases 02 to 09 will close the other four by doing work. This one closes only
when CloudNimbus appoints a second independent non-executive director, which is a board composition matter
and not a programme deliverable. Until then the programme runs with a single point of independent
challenge, and every claim this portfolio makes about internal audit reporting outside the management line
should be read against that fact rather than in place of it. The exposure is that Lorraine Kessler is
unavailable, resigns, or declines to press a point, and that there is no second voice to carry it.

A sixth item is open in a different sense. The clause 4.2 stakeholder register in 01.10 identifies the
interested parties; determining which of their requirements become ISMS requirements is Phase 02 work, and
that includes recording that no interested party has raised a climate-related information security
requirement under the note added to clause 4.2 by Amendment 1:2024.

## 5. Entry criteria for Phase 02

Phase 02 may begin when the following are true. The charter is approved by the CEO and ratified by the Audit
&amp; Risk Committee, and the budget envelope is board-approved. The clause 5.3 assignment is recorded and
communicated, and every activity in the responsibility matrix has a named accountable role. The four
external assurance engagements are contracted with their independence boundaries written into the
engagement terms rather than assumed. The obligations register is complete against the reviewed contract
population, with an owner against each entry. And the stakeholder register exists in a form Phase 02 can use
as the input to its clause 4.1 and 4.2 analysis.

Phase 02 inherits two constraints it may not relax without returning to the Audit &amp; Risk Committee: the
ISMS scope is the whole organisation (ADR-0003), and the SOC 2 system scope and the ISMS scope are not to be
forced into the same shape, because one describes a system and the other describes an organisation.

## 6. What could still go wrong with the choices made here

Phase 01's decisions are defensible. That is not the same as safe, and the register's habit is to name the
exposure at the point the decision is taken rather than in the retrospective.

The five-category scope may be larger than a 4.6-FTE programme can evidence. Sixty-one criteria across a
six-month window is a substantial evidence obligation, and Privacy alone added eighteen of them to a
forty-three-criterion baseline. If the evidence proves unsustainable mid-window, the options are all bad:
narrowing the scope inside the window would leave a category partially examined, and pressing on with thin
evidence would produce deviations rather than an absence of testing. PR-01 and PR-07 record this.

The four-way separation costs money and coordination, and coordination is the part that fails first. Four
firms with four schedules, four sets of context to maintain and no single relationship manager is a
structure that degrades quietly under delivery pressure, usually by someone asking the wrong firm a question
because it is the firm that happens to be on the call.

The independence boundaries themselves erode conversationally rather than contractually. Nobody will propose
that the certification body advise on the ISMS. Somebody will ask, informally, whether an approach would be
acceptable, and the answer will shape the approach.

The independence architecture terminates in one person. Every escalation route this phase built — an
independence question, an internal audit finding management would rather reframe, a charter amendment that
should not be self-authorised — arrives at Lorraine Kessler, and there is nobody behind her. The failure
mode is not that she is captured; it is the duller one, that a quarter goes by in which she is busy, the
pack is received rather than interrogated, and the accountability recorded in A-01 and A-16 becomes
decorative without anyone deciding that it should.

The internal audit coverage gap at CAL-14 is a decision to leave a specification undefined while the work is
outsourced. Where the client has not defined coverage, the supplier proposes it, and the client approves a
plan against nothing. PR-06 records the exposure; A.5.22 makes the monitoring of that supplier a control in
its own right.

Two assumptions carried out of this phase are load-bearing and unverified: that scheduled-job failures are
alerted (AS-01), and that row-level security is uniformly enforced across every data access path (AS-02).
Both are recorded as assumptions rather than as facts precisely because a programme that writes its
assumptions down can be shown to have been wrong about them.

And the evidence architecture rests on one person. Rahul Bhargava designed it, operates much of it and
reports on its completeness. At 4.6 FTE there is no obvious redundancy to build, and PR-08 records the
concentration rather than pretending it has been mitigated.
