## 1. Purpose

This is the reference chapter for the certification side of the programme, and the counterpart to
01.02. It states what ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requires, what Annex A is and is not, how certification is
obtained and from whom, and what the three categories of audit finding oblige. As with 01.02,
everything downstream assumes the positions taken here.

The standard is **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**, *Information security, cybersecurity and privacy
protection — Information security management systems — Requirements*, published in October 2022,
as amended by **ISO/IEC 27001:2022/Amd 1:2024**. The 2013 edition is gone: the transition period
closed on **31 October 2025**, and every certificate issued in 2026 is against the 2022 edition.
There is no residual question about which version applies.

## 2. What the standard requires

The requirements of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 are **clauses 4 to 10**. Conformity is assessed against
those clauses and nothing else. An organisation that has implemented a great many security controls
and has not built a management system does not conform; an organisation with a modest control set
and a functioning management system may well conform. That inversion surprises people arriving from
a controls-first tradition, and it is the reason this programme treats the ISMS as a thing to be
built rather than as a documentation wrapper around work Phase 05 was doing anyway.

**Clause 4, context of the organisation,** requires CloudNimbus to determine the external and
internal issues relevant to its purpose that affect its ability to achieve the intended outcomes of
the ISMS; to determine the interested parties relevant to the ISMS and their relevant requirements;
to determine the ISMS scope, having considered those issues, those requirements and the interfaces
and dependencies with other organisations; and to establish, implement, maintain and continually
improve the ISMS itself. The scope is documented information. For CloudNimbus the scope is the
whole organisation, and 01.06 and ADR-0003 explain the refusal to narrow it.

**Clause 5, leadership,** requires top management to demonstrate leadership and commitment — not to
delegate it — by ensuring the policy and objectives are established and compatible with strategic
direction, integrating ISMS requirements into business processes, providing resources,
communicating the importance of conformity, and directing and supporting the people who contribute.
Clause 5.2 requires a documented information security policy. **Clause 5.3 requires top management
to assign and communicate the responsibilities and authorities for the roles relevant to
information security**, in particular for ensuring the ISMS conforms to the standard and for
reporting on its performance. CloudNimbus has no CISO title, so this assignment could not be
inferred from an organisation chart and had to be written down explicitly — the subject of 01.08.

**Clause 6, planning,** is where the standard bites hardest. Clause 6.1.1 requires the organisation
to determine the risks and opportunities that need to be addressed. Clause 6.1.2 requires a defined
and repeatable information security risk assessment process with risk acceptance criteria, applied
consistently to produce comparable results, with identified risk owners. Clause 6.1.3 requires a
risk treatment process that selects treatment options, determines all controls necessary to
implement them, compares those controls against Annex A, produces a Statement of Applicability,
formulates a risk treatment plan, and obtains risk owners' approval of the plan and acceptance of
the residual risks. Clause 6.2 requires information security objectives that are measurable, with
plans stating what will be done, with what resources, by whom, by when, and how results will be
evaluated. Clause 6.3, new in the 2022 edition, requires changes to the ISMS to be carried out in a
planned manner.

**Clause 7, support,** covers resources, competence, awareness, communication and documented
information. Clause 7.2 requires the organisation to determine the necessary competence of people
doing work that affects information security performance, to ensure they are competent on the basis
of education, training or experience, and to retain documented information as evidence. Clause 7.4
requires it to determine what it will communicate about information security, when, to whom and
how. Clause 7.5 governs the creation, updating and control of documented information.

**Clause 8, operation,** requires the organisation to plan, implement and control the processes
needed to meet requirements and to implement the actions determined under clause 6; to keep
documented information sufficient to have confidence the processes were carried out as planned; to
control planned changes and review the consequences of unintended ones; and to ensure externally
provided processes, products and services relevant to the ISMS are controlled. Clauses 8.2 and 8.3
require the risk assessment to be performed at planned intervals or when significant changes occur,
and the risk treatment plan to be implemented.

**Clause 9, performance evaluation,** requires monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation
under 9.1; an internal audit programme under 9.2; and management review under 9.3. Clause 9.2 is
worth stating in full because it is where the standard is most often under-read: the organisation
shall conduct internal audits at planned intervals to provide information on whether the ISMS
conforms **both to the organisation's own requirements and to the requirements of this
International Standard**, and is effectively implemented and maintained. The audit programme must
consider the importance of the processes concerned and the results of previous audits. Auditors
must be objective and impartial, and must not audit their own work. **The obligation is to audit
the management system — clauses 4 to 10 — and not merely the controls.** Clause 9.3 sets out the
inputs management review must consider and the outputs it must produce.

**Clause 10, improvement,** contains 10.1 continual improvement and 10.2 nonconformity and
corrective action. When a nonconformity occurs the organisation must react to it, evaluate the need
for action to eliminate the cause so that it does not recur — by reviewing the nonconformity,
determining its causes, and determining whether similar nonconformities exist or could potentially
occur — implement any action needed, review the effectiveness of corrective action taken, and
retain documented information on the nature of the nonconformities and the results.

## 3. Amendment 1:2024 — climate action

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022/Amd 1:2024** is short, in force, and audited. It makes exactly two changes.

To **clause 4.1** it adds a sentence: the organisation **shall determine whether climate change is
a relevant issue**. Note the shape of the obligation. It does not require the organisation to
conclude that climate change *is* relevant, and it does not import any environmental management
requirement. It requires a determination, and therefore evidence of a determination. An
organisation that has simply never considered the question fails the requirement; an organisation
that has considered it and documented a reasoned conclusion either way conforms.

To **clause 4.2** it adds a note: relevant interested parties **can** have requirements related to
climate change. A note is guidance, not a requirement, but it is a strong signal about where an
auditor will look — customer contracts, investor agreements and regulatory expectations.

CloudNimbus's determination is recorded as part of the clause 4 context work in Phase 02. For a
remote-first company with no facilities of its own, the honest analysis runs through availability
of cloud regions, energy-related supply constraints affecting the subservice organisation, and the
resilience of a distributed home-working workforce — not through carbon accounting, which is not
what clause 4.1 asks about.

## 4. Annex A — a reference set, not the requirements

Annex A of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 contains **93 controls in four themes**.

| Theme | Range | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational | A.5.1–A.5.37 | 37 |
| People | A.6.1–A.6.8 | 8 |
| Physical | A.7.1–A.7.14 | 14 |
| Technological | A.8.1–A.8.34 | 34 |
| **Total** | | **93** |

The 2022 edition introduced **eleven controls** that did not exist in the 2013 Annex A: **A.5.7
threat intelligence**, **A.5.23 information security for use of cloud services**, **A.5.30 ICT
readiness for business continuity**, **A.7.4 physical security monitoring**, **A.8.9 configuration
management**, **A.8.10 information deletion**, **A.8.11 data masking**, **A.8.12 data leakage
prevention**, **A.8.16 monitoring activities**, **A.8.23 web filtering**, and **A.8.28 secure
coding**. Four of those eleven — A.5.23, A.8.9, A.8.16 and A.8.28 — are squarely in the operating
reality of a cloud-native engineering organisation, and CloudNimbus would have been implementing
them under other names in any case.

**Annex A is a reference set. It is not a checklist and it is not the requirements.** The mechanism
is clause 6.1.3: the organisation determines the controls it needs from its risk treatment, and
then **clause 6.1.3 c)** requires it to compare those controls against Annex A **to verify that no
necessary control has been omitted**. The direction of travel is risk to control, with Annex A as a
completeness check at the end. It is not Annex A to control with risk written up afterwards, even
though that is how a great many implementations are actually run.

The consequence is one that surprises people who have only worked with SOC 2: **a valid certificate
can be held with Annex A controls excluded.** Exclusion is legitimate where the Statement of
Applicability justifies it. This is the asymmetry that 01.06 builds the integration model around,
because the equivalent move is impossible on the other side — you cannot obtain an unmodified
opinion in a SOC 2 examination with an applicable trust services criterion left unaddressed.

## 5. The Statement of Applicability

Clause 6.1.3 d) requires a Statement of Applicability, and it must contain four things: the
**necessary controls**; the **justification for their inclusion**; **whether the necessary controls
are implemented or not**; and the **justification for excluding** any of the Annex A controls.

The third element is the one organisations quietly skip. The Statement of Applicability is not a
list of controls the organisation intends to have; it states, for each necessary control, whether
it is implemented. A control that is necessary and not yet implemented is disclosed as such, with
the risk treatment plan showing when it will be. That is conformant and honest. Claiming
implementation that does not exist is neither, and it is the kind of thing a Stage 2 auditor finds
by sampling.

Two further points about the instrument, before the question of what CloudNimbus's own Statement of
Applicability will say.

The first is about direction. Clause 6.1.3 d) asks for the necessary controls, and "necessary" is
determined by the risk treatment under 6.1.3 b), not by reading down Annex A and ticking. The
comparison against Annex A under 6.1.3 c) happens afterwards and answers one question only: has
anything necessary been omitted? A Statement of Applicability written the other way round — Annex A
first, risk narrative reverse-engineered to fit — is conformant in appearance and produces
justifications that collapse the first time a Stage 2 auditor asks why a control was needed here
rather than what it is called.

The second is about the standard of a justification. Both justifications the clause requires — for
inclusion and for exclusion — have to survive being read by someone who does not already agree.
"Not applicable to our environment" is not a justification; it is a conclusion with the reasoning
removed.

**CloudNimbus's Statement of Applicability is produced in Phase 03 and its determinations are Phase
03's to make.** This chapter does not state them, and a reader looking here for a count of necessary
controls or a list of exclusions is looking in the wrong phase. What can honestly be said at the
close of the foundation phase is where the analysis is expected to be hard.

It will be hard in **A.7, the fourteen physical controls**. CloudNimbus is remote-first with no data
centre, no rack, no cage and no comms room; the Denver suite is a meeting space and its internet
service is the landlord's building service. AWS operates every facility in which CloudNimbus's
information is processed, and AWS is a carved-out subservice organisation. On that footing the
temptation to sweep the whole A.7 theme aside is obvious and will have to be resisted control by
control, because a remote-first company is not an equipment-free company: 231 managed endpoints sit
on kitchen tables across 31 states and 4 countries, and an endpoint is equipment. Some A.7 controls
will apply in modified form rather than not at all, and the difference between "modified" and
"excluded" is the difference between a Statement of Applicability that holds up and one that does
not.

The programme's standing instruction to Phase 03 is therefore that **exclusions must be justified
rather than assumed**, and that the entries worth the most scrutiny are the ones where an exclusion
was argued for and refused. A Statement of Applicability made only of easy exclusions is a document
nobody argued about.

**ISO/IEC 27002:2022** provides implementation guidance for the Annex A controls and introduces
five **attribute** sets — control type, information security properties, cybersecurity concepts,
operational capabilities and security domains — which can be used to view and filter the control
set from different perspectives. 27002 is guidance. It is not certifiable and nobody is audited
against it.

## 6. The certification route

**ISO does not certify anyone.** ISO develops and publishes standards. Certification is granted by
a **certification body**, and a certification body that is worth anything is **accredited** by a
national accreditation body against **ISO/IEC 17021-1**, which sets requirements for bodies
providing audit and certification of management systems, and **ISO/IEC 27006-1:2024**, which sets
the ISMS-specific requirements. CloudNimbus's certification body is **Northgate Certification
Services, Ltd.**, accredited by **ANAB**, with lead auditor Ingrid Halvorsen and auditor Tomás
Ferreira.

Accreditation is also what makes the certification body's own independence enforceable: under
ISO/IEC 17021-1 a certification body **may not provide consultancy on the management system it
certifies**. Northgate cannot help CloudNimbus build the ISMS and then certify it, cannot tell
CloudNimbus what the answer should be at Stage 1 and then mark it at Stage 2, and cannot recommend
a solution to a nonconformity it raised. This is a feature and it is also a cost: the certification
body is the one external party in the programme that cannot be asked for help. 01.09 sets out how
that cost was accepted rather than avoided.

The initial certification audit has two stages. **Stage 1** is a review of documented information
and of readiness — the scope, the policy, the risk assessment and treatment, the Statement of
Applicability, the internal audit and management review records — conducted on-site or remotely,
and it determines whether Stage 2 can usefully proceed. **Stage 2** evaluates the implementation
and effectiveness of the ISMS, on site, by sampling. A certificate is valid for **three years**,
subject to **annual surveillance audits**, the first of which must be conducted **within twelve
months of the certification decision**, with a **recertification audit** in the third year.
CloudNimbus's Stage 1 is scheduled for **2026-10-12 to 2026-10-14** and Stage 2 for **2026-11-30 to
2026-12-04**. Stage 2 sits inside the SOC 2 observation window; that collision is accepted
deliberately in ADR-0005 and explained in 01.06.

## 7. Findings, and what each obliges

| Finding | Definition | Obligation |
|---|---|---|
| **Major nonconformity** | A failure that raises significant doubt about the ability of the ISMS to achieve its intended results — including the absence or total breakdown of a required process, or a number of minor nonconformities against one requirement showing a systemic failure | **Blocks certification** until correction and corrective action are submitted, and verified by the certification body |
| **Minor nonconformity** | An isolated lapse that does not raise significant doubt about the system as a whole | Correction and corrective action required, with a plan accepted by the certification body; does not by itself block certification |
| **Opportunity for improvement** | An observation that something could be done better | **Not a nonconformity. Carries no obligation** — the organisation may act on it or decline to |

The last row is stated deliberately. Treating opportunities for improvement as obligations is a
common self-inflicted wound: it inflates the corrective action backlog with items that have no
nonconformity behind them, and it trains the organisation to treat auditor commentary as
instruction. CloudNimbus logs every opportunity for improvement, assesses it, and records a
reasoned decision — including, where appropriate, a decision to take no action.

Note also the vocabulary boundary. **Nonconformity is an ISO word.** A SOC 2 finding is an
**exception** or a **deviation**. The two arise under different regimes and have different
consequences. This programme does not swap them, including in internal shorthand.

## 8. What was considered and deferred

**ISO/IEC 27701**, the privacy information management extension, was considered and deferred under
**DEC-111**, decided by Tobias Lund on 2026-03-18. CloudNimbus is taking on the Privacy trust
services category in the same year it builds its first ISMS, and stacking a second management
system extension onto an organisation not yet certified against the base standard would put the
base certification at risk to gain an assurance product no customer had asked for. It is deferred,
not rejected, and belongs to a later programme.
