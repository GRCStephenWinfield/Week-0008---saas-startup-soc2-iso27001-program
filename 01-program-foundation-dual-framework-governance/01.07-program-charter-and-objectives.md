## 1. Purpose and authority

This charter is the instrument by which the board of CloudNimbus, Inc. delegated authority to run a single
assurance programme producing two separate deliverables: a SOC 2 Type II examination report issued by an
independent CPA firm, and an ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate issued by an accredited certification body. The
board approved the budget and authorised the programme on 2026-01-19 (MS-01, DEC-101, ADR-0001). Everything
downstream in this repository — the scope decisions in Phase 02, the risk work in Phase 03, the control
library in Phase 04 — derives its authority from this document and may not quietly exceed it.

A charter earns its place only if it can be used to refuse things. This one is written so that it can be.
It states what the programme will produce, what it will not produce, who may decide what, what money is
committed, and what will count as success. Where the programme is asked to do something outside those
boundaries, the answer is not a negotiation between the Compliance Manager and whoever asked; it is a
change to this charter, approved by the same people who approved the original.

The programme is named the **Trust &amp; Assurance Programme**. It is sponsored by Marisol Vega, Chief
Financial Officer, who owns the budget and both external engagements. It is owned operationally by Karim
Haddad, VP Security &amp; Trust, and managed day to day by Rahul Bhargava, Compliance Manager. Those three
roles are not interchangeable, and section 4 explains why the separation matters more than it might appear
to in a 187-person company.

## 2. Scope of the programme

The programme covers the work required to place two assurance products in front of CloudNimbus's customers
and prospects.

The first is a **SOC 2 Type II examination report** over all five trust services categories — Security,
Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy — covering a six-month observation window of
2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31. The examination is performed under the AICPA attestation standards — AT-C section
105, concepts common to all attestation engagements, and AT-C section 205, *Assertion-Based Examination
Engagements* — against the applicable trust services criteria in TSP section 100, with the system
description prepared against the description criteria in DC section 200. The product of that engagement is
the service auditor's **opinion**. It is not a certificate, and no one at the conclusion of it will be
"SOC 2 certified", because no such status exists.

The second is an **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate** covering the whole organisation, granted by Northgate
Certification Services, Ltd., an ANAB-accredited certification body, following a Stage 1 and Stage 2 audit.
Conformity is assessed against clauses 4 to 10 of the standard. Annex A is a reference set consulted under
clause 6.1.3 c) and dispositioned in the Statement of Applicability under clause 6.1.3 d); it is not the
requirements, and the certificate is not granted for having implemented ninety-three controls.

Both deliverables are served by one control library, one risk assessment, one policy suite and one evidence
store. That integration is the subject of 01.06 and is recorded in ADR-0001.

**Expressly out of scope.** The programme does not deliver ISO/IEC 27701; it was considered and deferred by
Tobias Lund on 2026-03-18 (DEC-111). It does not extend the SOC 2 system boundary to CloudNimbus's corporate
back-office systems that do not serve the platform, nor to the professional services and implementation
offering. It does not produce a combined SOC 2 / ISO 27001 report, because there is no such artefact; there
is one programme and two deliverables, and any communication that blurs them is a communication this
programme will correct. And neither deliverable is a determination of compliance with any law — not the
GDPR, not the CCPA or CPRA, not any state privacy statute. Legal compliance is Tobias Lund's remit as
General Counsel and is informed by, but not discharged by, this programme.

### 3.1 OBJ-01 states an outcome CloudNimbus does not control

OBJ-01 is written in the form of a target, and it should not be. Read literally, it commits the programme to
producing an unmodified opinion. The programme cannot commit to that, and the charter says so here rather
than letting the ambiguity sit in a tracker for fourteen months.

The opinion in Section I of a SOC 2 report is the service auditor's. Meredith Vance, CPA, the engagement
partner at Ashcombe &amp; Doyle LLP, forms it on the basis of her firm's own procedures, her own evaluation
of whether the controls stated in management's description were suitably designed and operated effectively
throughout the period to meet the applicable trust services criteria, and her own professional judgement
about the severity and pervasiveness of anything her tests find. There are four forms that opinion can take
— unmodified, qualified, adverse, or a disclaimer. Which one appears is not a CloudNimbus decision, is not
negotiable, and is not something that can be brought back on track by a corrective action plan in December.

What CloudNimbus does control is the design and the operation of the controls that earn the opinion, the
completeness and accuracy of the system description it writes, and the quality of the evidence trail those
controls leave behind. Those are the things the programme is actually managing, and they are what OBJ-03,
OBJ-04 and OBJ-06 measure directly.

So OBJ-01 is recorded as a target and **tracked as a forecast**, not as a deliverable with an owner who can
be held to it in the way OBJ-07 holds Marisol Vega to a number she does control. The distinction is not
pedantry. A programme that believes it can decide its own audit opinion is a programme that will spend the
last month of the window arguing with its auditor about the characterisation of a deviation instead of
fixing the control that produced it. It will also, predictably, begin to treat the auditor as an obstacle to
a committed outcome rather than as the independent party whose judgement is the entire value of the product.
CloudNimbus has seen the shape of that failure already: the 2025 Type I was unmodified, and an unmodified
Type I is a statement about design as of a single date, with no tests of operating effectiveness behind it
and therefore no possibility of a test exception. Reading it as a prediction of the Type II outcome would be
exactly the error OBJ-01 invites.

One further note on OBJ-01's wording. It says "is able to express an unmodified opinion". That phrasing is
deliberate. It describes a state of the control environment that would permit an unmodified opinion, which
is a thing CloudNimbus can work towards, rather than the issuance of one, which is not. Test exceptions
disclosed in Section IV do not automatically modify an opinion; the auditor modifies only where the
deviations mean a criterion was not met. An unmodified opinion and a report containing exceptions are not
mutually exclusive, and this charter does not pretend otherwise.

OBJ-02 carries a lighter version of the same problem. The certification decision belongs to Northgate, and
ISO itself certifies no one. The date target of 2027-01-31 is a plan, contingent on the certification body's
own scheduling and on the resolution of any nonconformity raised at Stage 2.

## 4. Governance structure

Three standing bodies govern the programme. Their cadence is fixed by this charter and cannot be relaxed
without a charter amendment.

| Body | Cadence | Chair | Secretary | Standing membership | Primary function |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trust Committee | Monthly | Marisol Vega | Rahul Bhargava (non-voting) | Voting members: Karim Haddad, Nathan Oyelaran, Junia Okonkwo, Wes Delacroix, Tobias Lund, Hannah Brill, Ana-Sofia Cruz | Executive direction, budget, scope changes, objective tracking |
| Trust Working Group | Weekly | Rahul Bhargava | — | Karim Haddad, Junia Okonkwo, Wes Delacroix, Grete Lindqvist, Devon Ashby, plus control owners by rotation | Delivery, blockers, evidence status, remediation |
| Audit &amp; Risk Committee | Quarterly | Lorraine Kessler, independent non-executive director — **the only voting member** | Rahul Bhargava (non-voting) | Simone Bracco attends as Fairmount Ventures board observer, non-voting. Elise Fontaine, Marisol Vega and Karim Haddad attend **by invitation and not as members** | Independent challenge, internal audit programme, assurance over management's reporting |

Lorraine Kessler is not a member of the Trust Committee and does not attend it. That absence is
deliberate rather than an oversight of diary management: she chairs the body that provides independent
challenge to management's decisions, and a person who has sat in the management forum where a decision was
taken is a weaker challenger of it afterwards.

The Audit &amp; Risk Committee may convene at the chair's discretion, including out of cycle, and may meet
the outsourced internal auditor with no member of management present. That right is written into
Camberwell Risk Partners' engagement terms rather than left to custom.

Two external cadences sit alongside them. A **service-auditor checkpoint** with Ashcombe &amp; Doyle runs
monthly from 2026-04. A **certification-body checkpoint** with Northgate occurs at Stage 1 and Stage 2 only,
and at no other time — Northgate may not consult on the ISMS it will certify, and a standing monthly call
with the certification body would be indistinguishable from consultancy. A **sub-processor review** runs
quarterly under Tobias Lund. The reasoning behind both external boundaries is set out in 01.09.

### 4.1 What the Audit &amp; Risk Committee actually is, stated without flattery

CloudNimbus is not SEC-registered and carries no SOX obligation, so nothing requires it to have an audit
committee at all. Having decided to have one, the charter has to be honest about what it is, because the
composition above will not support the claim usually attached to a body with this name.

A 187-person private company cannot staff an independent audit committee in the sense a listed company
means. The pool of people who are both independent of management and competent to challenge an ISMS
programme is, at this size, one person: Lorraine Kessler. A committee with one voting member is not a
committee. Calling it one and then asserting that it constitutes structural independence would be exactly
the kind of paper governance this programme is supposed to be an argument against.

What independence is genuinely available here is narrower and worth stating precisely. There is one
non-executive chair with a direct line to the outsourced internal auditor. There are engagement terms with
Camberwell Risk Partners that give that auditor access to her without management in the room, so that a
finding management would prefer not to be raised has somewhere to go that does not pass through Karim
Haddad. And there is her ability to convene out of cycle, which means the quarterly cadence is a floor
rather than a constraint. Those three things are real, they are contractual rather than customary, and they
are the whole of it.

They are also thinner than the structure a mature company would have, and the thinness has a specific
shape: the arrangement depends materially on one person. If Lorraine Kessler resigns, is unavailable for a
quarter, or simply declines to press an uncomfortable point, there is no second independent voice to carry
it and no quorum mechanism that means anything. The charter does not claim otherwise. The position is
revisited when the board expands — the addition of a second non-executive director is the trigger — and
until then the programme carries the exposure rather than describing it away. It is recorded as a known gap
in 01.13.

The consequence for the RACI in 01.08 is that the ARC's accountability for two activities should be read
for what it does rather than for what the word "committee" implies. It does not mean management cannot
overrule the committee; with this composition, a determined executive team plainly could. It means internal
audit does not report to the person whose management system it audits. That is a smaller claim and it is
one the structure actually supports.

## 5. Decision rights and escalation

| Decision type | Decided by | Escalates to |
|---|---|---|
| Day-to-day delivery, sequencing, evidence approach | Rahul Bhargava | Karim Haddad |
| Control design and control owner assignment | Karim Haddad | Trust Committee |
| Change to either deliverable's scope (categories, ISMS boundary, observation window) | Trust Committee | Audit &amp; Risk Committee, then board |
| Spend that can be met from the contingency line without breaching the envelope | Marisol Vega | Trust Committee (noted) |
| Spend that would exceed the approved envelope | Board | — |
| Engagement or replacement of any external assurance party | Marisol Vega | Audit &amp; Risk Committee |
| Acceptance of a risk rated High | Trust Committee | Audit &amp; Risk Committee |
| Anything affecting the independence of a party in 01.09 | Audit &amp; Risk Committee | Board |

Three escalation thresholds are absolute. Any proposal to narrow the trust services categories, to narrow
the ISMS boundary, or to move the observation window goes to the Audit &amp; Risk Committee before it goes
anywhere else, because each of those changes what an external reader of the resulting deliverable would
understand it to cover. Any proposal that would put an external party on both sides of an independence
boundary — the readiness advisor auditing its own work, the certification body advising on the ISMS —
goes to the ARC and can be refused there. And any commitment beyond $1,400,000 is a board matter, not a
sponsor matter, because the board approved the envelope and only the board can raise it.

Resourcing is **4.6 FTE-equivalents**, and because a single aggregate number invites the reader to imagine
whatever shape suits them, the allocation is set out in full.

| Function | FTE | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| VP Security &amp; Trust — Karim Haddad | 0.6 | Programme ownership, ARC reporting, auditor relationship |
| Compliance Manager — Rahul Bhargava | 1.0 | Full-time programme management and evidence architecture |
| Engineering (Platform) — allocated | 1.1 | Control implementation across 63 microservices |
| Infrastructure &amp; SRE — allocated | 0.9 | Availability, backup, DR, access review execution |
| Product (Calculation Engine) — allocated | 0.4 | Processing integrity control design and reconciliation |
| Data Platform — allocated | 0.3 | Retention, deletion and data lifecycle controls |
| Legal / DPO — allocated | 0.2 | DPAs, sub-processor assurance, privacy commitments |
| People — allocated | 0.1 | Screening, onboarding/offboarding, awareness training |
| **Total** | **4.6** | Against a 187-person organisation |

Three features of that table deserve to be read rather than skimmed. Only one person on it is full-time:
Rahul Bhargava at 1.0. The programme owner is at **0.6**, not at full allocation — Karim Haddad continues
to run a security function while running this, and the charter would rather say so than describe him as
dedicated and then be surprised when he is not. And there is deliberately **no line for the security
function as a whole**. The other seven people in Security are not allocated to the programme; they continue
to run security operations, because a company that stops detecting and responding for fourteen months in
order to document that it detects and responds has misunderstood the exercise. That is a genuine
constraint, not a rounding decision, and it means the largest pool of relevant expertise in the company is
substantially unavailable to the work.

The largest single allocation after Rahul Bhargava is engineering at 1.1 FTE spread across a 78-person
organisation, which is a way of saying that no engineer is on this programme and every engineer is
occasionally on it. That is the resourcing model most likely to fail under delivery pressure, and PR-02
records it.

Four point six FTE is small for a five-category examination and a whole-organisation ISMS, and the
programme's own delivery risk register says so at PR-01.

Two features of this budget are worth naming. The first is that B-08, internal labour, is the largest single
line at $612,000 — larger than every external fee combined. This is what an integrated programme actually
costs, and showing it prevents the familiar distortion in which a programme is described by its audit fee
and then staffed as though the audit fee were the work. The second is that B-02, B-03 and B-04 are three
separate lines because they are three separate firms. A single provider bundling readiness, internal audit
and certification was available and was refused; 01.09 sets out that argument and records it as ADR-0004.
The separation shows up here as cost, and that is the honest place for it to show up.

## 7. Success criteria

The programme is successful if, at its conclusion, four things are true. A SOC 2 Type II examination report
exists covering all five categories for the full six-month window, and it is a report CloudNimbus is willing
to hand to a customer under NDA without a covering explanation. An ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate exists,
covering the whole organisation, issued by an accredited certification body, with no open major
nonconformity. The evidence behind both was produced once and used twice, at or above the 70% threshold in
OBJ-03. And the enterprise security-questionnaire turnaround has fallen from 11.4 days to three or fewer,
because that is the commercial reason the board funded any of this.

There is a fifth criterion that cannot be measured but is stated anyway: that the programme's own account of
what happened is accurate. A report with exceptions honestly described is a better outcome than a report
with a thinner scope and nothing to disclose, and this charter would rather be judged on the first.

ratified, and no amendment may be made retrospectively to accommodate something that has already happened.
