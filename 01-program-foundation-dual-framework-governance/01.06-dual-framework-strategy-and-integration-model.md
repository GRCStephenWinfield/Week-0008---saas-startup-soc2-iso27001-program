## 1. Purpose

CloudNimbus is pursuing a SOC 2 Type II examination and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification across the
same fourteen months — board approval on 2026-01-19 to the closing board and Audit &amp; Risk
Committee report on 2027-03-11 — with 4.6 FTE-equivalents and eight people in Security. Two
programmes would
mean two control sets, two evidence collections, two sets of interviews with the same engineers,
two policy suites drifting apart and two calendars competing for the same quarter. One programme
risks the opposite failure: blurring two genuinely different assurance products and satisfying
neither auditor.

This document sets out the model that avoids both — **one control library, one evidence store, two
deliverables** — and, more importantly, the places where integration stops. It records
**ADR-0001**, decided by the Board at kickoff on 2026-01-19 and logged as **DEC-101**.

## 2. Two products that are not interchangeable

The single most useful thing a programme like this can do early is refuse to treat the two
frameworks as competing brands of the same thing. They are different in kind.

| Statement | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **There is no "SOC 2 certification."** SOC 2 produces a CPA's **opinion**, not a certificate. Nobody is "SOC 2 certified" | The single most common error in this space |
| **ISO 27001 produces a certificate, not an opinion** | The two assurance products are not interchangeable, and neither is "stronger" |
| **SOC 2 assesses controls against criteria. ISO 27001 certifies a management system.** You can hold a valid ISO 27001 certificate with Annex A controls excluded; you cannot pass a SOC 2 examination with a trust services criterion unaddressed | This asymmetry drives the whole integration design |
| **There is no such thing as a combined SOC 2 / ISO 27001 report.** One evidence programme, **two separate deliverables**: an examination report and a certificate | State this explicitly; do not blur it |
| **A framework mapping is an assertion by the mapper, not an authority.** No regulator or standards body blesses a TSC↔Annex A crosswalk | CloudNimbus's mapping is CloudNimbus's, and its defensibility is its own problem |
| **Neither is a determination of compliance with any law** — not GDPR, not the CCPA/CPRA, not any state privacy statute | Standing legal framing, non-negotiable |
| **Scope boundaries differ by nature.** SOC 2 scope is a **system** (infrastructure, software, people, procedures, data serving a service). ISO scope is an **ISMS boundary**, which is organisational. Forcing them to coincide is an error | The Phase 02 argument |

The third row is the load-bearing one. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 assesses conformity of a **management
system** against clauses 4 to 10; Annex A is a reference set, and a control may be excluded where
the Statement of Applicability justifies it. A SOC 2 examination evaluates whether **controls** met
**criteria**; there is no partial category and no justified exclusion of an applicable criterion.
One framework tolerates a reasoned "not applicable" at the control level; the other does not
tolerate one at the criterion level. Any integration design that ignores this over-builds for ISO
or under-builds for SOC 2.

And to be unambiguous about the output: **there is no combined report.** CloudNimbus will hold a
restricted-use SOC 2 Type II examination report carrying Ashcombe and Doyle's opinion, and a
certificate issued by Northgate Certification Services under ANAB accreditation. Two documents,
two issuers, two lifecycles, two audiences. What is shared is the work behind them.

## 3. One control library

The unified control library, built in Phase 04, contains **148 controls**.

| Population | Count |
|---|---|
| Controls serving both frameworks | 112 |
| Controls serving SOC 2 only | 21 |
| Controls serving ISO/IEC 27001:2022 only | 15 |
| **Total** | **148** |

A control in this library has one owner, one description, one operating frequency and one evidence
definition, and carries whichever framework references apply to it. It is not written twice with
different vocabulary. The 112 dual-purpose controls are where the integration dividend lives: one
quarterly access review, performed once, evidenced once, serving part of CC6.2 and CC6.3 and part
of A.5.15, A.5.16 and A.5.18 at the same time. The 19-policy suite is built the same way — one
access control policy, carrying the references rather than the duplication.

## 4. Why the overlap is uneven in both directions

The two exclusive populations exist because neither framework is a subset of the other.

**Twenty-one controls serve SOC 2 only.** The largest cluster is the COSO material in CC1. The
Common Criteria are organised against the 17 COSO principles, and much of CC1 is about the control
environment in the internal-control sense — board oversight of the entity's control
responsibilities, organisational structure and reporting lines, commitment to competence, and
holding individuals accountable. ISO/IEC 27001 requires leadership commitment under clause 5.1 and
role assignment under clause 5.3, but it does not carry the COSO control environment as a control
set, and Annex A has nothing that maps cleanly onto it. CC2's communication criteria are similar:
communication of control responsibilities and of matters affecting the functioning of internal
control is broader than clause 7.4 and broader than A.6.3 awareness training.

**PI1 has no clean Annex A counterpart, and the overstated version of that claim is worth
resisting.** It reaches Annex A in one place: **A.8.26 application security requirements**, whose
implementation guidance in ISO/IEC 27002:2022 covers input validation, output validation and
transaction integrity. Part of the PI1 population will map there — the controls over whether inputs
to the calculation engine are complete and well-formed, whether outputs are validated before they
leave through the integration tier, and whether a transaction that begins completes or rolls back.
Claiming no overlap at all would be a mapping error in the other direction, and this programme is
as interested in avoiding those as in avoiding forced mappings.

What no Annex A control addresses is whether **a derived amount is arithmetically correct**. A.8.26
asks whether an application validates what it receives and what it emits; it does not ask whether
the overtime premium the engine computed for a night shift crossing a period boundary is the right
number. That question — accuracy of derivation, and the reconciliation that would detect an error
in it — is what **PI1.2 through PI1.5** are about, and it is the reason Processing Integrity is
real at CloudNimbus rather than decorative. So most of the PI1 population is **SOC 2 only**: a
smaller share maps to A.8.26, and the remainder has no counterpart because information security
control sets are not written to ask whether the arithmetic is right.

**Fifteen controls serve ISO only.** Several are Annex A controls with no criterion they naturally
answer. **A.5.7 threat intelligence** requires collection and analysis of information about
information security threats; nothing in the trust services criteria asks for it, though a mature
CC7.1 or CC7.2 implementation might use it. **A.5.30 ICT readiness for business continuity**
addresses ICT continuity planning against business continuity objectives, which sits alongside but
does not coincide with A1.2's availability and recovery focus. **A.8.23 web filtering** requires
management of access to external websites, which no trust services criterion requires. Others exist
because the ISMS itself needs operating: the clause 9.2 internal audit programme, the clause 9.3
management review, Statement of Applicability maintenance under clause 6.1.3, the clause 10.2
corrective action process. That is management system machinery, and a SOC 2 examination does not
require a management system.

The uneven counts are therefore a finding, not an accounting artefact. If the library had come out
symmetrical, someone would have been forcing mappings.

## 5. A mapping is an assertion by the mapper

**No regulator, standards body or accreditation authority blesses a crosswalk between the trust
services criteria and Annex A.** The AICPA does not publish one that binds anyone. ISO does not.
ANAB does not. Every mapping in circulation — vendor mappings, consultancy mappings, the ones in
compliance automation platforms, and CloudNimbus's — is **an assertion by whoever made it**, and
its authority is exactly the quality of the reasoning behind it.

Three rules follow, and they govern the Phase 04 library.

**First, CloudNimbus owns the defensibility of its own mapping.** Neither auditor is obliged to
accept it. The service auditor evaluates whether CloudNimbus's controls meet the criteria; if the
answer to CC6.6 is "we mapped it to A.8.20 network security", that is a reference, not an answer.
The certification body evaluates conformity with clauses 4 to 10 and the Statement of
Applicability; a note saying "this also covers CC6.6" is of no interest to it.

**Second, the reasoning is documented per mapped pair, not per framework.** Each of the 112 dual
controls carries a short statement of why it satisfies the specific criterion and the specific
Annex A control it claims, and where it only partially does so, what else is needed. A control
matrix with two columns of identifiers and no reasoning is a claim nobody can check — including the
person who wrote it.

**Third, a mapping is never a substitute for a test.** Evidence that a control operated is what
either auditor examines. A mapping tells you which questions a piece of evidence might help answer;
it does not answer them.

## 6. One evidence store — and the limit of it

**DEC-112**, decided by Rahul Bhargava on 2026-03-25, established a single shared evidence store
rather than one per framework. Artefacts are collected once, tagged with the control they evidence,
the period they cover and the frameworks they serve, and retrieved by either audit. The
benefit is intended to be quantified rather than asserted, and OBJ-03 states the measure — the
share of evidence artefacts serving both deliverables, targeted at 70% or better. The actual figure
is Phase 09's to report, and Phase 01 does not have it.

The limit is more interesting than the benefit: **the same artefact can serve both audits while
answering two different evidential propositions.** A completed quarterly access review supports,
for the service auditor, the proposition that the control operated on each occasion across a
defined period, tested by sampling from a population; and, for the certification body, the
proposition that the ISMS is operating as described and the risk treatment is being carried out.
The first is a statement about instances, the second about a system. An evidence store designed
only for the second — narrative summaries, a representative example, a screenshot of the most
recent cycle — will not survive the first. The store is therefore designed for the harder
proposition, the SOC 2 one, on the principle that evidence sufficient for a Type II sample is
always sufficient for a Stage 2 sample and the converse is not true. That is the direct lesson of
ML-1 in the prior Type I management letter, and the reason programme risk **PR-04** exists.

## 7. Two scopes, deliberately not identical

The SOC 2 scope is a **system**: the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform — the multi-tenant production
service across all three AWS regions, comprising the web application, the customer admin console,
the iOS and Android mobile apps, the calculation engine, the integration and API tier, and the
infrastructure, people, procedures and data that support them. It **excludes** the corporate
back-office systems that do not serve the platform, such as the finance ledger and the CRM as a
system of record for sales pipeline, and it excludes the professional services and implementation
offering.

The ISMS scope under clause 4.3 is the **whole organisation** — all 187 staff, all locations
including home working, all information assets — recorded as **DEC-103** and **ADR-0003**.

These two boundaries do not coincide, and making them coincide would have damaged one of them.
Narrowing the ISMS to platform engineering was the cheaper option and was refused for two reasons.
The first is honesty: in a 187-person remote-first company, Sales and Customer Success staff
routinely handle customer data, and a boundary drawn around engineering would exclude the people
most likely to mishandle it while certifying the people least likely to. The second is commercial:
a certificate whose scope statement reads "platform engineering function" invites the next question
rather than closing it. Conversely, widening the SOC 2 system to match the ISMS would drag the
finance ledger and the implementation practice into a description of a service they do not
provide.

So the model holds two boundaries at once, and the library reconciles them at the control level
rather than the boundary level: a control may be in the ISMS and out of the system, or in both, and
the library records which. Phase 02 draws both boundaries formally.

## 8. Sequencing, and the collision that was accepted

**DEC-106**, decided by Karim Haddad on 2026-02-18, set the Type II observation window at
**2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31** — six months. Six was chosen over three because a three-month window
gives a quarterly control one occurrence and therefore a population of one; and over twelve because
the enterprise-tier pipeline could not wait two further quarters. Six months gives every quarterly
control two occurrences and every monthly control six.

**DEC-107**, decided the same day, accepted that **ISO Stage 2 falls inside that window.** Stage 2
is scheduled for 2026-11-30 to 2026-12-04; the window closes on 2026-12-31. The alternative was to
move Stage 2 into the first quarter of 2027, which would have pushed the certification decision
past the point where it was commercially useful and stacked it against SOC 2 fieldwork in the same
weeks.

The risk accepted is precise, recorded as **PR-05** and argued in **ADR-0005**. Anything Northgate
raises at Stage 2 — a nonconformity of any grade — arises **inside** the period the service auditor
is examining, and is **contradictory evidence** the service auditor must consider. The natural place
for that consideration is the monitoring criteria, CC4.1 and CC4.2: if an independent third party
identifies a deficiency during the period, the question is whether CloudNimbus's own monitoring
controls were operating such that CloudNimbus would have found it. There is also a disclosure
dimension, since material information about the period known to management is not something a
description may simply omit.

The programme's position is that this is a risk worth accepting *knowingly*, and the knowing part
is what makes it defensible. It was minuted at the Trust Committee, escalated to the Audit and Risk
Committee, and written into an ADR nine months before Stage 2. A programme that discovers this
collision in December is explaining itself to its auditor; a programme that documented it in
February is demonstrating CC4.1 in the act of accepting it.

## 9. What integration deliberately does not do

Three things the shared model may not do, stated because each is a plausible drift.

It does not let **one auditor rely on the other's work**. Northgate takes no comfort from the
existence of a SOC 2 engagement, and Ashcombe and Doyle does not treat a certificate as evidence.
Each forms its own conclusion under its own standards.

It does not create a **single point of external contact**. Under ISO/IEC 17021-1 the certification
body may not consult on the ISMS it certifies; the CPA firm may not design, implement or operate
the controls it examines. The four-way separation is set out in 01.09 and ADR-0004, and integration
operates strictly on CloudNimbus's side of those lines.

It does not produce **shared language in customer communications**. The trust centre describes a
restricted-use examination report available under NDA, and separately a certificate with a scope
statement, an issuing body and a validity period. It does not describe "our compliance
certifications", and it never describes CloudNimbus as SOC 2 certified, because that thing does not
exist.

## 10. Decision record

**ADR-0001 — Dual-framework integrated operating model: one control library, one evidence store,
two deliverables.** Decided by the Board on 2026-01-19; logged as DEC-101. Alternatives considered
and rejected: two independent programmes sequenced across successive years, affordable in attention
but not in calendar and doubling the evidence burden on the same engineers; and a single "unified
compliance framework" treating the two as one target, rejected because it requires exactly the
blurring that section 2 refuses. Related records: DEC-103 and ADR-0003, DEC-106, DEC-107 and
ADR-0005, DEC-112, and ADR-0002.
