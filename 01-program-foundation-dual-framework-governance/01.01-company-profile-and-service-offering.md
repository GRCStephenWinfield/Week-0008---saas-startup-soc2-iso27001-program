## 1. Purpose

This document describes CloudNimbus, Inc. as an assurance subject rather than as a company. The
distinction matters. A company profile written for a website answers *what do you sell*. A company
profile written for the foundation of a SOC 2 examination and an ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification
has to answer harder questions: what does the service actually do to data, who is exposed if it
does it wrong, where does the processing physically happen, and which of the assurance obligations
that follow are genuinely ours rather than someone else's. Everything in the two decision chapters
that follow — 01.05 on trust services category selection and 01.06 on the integration model —
depends on the facts set out here.

Two of these lines do more work than the rest. The company is **not SEC-registered**, so there is
no Sarbanes-Oxley obligation and no internal control over financial reporting programme to
piggyback on; whatever control discipline exists in this organisation, this programme has to build
it. And the company has **eight people in Security** serving a platform used by 1.24 million
individuals. The ratio is the reason the operating model in 01.06 is built around doing work once
rather than twice.

## 3. What the platform does

The CloudNimbus Workforce Platform is a multi-tenant B2B SaaS product sold to employers. It covers
five functional domains. **Time capture** records when an employee starts and stops work, through a
web application, a customer-branded admin console, and iOS and Android mobile apps.
**Scheduling** publishes shifts, manages swaps and coverage, and enforces the employer's rules
about consecutive hours, rest periods and minimum staffing. **Absence and leave accrual** tracks
entitlement, carries balances forward, and applies the accrual formulas the employer configures.
**Expense capture and policy evaluation** takes receipts, categorises them, and evaluates each item
against the employer's expense policy. And the **calculation engine** turns all of that into
numbers that leave the platform: gross earnings components and reimbursement amounts, delivered
through the integration and API tier into the customer's payroll provider.

The customer is the employer. The people whose data the platform holds are the employer's
employees, and they interact with CloudNimbus directly, every working day, through the mobile app.
That triangle — CloudNimbus, the employer who contracts, and the 1.24 million individuals who do
not — is the structural fact that drives the Privacy argument in 01.05.

## 4. What it calculates, and why that is the whole point

Most SaaS stores and retrieves. CloudNimbus stores, retrieves and then **derives**. The calculation
engine computes overtime eligibility and overtime premium, shift differentials for night and
weekend work, accrual balances as entitlement earns and is consumed, and reimbursement amounts
after policy evaluation. Those outputs are not reports for a human to interpret. They are inputs to
a payroll run, and once a payroll run executes, the number becomes a payment into an individual's
bank account.

This is why the Processing Integrity category is real here rather than decorative. Processing
integrity is about the completeness, validity, accuracy, timeliness and authorisation of
processing. In a system that files documents, that framing is close to empty and most service
organisations sensibly leave the category out. In a system where a rounding rule, an incorrectly
applied accrual carry-forward or a differential applied to the wrong shift boundary produces a
wrong paycheque for a real person, it is the product. Grete Lindqvist, Principal Product Manager
for the Calculation Engine, owns Processing Integrity for exactly this reason: the criterion is
about the thing she builds, not about a control layer bolted around it.

The consequence for the risk model, developed in Phase 03, is that the worst case for CloudNimbus
is not an outage. It is a wrong number at scale, or a cross-tenant disclosure of compensation data.
An outage is visible, bounded and recoverable. A systematically wrong overtime calculation can run
for months, be paid out, and be discovered by a state labour claim.

## 5. The data the platform holds

| Data category | Description | Assurance significance |
|---|---|---|
| Employment records | Employee identifiers, role, cost centre, employment status, manager relationships | Personal information; drives access decisions inside the customer tenant |
| Hours and shift data | Clock-in and clock-out events, breaks, schedule adherence | Input to the calculation engine; completeness and timeliness are Processing Integrity concerns |
| Pay rates and differentials | Base rate, premium rates, differential rules | Confidential compensation data under the MSA |
| Accrual balances | Leave entitlement earned, consumed, carried | Derived data; an error compounds silently across periods |
| Expense items and receipts | Uploaded images, amounts, categories, policy evaluation outcomes | Contains incidental personal information in receipt images |
| Calculated outputs | Gross earnings components, reimbursement amounts | The output that reaches payroll; the Processing Integrity subject matter |
| Geolocation at clock-in | Device location captured by the mobile app at the moment of clock-in | Personal information collected **directly from the data subject** |

One thing CloudNimbus does **not** hold: employee bank account details or payment tokens. The
reimbursement and earnings figures are delivered to the customer's payroll provider, and the
payroll provider holds the banking relationship and makes the payment. CloudNimbus computes the
amount; it never moves the money. This is stated explicitly because it is routinely assumed
otherwise in security questionnaires, and because it changes the shape of the risk: the exposure
here is a wrong amount or a disclosed amount, not a diverted payment.

Geolocation deserves its own sentence. The mobile app captures device location at clock-in, on the
employer's instruction and to serve the employer's purpose, but it captures it **from the
individual's own device, through CloudNimbus's own interface**. That is direct collection. It is
the single fact that makes the Privacy category argument in 01.05 hard, and it is the reason the
argument for excluding Privacy was made properly and then refused.

## 6. Footprint, residency and the physical estate

Production runs on AWS only. `us-east-1` is primary, `us-west-2` is the disaster recovery region,
and `eu-central-1` serves the 41 customers whose contracts carry an EU data-residency addendum.
The residency commitment is a contractual obligation with a continuous trigger — personal data at
rest for those customers stays in `eu-central-1` — and it will appear in 01.11 as obligation O8. It
is also the reason the ISMS scope cannot quietly stop at the US border, a point that Phase 08's
internal audit programme has to respect.

The physical estate is almost nothing. The Denver suite is a meeting space of about 4,100 sq ft. It
has no server, no network closet and no comms room, and its internet service is the landlord's
building service. There is no data centre, no rack, no cage, no generator. AWS operates the
facilities in which CloudNimbus's information is processed, and AWS is treated as a subservice
organisation under the carve-out method.

This matters more than it sounds, and it matters in both directions at once, which is why the
fourteen physical controls in Annex A's A.7 theme are the part of the Statement of Applicability
that will take the most argument when Phase 03 comes to write it.

In one direction, a company that operates no facility containing information processing equipment
genuinely does not face some of what A.7 was written for. Supporting utilities, cabling, comms
rooms and loading bays describe a world CloudNimbus does not inhabit, and pretending otherwise
would produce controls with nothing to control.

In the other direction, **"we have no office" is not the same statement as "we have no equipment",
and the second statement would be false.** There are **231 managed endpoints** — 187 staff laptops
and 44 test and lab devices — and every one of them sits somewhere physical: a kitchen table in one
of 31 US states, a rented desk in one of four countries, an airport lounge. Physical security
monitoring, the siting and protection of equipment, storage media and equipment maintenance all
have something to say about that estate, in a modified form rather than not at all. A prohibition
on removable media is an implementation of a storage media control, not a reason to exclude one.
Endpoint maintenance performed under MDM is equipment maintenance.

This document does not state which A.7 controls end up necessary and which do not; that
determination is Phase 03's and appears in the Statement of Applicability under clause 6.1.3 d),
where each exclusion has to carry a justification that survives being read by a Stage 2 auditor who
does not already agree. What Phase 01 fixes is the fact pattern the determination has to work
from — no facility, and 231 pieces of equipment — and the instruction that follows from it: an
exclusion in the A.7 theme has to be argued rather than assumed from the absence of a building.

## 7. The remote-first working model

187 people across 31 US states and 4 countries means the perimeter is the identity layer and the
endpoint, not a building. Every control that a traditional company would implement physically has
to be implemented logically: device management rather than a locked office; conditional access
rather than a network boundary; a prohibition on removable media rather than a media safe. There
is no shoulder-surfing control that consists of a badge reader.

It also means the population that has to absorb control friction is dispersed and cannot be
supervised into compliance. Change management enforced by team convention works in a room and
fails across twelve time zones — a lesson the prior Type I management letter already recorded, and
which 01.04 takes up.

## 8. The commercial driver

CloudNimbus did not begin this programme because it wanted a management system. It began it
because deals were stalling in security review.

Of the 640 customers, **118 are enterprise-tier**, and enterprise-tier contracts carry a delivery
obligation: the SOC 2 report within 30 days of issuance. Prospects in that tier open with a
security questionnaire, and the current mean turnaround to return a completed questionnaire is
**11.4 days**.

That figure needs reading precisely, because it is easy to inflate and the inflated version would
not survive scrutiny. It is **elapsed time, not effort**: eleven days between a prospect sending a
questionnaire and CloudNimbus returning it completed. It is not eleven days of anybody's labour,
and this document does not claim it is. The actual work — finding the answers, assembling them,
having someone qualified check them — is a fraction of that. The rest is the questionnaire sitting
in a queue while Sales waits on Security, or Security waits on Engineering, or somebody waits on
whoever wrote the answer last time.

What that elapsed time costs is not staff hours. It is that **the deal sits in security review for
eleven days**, and a deal in security review is a deal not closing. Answers are assembled by hand,
so answers given in March and answers given in June do not always match; and — the expensive
part — a competitor holding a current examination report clears the same gate in a day by handing
over a document. OBJ-05 targets a reduction of that elapsed mean to three days or fewer, which is
achieved by having something to hand over rather than by making anyone type faster.

The honest framing is that assurance is being bought here as a commercial instrument, and there is
nothing wrong with that as long as it does not distort what the programme says. A programme run to
win deals is tempted to overstate. The countermeasure is structural and appears throughout this
phase: management's assertion is signed by the CEO, not by the compliance team; the readiness
advisor is barred from auditing its own work; and the examination report is restricted-use, given
under NDA to parties with sufficient knowledge to read it, never published as a marketing asset.

## 9. What the programme is for

**CloudNimbus calculates money that reaches individuals, and holds compensation and location data
about 1.24 million people who never chose it as a supplier. The programme exists to make the
controls over that processing good enough to withstand independent examination, and then to obtain
that independent examination — a SOC 2 Type II report carrying a service auditor's opinion, and an
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate from an accredited certification body — so that what CloudNimbus
tells its customers about itself is something a third party has tested.**
