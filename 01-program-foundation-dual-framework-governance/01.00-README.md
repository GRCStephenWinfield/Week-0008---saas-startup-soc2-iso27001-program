## 1. What this phase is

Phase 01 is the foundation of the CloudNimbus Trust and Assurance Programme: a single programme
producing two entirely separate assurance deliverables — a SOC 2 Type II examination report
carrying a CPA firm's opinion, and an ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate issued by an accredited
certification body. It sets out who the company is, what the two frameworks require, what the prior
Type I told us, which trust services categories we chose and why, how one programme serves two
deliverables without pretending they are the same thing, and who is accountable.

Where the programme took a position that a reasonable practitioner could argue with, the argument is
set out on both sides and the decision is recorded with a name and a date. Four of the five Phase 01
architecture decision records exist because something cheaper, faster or narrower was available and
was refused.

## 2. What Phase 01 establishes

Phase 01 fixes what everything downstream depends on and what would be expensive to change later:
the objectives OBJ-01 to OBJ-08; the selection of all five trust services categories, including the
Privacy category argued for exclusion and kept; the ISMS scope as the whole organisation; the
integrated operating model of one control library, one evidence store and two deliverables; the
four-way separation of readiness advisor, internal auditor, certification body and service auditor;
the observation window of 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31 and the knowing acceptance that ISO Stage 2 falls
inside it; the budget and resourcing; and the roles, RACI, stakeholders, obligations O1 to O12 and
the recurring assurance calendar.

## 3. What Phase 01 deliberately does not establish

Phase 01 does not define the SOC 2 system description or the clause 4.3 ISMS boundary in the form
the auditors will read — that is Phase 02, and the two boundaries are deliberately not made
identical. It does not perform the risk assessment or produce the Statement of Applicability; those
belong to Phase 03, and no risk rating or forecast appears here. It asserts that the unified control
library will contain 148 controls, but Phase 04 builds them. The counts of complementary user entity
controls and complementary subservice organisation controls are not stated here because they are an
output of the system description, not an input to it. Phase 01 also leaves one thing deliberately
undefined and says so: the coverage of the clause 9.2 internal audit programme, which CAL-14
describes only as "annual programme, risk-weighted", is deferred to the internal audit charter.
That is a decision, not an oversight, and 01.11 records it as a known gap.

## 6. How to read this phase

Read 01.01 for the business, then 01.02 and 01.03 back to back — they are the reference chapters
for the two frameworks and everything later assumes them. 01.04 explains what the prior Type I did
and did not prove. 01.05 and 01.06 are the two decision chapters, and they are where the argument
lives. 01.07 to 01.12 are the governance mechanics. 01.13 closes the phase and states the entry
criteria for Phase 02.

If you are reading for a single question, the fastest routes are: *what is in scope* — 01.05 and
01.06; *who is accountable* — 01.08; *who is independent of whom, and why it cost money* — 01.09;
*what we are contractually on the hook for* — 01.11; *when* — 01.12.

## 7. Terminology you must not get wrong

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "SOC 2 certified", "SOC 2 certification", "SOC 2 compliant" | A SOC 2 Type II examination report; an unmodified opinion | SOC 2 is an attestation examination performed by a CPA firm. It produces an opinion. There is no certificate and no certifying authority |
| "ISO certified us" | An accredited certification body — here Northgate Certification Services, Ltd., accredited by ANAB — issues the certificate | ISO writes standards. ISO does not certify anyone |
| "The points of focus are the requirements" | Points of focus are considerations that may assist in evaluating whether a criterion is met | An entity need not address every point of focus, and no auditor may treat them as a checklist |
| "Annex A is the requirements" | Conformity is assessed against clauses 4 to 10. Annex A is a reference set of 93 controls | Clause 6.1.3 c) requires comparison against Annex A to check nothing necessary was omitted. That is not the same as compliance with Annex A |
| "We will publish our SOC 2 report" | The report is restricted-use, provided under NDA | Intended for management, user entities during the period, and other specified parties with sufficient knowledge and understanding |
| "Nonconformity" for a SOC 2 finding, "exception" for an ISO finding | **Nonconformity** is ISO. **Exception** or **deviation** is SOC 2 | The two words describe findings under two different regimes with different consequences |

One more, because it is the sentence this programme repeats most often: a test exception disclosed
in Section IV does not automatically modify the opinion. The service auditor modifies only if the
deviations mean an applicable trust services criterion was not met. A report with an unmodified
opinion and disclosed exceptions is a normal, honest report.

Finally, neither deliverable is a determination of compliance with any law — not GDPR, not the
CCPA/CPRA, not any state privacy statute. That framing is standing and non-negotiable.

## 8. The date convention, and the vantage point each phase writes from

Every document in this repository carries the same metadata date, `2026-08-11`, and the same version, 1.0.
**That date is the repository's publication date and nothing more.** It is the day the consolidated record
was issued. It is not the date any document was written, not the date the work it describes was performed,
and — this is the part that matters — **it is not the vantage point from which the document speaks.**

The convention this phase follows is the one that makes a nine-phase portfolio readable at all. **Each
phase is written from its own position in the programme, and speaks as at the close of that phase's work.**
**Phase 01 speaks as at the close of the foundation phase, 2026-03-31** — the month end that follows
DEC-112 of 2026-03-25, the last decision Phase 01 records. Everything in these fourteen documents is
written from that standpoint: decisions taken up to that date are recorded as taken, and everything after
it is recorded as planned, targeted or scheduled.

The reason for adopting a vantage rather than simply writing everything from the publication date is that
**a phase does not know what later phases discover.** Phase 01 is the chapter that chooses a method,
appoints the parties, fixes the calendar and accepts the risks. Phase 03 performs the risk assessment and
determines the Statement of Applicability. Phase 04 builds the control library. Phase 05 runs the technical
testing. Phase 08 conducts the audits. Phase 09 reports the outcomes. If Phase 01 were written from
2026-08-11 it could report the results of Phase 03, and it would then be a document that had decided its
own conclusions before making the argument for them — the exact failure the portfolio is meant to
demonstrate the absence of. A foundation phase that already knows the answer is not a foundation phase; it
is a retrospective wearing one's clothes.

Two consequences follow, and both are deliberate rather than defective.

**A Version 1.0 charter carries a publication date months after its own approval.** The charter in 01.07
was approved on 2026-01-19 and this repository is dated 2026-08-11. Under a live document-control regime
that gap would be an obvious defect, and a reader is right to notice it. Here it is the publication
convention doing its work: the programme's working papers were kept contemporaneously, and the record was
consolidated and issued once. Version 1.0 means "first issue of the record", not "first revision of the
control".

**Nothing in Phase 01 states an outcome that Phase 03 or a later phase produces.** No Statement of
Applicability determination appears here — not which Annex A controls were found necessary, not which were
excluded, not how many. No risk rating, no register population and no forecast of a close position appears
here. No penetration test result appears here, only the decision to commission the test and the reasoning
behind its scope. No management-letter matter is reported closed, because the closure target is 2026-06-30
and Phase 01 stands three months before it. Where a later outcome is relevant, the document names the phase
that will produce it and stops. Statements about what is *planned* for later dates are everywhere and are
expected; statements about what *happened* at later dates are absent by design, and if one has survived,
it is an error rather than a licence.

The same convention governs the other eight phases, each from its own close. A reader who wants the
chronology should read 01.12 and the timeline it carries, not the metadata blocks.
