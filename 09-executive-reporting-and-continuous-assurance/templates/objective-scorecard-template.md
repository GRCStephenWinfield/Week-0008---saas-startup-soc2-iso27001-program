Completed at each objective-setting cycle under **`CNB-C-016`** and at the close of each programme or annual
period, and tabled at the Trust Committee and the Audit &amp; Risk Committee. **`CNB-C-144`** requires each
objective's measure to be **read from the evidence store rather than from a status report**, and requires an
objective whose measure cannot be evaluated from evidence to be given one that can be, or withdrawn, with
the decision minuted.

**The form is built around one rule and every section serves it.**

> **A measure is not changed in the document that scores it.** An objective that misses its target is scored
> against the target it was set. Where the measure turns out to have been the wrong one — which happened to
> **OBJ-03**, and is a real finding rather than an excuse — the better measure is published **beside** the
> score and never **in place of** it, and the re-measurement is referred to the body that sets objectives.
> **A measure changed at scoring time has not been changed; it has been chosen.** ADR-0043.

## 1. The scorecard itself

The scored table carries **four columns and no more**, one row per objective.

| Column | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **ID** | `OBJ-nn`, as set in the charter. Identifiers are permanent and are never reused |
| **Target** | **Quoted from the charter, verbatim**, including the measure. Not paraphrased, and not restated in the units that happen to be available |
| **Actual** | The figure, with its numerator and denominator where it is a proportion, **read from a named artefact in the evidence store** |
| **Verdict** | **Met** or **Missed**. Two values, and the form does not offer a third |

**There is no `Partially met` and no `On track`.** A partial band, once invented, exists only to be used on
the objective that failed — and it will be. `On track` is a status against a plan, not a verdict against a
target, and an objective scored at the close of a period is either at its target or it is not.

**Commentary does not go in the table.** Every objective has a caveat worth stating — an outcome another
party controls, a margin that is not evidence of planning, a measure taken in the quarter the thing it
measures arrived — and each belongs in prose beneath the table where it can be argued with. **A fifth column
of commentary turns a scorecard into an explanation, and the explanation always reads better than the
score.**

## 2. Per-objective working, held behind each row

| Field | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Measure as set | The charter's words, with the date the objective was set |
| Owner as set | The named individual, not a function |
| **Evidence artefact** | The **EV identifier and the evidence class**. `CNB-C-144`'s requirement is not satisfied by a figure somebody computed for the scorecard; it is satisfied by a figure a reader can recompute from a stored artefact |
| **Numerator and denominator** | Both, always, for any proportion. **A percentage without its denominator is a claim about a population nobody has seen** |
| **What the denominator is a property of** | Where the denominator is partly outside the organisation's control, say so. OBJ-03's overlap figure is a property of **two independent request lists** at least as much as of CloudNimbus's evidence architecture, and neither list was CloudNimbus's to write |
| **What the measure does not see** | One line. OBJ-06 measures whether the review happened and cannot see whether it reviewed; OBJ-08 measures completion against a headcount that includes people who cannot complete it |

## 3. Where an objective is missed

| Field | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Order of reporting** | **The miss is reported before it is explained**, and before the objectives that were met. Every explanation available for a missed objective is more comfortable than the number, and a chapter that reaches the number after two paragraphs of context has told the reader how to feel about it first |
| The figure | Stated against the target, with the ratio if the gap is large. **16.9% against 70% is a factor of four and the form says so** |
| **Is the objective wrong, the measure wrong, or the performance wrong?** | The three are different findings and only one of them is a failure of delivery. Answer it explicitly. **Where the answer is "the measure", that is a second finding about the people who wrote the objective, not a mitigation of the first** |
| **Who identified the better measure, and when** | Named. OBJ-03's was identified in Phase 08 **by an adversarial reader**, not by the objective's owner and not by fourteen months of committee tracking, and recording that is the only part of the answer another organisation can use |
| **The better measure** | Published **beside** the score, with its own numerator and denominator, and explicitly **not substituted for it** |
| **Re-measurement** | Referred to the objective-setting occurrence under `CNB-C-016`, with an owner and a register entry. **Not decided here** |

## 4. Where an objective is met

| Field | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Is the outcome the organisation's to deliver?** | If not, say so at the row rather than in a footnote. OBJ-01 measures the form of an opinion, which is the service auditor's; 01.07 §3.1 said at chartering that it should never have been written as a target and the scorecard repeats it rather than banking the green row |
| **What the margin rests on** | A date target met by nine days rests on something. OBJ-02's nine days rest on a major nonconformity closed in forty-four, and **the margin is not evidence of planning** |
| **When the measure was taken** | A measure taken in the first period after the thing it measures arrived is a measure of the arrival. OBJ-05's 2.6 days was measured in the quarter a report first existed |
| **What being met did not achieve** | The sharpest field on the form. OBJ-04 closed all three management letter matters before the window opened, **and two of those matters produced test exceptions eighteen months later.** Closing a management letter point does not close the thing it was about |

## 5. What this form does not do

**It does not aggregate.** There is no score out of eight, no percentage of objectives met and no traffic
light for the programme. **Seven of eight met is a fact about eight independent objectives**, and a single
figure derived from it would be quoted in a board pack within a month and would have no meaning at all — the
same reasoning that keeps an aggregate score out of the risk register.

**It does not withdraw an objective that can be measured.** `CNB-C-144` offers withdrawal for a measure that
cannot be evaluated from evidence. OBJ-03's measure **can** be evaluated from evidence — that is how 16.9%
was produced — so withdrawal was not available, and the form refuses it in that case.

**It does not measure security, and it does not measure assurance.** The objectives measure programme
delivery and, in two cases, the existence of a document another party produced.

**And it does not replace the charter's own success criteria.** 01.07 §7 sets four that can be measured and
a fifth that cannot — that the programme's own account of what happened is accurate. **The four are scored
separately from the objectives and at the same close**, which is 09.11 §6 and is a step this form did not
require until a reader found the omission; the objectives are not a substitute for them, and a scorecard
that scores eight targets while the charter's four go unmarked has picked the instrument that flatters. The
nearest thing this form offers on the fifth is §3's ordering rule: **the miss is reported first, on the
measure it was set against, in the document that scores it.**
