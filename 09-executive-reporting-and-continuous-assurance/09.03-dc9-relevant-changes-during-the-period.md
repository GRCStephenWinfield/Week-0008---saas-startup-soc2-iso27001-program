## 1. The criterion, and why it is the one that gets missed

**DC9 requires the description of the system to disclose relevant changes to the system during the period
covered by the description.** It is one sentence, it has no sub-limbs, and it is the description criterion a
service organisation is most likely to answer badly — not by getting a fact wrong, but by never having
noticed that a fact was a change.

`02.02` §2 named the failure mode in March 2026, before there was a period for anything to change during. A
description assembled at the end of a period out of whatever documents happen to exist by then will
reconstruct DC1 and DC3 comfortably from architecture diagrams. **DC4 and DC9 are the two criteria nobody
writes in advance**, because both require somebody to have been recording events against the criterion while
they happened — incidents as incidents, and changes as changes rather than as ordinary work. Every item in
§3 below was recorded in the phase that produced it, with a date and a decision behind it, and none of them
was discovered while assembling this chapter.

That is not a claim about diligence. It is a claim about sequencing, and it is checkable: the six items
below carry Phase 04, Phase 06 and Phase 07 identifiers and dates, and the one item that is **not** a change
carries a September decision made at source.

## 2. The three-way test

**This is the sharpest rule in the phase and it is stated once, here.**

> **DC9 asks for changes during the period.** The period is 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31, and a change belongs
> in one of three intervals with a different disclosure attaching to each.
>
> **A change before 2026-07-01 is not a DC9 disclosure, however important it was.** The **tenant-predicate
> build check of 2026-05-26** — the remediation for the Critical tenant isolation finding, `R-37`'s
> treatment, and the most consequential engineering change this programme made — **is not here.** It was
> designed, deployed and retested clean on 2026-06-11, three weeks before the window opened. The system the
> description describes is the system as it existed from 2026-07-01, and that change is part of what the
> system **was**, not something that happened to it.
>
> **A change after 2026-12-31 is not a DC9 change either.** It is a **subsequent event** — considered
> through the report date, evaluated for whether it requires adjustment to or disclosure in the description,
> and represented in the representation letter. **`CNB-C-150`'s admission on 2027-01-08 is exactly that**,
> and so is the certificate of 2027-01-22. Both are in the report; neither is in this chapter.
>
> **Three intervals, three different disclosures, and putting a change in the wrong one is not a rounding
> error: it tells a reader the system changed when it did not.** A reader who finds the May build check
> under DC9 concludes that multi-tenant isolation was rebuilt mid-period, in a window whose testing they are
> about to read. A reader who finds `CNB-C-150` there concludes that a control was added to the population
> the examination tested, when it has no population inside the window at all.

**The test is applied to the date the system changed, not to the date somebody decided it would.** That
distinction does real work in §3's last row and is the reason the row is there.

## 3. The disclosure

**Six relevant changes, in date order.**

| # | Change | Date | Why it is relevant |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The ten annual controls re-scheduled under **ADR-0019** operated on their re-scheduled dates rather than their anniversaries | Decided 2026-06-01; effective on each control's re-scheduled occurrence, or on the date it fell due where the occurrence did not take place — all inside the window | The operating cadence of ten controls **stated in the description** was different during the period from what it had been. `04.11` §4.4 committed the programme to disclosing this where a reader would look, before anybody knew whether it would be convenient |
| 2 | The `cnb-security` log archive's EU partition cut over to a bucket in `eu-central-1` under EU-scoped keys | 2026-07-20 | A change to **where a component of the system holds data**, three weeks into the period, on the boundary **O8** and SC-04 make the most expensive one in the register |
| 3 | The `eu-central-1` geolocation capture table rebuilt as a partitioned relation by schema migration | 2026-08-17 | A change to the **data component** — and the change that caused the incident DC4 discloses. It appears under both criteria, and §4 says why that is correct rather than duplicative |
| 4 | **`CNB-C-096` amended** to require the synthetic availability probe to perform a write | Amended 2026-09-15 (DEC-609); write probe deployed 2026-09-19 | **A control stated in the description changed mid-period.** Section IV's test population for it is split: the control operated under the original statement from 2026-07-01 to 2026-09-18 and under the amended statement from 2026-09-19, and it was tested against both |
| 5 | **`CNB-C-149` admitted** to the control library — the independent post-run verification of scheduled deletion against pre-deletion partition counts | 2026-10-28 (DEC-706) | **A control added mid-period**, with a population of **65 of the window's 184 nights** and none at all in the 119 before it |
| 6 | The replacement outbound email delivery sub-processor engaged | Notice issued 2026-10-02; effective 2026-11-05 | A change to the **third parties inside the system boundary**, notified under **O5** at thirty-four days against a thirty-day commitment |

**Row 1 is the row a reader should test this chapter on, because on §2's own rule it looks wrong.** The
decision was taken on 2026-06-01, a month before the window opened, and §2 says a change before 2026-07-01 is
not a DC9 disclosure. The answer is the distinction §2 ends on: **DC9 asks when the system changed, not when
somebody decided it would.** `CNB-C-031`'s natural anniversary was June 2027; it operated on 2026-09-17.
`CNB-C-136`'s was re-scheduled into August 2026 and did not happen at all. **The cadence of each of the ten
changed on the date its re-scheduled occurrence displaced its anniversary — and, for `CNB-C-136`, on the date
that occurrence fell due and did not take place. For all ten the date is inside the window.** The May build
check fails the same test for the opposite reason: it was decided, deployed and retested clean before
2026-06-11, and nothing about the system changed after 2026-07-01 because of it.

**Row 3 appears twice in the description and that is not duplication.** Under **DC9** it is a change to the
data component: a table was rebuilt, in one region, as a different kind of object. Under **DC4** it is the
mechanism of an identified system incident: the migration created a new partitioned relation and left the
old one behind, the RT-02 deletion job kept addressing the emptied original, and for sixty-eight nights it
reported success and deleted nothing. **The two criteria ask different questions about the same event** —
DC9 asks what changed, DC4 asks what an incident was and what it engaged — and a description that filed it
under one of them would have answered the other with silence. `07.03` §8 owns the DC4 disclosure and this
chapter does not restate it.

## 4. SC-01's correction is not a change to the system

**On 2026-09-24, `02.12` was reissued at v1.1 to state the announced-maintenance exclusion that SC-01 has
always carried.** Version 1.0 published the 99.9% monthly commitment without it. The master services
agreement measures availability against elapsed minutes in the calendar month **less any maintenance window
announced at least fourteen days in advance**, and it did so before this programme existed.

**That is a correction to the description of a commitment. It is not a change to the system, and it is not a
change to the commitment.** **DEC-904**, taken by Rahul Bhargava on 2027-02-12, files it as such.

The distinction is the same one §2 draws, drawn in kind instead of in time.

> **Filing it under DC9 would tell a reader that SC-01 changed mid-period.** It did not. What changed was
> CloudNimbus's own statement of it, which was incomplete under **DC2** and was completed. A reader who
> found *SC-01 amended, 2026-09-24* in a list of relevant changes would be entitled to ask what CloudNimbus
> had promised before that date and whether the September availability figure was measured against the old
> promise or the new one — and the honest answer to a question that should never have been raised is that
> both are the same promise and the question is an artefact of the filing.
>
> **A commitment that always carried an exclusion did not acquire one.** The correction is disclosed where
> corrections belong: at source, in the document that carries DC2, with a version number and a decision
> date, so that a reader comparing `02.12` v1.0 with v1.1 can see exactly what was wrong and when it was
> put right.

**The cost of the correction is recorded rather than absorbed.** The exclusion consumed a **171-minute
window notified 2026-08-05** and excluded from August's measurement, and August's `us-east-1` figure of
99.97% is computed with it. Had the description carried the headline commitment and the operating record
carried the exclusion, the two would have disagreed by 171 minutes in a document a customer measures its own
credits against.

## 5. The two that are deliberately not here

**The Statement of Applicability reissue of 2026-10-30 and the ISMS scope statement reissue of 2026-10-28
are not in §3, and their absence is a decision rather than an oversight.**

Both fall squarely inside the period. Both are significant documents. Both were notified to, or considered
against, the certification body's requirements — the scope statement under **O10**, inside the thirty days,
and the Statement of Applicability reissue determined not to be a change to the ISMS, its scope or the
organisation.

**Neither is a change to the system described in a SOC 2 report.** The Statement of Applicability is the
clause 6.1.3 d) record of which Annex A controls are necessary, why, and whether each is implemented; the
scope statement is the clause 4.3 boundary of the information security management system, which is the whole
organisation. **The ISMS boundary and the system boundary are two different objects and neither contains the
other** — the system reaches past the ISMS through AWS and Halcyon Identity, and the ISMS reaches past the
system through corporate functions the platform does not depend on. A description that listed an ISO
artefact among its relevant changes would be asserting that the artefact is part of the described system,
which is the same error `08.12` refused when it declined to put the major nonconformity in Section III.

**Both are disclosed instead in Section V — other information provided by management, not covered by the
service auditor's opinion — where `09.07` carries them.** That is not a demotion. It is the only section in
which an ISO fact can appear in this report without a reader inferring that a CPA firm has expressed a view
about a management system it did not examine.

## 6. What DC9 does not do

**It does not disclose every change.** The window contained **312 changes** promoted through the pipeline
and the description lists six. DC9 asks for **relevant** changes — changes to the system, its boundary, its
components, its commitments or the controls stated in the description — and a description that listed 312
deployments would have buried the six that matter under a change log nobody can read.

**It does not grade the changes it discloses.** Nothing in §3 says a change was an improvement. `CNB-C-149`
was admitted on the evidence of a failure and its own limits were recorded on the face of the decision: 65
nights of 184, an alerting limb that has never fired, and a blind spot on partial non-deletion. **A change
disclosed as a change is a fact; a change disclosed as a remedy is a claim**, and DC9 asks for the first.

**And it does not reach forward.** The 2027 observation window opened on 2027-01-01 and is ten weeks old at
this vantage. Whatever changes it holds belong to a description that does not exist, will be disclosed under
its own DC9, and are not anticipated here.
