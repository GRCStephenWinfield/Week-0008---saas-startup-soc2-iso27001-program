## 1. The next cycle, and what it costs

**The 2027 observation window runs 2027-01-01 to 2027-12-31 — twelve months, not six.** It is **ten weeks
old** and has produced nothing this document reports as an outcome.

Doubling the window is neither a doubling of effort nor a halving of it. What it does is change the
arithmetic of every control in the library, and the change runs in three directions depending on cadence.

| Cadence | Window population, 2026 | Window population, 2027 | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily and continuous | 184 runs | **365** | Populations roughly double; a tested-in-full population becomes a sampled one |
| Monthly | 6 occurrences | **12** | A single miss falls from 16.7% to 8.3% — and there are twice as many chances to miss |
| Quarterly | 2 occurrences | **4** | Two occurrences are argued individually; four begin to look like a rate |
| Annual | 1 occurrence | **1** | **Unchanged.** The ten controls re-scheduled under ADR-0019 still have a population of one |

**The monthly row is the one to read twice.** `CNB-C-098` missed one of six restore tests in 2026 and the
window rate was 16.7%. The same single miss across twelve occurrences is 8.3%, and a reader ranking
exceptions by rate would call the second half as serious as the first. **It is the same missed test.**
Halving the arithmetic weight of a miss is not an improvement in the control environment; **a programme
reporting a falling deviation rate across a lengthening window is reporting the length of the window.**

**The annual row does not change and matters most.** Ten controls have a population of one across twelve
months exactly as across six. A hundred per cent deviation rate on a population of one is what `D-06-05`
was, and the only defence is that the occurrence happens on the date the calendar says — a scheduling
discipline rather than a control design.

**The first surveillance audit is scheduled for 2027-11**, within twelve months of the **certification
decision of 2027-01-20** as **O11** requires. It is a date on a calendar and nothing about it is an outcome
on this vantage. **Recertification falls in year three**, against a certificate valid to 2030-01-21. The
verification of the four Stage 2 minor nonconformities' corrective actions falls at that surveillance audit
and not before it — `DP-37` has said so since Phase 08.

**Sixteen open, none closed**, and the number is not evidence of neglect. `CNB-C-148` **refuses closure
until the effectiveness check is complete**, and an effectiveness check is a control occurrence rather than
a management opinion. Twelve attach to controls whose next relevant occurrence falls outside this vantage,
and four are Northgate's to verify at an audit eight months away. **The sixteen are the twelve `CA-` rows
and the four limbs against the Stage 2 minors. The seventeenth row is a nonconformity and not a corrective
action**, which is why every count of corrective actions in this phase reads sixteen and this table has
seventeen rows.

**Three could have been argued closed and were not.** `CA-06-03` has sixteen weeks — under four months — of
a corrected emergency change path with no recurrence; `CA-07-02`'s correction limb is complete and
independently verified, with a new control operating for 65 nights and then a further ten weeks; `CA-07-04`
has one sub-processor change through the corrected path. Each is an argument that something worked and none
is an effectiveness check across a cycle. **DEC-913** records the refusal and **ADR-0045** the reasoning:
**the rows a reader should trust least in any closing report are the ones that closed in the fortnight
before it.**

**One numbering point is recorded rather than tidied.** The four minor nonconformities' corrective actions
carry no `CA-` identifier, because they were submitted to Northgate as limbs of a single plan on 2026-12-19
and the improvement register has carried them by finding identifier since. Renumbering them here to make the
column uniform would change a record eight weeks after an external party accepted it. **`NC-INT-2027-01`
carries a third form of identifier for the same kind of reason**: Camberwell's 2026 audit raised `NC-INT-01`
and `NC-INT-02`, the correction audit belongs to the **2027** internal audit programme, and 08.07 recorded
that its findings enter the improvement register under that programme and carry its identifiers.

### 2.1 The clause 7.4 minor, and its clause 10.2 disposition

**The correction audit of 2027-01-05 to 01-09 raised 1 minor nonconformity and 4 opportunities, and until
this section the minor appeared in Phase 09 only inside `AS-41`, as part of a sentence about what one audit
can and cannot establish.** It had no row here, no corrective action named against it and no register entry.
**The reason is not a judgement and is worse than one**: §2's table was assembled from the SOC 2 deviations
and the Stage 2 findings, which are the two populations this phase was thinking about, and **nobody asked
what the correction audit itself had raised.** In a phase whose central claim is that nothing was left out
to keep the record tidy, a finding that goes missing because of the shape of a table is the finding that
tests the claim.

**A corrective action does arise, and clause 10.2 says what it has to contain.**

**React to the nonconformity, and deal with the consequences — and here there is no instance to correct.**
The finding is that communication about the ISMS is performed but is not **planned** as a set: on what, when,
with whom, by whom and how. No communication was wrong, no interested party was misinformed, and no
notification obligation was engaged — **O10**'s notification of the scope statement reissue ran inside its
thirty days and is evidenced. **The absence of a plan is the whole of the finding**, so the react limb is
answered by recording that no correction is available rather than by inventing one.

**Evaluate the need for action to eliminate the cause, so that it does not recur.** **The cause is that ISMS
communication had an owner and no plan**, and it was a known cause: `08.06` §8 records that **the same
condition was offered as `OFI-01` at Stage 2, closing 2026-12-04, and declined** — five weeks before
Camberwell raised it as a nonconformity in the audit CloudNimbus itself commissioned. Declining an
opportunity breached nothing, the declination was recorded with its reason, and Northgate is owed nothing on
it; the condition it described simply did not change because CloudNimbus had decided not to act on it. The
corrective action is a clause 7.4 communication plan stating what is communicated about the
ISMS and its changes, to whom, on what trigger or cadence, by whom, and through what channel — owner **Rahul
Bhargava**, carried in the 2027 improvement register.

**Review the effectiveness of the action taken.** On `CNB-C-148`'s rule, which this phase applies to
everything else in the table: **not yet, and not by anybody at this vantage.** The check is an occurrence of
the plan against a real change to the ISMS, and the 2027 window is ten weeks old. **Northgate does not close
this one either** — an internal audit finding is closed inside the ISMS, and what the certification body
sees at the **2027-11** surveillance audit is whether the internal audit process handled its own finding,
which is a different question and is scheduled rather than answered.

**Retain documented information on the nature of the nonconformity, the action taken and the result.** That
is what this row and this section are. **Open, with a disposition and without a closure**, exactly like the
sixteen above it.

## 3. The register, and the three-year matrix

The register stands at **38 — 0 High · 17 Moderate · 21 Low**, held at the March CAL-06 review of
2027-03-09, and 09.10 takes that apart against the forecast.

**The rewritten audit programme has been executed once.** The three-year coverage matrix adopted under
DEC-808 covers clauses 4 to 10, the Statement of Applicability and every region and legal entity inside the
ISMS scope, and it has produced exactly one audit — the correction audit of 2027-01-05 to 01-09. A matrix
complete for a cycle it has not traversed is a plan. `PR-50` carries that, and `IS-36` the sharper half:
**`CNB-C-150`, the control that would detect the matrix becoming incomplete, has never operated and cannot
be tested by anybody until the 2028 programme is set.** Northgate's acceptance rested on the rewritten
document and the January audit it produced, **not on that control**, and `AS-40` records the assumption as
unverified and unverifiable for a year.

## 4. What continuous assurance actually means here

> The programme's assurance is now **a calendar and an evidence store**: sixteen recurring activities with
> named owners, 150 controls with declared cadences, twenty-four evidence classes each with a sampling unit
> defined before the control was built. That is a real asset and a narrower claim than the phrase
> "continuous assurance" invites.
>
> **The transferable claim is this: the second year costs less than the first only if the evidence store
> keeps working in the eleven months when nobody is auditing.** In 2026 it worked under continuous
> observation — a service-auditor checkpoint every month from April, a certification body at two stages, an
> internal audit, a management review, and a team whose whole job was watching it. **None of that is true of
> 2027.** The store's classes depend on scheduled exports, and 04.12 §6 named the limitation that survives:
> **an export that silently stops produces a gap that looks exactly like a control that stopped operating.**
> No such gap was found in 2026, which is not the same as there having been none.
>
> **That is the single largest untested assumption the programme leaves behind, and naming it is worth more
> than a maturity model.** It is carried as `AS-42`.
>
> **And the register is the thing that will decay first.** Nine months of *waiting for the population* was
> defensible inside a window with a defined end, and 08.08 §5.5 defended it. Across a twelve-month window it
> is nine months of a review body that does not review — and the discipline that made the December movement
> honest is the same discipline that will make a 2027 review look like inertia. The March review moved
> nothing and said why; the June review will have the same sentence available, and the September review
> after it. **`IS-37`**, owner Karim Haddad, carried into the 2027 programme, and **DEC-914** opens it.

## 6. The question the committee asked

Lorraine Kessler asked **why the organisation's own records contained the major nonconformity eleven months
before an auditor raised it, and what has changed so that the next one is acted on rather than filed.**

The question is exact and admits of no comfortable answer, and the eleven months in it run from a document
this chapter has to name rather than leave to GOV-36. **The audit programme document was written at
chartering in January 2026 and scoped by reference to the Statement of Applicability**, which in January was
the only complete inventory of auditable things the organisation had. `MAJ-01` was raised against that scope
on **2026-12-02** — eleven months, and every date this chapter gives inside them is a matter of weeks.
Camberwell's report of 2026-09-25 stated the
scope limitation at §1.3 and recommended it be reconsidered before the certification audit; Karim Haddad
read it, agreed with it and scheduled the coverage into the 2027 programme; Elise Fontaine took the same
note to the clause 9.3 management review on 2026-09-30 under input d) 3) and confirmed the deferral.
**Nothing was missed and nothing was hidden. `MAJ-01` was raised on 2026-12-02 against a decision that was
minuted twice.**

The answer given was **`CNB-C-150`**, the **three-year coverage matrix**, and the escalation of any
uncovered clause or location **to the Audit &amp; Risk Committee itself** rather than to the programme owner
whose scope decision produced the finding.

**The committee recorded the answer and then recorded the limit of it.**

> The answer to *why did governance not catch it* is *governance will now be told*, which is an improvement
> and not a guarantee. **A committee that is told something is a committee that can defer it, and this one
> already has** — the same information reached the highest governance body in the ISMS on 2026-09-30 and was
> deferred there. What has changed is the route and the cadence, not the disposition of the body at the end
> of it.

That paragraph is minuted at **GOV-36** and it is the best sentence in the governance record, **because the
committee wrote it about itself.** 01.07 §4.1 said at chartering that a committee with one voting member is
not a committee in the sense a listed company means, and that the arrangement depends materially on one
person. Fourteen months later, the one person is the one who wrote the limitation down. **That is what the
structure supports, and it is a smaller claim than the word "committee" implies.**

## 7. What this chapter does not report

**No outcome after 2027-03-11.** The surveillance audit is scheduled, not held. The 2027 window is ten weeks
old and has produced no rate, no deviation and no position. Recertification is in year three. **None of them
is stated here as a result, on ADR-0015's rule applied for the fifth phase running.**

**No corrective action closed for the convenience of this document**, and **no claim that the programme is
self-sustaining.** It is a calendar, an evidence store, a library of 150 controls, an obligations register
and 4.6 FTE. Whether that survives a year without an auditor in the room is the question `AS-42` names and
no document written today can answer.
