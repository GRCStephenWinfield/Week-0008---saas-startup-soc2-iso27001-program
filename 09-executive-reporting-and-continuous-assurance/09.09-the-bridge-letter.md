## 1. Eleven requested, nine issued, two open

**O3** commits CloudNimbus to providing a bridge letter covering the gap between the report period end and
the requesting customer's own fiscal year end, on request. **Eleven requests had been received by 2027-03-11
and nine letters were issued; two remain open because their gap periods have not ended.**

Requests arrived from **2027-01-14**, six weeks before the report existed, which is the ordinary pattern:
customers close their own books on their own calendar and ask for the covering instrument when they need it
rather than when it can be produced. None could be answered before **2027-02-26**, because a letter that
bridges from a report period end presupposes a report to bridge from, and a letter issued in January
covering a period whose report did not yet exist would have been an assurance about nothing. The first was
issued **2027-03-01** and the ninth on **2027-03-10**.

| Gap period covered | Requesting customers | Letters issued | Issued on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2027-01-01 to 2027-01-31 | 4 | 4 | 2027-03-01 |
| 2027-01-01 to 2027-02-28 | 5 | 5 | 2027-03-04 to 2027-03-10 |
| 2027-01-01 to 2027-03-31 | 2 | **0 — the gap period has not ended** | **Open** |
| **Total** | **11** | **9** | — |

Every gap period begins on **2027-01-01**, the day after the report period ended, and ends on the requesting
customer's fiscal year end. **No letter covers a period that had not finished when it was signed.** That
constraint sounds obvious and is the one most often relaxed, because a customer with a 31 March year end
would like the letter in February and the letter is cheap to write. It cannot be written: management can
state what it is aware of up to the date it signs and not one day further, and a letter dated 2027-02-20
covering a gap ending 2027-03-31 represents a state of knowledge nobody had. The two customers who asked for
that were told so, and their requests remain open against their own year ends.

## 2. What a bridge letter is

**This is the argument this chapter owns.**

> A bridge letter is **management-issued and unaudited**. **Ashcombe &amp; Doyle LLP does not sign it, does
> not review it and is not named in it.** It carries **no practitioner opinion, no tests and no assurance**.
> What it contains is a statement by management, on the date it is signed, that it is not aware of changes
> to the system or to the controls described in the report, or of incidents, in the gap period, that would
> affect the description or the conclusions the report reaches.
>
> **It covers a gap. It does not close one.** The distinction is the whole of the instrument. A report
> covering 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31 says what was tested and what the tests found across 184 days. A bridge
> letter covering 2027-01-01 to 2027-02-28 says that management, which was there, has nothing to report —
> and no procedure was performed by anybody to check that. Those are not two grades of the same assurance.
> They are an examination and a representation, and the second is worth exactly the credibility of the
> management that signs it. **A bridge letter from an organisation with a poor disclosure record is worth
> nothing, and one from an organisation with a good record is worth something no standard can measure and
> no reader can verify.**

01.11 §4 said in January 2026 that O3 is the obligation most often misunderstood by the parties who ask for
it, and fourteen months of customer conversations have not changed that. **Three of the nine requests asked
for the letter to be "signed off by the auditor".** All three were answered the same way, in the covering
note rather than in a call: the auditor does not sign it, cannot be asked to sign it without changing what
the instrument is, and a bridge letter with a practitioner's signature on it would be a different engagement
with its own report.

## 3. The first one had something to disclose

**`CNB-C-150` was admitted to the control library on 2027-01-08.** That date is inside every one of the nine
gap periods.

**A bridge letter that said "no changes" would have been false**, on the first occasion this programme ever
issued one. All nine letters therefore carry the admission, and each states three things about it: that it
is a control **added** rather than a control removed or weakened; that it arises from the corrective action
for the Stage 2 major nonconformity, which Section V of the report discloses; and that **it has no bearing
on the period the report covers, because it did not exist in that period and has no population inside it.**

That last clause is not a softening. It is ADR-0040's position stated to the customer in the customer's own
instrument: `CNB-C-150` was admitted eight days after the observation window closed, was deliberately
excluded from the examination, and cannot have improved anything the report says. **A letter that disclosed
the addition and let the recipient infer that the control environment had improved since the period would
have used a disclosure as a claim**, which is a worse failure than not disclosing it.

**DEC-905**, taken by Marisol Vega on 2027-03-02, records the consequence for the form rather than for the
instance. The template at `templates/bridge-letter-template.md` asks the changes question in a form that
**cannot be answered by leaving a field blank**: it requires a positive statement of what changed or a
positive statement that nothing did, with the population searched named in either case. A blank field in a
letter of representation is indistinguishable from a question nobody asked, and the first letter this
programme issued is the proof that the question is not rhetorical. **ADR-0044** records the decision and the
alternative that was available.

**One thing the nine letters do not carry, and the omission is deliberate.** The ISO/IEC 27001:2022
certificate was issued on **2027-01-22**, also inside every gap period, and it is a better fact about
CloudNimbus than anything else that happened in those eight weeks. It is not in the letters. A bridge letter
answers a question about changes to the system and the controls the report describes, and a certificate is
neither: it is a certification body's decision about a management system with a different boundary. **A
letter that answered the question it was asked and then added a fact it was not asked for would be a
marketing document with a signature block**, and the restraint 09.07 §4 applies to Section V applies here
for the same reason. Customers who want the certificate can have it, and seven of the nine already had.

## 4. What the nine letters actually say

Each letter is one page and carries five statements, and the fifth is the one that costs something to write.

**The report it refers to**, by title, service auditor, period and report date, with the statement that the
report is restricted-use and that the letter does not extend its distribution.

**The gap period**, with both dates, and the statement that management's knowledge is stated as at the date
of signature.

**Changes to the system or to the controls described in the report**: the `CNB-C-150` admission, described
above, and nothing else. No component of the system was added, removed or relocated in the gap period; the
three AWS regions, the seven accounts and the two subservice organisations are as the description states
them.

**Incidents**: no Severity-1 or Severity-2 incident was declared and no security incident was determined in
the gap period. Neither is a statement that nothing occurred — 10,336 alerts in the report period produced
zero security incidents through a disposition on each, and the same detective controls produce the same kind
of zero here — and the letter says which kind of zero it is.

**And the limitation, in the letter's own last paragraph**: that the letter is unaudited, carries no
practitioner opinion, was not reviewed by Ashcombe &amp; Doyle, and is not a substitute for the report or for
any examination of the period it covers. **That paragraph is on the face of the letter and not in a
covering email**, on exactly the reasoning DEC-813 applied to Section V: the obligation to say what a
document is worth belongs to the party writing it. **09.07 §1** owns why that follows.

## 5. What was considered and not done

**A single form letter issued to all nine.** Available, cheaper, and refused. The gap periods differ by a
month, and a letter stating that management is not aware of changes through 2027-02-28 cannot be reused for
a customer whose year ended on 2027-01-31 without asserting knowledge on that customer's behalf about a
month it did not ask about. Nine letters, two gap periods, one signature process.

**Issuing the letters unprompted to all 118 enterprise-tier customers.** Also available, and it would have
looked diligent. It was refused because O3 is a commitment to provide on request and an unrequested bridge
letter is an unrequested representation — an instrument whose only content is management's knowledge,
pushed at recipients who have not said they need it and may not know what it is. **The programme has spent
nine phases refusing to hand people documents they have not asked for and cannot evaluate**, and doing it in
the last fortnight for presentational reasons would have undone the argument.

**Waiting for the two customers with 31 March year ends.** Refused as a matter of sequencing rather than
principle: their letters cannot be written yet, their requests stay open on the register, and holding this
chapter until they can be would have made a document about nine letters into a document about eleven that
does not exist. `DEC-909` records the nine as issued and the two as pending.

## 6. What this chapter does not claim

**It does not claim the gap is covered in any sense the report covers the period.** Two months of
management's stated awareness is two months of management's stated awareness. No control was tested, no
population was sampled, and the letter would read identically if a control had failed in a way management
had not detected — which is, on this programme's own record, exactly what happened for sixty-eight nights
inside a period that *was* examined.

**It does not claim the nine recipients read it correctly.** Three of the nine asked for an auditor's
signature before they received it, which is evidence about how the instrument is understood in the market,
and the covering note is the only lever CloudNimbus has.

**And it does not claim that having something to disclose on the first letter is a credit to the
programme.** It is a fact about a control admitted on 2027-01-08 and about a template that asked the
question properly. **The letter is honest because the form made the omission harder than the disclosure**,
which is a design outcome rather than a character reference — and it is the only reason worth recording,
because it is the only one another organisation can copy.

## 7. Where a bridge letter sits against the criteria, which is nowhere

The honest answer, and it is short.

**A bridge letter answers no trust services criterion.** It is not part of the description, it is not a
control, it is not tested, and it appears in no section of the report. It exists because **O3** exists, and
O3 exists because a customer's fiscal year does not end when CloudNimbus's observation window does.
`CNB-C-014` carries O3 in the obligations register and that is the whole of the control coverage.

**Two consequences follow and neither is comfortable.** First, the quality of a bridge letter is governed by
nothing except the diligence of the person who signs it — there is no cadence, no evidence class and no
sampling. Second, and this is the one worth carrying into 2027: **the letter makes a statement about a
period nobody has examined**, and the more useful it is to a customer the more weight it puts on an
unexamined interval. **A programme that issues bridge letters routinely and never notices that is a
programme extending its report's credibility into months it did not earn.**

That is why all nine of these disclose `CNB-C-150` rather than treating a control addition as immaterial,
and why the template asks the change question in a form a blank field cannot answer.
