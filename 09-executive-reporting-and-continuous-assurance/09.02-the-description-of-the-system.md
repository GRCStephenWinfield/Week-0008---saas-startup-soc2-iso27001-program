## 1. Section III, and who writes it

**Section III of the report is management's description of the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform.** CloudNimbus
wrote it. The service auditor did not write it, did not draft it and did not correct it — what Ashcombe
&amp; Doyle LLP did was examine whether it is **presented in accordance with the description criteria**,
which is the first of the three limbs of the opinion at `09.04` §2.

That first limb is the one a reader underrates, and `02.02` §1 said so in March 2026, fifteen months before
anybody read the finished document. **A description can be entirely truthful and still fail, because it
omitted a criterion the description criteria require it to address.** Truth is not the test. Completeness
against **DC section 200 — the 2018 *Description Criteria for a Description of a Service Organization's
System in a SOC 2 Report*, DC1 to DC9** — is.

**The description was not assembled in January 2027.** It was assembled from documents written when the
facts were fresh, against a coverage map published at the start of the programme and with an owner for each
row. `02.02` §2 gave the reason: a team that starts writing its description in January for a period that
ended in December will reconstruct DC1 and DC3 comfortably from architecture diagrams and will find that
the evidence for **DC4 and DC9** is in people's memories. **This chapter carries DC1 to DC8. `09.03` carries
DC9, which is the one that has to be noticed as it happens.**

## 3. DC1 to DC3 — what the description says the system is

**DC1 is short because the business is.** CloudNimbus sells one service. The platform captures time,
publishes schedules, records absence and maintains accrual balances, captures expenses and evaluates them
against the employer's policy, and — the capability that shapes every later argument — **derives the
numbers**: overtime, differentials, accrual balances and reimbursement amounts, output to the customer's
payroll provider. **The output of the calculation engine becomes a person's pay**, which is why Processing
Integrity is the product here rather than a formality and why `01.05` selected it rather than defending its
absence.

**DC2 is the criterion the programme corrected at source.** `02.12` v1.0 published **SC-01** — 99.9% monthly
availability — without the exclusion the master services agreement carries for maintenance announced at
least fourteen days in advance. The omission was found in September when the first announced window of the
period was measured against it, and the document was reissued at **v1.1** on **2026-09-24** under **DEC-613**
rather than annotated downstream. **A commitment stated without its exclusion is a commitment a reader will
measure differently from the party who wrote it**, and an incomplete DC2 is a description defect whatever
the rest of the document says. `09.03` §3 explains why that correction is not a DC9 change.

**DC3 is where the two boundaries show.** The description states the components inside the SOC 2 system —
**5 of 7 AWS accounts, 3 of 4 EKS clusters, 5 of 6 Aurora clusters, 168 of 214 S3 buckets, 63 microservices
and 4 client applications, 164 of 187 people, 78 of 130 documented procedures**, and the data component
including the twelve personal data categories PD-01 to PD-12. Every figure smaller than the organisation's
total is the boundary at work: **the ISMS is the whole organisation, the system is the platform, and neither
contains the other.** The 23 people outside the people component are quota-carrying sales and marketing
staff who can reach neither production nor customer data nor an in-scope procedure — and all 23 remain
inside the ISMS, with the same endpoints, training and joiner-mover-leaver process as everybody else.

## 4. DC4, answered twice, on limbs that ask different questions

**DC4 requires disclosure of identified system incidents that resulted from controls that were not suitably
designed or did not operate effectively, or that resulted in a significant failure in the achievement of one
or more service commitments and system requirements, during the period.** Two nouns, two limbs, two
questions.

**The description carries two DC4 disclosures and they engage the limbs differently.** The Severity-1
availability incident of **2026-09-08** — 71 minutes, `INC-2026-031` — engages the **second limb alone**:
SC-01 was missed in September for the 599 customers served from `us-east-1` and met for the 41 served from
`eu-central-1`. The first limb is not engaged, because `CNB-C-096` did not cause the outage; it failed to
measure it. The **RT-02 over-retention** in `eu-central-1` between 2026-08-17 and 2026-10-23 engages
**both**: the first because `CNB-C-127`'s alert condition could not detect the failure mode that occurred,
and the second because **SR-08** — retention enforced by scheduled deletion jobs whose failure is alerted —
was not achieved for sixty-eight nights.

**That is the whole of what this chapter says about either.** `06.06` and `07.03` §8 own them, both
disclosures were drafted at their own vantages under **ADR-0028** and re-confirmed at period end, and
restating either here would be the fourth telling of a story that has two proper homes. What is worth one
sentence is the shape: **a description that had only ever seen the second limb would be a description
written by somebody who thought DC4 was about outages**, and it took a retention failure eleven weeks before
period end to prove the first limb was live.

## 5. DC5 and DC8 — the criteria, the controls, and an answer of "none"

**DC5 is the join between the criteria and the library.** Sixty-one criteria are in scope — Security 33,
Availability 3, Processing Integrity 5, Confidentiality 2, Privacy 18 — and the description states, for each
one, the controls management says meet it. **The trust services criteria contain no controls**; management
designs and describes them, and the practitioner evaluates whether what management describes meets the
criteria. A published list of "SOC 2 controls" does not exist and this description does not pretend to be
one.

The library stood at **148 rows when the window opened and 149 when it closed**, and at **150** on this
vantage; the **16 ISO-only rows appear in no criterion's control set at all** — which is why `CNB-C-146`, the
internal audit programme control, has nowhere in Section IV for a finding against it to sit. `09.07` carries
the consequence, and `09.11` §4 carries what the two rows added mid-programme did to the three-way split.

**DC8's answer is "none", and its shortness is the consequence of a decision taken in January 2026.**
Management selected all five trust services categories, so no criterion was set aside and there is nothing
for DC8 to disclose. Two things follow, and `04.03` §7 stated both rather than letting the empty row read as
complacency. **A criterion can be in scope and thinly served** — CC5.3 is served by one control with a
population of one — and DC8 is not where that is disclosed. And **DC8 is not a place to record a criterion
the entity found inconvenient**: had Privacy been excluded, DC8 would have carried eighteen criteria and the
reasoning would have had to survive a reader who could see the mobile application capturing geolocation at
clock-in.

## 6. DC6 and DC7 — the two sets of controls the description does not contain

**DC6 and DC7 exist because a description of a multi-tenant service hosted on somebody else's
infrastructure is incomplete without saying what other people have to do.**

**Eleven complementary user entity controls are disclosed.** Fifteen were proposed and four were refused —
including one asking customers to verify their own deletion, refused on the ground that a commitment cannot
be discharged by asking the counterparty to check it. Each of the eleven **names the criteria it is
necessary for**, which is what DC6 asks for and what makes it testable by the user entity's own auditor.
**Nobody in this engagement tested whether 640 employers perform them**, and the description says what each
requires rather than how many do it — a restraint that costs something, because CUEC-05 has 411
confirmations, 229 non-responses and no control governing the outreach.

**Two subservice organisations are disclosed, both carved out.** **SUB-01 Amazon Web Services** with 11
complementary subservice organisation controls and **SUB-02 Halcyon Identity, Inc.** with 3 — fourteen in
total. Under the carve-out method their controls are excluded from the description and the complementary
controls are stated instead; what was examined is **CloudNimbus's configuration of the services those
organisations provide**, which is a different subject. Halcyon Identity was reclassified from ordinary
vendor on 2026-03-03, six weeks after chartering, which is `ML-3` repeating — and the vendor population is
84 with 11 sub-processors among them, overlapping sets and never a three-way partition.

## 7. What the description is not

**It is not the assertion.** Section III says what the system is; Section II asserts that the description is
fairly presented and that the controls were suitably designed and operated effectively. `09.01` holds them
apart.

**It is not Section IV.** The description states what a control **is**; Section IV states what was tested,
on what population, and what was found. **Management writes the first and the service auditor writes the
second**, and `09.06` keeps them apart for the ninth phase running.

**It is not a description of the ISMS.** The Statement of Applicability, the clause 9.2 audit programme and
the certificate are not components of the described system, and none of them appears here. Where an ISO fact
has to reach a reader of this report it does so through **Section V**, which is `09.07`'s, and which is not
covered by the opinion.

**And it is not published.** The report carrying this description is **restricted-use** and is provided
under non-disclosure on request. `09.08` says who may have it and why the restriction is not the
confidentiality agreement it arrives with.
