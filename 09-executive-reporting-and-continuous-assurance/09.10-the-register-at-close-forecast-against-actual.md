## 1. The two numbers

**The forecast, published in Phase 03 on 2026-04-24 under ADR-0015 and only after the build harness had
proved it reachable: 0 High · 16 Moderate · 22 Low = 38.**

**The actual, reached at the December CAL-06 review of 2026-12-29 and held at the March review of
2027-03-09: 0 High · 17 Moderate · 21 Low = 38.**

0 + 16 + 22 = 38. 0 + 17 + 21 = 38. Four phases have written that the comparison between them belongs here
rather than where the close position was produced. This is the chapter that does it — entry by entry, in
both directions, averaging nothing.

## 2. The band-level comparison, and why it is nearly empty

| Band | Forecast | Actual | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | 0 | 0 | — |
| Moderate | 16 | **17** | **+1** |
| Low | 22 | **21** | **−1** |
| **Total** | **38** | **38** | — |

**One entry.** On a population of thirty-eight, forecast fourteen months in advance, that is close enough
that a board pack could report it as a hit and stop there.

**Stopping there would be the error this chapter exists to name.** The band count is an aggregate of
thirty-eight ordinal ratings with no memory of which entry produced which number, and its central property —
the one that makes it useful in a board pack and dangerous everywhere else — is that **it cancels.** An entry
that lands a band above its forecast and an entry that lands a band below produce a difference of zero, and a
reader who checks only the total sees a forecast that worked.

The zero on the High row is real and is worth a paragraph before it is set aside. Nine entries occupied
the band across the register's life and **all of them left it. Eight left on a population, and none of those
eight left because a treatment item was marked complete** — every movement GOV-32 records states the
population of window occurrences it moved on. **`R-37` is the ninth and it is not one of them.** It was
admitted on penetration test evidence at 4 × 5 = 20 on 2026-05-22 and left High at the review of
2026-06-15, three weeks before the window opened, on a remediation deployed 2026-05-29 and retested clean on
2026-06-11 — a retest, which is evidence about a fix rather than a population of occurrences, and the only
thing in the register's life that ever carried an entry out of High on that basis. It was the first review
of a register that had existed for nine weeks, and there was no window yet to have a population in. Zero
High is the one figure the forecast and the actual agree about; **they do not agree on it for the same
reason on every entry**, and R-37 is the entry that shows why.

## 3. One band, five entries

**Five entries land in a different band from the one the forecast placed them in.** Three moved up against
the forecast and two moved down.

| Entry | Forecast | Actual | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R-08** | 2 × 3 = 6, Low | **3 × 3 = 9, Moderate** | Raised to 5 × 3 = 15 in September on nine occurrences of its described event in ninety-two days, and brought back **two steps and not three**: six weeks of `CA-06-03`'s corrected emergency change path is not a window, and DEC-810 is the risk owner refusing the rating that would have suited the register |
| **R-09** | 2 × 3 = 6, Low | **4 × 3 = 12, Moderate** | **Its described event occurred** — access provisioned ahead of recorded manager approval in 2 of the window's 40 provisioning requests, tested in full, which is exception 8. The entry did not move at all |
| **R-18** | 2 × 3 = 6, Low | **3 × 3 = 9, Moderate** | **Its described event occurred**, on 2026-09-08: a 71-minute Severity-1 and SC-01 missed in September for the 599 customers served from `us-east-1` |
| **R-13** | 3 × 3 = 9, Moderate | **2 × 3 = 6, Low** | The control changed — `CNB-C-032` now requires a phishing-resistant factor on every path reaching production — **and the window tested it.** The forecast reasoned about what a successful phish yields; the review found the control had changed the path instead |
| **R-14** | 3 × 3 = 9, Moderate | **2 × 3 = 6, Low** | CUEC-05's outreach and the window's export record — specifically `CNB-C-111`'s band check, CloudNimbus's own preventive control sitting in front of the customer step. **GOV-32 records it as the weakest of the nineteen movements** |

**Three up, two down, net one.** 16 + 3 − 2 = 17; 22 − 3 + 2 = 21.

## 4. And this is the chapter, so do not soften it

> **A forecast that is right at the band level and wrong five times underneath it is not a good forecast that
> got lucky, and it is not a bad one.** It is a forecast whose aggregate absorbed its errors. Three entries
> moved the wrong way and two moved the other wrong way; **the total absorbed four of the five and showed
> the fifth**, and the one it showed is not more important than the four it hid — it is just the one that
> did not have a partner.
>
> **A programme that checked only the aggregate would have declared the forecast a success and learned
> nothing from the five.** That is the whole of what this chapter has to say, and it is the reason ADR-0015
> required the forecast to be published in the first place. **A forecast nobody publishes is a forecast
> nobody can be wrong about**, and a forecast published only as three band counts is a forecast nobody can
> be wrong about in any way that shows.
>
> Week 7 published a close forecast its own rules made unreachable and had to report the miss. This one
> published a forecast the harness proved reachable and still missed five entries at the band level and six
> underneath. **The improvement is not that the forecast was better. It is that the miss is legible.**

## 5. Six more underneath, where no band moved

08.08 §7 promised that this reconciliation would include the entries whose forecast and actual ratings
differ without either band changing. There are six, and they are invisible in every summary the register has
ever produced.

| Entry | Forecast | Actual | Band | Why the ratings differ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R-06** | 2 × 4 = 8 | **3 × 4 = 12** | Moderate both | Moved one step and not two, on `CNB-C-149`'s 65 of 184 nights. A two-step move would have placed a silent deletion failure at *foreseeable but not expected within the cycle* in the quarter R-38 was admitted |
| **R-15** | 2 × 2 = 4 | **3 × 2 = 6** | Low both | Moved one step on `CNB-C-128`'s six monthly residue confirmations. The forecast assumed likelihood 2; the review moved on the population it had |
| **R-24** | 2 × 4 = 8 | **3 × 4 = 12** | Moderate both | **Its described event occurred.** Forecast immovable on the eight floor, it moved **upward** on 2026-10-28 under DEC-715. The floor stopped it going down and nothing stopped it going up |
| **R-25** | 2 × 2 = 4 | **3 × 2 = 6** | Low both | Held. The evidence architecture's dependency on one person is unchanged and no reduction was proposed |
| **R-30** | 2 × 2 = 4 | **3 × 2 = 6** | Low both | Held. **Its described event occurred twice** — `NC-INT-01` and `MIN-01`, documented information relied on past its review date |
| **R-34** | 2 × 2 = 4 | **3 × 2 = 6** | Low both | Held. `CNB-C-064` ran six times with every discrepancy resolved, which would have supported a reduction. **None was proposed, because the entry was already Low** |

**Eleven of thirty-eight ratings differ from the forecast and five of those differences reach a band. The
band counts differ by one.** That ratio — eleven to one — is the measurement this chapter is for, and it is
not a defect of this register but a property of banded ordinal scales: any programme that publishes a close
forecast in bands and marks it in bands gets the same reassuring answer whatever happened underneath.

**R-34 is the row a reader should be least comfortable with.** Its evidence was clean, its forecast said it
would move, and it did not move because nobody proposed a reduction for an entry already in the band the
reduction would have kept it in. Nothing about that breaks the register's rules — a review is entitled to
spend its four hours where the ratings change — and it means the twelve already-Low entries are the
least-reviewed part of a register that has been reviewed four times. There are five CAL-06 occurrences and
the first of them, in 2026-03, reviewed the risk assessment method: no register existed until the baseline
of 2026-04-10. `IS-37` is where that goes.

## 6. Error or event, which 03.07 §7 required this phase to state

03.07 §7 set the obligation in advance: where the forecast and the actual differ, Phase 09 must say whether
the difference is **an error** — a movement rule applied wrongly, a floor missed, an entry misjudged — or
**an event**, meaning something happened during the programme that contradicted a likelihood judgement. It
required the answer one entry at a time and forbade averaging them. The eleven divide into three groups and
the third is the one the instruction did not anticipate.

**Five are events, and none of them is a forecasting failure.** R-09's described event occurred twice in
forty provisioning requests, R-18's once for seventy-one minutes on 2026-09-08, R-24's across sixty-eight
consecutive nights in `eu-central-1`, R-30's twice in September and December, and R-08's nine times in
ninety-two days. **A likelihood forecast contradicted by the event actually occurring is not a forecast that
should be honoured**, and in every one of the five the register recorded what happened rather than what was
expected — the forecast being overtaken by evidence, which is what ADR-0015 was designed to make visible
rather than to prevent.

**Five are errors, and they share one cause.** R-06, R-13, R-15, R-25 and R-34 diverge because **the
forecast assumed every entry whose treatment worked would arrive at likelihood 2**, and the December review
moved a step at a time on the population the window produced, requiring any second step to be argued
separately. Four of the five stopped short of the forecast; **R-13 overshot it**, because the forecast
reasoned about the yield of a successful phish when the control had changed the path. **The method erred in
both directions**, which is the useful form of the finding: not that the forecast was optimistic, but that
**the forecast modelled treatment completion and the review modelled evidence.**

**And one is neither.** **R-14** was forecast at 3 × 3 = 9 on the explicit reasoning that forecasting a user
entity's diligence down to likelihood 2 would be forecasting somebody else's behaviour on no evidence, and
that reasoning remains correct: 411 of 640 customers confirmed, 229 did not respond, and the 411 are
unverified self-assertions carrying no assurance weight. **The review did not overturn the reasoning. It
moved the entry on a basis the forecast had not contemplated** — `CNB-C-111`, a CloudNimbus control, sitting
in front of the customer step. That is neither error nor event but a forecast about one thing displaced by a
decision about another, and ADR-0039 records the movement as the weakest of the nineteen for exactly this
reason. **A reader who thinks R-14 should still be at 9 has GOV-32's minute to argue with, and the minute
was written so that they could.**

## 7. The March CAL-06 review of 2027-03-09

**The March review held all thirty-eight entries and moved none.** That is **GOV-35**, chaired by Karim
Haddad, and **DEC-910** records the reason rather than the absence, because a review that moves nothing and
says nothing is indistinguishable from a review that did not happen.

Three reasons, and each of them is about a population.

**The entries whose events occurred in the fourth quarter have at most three months behind them.** R-09's
two provisioning deviations were found on 2026-12-18 and R-10's three late revocations on 2026-12-29;
`CA-08-01` and `CA-08-02` address them and neither has produced an occurrence this review could look at.
Moving an entry on ten weeks of a corrected path is the error DEC-810 refused in December on six.

**Ten entries are at their floors.** R-01, R-02, R-03, R-07, R-19, R-20 and R-37 sit at 2 × 5 = 10 with the
next likelihood step reserved and no consequence changed; R-05, R-11 and R-12 sit at 2 × 4 = 8, and eight is
a floor. GOV-35 §3 records the floor on each of the ten in its Basis column — seven of them as *at its
floor* and three as *immovable* — and a floor counted only where the counting is convenient is not a floor.

**And the 2027 observation window is ten weeks old**, having produced sixty-eight days of record against
controls whose cadences are monthly, quarterly and annual. **The first review of a new cycle has nothing to
move on**, and saying so is the discipline that made the December movement defensible: every review from
June to September stated on the record that it was waiting for a population, and the review that moved
nineteen entries is only honest if the review after it is willing to move none.

**Register at this vantage: 38 — 0 High · 17 Moderate · 21 Low. Nothing was closed and nothing was removed,
at this review or at any review in the register's life.**

## 8. What the comparison does not settle

**It does not settle whether the forecast was worth publishing.** It was published under a rule about
publication rather than about prediction, and the rule paid for itself in a way the numbers do not show: the
harness that proved 0 · 16 · 22 reachable is the same harness that would have refused a forecast placing an
impact-4 entry in the Low band, and the twelve floor-bound entries it identified in advance **behaved exactly
as the floor rule said they would** — which is a narrower claim than behaving as predicted, and the narrower
one is the true one: R-06 and R-24 are floor-bound entries and both are among the eleven divergences this
chapter reports. **The part of the forecast that came from a rule was right. The part that
came from a judgement about how far each treatment would carry an entry was wrong eleven times.**

**It does not settle the March position.** Holding thirty-eight entries in a review that had nothing to move
on is correct once; held twice it is inertia, held four times it is a register nobody maintains, and no band
count shows the difference. That is `IS-37`, owner Karim Haddad, and 09.12 §4 carries it.

**And it is not a statement about the examination.** A risk register is management's instrument, no rating
in it appears in Section IV, and nothing here says whether any applicable trust services criterion was
achieved — which is the service auditor's conclusion and is in 09.04.

## 9. What the register answered to, across fourteen months

The register is not a portfolio artefact. It is the thing four criteria and two clauses require, and this is
the last chapter that will say so.

| | What the register evidences |
|---|---|
| **CC3.1** — the entity specifies objectives with sufficient clarity to enable the identification and assessment of risks relating to them | `CNB-C-016`, the annual setting of OBJ-01 to OBJ-08, which 09.11 scores |
| **CC3.2** — the entity identifies risks to the achievement of its objectives, analyses them as a basis for determining how they should be managed | `CNB-C-017` (the assessment against the ISMS asset inventory on the likelihood-by-impact scale), `CNB-C-018` (every entry carries a named risk owner who confirms its rating) and `CNB-C-020` (the TH-01 to TH-14 taxonomy reviewed each quarter) |
| **CC3.3** — the entity considers the potential for fraud in assessing risks | `CNB-C-019` |
| **CC3.4** — the entity identifies and assesses changes that could significantly impact the system of internal control | `CNB-C-021` and `CNB-C-022`, which are why R-37 and R-38 were admitted between reviews rather than waiting for one |
| **Clauses 6.1.2 and 6.1.3** — the information security risk assessment and treatment processes | `CNB-C-143`, executed each quarter, which is the CAL-06 occurrence this chapter has been counting |

**And the defect this chapter found in the library while writing that table**, which is the last finding the
programme records and is a fitting one. **`CNB-C-023` as published says "Each of the 148 library controls
carries an owner-confirmed operation record."** The library is **150**. `CNB-C-149` was admitted on
2026-10-28 and `CNB-C-150` on 2027-01-08, and **nobody updated the control that counts the controls.**

It is not a deviation in the window — `CNB-C-023` operated on every control including `CNB-C-149`, and the
number in its statement is descriptive rather than operative. **It is a documented information defect under
clause 7.5, and it is the same failure mode as the major nonconformity in miniature: a statement that was
true when it was written, describing a practice that moved without it.** Recorded as **`IS-39`**, owner
Rahul Bhargava, carried to the next library issue. **The programme's last finding is a number in a control
statement, found by counting.**
