## 1. Who the report was prepared for

The report issued on **2027-02-26** carries a paragraph restricting its use, and the paragraph is the
practitioner's rather than CloudNimbus's. It names three classes of intended user: **the management of
CloudNimbus, Inc.**; **user entities of the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform during some or all of the period
2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31**; and **other specified parties who have sufficient knowledge and understanding** of
the nature of the service, the system and its boundaries, internal control and its inherent limitations, the
applicable trust services criteria, the complementary user entity and subservice organisation controls, and
how those things interact.

Two phrases in the second class do more work than a reader expects. **"During some or all."** A customer
that contracted in October 2026 was a user entity for part of the period and is inside the class on the
paragraph's own words; a customer that signed its master services agreement on 2027-02-01 was not a user
entity at any point in the period the report covers, and the report says nothing about the six months in
which that customer was not being served. It may still receive the report — the third class is available on
its own terms and CloudNimbus's own commitment in **SC-10** is wider than the restriction — but it receives
it as a specified party whose knowledge and understanding have been considered, not as a user entity whose
own controls the description assumes. **"Sufficient."** The third class is not a formality
discharged by counting signatures. It is a judgement about a named recipient, and §4 records how CloudNimbus
made it.

This chapter is owned by the General Counsel rather than by the programme owner for the same reason 01.11
was: the instruments in play here are contracts and an attestation standard's limitation on use, and the two
are governed by different bodies of rule and do different jobs. Conflating them is §3, and §3 is the reason
this chapter exists.

## 2. The distribution under O2

**O2** requires the report to be provided to the **118 enterprise-tier customers within thirty days of
issuance**. Issuance was 2027-02-26, which placed the deadline on or before **2027-03-28** for every one of
the 118. **All 118 were delivered by 2027-03-06 — eight days after issuance and twenty-two days inside the
obligation.**

**2027-03-06 was a Saturday, and the date is the last acknowledgement rather than the last despatch.** Every
one of the 118 left the controlled channel by Friday **2027-03-05**; two named recipients returned their
acknowledgements on the Saturday, and this record counts a delivery only where the acknowledgement is in it —
`templates/report-distribution-record-template.md` §4 makes an unacknowledged despatch a despatch and not a
delivery. Nobody operated the channel over the weekend, and the point is said rather than left to be noticed,
because Phase 08 left a Saturday sitting unremarked in a record and this phase would rather remark on one.

That is not a statement about how hard the team worked in the first week of March. It is a statement about
what was built in the preceding nine months. **01.11 §8 recorded in January 2026 that a manual process
invented in March will not complete in March**, and the consequence it drew — that the distribution
mechanism must exist before the report does — was assigned to Ana-Sofia Cruz as part of the customer
communication plan and tracked as `PR-51` for the rest of the programme. What the mechanism holds is a named
recipient for each of the 118, that recipient's confidentiality position, and a controlled delivery channel
with a per-recipient record of despatch and acknowledgement. Assembling those three things for 118 customers
is a quarter's work at a company of this size, and the eight days are what happens when it has already been
done.

The last row is there because a reader who has followed both workstreams for nine phases might expect it not
to be. **Northgate certified a management system and has no standing as a user of an examination report on a
different object**, and providing one to a certification body in the absence of a request that had a reason
behind it would be treating the two deliverables as interchangeable in exactly the way ADR-0001 refused to.

**37 of the remaining 522 requested the report by 2027-03-11 and 37 were delivered.** That figure is
reported without a percentage attached to it, because the denominator is not a population of people who were
asked. Nobody was asked. **O1 is a commitment to make the report available on request, and the 485 who have
not requested it have not declined anything**; some will request it at their next vendor review, some at
their next renewal, and some never, and none of those three is distinguishable from the others in the record
CloudNimbus holds. A programme that reported 7.1% here would be reporting the shape of its customers' own
procurement calendars as though it were an outcome of this one.

## 3. The restricted-use paragraph is not a confidentiality agreement

**This is the argument this chapter owns, and it is stated once, here.**

> The restricted-use paragraph and the non-disclosure agreement are routinely conflated, and they are
> conflated because they arrive in the same envelope. **The restricted-use paragraph is the practitioner's
> limitation on the use of the report** under the attestation standards: it says who the report was prepared
> for and who can be expected to understand it, and it exists because a report whose subject matter is a set
> of specified criteria over a specified period, tested by specified procedures, will mislead a reader who
> does not know what those specifications are. **The non-disclosure agreement is CloudNimbus's contract with
> its customer** and does an entirely different job: it governs what the recipient may do with information
> it has been given, and it is enforceable by CloudNimbus against that recipient and by nobody else against
> anybody.
>
> **Neither can do the other's work.** A customer that signs the non-disclosure agreement has not thereby
> become a specified party — CloudNimbus cannot confer on a recipient the understanding the restriction
> describes by presenting a signature page. And a specified party who has signed nothing has not thereby
> become free of the confidentiality terms in its own master services agreement. **The two instruments
> overlap in most cases and are independent in all of them.**

The practical consequence is the one a vendor risk analyst will actually meet. **A user entity's own
external auditor is ordinarily a specified party** — they have the knowledge of internal control, of
criteria and of the limitations of an examination that the paragraph describes, and they are reading the
report for the purpose it was prepared for.

**And a prospective customer is not excluded by the fact that it has not yet contracted.** The AICPA's
illustrative restricted-use paragraph expressly names **prospective user entities**, business partners of
the service organisation and of user entities, their practitioners and their regulators among the parties a
report may be intended for — **in every case subject to their having sufficient knowledge and
understanding.** **The test is knowledge, not contractual status.** A prospective customer whose security
function has been reading examination reports for a decade can satisfy it; a contracted customer whose
nominated recipient sits in procurement with no audit function behind them may not, and two of the 118 did
not. What a prospective customer is **not** is a user entity during any part of the period, which is the
second class and is a question of fact about dates rather than a judgement about a reader. The determination
is therefore made about the named person and not about the stage of a sales conversation, and §4 records how.

That is why **SC-10** commits CloudNimbus to providing the report under non-disclosure **on request**, and
why no document in this programme, in nine phases, has ever committed to making it generally available. It
is not posted to the trust centre. It is not attached to an outbound mailing. It is not a marketing asset,
and the fact that customers ask for it is not a reason to treat it as one. **DEC-908** records that position
at the point it would have been easiest to relax: the week the report existed and Sales asked for a link.

## 4. The specified-party determination, made one recipient at a time

**DEC-907**, taken by Tobias Lund on 2027-02-27, records that the specified-party determination is made
**per recipient and not per tier**.

The convenient alternative was to determine it per class — enterprise customers in, everybody else out, and
a rule that could be applied by whoever was operating the delivery queue. It was refused for a reason that
is visible in the 118 themselves. The enterprise tier is a commercial classification based on seat count and
contract form; it says nothing about whether the person receiving the report understands what an unmodified
opinion is or what a complementary user entity control obliges their own organisation to do. **Two of the
118 nominated a recipient in procurement with no security or audit function behind them**, and in both cases
the delivery was made with a covering note naming the sections that carry the complementary user entity
controls and the fact that Section V is not covered by the opinion — not as a courtesy, but because the
third class in the restriction is a judgement CloudNimbus has to be able to say it made.

The determination is recorded against each delivery in the distribution record, and the form it uses is at
`templates/report-distribution-record-template.md`. The field that matters is not the recipient's name. It
is the one-line statement of **which class of intended user this recipient falls in and why** — because a
distribution record that holds 155 despatch timestamps and no determinations is a record of an operation
rather than of a decision.

**Eleven requests for a bridge letter arrived alongside these deliveries and are 09.09's — nine answered,
two open.** They are mentioned
here only to say that they arrived in the same fortnight as the board report and the distribution exercise,
exactly as 01.11 §8 said they would, and that the compression landed on the same four people.

## 5. What the report is not, restated where it will be read

The covering note that accompanied all 155 deliveries says four things, and it says them on **09.07 §1**'s
reasoning about what a reader cannot be assumed to know, applied to an envelope instead of a section.

**The opinion covers Sections I to IV and does not cover Section V.** That statement also appears on the
face of Section V itself, which is 09.07's subject and **DEC-903**'s requirement.

**The report is a statement about the period 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31 and about nothing else** — not about
February 2027, not about the system as the recipient will find it, and not about any period the recipient's
own fiscal year happens to cover. The instrument for the gap is the bridge letter, it is management's, and
it is unaudited.

**Nine test exceptions are disclosed in Section IV**, and the covering note names the number rather than
leaving it to be discovered. A recipient who finds nine exceptions after being told the opinion was
unmodified has been handled badly by the sender, whatever the report says.

**Neither the report nor the certificate is a determination of compliance with any law.** That sentence has
appeared in every phase of this programme since the first and it appears in the covering note for the same
reason it appears here: it is the inference a reader is most likely to draw unprompted, and the only way to
prevent it is to say so.

## 6. What restricted use does not do, and what this chapter does not claim

**Restricted use does not stop a recipient forwarding the report.** Nothing in an attestation standard binds
a third party who has not agreed to anything; what binds the recipient is the confidentiality term in its
own agreement, which is CloudNimbus's to enforce and is a commercial question rather than a professional
one. **A programme that describes the restricted-use paragraph as protecting the report has described the
wrong instrument**, and would have no answer when asked who could enforce it.

**It does not make the report comprehensible.** The third class assumes understanding rather than creating
it, and **09.07 §1** owns what follows from that. The consequence here is narrower and is CloudNimbus's own
experience: the section a recipient most often misreads is Section IV, and the misreading is always the same
one — that a disclosed exception is a failed audit. The covering note names the exceptions rather than
waiting for that question, and 09.06 states in terms that nine criteria carry the nine exceptions and
fifty-two carry none, without inviting anyone to read it as a score.

**And the distribution is not an outcome.** 118 deliveries inside a thirty-day obligation is an obligation
discharged, which is the ordinary condition of a contract rather than an achievement. The number worth
watching is not 118 and not 37. It is whether the mechanism that produced them still exists in February
2028, when the next report is due and the team that built this one is doing something else — and no document
written on 2027-03-11 can say anything about that. `PR-51` closes here because the exercise it describes is
complete. What replaces it is a question 09.12 takes.

## 7. The criteria and obligations this chapter answers to

A chapter about who receives a report is a chapter about communication with external parties, and the
criteria say so.

| | What the distribution evidences |
|---|---|
| **CC2.1** and **CC2.3** — the entity obtains or generates and uses relevant quality information to support internal control, and **communicates with external parties regarding matters affecting the functioning of internal control** | `CNB-C-013`, which has Customer Success confirm each quarter that the trust centre's security overview, sub-processor list and availability history match the current position, and `CNB-C-014`, which has the General Counsel maintain the **O1 to O12** obligations register. The report's distribution is the largest single act of external communication the programme performs, and both controls carry it |
| **SC-10** — provision of the assurance report under non-disclosure on request, and a bridge letter where asked | Sourced from **O1, O2 and O3** together, and discharged for the first time by this distribution. `02.12` §2 said in March that SC-10 concerns a restricted-use report; **this is that sentence arriving at a delivery record**. **The two limbs do not reach the same place.** The **report** limb is external communication about matters affecting the functioning of internal control and reaches **CC2.3** through `CNB-C-013` and `CNB-C-014`. The **bridge-letter** limb reaches **no trust services criterion at all** — it is not part of the description, not a control and not tested — and `CNB-C-014`'s obligations register is the whole of its coverage. **09.09 §7** owns that, and this row states it rather than letting the commitment's single identifier imply that both limbs land in the same column |
| **O2** — the report to the 118 enterprise-tier customers within thirty days of issuance | Delivered by 2027-03-06, twenty-two days inside |
| **O1** — a current report available under non-disclosure on request to all 640 | 37 of the remaining 522 requested; 37 delivered |

**One thing this chapter cannot evidence, and it should be said here rather than left out.** Neither
`CNB-C-013` nor `CNB-C-014` requires anybody to check that a recipient **is** a specified party within the
meaning of the restricted-use paragraph. The controls record that the report was sent and to whom; they do
not record the judgement that the recipient had the knowledge and understanding the paragraph describes.
**That judgement was made, customer by customer, and it left no artefact.** Recorded as **`IS-38`**,
referred to the 2027 programme.
