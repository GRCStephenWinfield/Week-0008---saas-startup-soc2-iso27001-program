## 2. What was reported

Six items, and nothing was withheld from any of them.

**The opinion and the nine exceptions.** The report was issued by Ashcombe &amp; Doyle LLP on **2027-02-26**
for the period 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31 across all five trust services categories, and the form of the
opinion is **unmodified**. **Nine test exceptions are disclosed in Section IV under nine of the sixty-one
applicable criteria**, each with the control as stated in the description, the test performed, the
population, the result and the service auditor's evaluation. It was reported that the engagement team raised
on 2027-01-28 that it was considering whether exception 5 required a modification, that the reasoning on
both sides is set out in the report and in the communication to those charged with governance of
**2027-02-10**, and that **the distance between an unmodified opinion and a qualified one in this engagement
was one judgement about one denominator.**

**The certificate and the major nonconformity, together.** The ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate was issued
**2027-01-22** by Northgate Certification Services, Ltd., an ANAB-accredited certification body, covering
the whole organisation and valid to 2030-01-21. The **major nonconformity** raised at Stage 2 on 2026-12-02
against clause 9.2 — an internal audit programme that audited Annex A controls and did not cover clauses 4
to 10 or `eu-central-1` — was accepted without contest, corrected, and verified closed on 2027-01-15. Both
are in **Section V** of the report, which is not covered by the service auditor's opinion and which states
that on its own face.

**The register at close against the forecast.** 38 entries — **0 High · 17 Moderate · 21 Low** — against the
Phase 03 forecast of **0 · 16 · 22**, published 2026-04-24 under ADR-0015 after the build harness proved it
reachable. A band difference of one, **eleven ratings that diverge underneath it**, and five of those
eleven crossing a band. The March CAL-06 review of 2027-03-09 held all thirty-eight and moved none.

**The objective scorecard**, at §3.

**The cost.** **$1,366,000 committed against a $1,400,000 envelope**, headroom $34,000, which is **2.4%** and
is a rounding error rather than a saving. The largest single line is **internal labour at $612,000**, larger
than every external fee combined. Resourcing across the programme was **4.6 FTE-equivalents** against a
187-person organisation.

**And the open corrective actions.** **Sixteen open, none closed** — twelve carrying `CA-` identifiers and
four being the limbs against the Stage 2 minor nonconformities, whose verification falls to Northgate at the
**2027-11 surveillance audit**. Three of the sixteen had an argument for closure available and were named as
such rather than closed.

## 3. The objective scorecard, as tabled

**Seven of the eight objectives set in the charter of 2026-01-19 were met. One was missed.**

| Objective | Target | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| **OBJ-03** — one control library and one evidence store serving both frameworks | **≥ 70%** of evidence artefacts serving both deliverables | **16.9% — 356 of 2,103. Missed.** |

The scorecard was presented with the miss stated first and the seven met objectives after it. The committee
was told three things about OBJ-03 and in this order: that it missed its stated target by a factor of four, on
the measure the objective itself names; that **the measure was wrong**, because most evidence is
framework-specific and the integration dividend sits in the **113 of 150 controls that serve both frameworks —
75.3%**, a split of 113 dual, 21 SOC 2-only and 16 ISO-only re-derived at 150 rather than carried forward from
the 112 of 148 published in Phase 04; and that **the better measure was identified by an adversarial reader of
Phase 08 and not by the people who wrote the objective or by the committees that tracked it for fourteen
months.**

**The measure was not restated at this meeting and OBJ-03 was not re-scored.** Re-measurement is referred to
the annual objective-setting occurrence under `CNB-C-016`, which falls after this vantage, and `PR-48`
carries it. **ADR-0043** and **DEC-911** record the scoring; **DEC-912** records the referral.

Of the seven met, three were reported with the caveat attached rather than as clean results. **OBJ-01** — an
unmodified opinion — is an outcome the charter's own §3.1 said in January 2026 should never have been
written as a target, because the opinion is another party's. **OBJ-02** was met nine days inside its date
only because a major nonconformity was closed in forty-four days, and **the margin is not evidence of
planning**. **OBJ-04** closed all three 2025 management letter matters before the window opened, and **ML-1's
and ML-2's subject matter produced two of the nine exceptions anyway.**

## 4. The question the committee asked

Lorraine Kessler asked **why the organisation's own records contained the major nonconformity eleven months
before an auditor raised it, and what has changed so that the next one is acted on rather than filed.**

The facts put to the committee in answer were not in dispute and had all been minuted before. The audit
programme document was written at chartering in January 2026 and scoped by reference to the Statement of
Applicability. Camberwell Risk Partners' internal audit report of 2026-09-25 stated the scope limitation at
**§1.3** and recommended it be reconsidered before the certification audit. Karim Haddad read it, agreed with
it and scheduled the coverage into the 2027 programme — **DEC-801**. Elise Fontaine took the same note to the
clause 9.3 management review on 2026-09-30 under **input d) 3)** and confirmed the deferral — **DEC-802**.
`MAJ-01` was raised on **2026-12-02**.

**Nothing was missed and nothing was hidden.** The finding was written down in advance by the people it was
later raised against.

The answer given to the second half of the question was three things: **`CNB-C-150`**, the annual review of
the coverage matrix against the ISMS scope and the Statement of Applicability; the **three-year coverage
matrix** itself, adopted under DEC-808 on 2026-12-18; and the escalation of any uncovered clause or location
**to the Audit &amp; Risk Committee itself**, rather than to the programme owner whose scope decision produced
the finding.

Two limits on that answer were also put to the committee rather than left for it to find. **`CNB-C-150` has
never operated and cannot be tested by anybody until the 2028 audit programme is set** — `IS-36`, and
Northgate's acceptance of the corrective action rested on the rewritten programme document and the January
audit it produced, not on the control. And **`IS-35` records the class rather than the instance**: a control
statement and the procedure that carries it can drift apart silently anywhere in a library of 150 rows, the
library side gives no signal because the statement is correct, and nothing has been enumerated.

## 5. The limit the committee recorded about itself

The committee recorded the answer and then recorded the limit of it, in these terms.

> The answer to *why did governance not catch it* is *governance will now be told*, which is an improvement
> and not a guarantee. **A committee that is told something is a committee that can defer it, and this one
> already has** — the same information reached the highest governance body in the ISMS on 2026-09-30, in
> complete form, with the recommendation on its face, and was deferred there. What has changed is the route
> and the cadence, not the disposition of the body at the end of it.

**The paragraph is the committee's own and was dictated by its chair.** 01.07 §4.1 recorded at chartering
that a committee with one voting member is not a committee in the sense a listed company means, that the
independence genuinely available at a 187-person private company is one non-executive chair with a
contractual line to the outsourced internal auditor and the right to convene out of cycle, and that the
arrangement depends materially on one person. **Fourteen months later, the one person is the one who wrote
the limitation down.** That is what the structure supports and it is a smaller claim than the word
"committee" implies.

## 7. What was not reported

**No forward statement about the 2027 window.** It opened 2027-01-01, is ten weeks old, and has produced no
rate, no deviation and no position. Nothing at this meeting characterised it.

**No forecast of the surveillance audit's outcome**, and no statement that the four minor nonconformities
will be verified closed at it.

**No claim that the programme is complete.** The charter's deliverables exist; sixteen corrective actions,
seventeen issues, a clause 7.4 minor nonconformity from the correction audit and thirty-eight register
entries do not stop existing because a board meeting has happened.

**And no legal conclusion about anything.** **O1** describes a steady state CloudNimbus did not occupy from
2024 until 2027-02-26, the portfolio has recorded that throughout as an open contractual gap, and whether
any contract was breached is a question this committee did not ask and this record does not answer.
