## 1. The miss, first

**OBJ-03 was missed.** It targeted **70% of evidence artefacts serving both deliverables** and the programme
produced **16.9% — 356 artefacts of 2,103.** That is not a near miss and not a measurement problem
discovered at the end. It is the objective missing its own stated target **by a factor of four**, on the
measure the objective itself names, computed from the evidence store it was written about.

**And OBJ-03 is the integration objective** — the one that measures the single control library, the single
evidence store and the produce-once-use-twice argument ADR-0001 made in January 2026 and every subsequent
phase was built on. Of the eight objectives, the one the programme was designed around is the one it missed.

The other seven were met. This chapter states them, states what each cost or concealed, and then spends §4
on OBJ-03. **The order is deliberate and it is the point.** A close-out that opened with seven green rows
and reached the red one on page four would have taught a reader that objectives are mostly met, which is
not what happened here and is not a lesson.

## 2. The scorecard

01.07 §3 set eight objectives, each with a named owner, a measure readable from evidence rather than
asserted in a status report, and a target. All eight are scored below on the measure as it was written.

| ID | Target | Actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| OBJ-01 | Unmodified opinion, form of opinion in Section I | Unmodified, 2027-02-26 | **Met** |
| OBJ-02 | ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate issued by 2027-01-31 | Issued 2027-01-22 | **Met** |
| OBJ-03 | ≥ 70% of evidence artefacts serving both deliverables | **16.9% — 356 of 2,103** | **Missed** |
| OBJ-04 | ML-1, ML-2 and ML-3 closed and verified, 3 of 3 by 2026-06-30 | 3 of 3 closed and verified | **Met** |
| OBJ-05 | Mean questionnaire turnaround from 11.4 days to ≤ 3.0 days | 2.6 days across 47 questionnaires, Q1 2027 | **Met** |
| OBJ-06 | Register maintained; quarterly review cadence, no lapse | Five CAL-06 occurrences, no lapse | **Met** |
| OBJ-07 | Committed spend ≤ $1,400,000 | $1,366,000 | **Met** |
| OBJ-08 | Awareness training completion ≥ 95% of 187 staff | 96.8% — 181 of 187 | **Met** |

**Seven met, one missed.** Every figure in the Actual column is read from an artefact in the evidence store,
which is what `CNB-C-144` requires and what 01.07 §3 promised. **The scorecard is checkable, and so is the
miss.**

## 3. The seven, with what each of them cost or concealed

**OBJ-01 is met on an objective the charter disowned in January.** 01.07 §3.1 said at kickoff that OBJ-01
states an outcome CloudNimbus does not control: the opinion in Section I is Meredith Vance's, formed on her
firm's procedures and her own judgement, and it is not a thing a corrective action plan can bring back on
track in December. The charter recorded it as a target and tracked it as a forecast. **So the programme's
most visible success is the objective its own charter said should never have been written as a target** —
and the honest reading of the green row is that it records what an independent party concluded, not what
CloudNimbus delivered.

**OBJ-02 is met by nine days, and the margin is not evidence of planning.** The certificate was issued
2027-01-22 against a target of 2027-01-31, nine days inside, because a **major nonconformity raised on
2026-12-02 was corrected, verified and closed in forty-four days** — plan submitted 2026-12-19, a full clause
4 to 10 and `eu-central-1` audit conducted 2027-01-05 to 01-09, a supplementary audit on 2027-01-13, closure
verified 2027-01-15, certification decision 2027-01-20. A programme with nine days of slack that then
absorbed a major nonconformity inside them did not have nine days of slack; it had a certification body that
scheduled quickly and a team that spent Christmas on it. **The date target was met. Nothing about the
interval before it was planned.**

**OBJ-04 is met, and closing a management letter point did not close the thing it was about.** All three
2025 matters were closed and verified before 2026-06-30. ML-1 said the access review evidence would not
support a Type II sample; the activity now produces 24 selectable `EC-01` units across two quarterly
certifications. ML-2 said change-management peer review was enforced by convention rather than by branch
protection; it is now enforced by the pipeline. **Eighteen months later, ML-1's subject matter produced
exception 9 and ML-2's produced exception 4.** **Remediating a control is not the same as remediating the
evidence trail it leaves behind, and it is not the same as remediating everything the control's failure mode
can reach.** That is the whole of what OBJ-04's green row is worth, and it is the sentence this portfolio
would keep if it could keep one from the objective set.

**OBJ-05 is met, with a caveat that belongs before the number.** The mean turnaround on an enterprise
security questionnaire fell from **11.4 days to 2.6**, across **47 questionnaires in the first quarter of
2027**, against a target of three or fewer, and it is the commercial reason the board funded the programme.
It was measured **in the quarter in which a report first existed**, and part of the improvement is not
process efficiency at all: the answer to a large share of the questions is now *see the report*, which
converts a research task into a delivery task. **A measure taken in the first period after the thing it
measures arrived is a measure of the arrival.**

**OBJ-06 is met on cadence, which is what it measured.** Five CAL-06 occurrences, no lapse — 2026-03, which
reviewed the risk assessment method because no register existed until 2026-04-10, then 2026-06-15, 2026-09-29,
2026-12-29 and 2027-03-09. **The measure is that the review happened; it does not ask what the review did.**
Two moved one entry each — R-37 in June and R-08 in September — one moved nineteen, one moved none, and the
first reviewed a method rather than a register. All five are defensible on their own record, **but a cadence
measure would have returned the same green row if every one had been a fifteen-minute call approving the
previous position.** `IS-37` exists because that is a real risk across a twelve-month window, and OBJ-06's
measure would not see it.

**OBJ-07 is met with $34,000 of headroom, which is 2.4% and a rounding error rather than a saving.**
$1,400,000 − $1,366,000 = $34,000, and on a fourteen-month programme with ten committed lines that is inside
the noise of any one of them — the contingency line alone is $61,500. **The interesting number is not the
headroom but that internal labour at $612,000 is larger than every external fee combined**: $118,000 for the
examination, $47,500 for certification, $96,000 for readiness advisory, **$34,000 for the outsourced ISMS
internal audit** — that is **B-04** in 01.07 §6, Camberwell Risk Partners, and it is **the same figure as the
headroom by coincidence rather than by construction**, which is why this sentence names the line and the
firm and not the amount alone — and $72,000 for penetration testing. 01.07 §6 said so before any of it was
spent.

**OBJ-08 is met at 96.8%, and the six who did not complete are worth naming rather than rounding.** 181 of
187 completed annual security awareness training; **four were on long-term leave and two had left the
organisation.** The assignment rule suspends the training on the day long-term leave begins and re-issues it
on return, so **long-term leave bars completion inside the annual cycle exactly as leaving does, and none of
the six could complete.** One ceiling follows from that and it has to be stated rather than softened:
**the practical ceiling was 96.8%, which is the actual.** The objective was met at its ceiling. A measure
whose ceiling is its result cannot distinguish a programme that chased every completable person from one
that did not — the same 96.8% would have come back either way — and that is a finding about the measure, not
a credit to the performance. It also means the measure will read differently in a year with more leave and
no other change.

## 4. OBJ-03, missed on what it said and met on what it meant

**The programme's integration objective is the one it missed, and it missed it on its own stated measure by
a factor of four.** 356 artefacts of 2,103 were requested by both Ashcombe &amp; Doyle LLP and Northgate
Certification Services and produced once. The target was 70%. **This chapter reports the miss before it
explains it, because the order matters and because every available explanation is more flattering than the
number.**

**Now the explanation, and it is a second finding rather than an excuse: the measure was wrong.**

**Most evidence is framework-specific and always will be.** A clause 9.2 audit plan is not a SOC 2 artefact
and a Section IV test population is not an ISO one. Camberwell's independence check, Northgate's Stage 1
documented-information review, the Statement of Applicability's status fields and the clause 9.3 minutes
have no counterpart in an examination; the reconciliation records, the export delivery acknowledgements, the
complementary user entity control dispositions and the description of the system have no counterpart in a
certification audit. **Two frameworks assessing different objects ask for different things**, and a design
producing a high artefact overlap would be one that had flattened one of the two into the other.

**The integration dividend was never in the artefacts. It is in the controls.** **113 of the library's 150
controls serve both frameworks — 75.3% — which clears the 70% bar on the measure the objective should have
used.** The three-way split at 150 is **113 dual · 21 SOC 2-only · 16 ISO-only**, and it had to be re-derived
before it could be stated here. **`04.03` published 112 dual, 21 SOC 2-only and 15 ISO-only out of a library
of 148, `08.09` restated it, and neither figure was recomputed when the library grew**: `CNB-C-149` was
admitted on 2026-10-28 citing **P4.2 and P4.3** and **A.8.10 and A.8.16**, which makes it dual, and
`CNB-C-150` on 2027-01-08 cites neither a trust services criterion nor an Annex A control, which makes it
ISO-only. **Two controls were added mid-programme and nobody re-derived the split** — which is `IS-39`'s
failure mode exactly, a number that was right when it was written describing a population that moved without
it, found the same way `IS-39` was found, by counting.

That split is where the design cost, the ownership, the scheduling, the training and the operating cost
were saved: one access provisioning control rather than two, one change peer review rather than two, one
retention job set rather than two, each producing evidence once into one store in one declared sampling
unit, whether or not that unit was ever requested twice. **An integration argument defended on artefact
counts is an integration argument that will lose**, because the artefact count is the one number in the
design a sceptic can compute and it goes the sceptic's way.

**So OBJ-03 is missed on what it said and met on what it meant, and both halves of that sentence are
findings.** The first is a failure of an objective; the second is a failure of the people who wrote it,
which is the harder to admit because the alternative reading — that the integration model did not work — is
less flattering to the design and more flattering to the judgement exercised in January 2026.

**A programme that quietly restated the measure at the end would have been marking its own homework.** This
one reports the miss, publishes the better measure alongside it, and records how that measure was arrived
at.

**It was identified in Phase 08, by an adversarial reader of that phase, and not by the people who wrote the
objective.** 08.09 §4 states the real number first and the reason it is the wrong one second, on the
reasoning that the first order is the only one in which the second statement is worth anything. Fourteen
months of Trust Committee meetings tracked OBJ-03 against 70% without anybody asking whether 70% of
artefacts was the thing the objective was about. **DEC-911** records the scoring.

**And the measure is not restated here.** **DEC-912** refers it to the annual objective-setting occurrence
under `CNB-C-016`, whose next falls after this vantage. The alternative — declare a corrected measure in the
close-out chapter, score against it, and report seven met and one met-on-revision — was available, would
have been defensible on the merits, and was refused: **a programme that changes a measure in the document
that scores it has scored itself.** `PR-48` carries the re-measurement to the body that sets objectives
rather than the chapter that grades them. **ADR-0043** records the decision, the order and the
alternative.

## 5. The programme in figures

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Controls in the unified library | **150** — **113 dual-serving · 21 SOC 2-only · 16 ISO-only** |
| Trust services criteria in scope | **61** — Security 33, Availability 3, Processing Integrity 5, Confidentiality 2, Privacy 18 |
| Annex A controls considered | **93** — **91 determined necessary**, 2 determined not necessary and excluded in the Statement of Applicability |
| Risk register at close | **38 entries** — 0 High · 17 Moderate · 21 Low. **2 added on evidence, none closed and none removed** |
| Decisions recorded | **116**, DEC-101 to DEC-915 |
| Architectural decision records | **45**, ADR-0001 to ADR-0045 |
| Governance records | **36**, GOV-01 to GOV-36 |
| Evidence artefacts produced | **2,103**, from one shared evidence store |
| Committed spend against the envelope | **$1,366,000** against **$1,400,000** |
| Resourcing | **4.6 FTE-equivalents**, against a 187-person organisation |
| Phases delivered | **9** — **Phase 01 re-issued once, Phase 02 once, Phase 04 three times** |

Two rows carry a comment and the rest do not.

**The register row.** Two additions and no closures is the register's whole population history, and the zero
in the closure column is the figure a reader should test the programme on. A risk that stops being likely is
re-rated, not deleted.

**The re-issue row.** Phase 04 was re-issued three times because three amendments were made at source rather
than corrected downstream. **A library that corrects itself silently cannot demonstrate that it was ever
wrong**, and five re-issues across three phases is the cost of that position rather than a defect in it.

## 6. The charter's own success criteria, scored

**01.07 §7 set four measurable success criteria and a fifth it called unmeasurable. Until this section they
had never been scored anywhere in this portfolio.** The eight objectives were tracked monthly for fourteen
months and are scored above. The four criteria that say what *success* means were set at chartering and then
never marked — and the only part of 01.07 §7 this phase ever reached back for was the **fifth**, the
unmeasurable one, which §7 below and 09.13 §5 both quote. **This section exists because an adversarial
reader of Phase 09 noticed that, and not because the programme volunteered it.**

| # | The criterion, as 01.07 §7 states it | What the record shows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A **SOC 2 Type II report exists** covering all five categories for the full six-month window, and it is a report CloudNimbus is willing to hand to a customer under non-disclosure **without a covering explanation** | The report was issued 2027-02-26 over 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31, across Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy, and 155 recipients hold it | **Met**, and §6.1 says what the second limb costs |
| 2 | An **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate exists**, covering the whole organisation, issued by an accredited certification body, **with no open major nonconformity** | Issued 2027-01-22 by Northgate Certification Services, Ltd. under ANAB accreditation, whole organisation, valid to 2030-01-21. `MAJ-01` was verified closed 2027-01-15, five days before the certification decision | **Met.** Four **minor** nonconformities are open and the criterion did not ask about them — `MIN-01` to `MIN-04`, verified at the 2027-11 surveillance audit |
| 3 | The evidence behind both was **produced once and used twice, at or above the 70% threshold in OBJ-03** | **356 of 2,103 artefacts — 16.9%**, against a threshold of 70% | **Not met** |
| 4 | Enterprise **security-questionnaire turnaround has fallen from 11.4 days to three or fewer** | **2.6 days across 47 questionnaires** in the first quarter of 2027 | **Met**, on OBJ-05's caveat: it was measured in the first quarter in which a report existed |

**Three of four met and one not met, and the one not met is the third.** It fails on the same figure OBJ-03
fails on, because it is the same measure — the charter wrote the objective's threshold into its own
definition of success, so the miss propagates and cannot be scored around. **The better measure of 75.3% is
not admitted here either**, for the reason ADR-0043 gives: it is a measure this programme arrived at after
the result, and 01.07 §7 names OBJ-03's threshold and no other.

**A close-out that reports seven of eight objectives met while leaving the charter's own success test
unscored has chosen the flattering instrument.** Eight objectives with seven green rows is a better-looking
sentence than four success criteria with one red one, and the second is the test the board actually
approved. The two instruments were both available on 2027-03-11; only one of them was used, and the choice
was not made deliberately, which is worse rather than better — nobody weighed the two and picked. **It went
unscored because nothing required it to be scored, and no control in the library asks whether a charter's
success criteria have been marked at close.** That is what the eight objectives had and the four criteria did
not.

### 6.1 The second limb of the first criterion, which is the one worth arguing with

**Every one of the 155 deliveries carried a covering note**, and the note names the nine exceptions, states
that Section V is not covered by the opinion, states the period and states that neither deliverable is a
determination of compliance with any law. A reader can fairly ask whether a report that travels with a note
is a report handed over *without a covering explanation*.

**The distinction this section draws, and it is arguable either way**: the note explains what the document
**is**, and it does not explain the document's contents **away**. It names the exceptions rather than
waiting to be asked about them, which is the opposite of the failure the criterion was written against — a
report a company has to talk a customer through before the customer will accept it. Scored on that reading
the criterion is met. **Scored on the words alone it is not**, and this section records the argument rather
than settling it, because the charter did not define the limb tightly enough to be scored mechanically and
the honest answer to *which reading did the programme use* is *the one that suits it*.

## 7. What the scorecard does not measure

**It does not measure whether the controls will operate in 2027.** Every figure above describes a period
that has ended or a position on 2027-03-11.

**It does not measure security.** Nothing in eight objectives, in an examination report or in a certificate
does. OBJ-01 measures the form of an opinion, OBJ-02 the existence of a certificate, and the rest measure
programme delivery.

**And it does not measure the thing 01.07 §7 called the fifth success criterion** — that the programme's own
account of what happened is accurate. §6 scores the four criteria that can be measured; this is the one that
cannot, it was stated as unmeasurable when it was written, and it remains so. The nearest thing to evidence
for it is that the one objective the programme missed is reported as missed, on the measure it was written
against, in the first paragraph of the chapter that scores it — and that §6 exists at all, having been
written after somebody outside the programme asked why the charter's own test had never been marked.
