## 1. Who writes Section IV

**Section IV carries, for each applicable trust services criterion, the controls management states meet it,
the tests the service auditor performed, and the results — including any deviation and the service auditor's
evaluation of it.** Four things, and the first is management's while the other three are not.

> **Management writes Section III. The service auditor writes Section IV.** The description states what a
> control **is**; Section IV states what was **tested**, on what population, and what was **found**. **A
> portfolio that blurs the two has misunderstood the document**, and this one has kept them apart for nine
> phases: `04.05` and `04.06` publish the control statements, `08.10` records the populations CloudNimbus
> produced and the selections Ashcombe &amp; Doyle made from them, and nothing in either is a test result.

`08.11` published the nine in February, on the last day of fieldwork, with the criterion, the control, the
population, the deviations and the rate. **It could not publish the other two columns, because they did not
exist yet.** This chapter adds them: **the test performed** and **the service auditor's evaluation**, both
as they appear in the report issued on 2027-02-26.

**A test exception is a fact about a sample — or, here, more often about a population.** Ashcombe &amp;
Doyle tested in full every population at or below sixty. **Two deviations in a population of forty license
no inference at all: they are what happened, forty times, twice.**

## 2. The nine as published

| # | Criterion | Control | Population | Deviations | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A1.2** | `CNB-C-098` | 6 monthly restore tests | 1 | **16.7%** |
| 2 | **A1.1, A1.2** | `CNB-C-096` | 1 event | 1 | **—** |
| 3 | **PI1.3** | `CNB-C-108` | 58 reconciliation exceptions | 2 | **3.4%** |
| 4 | **CC8.1** | `CNB-C-082` | 15 emergency changes | 2 | **13.3%** |
| 5 | **P4.2** | `CNB-C-126`, `CNB-C-127` | 184 nights of RT-02 in `eu-central-1` | 68 | **37.0%** |
| 6 | **CC9.2** | `CNB-C-092` | 24 quarterly Tier 1 readings | 2 | **8.3%** |
| 7 | **P5.1** | `CNB-C-129` | 58 data subject requests | 1 | **1.7%** |
| 8 | **CC6.2** | `CNB-C-037` | 40 provisioning requests | 2 | **5.0%** |
| 9 | **CC6.3** | `CNB-C-040` | 47 revocations arising | 3 | **6.4%** |

**Every rate above is recomputable from the two columns to its left**, which is the property the table is
built for and the reason exception 5's population reads 184 rather than 4,416.

**Exception 2 carries a dash rather than a rate, and the dash is the finding.** `CNB-C-096`'s synthetic
probe performed a read, so through seventy-one minutes in which reads succeeded and writes failed, no burn
registered against the error budget. **Every word of the control was satisfied.** There is no population of
occurrences in which the control sometimes worked and sometimes did not; it worked on all of them and
measured the wrong thing on the one that mattered. **A design deficiency has an event, not a rate**, and
expressing it as one-in-something would invite a reader to divide it into insignificance.

**Exception 5's denominator is stated as the nights of the rule that failed, in the region it failed in.**
The whole scheduled-deletion population is 8 rules × 3 regions × 184 nights = **4,416 rule-nights**, against
which the same sixty-eight nights read as **1.5%**. Both numbers are true; `09.05` §2 publishes both, says
which this programme thinks is the honest one and why, and that argument is not restated here.

**And exception 4's 13.3% is the second-largest rate in the table and the smallest matter in it.** The
population is the fifteen emergency changes, **not the 312 changes deployed in the window**. The emergency
path is a designed escape hatch used fifteen times in 312 — under five per cent of deployments — and every
one of the fifteen reached production through the pipeline with a ticket and a rollback plan. **Expressing
two late approvals as 2 of 312 would be dishonest and expressing them as 13.3% is correct and invites a
misreading**, which is why the population column says fifteen.

## 3. The test performed

| # | What Ashcombe &amp; Doyle did | Selection |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inspected the restore test record for each monthly occurrence, agreeing the test date, the source snapshot, the restore target and the integrity comparison result | **6 — tested in full** |
| 2 | Inspected the control statement in force on each date, the probe configuration under both statements, and the error-budget record across `INC-2026-031`; reperformed the availability computation for September | The single event, and both statements |
| 3 | Agreed the raise time and the clearance time of every reconciliation exception against the two-business-day service level | **58 — tested in full** |
| 4 | Agreed the deployment date and the retrospective approval date of every emergency change against the five-business-day requirement, and inspected the six monthly log reviews | **15 and 6 — tested in full** |
| 5 | Selected job-nights across all eight rules and all three regions and agreed the completion record to the deleted-row count; inspected the disclosed condition in full, including the catch-up deletion and the independent partition counts | **60 job-nights of 4,416**, plus the disclosed condition |
| 6 | Inspected the assurance artefact held at each quarterly Tier 1 refresh, including the negative reading form where none was current | **24 — tested in full** |
| 7 | Agreed the receipt date and the delivery date of every data subject request against the ten-business-day commitment | **58 — tested in full** |
| 8 | Compared the recorded manager approval timestamp with the provisioning timestamp for every access request, and agreed the entitlements granted to the role profile | **40 — tested in full** |
| 9 | Inspected each entitlement certification unit for completion and evidence, and agreed the action date of every revocation arising to the review closure date | **24 units and 47 revocations — tested in full** |

**Exception 5's row shows what a disclosed condition does to a test.** The sixty-job-night selection is not
the procedure that found the failure and nothing about it made the deviation visible. **The entity had
disclosed the condition in October, four months before the test, and the test inspected it in full because
it was there to be inspected.** A reader who takes 60 of 4,416 as the evidence for exception 5 has the
mechanism backwards.

## 4. The service auditor's evaluation

**Everything in this section is the service auditor's and appears in Section IV in the service auditor's
words.** Management neither endorses nor contests it, `09.05` §5 owns that position, and it applies to all
nine rather than only to the fifth.

| # | The evaluation as published |
|---|---|
| 1 | The occurrence was not performed and was not re-performed and back-dated; four other controls serve **A1.2** across the window, including the nightly backup job and the region-pinned recovery module. The deviation does not indicate that A1.2 was not achieved for the period |
| 2 | The control operated on every occurrence under both statements; the deficiency is in the **design** of the original statement, which was silent on what the probe must exercise. The statement was amended mid-period and the amended probe operated for the remainder. **A1.1 and A1.2** are each served by further controls that operated throughout |
| 3 | Both exceptions arise from a service level expressed in business days against a queue worked weekly, and both were cleared. **PI1.3** is served by the reconciliation itself, the export hold and the daily controls around it; the deviations do not indicate that PI1.3 was not achieved |
| 4 | Both changes reached production through the designed emergency path with a ticket and a rollback plan, and both were detected and escalated by the control's own second limb. **CC8.1** is served by nine controls and the deviation is against one limb of one of them |
| 5 | **The evaluation set out in `09.05` §3, in full.** The failure was confined to one rule in one region, was detected inside the period, was corrected and independently verified, and produced a new control admitted on its evidence — against a duration of 37% of the period, on the most sensitive category the platform holds, and a control set that could not have detected it. **The deviations do not mean P4.2 was not achieved for the period taken as a whole** |
| 6 | The control **detected** the lapse at both refreshes and completed the negative reading form each time; what did not happen is escalation on a second consecutive negative reading. **CC9.2** is not indicated as unachieved by a detection that worked and an escalation that was not defined |
| 7 | One request in fifty-eight exceeded the ten-business-day commitment by two business days, with the record set delivered complete; the delay arises from the tenant scoping predicate that keeps one employer's data away from another. **P5.1** is not indicated as unachieved |
| 8 | The defect is the **sequence**: provisioning preceded the recorded approval in two of forty requests, with the approval recorded the same day and the entitlements generated from the role profile in both cases, so no entitlement was granted outside the model. **The sequence is what CC6.2 asks about**, and the deviations are evaluated against the whole of the criterion's control set |
| 9 | **Both quarterly certifications were completed on time and evidenced.** Three of the forty-seven revocations arising were applied between four and eleven days after the review was recorded closed. The deviation is against the closure gate rather than against the certification, and does not indicate that **CC6.3** was not achieved |

**Row 2 is the one worth reading against row 5.** Exception 2 is a design deficiency with an event and no
rate; exception 5 is an operating failure with a rate of 37.0% and no design defect in the control that
failed. **They are opposite kinds of finding and the evaluation reaches the same place about both**, which
is what an evaluation looks like when it is applied rather than inferred from a percentage.

## 5. Nine criteria, and the fifty-two that carry none

**Nine of the sixty-one applicable criteria carry the nine exceptions. Fifty-two carry none.**

**That is stated because a reader will otherwise count it wrongly, and it is stated flatly because it is not
a score.** Fifty-two criteria carrying no deviation is not fifty-two successes; several of them are served
by controls with a population of one, and `CC5.3` is served by a single control that operated once in the
window. **A criterion with no exception against it may have been tested exhaustively or may have had one
occurrence to test**, and Section IV shows which, criterion by criterion, in the population column. The
number to read is not nine and it is not fifty-two. **It is the population beside each row.**

**Where the nine fall is not random and is not flattering in the way it first looks.** Four sit in the
Common Criteria — change management, vendor assurance, access provisioning and access review — and five sit
outside them, in Availability, Processing Integrity and Privacy. **The 2025 Type I examined Security
alone.** So five of the nine are in categories no service auditor had ever looked at, and four are in the
one that had been examined once, for design. **A category never examined and a category examined once for
design are not two degrees of the same thing**, and the distribution says less about where CloudNimbus is
weak than about where anybody had previously looked.

**And two of the nine are the 2025 management letter's subject matter, eighteen months on.** ML-1 said the
access review evidence would not support a Type II sample; ML-2 said change-management peer review was
enforced by convention rather than by branch protection. Both controls were rebuilt, both matters were
closed and verified before 2026-06-30, and **exception 9 and exception 4 arose anyway** — because
**remediating a control is not the same as remediating everything the control's failure mode can reach.**

## 6. What Section IV is not

**It is not a list of failures.** It is the record of what was tested and what was found, and the
overwhelming majority of what it records is controls operating as described across populations in the
hundreds and thousands. The nine are the entries a reader will look at, which is a fact about readers.

**It is not the opinion.** The opinion is Section I, it is expressed on the report as a whole, and **test
exceptions disclosed in Section IV do not automatically modify it.**

**It is not where an ISO nonconformity goes.** `MAJ-01` is a clause 9.2 finding; clause 9.2 has no trust
services criterion, `CNB-C-146` is ISO-only and appears in no criterion's control set, and **a control that
is not in Section IV cannot have a deviation reported in Section IV.** It is in Section V, and `09.07`
carries it.

**And it is not a document CloudNimbus can amend.** Section IV is the service auditor's, and what
CloudNimbus holds is the report as issued, retained exactly as delivered.
