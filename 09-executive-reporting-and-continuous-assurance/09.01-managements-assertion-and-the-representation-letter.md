## 1. Two instruments, and the reader sees one

**Under AT-C section 205 — *Assertion-Based Examination Engagements* since SSAE No. 21 — the responsible
party provides a written assertion about the subject matter.** In a SOC 2 examination that assertion is
**Section II of the report**, it is **management's**, and it is the thing the practitioner's opinion in
Section I is expressed in relation to. It is not drafted by the service auditor, not agreed with the service
auditor line by line, and not improved by the service auditor if it is thin.

**The representation letter is a different instrument entirely.** It is addressed to Ashcombe &amp; Doyle
LLP, it is required by **O12** and by the engagement letter, it is dated the report date, and **no user of
the report ever sees it.** It is not a section, not an appendix and not a schedule.

**Say the distinction once and plainly: the assertion goes in the report and the reader sees it; the
representation letter goes to the practitioner and the reader never does.** Conflating them is the commonest
error in describing a SOC 2 engagement, and the reason it is common is that both are signed by the same two
officers, on the same day, in the same meeting. **The signatures are the only thing they share.** One is a
statement to the market about the system; the other is a statement to the auditor about what the auditor has
been given.

This chapter is owned by the Chief Executive Officer rather than by the programme owner because both
instruments carry her signature and neither is delegable. `01.08`'s convention holds: the RACI governs the
conduct of the activity and Rahul Bhargava assembled both documents, and the decision to sign is recorded
against the person who signed.

## 2. What the assertion says

**The assertion is dated 2027-02-26 — the report date — and is signed by Elise Fontaine, Chief Executive
Officer, and Nathan Oyelaran, Chief Technology Officer.** The board approved it on **2027-02-24**;
**DEC-901**, and the record is **GOV-33**.

It asserts, for the period **2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31**, three things and identifies the criteria against
which each is asserted.

**That the description presents the CloudNimbus Workforce Platform as it was designed and implemented
throughout the period, in accordance with the description criteria.** Those criteria are named: **DC section
200, the 2018 *Description Criteria for a Description of a Service Organization's System in a SOC 2
Report*, DC1 to DC9.** `09.02` walks DC1 to DC8 against where each is answered and `09.03` carries DC9.

**That the controls stated in the description were suitably designed** throughout the period to provide
reasonable assurance that the applicable trust services criteria would be achieved if those controls
operated as described.

**That those controls operated effectively** throughout the period to provide reasonable assurance that the
applicable trust services criteria were achieved. The criteria are named: **TSP section 100, the 2017 trust
services criteria with revised points of focus, 2022** — **sixty-one criteria** across Security,
Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy.

The assertion also states what the description does not include, because a carve-out is a statement about
scope and belongs on the face of the assertion rather than in the small print of the description: **AWS and
Halcyon Identity are subservice organisations, both carved out**, their controls excluded from the
description, with **fourteen complementary subservice organisation controls** stated instead; and **eleven
complementary user entity controls** are stated, each naming the criteria it is necessary for.

**Nothing in the assertion is a claim about certification, and nothing in it is a legal conclusion.** A SOC 2
examination produces an opinion. It does not produce a certificate, there is no such thing as being
certified against it, and no document in this programme has ever said otherwise.

## 3. Signing an assertion of operating effectiveness over a period containing nine exceptions

**This is the chapter's point and it has to be met head-on.** Management asserted that the controls operated
effectively throughout a six-month period in which the service auditor's testing produced **nine test
exceptions**, disclosed in Section IV. A reader who takes the assertion to mean *every occurrence of every
control was clean* will read the two documents as contradicting each other.

They do not, and the reason is the same reason the opinion turns the way it does.

> **The assertion is about whether the applicable trust services criteria were achieved, not about whether
> every occurrence of every control was clean.** A criterion is met by everything that serves it, over a
> period, to the standard of *reasonable assurance* — which is a high level of assurance and is not
> absolute. It is the same distinction the opinion turns on and the same one Section IV exists to make
> visible: the exception is printed, with its population and the practitioner's evaluation, so that a reader
> can see what the assertion is being made in spite of.
>
> **A period with no exceptions in a population of 312 changes, 184 nights of scheduled deletion and 10,336
> alerts would be a period whose testing had not looked hard enough.** That sentence is not a defence of
> nine. It is a statement about what a zero would have meant, and it cuts both ways: a programme entitled to
> say it is a programme that has to accept the exceptions when they arrive.

**But the signature is not a formality, and what was considered before it belongs on the record.**

**Two of the nine were put to the board on 2027-02-24 as candidates for not signing.** The consideration is
minuted at GOV-33 and recorded as **DEC-902**, taken by Lorraine Kessler as chair of the Audit &amp; Risk
Committee.

**Exception 5**, on the ground that **P4.2's support in the affected region was thin for 37% of the
period**. The RT-02 deletion job addressed an emptied relation in `eu-central-1` for sixty-eight consecutive
nights; `CNB-C-126` and `CNB-C-127` are the whole of P4.2's support in that region for the 119 nights before
`CNB-C-149` existed, and the sixty-eight sit inside those hundred and nineteen. An officer asked to assert
operating effectiveness has to be able to say what was operating.

**Exception 1**, on the narrower ground that **a monthly control with a population of six cannot absorb a
miss**. One missed restore test is 16.7% of everything `CNB-C-098` was required to do, the occurrence cannot
be recovered, and the assertion is about the period rather than about the intention.

The board resolved that the assertion could be signed, on the reasoning `09.05` sets out for exception 5 and
on the four other mechanisms serving A1.2 for exception 1. **What matters more than the conclusion is that
the alternative was live.** **Had management concluded it could not sign, the engagement would have ended
there and no report would exist** — there is no version of a SOC 2 examination in which a practitioner
opines on an assertion the responsible party has declined to make. That is not a rhetorical point about
integrity. It is the mechanics of an assertion-based engagement, and it is the reason a board minute
recording that the question was asked is worth more than a signature recorded without one.

## 4. The representation letter under O12

**Addressed to Ashcombe &amp; Doyle LLP, signed by the same two officers, dated 2027-02-26.** It covers the
period **and the interval between the period end and the report date** — a span that matters, because
2026-12-31 to 2027-02-26 contains two of the facts a reader of the report will meet.

It represents, among other things, that management has disclosed to the practitioner **all known instances
of noncompliance with laws or regulations, or of uncorrected deficiencies, relevant to the subject matter**;
**all knowledge of actual, suspected or alleged fraud** affecting the entity's controls; **all deficiencies
in the design or operation of controls of which management is aware**, whether or not they were selected for
testing; and **all subsequent events** through the report date that would require adjustment to or
disclosure in the description.

**Two subsequent events were represented.** The admission of **`CNB-C-150`** to the control library on
**2027-01-08**, which is the corrective action limb for the Stage 2 major nonconformity and which
`ADR-0040` records as having no population inside the observation window. And the **ISO/IEC 27001:2022
certificate issued on 2027-01-22** by Northgate Certification Services, Ltd., an ANAB-accredited
certification body.

**Neither is a DC9 change.** DC9 asks for relevant changes to the system **during** the period, and both of
these fall after it — which is `09.03` §2's rule and the reason they arrive here, in a letter about
subsequent events, rather than in the description.

The representation letter is also where the assertion's own foundations are stated to the practitioner:
that management is responsible for the description, for selecting the criteria, for designing and operating
the controls, and for the assertion itself. **That responsibility statement is the condition of the
practitioner's independence under AICPA ET section 1.295** and the reason Solstice Trust Advisors performed
the readiness work and Ashcombe &amp; Doyle did not. An entity unwilling to accept management responsibility
in writing is an entity the firm cannot examine.

## 5. What each instrument is worth, and what neither can do

**The assertion is a claim, not evidence for itself.** It is the thing examined. A reader who treats Section
II as corroborating Section I has the order backwards: the opinion is expressed *on* the assertion, and an
assertion carries no assurance of its own. The assurance is in Section I and the basis for it is in Section
IV.

**The representation letter is not a substitute for procedures and no practitioner treats it as one.**
Representations are evidence, but they are the weakest kind and they cannot make up for testing that was
not performed. What the letter does is close the gap between what the practitioner asked about and what
management knows — and its value is concentrated in the parts nobody could have asked about, which is why
the subsequent-events representation is the operative clause and the fraud representation is the one that
would matter most if it were ever untrue.

**Neither instrument reaches Section V.** The certificate and the major nonconformity that `09.07` places
there are information provided by management and are **not covered by the service auditor's opinion**; the
assertion is not made about them and the letter does not represent them as tested. Management states that on
the face of the section, and `ADR-0042` records why.

**And neither instrument says anything about 2027.** The assertion is bounded by the period, the letter is
bounded by the report date, and the only instrument CloudNimbus has for the interval after that is the
bridge letter at `09.09` — management-issued, unaudited, and worth exactly the credibility of the management
that signs it.
