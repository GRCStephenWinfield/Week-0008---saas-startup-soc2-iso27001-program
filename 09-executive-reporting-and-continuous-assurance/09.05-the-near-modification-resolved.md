## 1. What was raised, and what it was about

**On 2027-01-28, at the third fieldwork status meeting, Meredith Vance raised that the engagement team was
considering whether exception 5 required a modification of the opinion in respect of P4.2.** Phase 08 ended
with that question open, on the vantage of 2027-02-05, and said in terms that management did not know the
answer and would not predict it. **It closes here.**

The facts on the table were these. The RT-02 retention rule governs geolocation captured from the individual
at clock-in under a thirteen-month rule that exists irrespective of contract term. On 2026-08-17 a schema
migration partitioned the `eu-central-1` geolocation capture table; it could not partition in place, so it
created a new relation and left the old one behind. **The deletion job kept addressing the emptied original
for sixty-eight consecutive nights, completing successfully and writing a completion record every time, and
deleting nothing.** It was found on 2026-10-23 by somebody assembling a retention evidence sample for the
examination, and **1,847,206 capture points were sitting in a region where they should not have been**,
serving the 41 customers whose contracts oblige CloudNimbus to keep their personal data at rest inside it.

**A whole region. Sixty-eight consecutive nights. The most sensitive category of personal information the
platform holds.** That is the shape of the question, and it is why the question was asked.

## 2. The two rates, and neither is the test

| Framing | Population | Deviations | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Against the nights of the rule that failed, in the region it failed in | **184** | 68 | **37.0%** |
| Against the whole scheduled-deletion population — 8 rules × 3 regions × 184 nights = 4,416 rule-nights | **4,416** | 68 | **1.5%** |

**Both numbers are true and neither is the test.** 68 ÷ 184 = 37.0%; 68 ÷ 4,416 = 1.5%; and the same
sixty-eight nights produce both. **The test under AT-C section 205 is whether the deviations mean the
applicable criterion was not achieved for the period taken as a whole, and a rate is an input to that
judgement rather than a substitute for it.** No threshold exists to compute against, because none of the
standards supplies one — the four opinion forms turn on an evaluation and on nothing about a percentage.

**A programme that quotes 1.5% is choosing a denominator; a programme that quotes 37.0% is choosing a
different one. This one publishes both and says which it thinks is the honest one.**

> **The 37.0%.** The failure was not distributed across the estate: it was total, on one rule, in one
> region, for sixty-eight nights running. Stating it against 4,416 rule-nights averages a complete failure
> of one rule into the successful operation of seven others in three regions, and produces a number whose
> only property is that it looks small. **The wider denominator was the most defensible-looking dishonesty
> available in this engagement**, and `08.11` refused it in January for the same reason this chapter refuses
> it in March: **the rule that failed is the rule whose subject matter is the most sensitive category the
> platform holds**, and a reader entitled to know how often scheduled deletion works is not the reader this
> disclosure is for.

**The 1.5% is nonetheless published rather than suppressed.** A programme that named only the number
supporting its own discomfort would be choosing a denominator too. Both are in the table, the arithmetic for
both is stated, and a reader who disagrees with the choice has everything needed to disagree with it.

## 3. The service auditor's evaluation, as recorded

**The evaluation below is the service auditor's**, reproduced from Section IV as published and from the
communication to those charged with governance of **2027-02-10**, which is **GOV-34**. It is not
CloudNimbus's reconstruction of it and it is not drawn from any other source; the engagement team's working
papers are the engagement team's, and `logs/evidence-index.md` records that CloudNimbus holds those two
documents and nothing else about the reasoning.

**For the conclusion that P4.2 was achieved — five facts.**

The failure was **confined to one of eight rules in one of three regions**, and the other seven operated in
all three throughout the window. **The entity detected it inside the period** — on 2026-10-23, while
assembling the fourth-quarter retention evidence sample — and corrected the job's target the next day. The
affected records were **deleted on 2026-10-25** and the deletion **independently verified on 2026-10-27**
against partition counts taken before the run rather than derived from the job's own output. **A new control
was admitted on its evidence**: `CNB-C-149` entered the library on 2026-10-28, inside the window, and
operated for 65 of the period's nights with no alert. And the entity **disclosed it, notified 41 customers
under no obligation to do so, held the deletion certification open until the backup residue window expired
on 2026-11-29, and did not back-date anything.**

**Against — three facts.**

The failure ran for **37% of the period**, on the platform's most sensitive personal information, in the one
region where a residency commitment makes location itself the subject of the promise. **No control in the
library would have detected it** — nothing in 148 rows could distinguish a rule with nothing to delete from
a rule that could no longer see what was due, and the detection was incidental to an evidence request, which
is a fact about the control set rather than about the entity's diligence. And **P4.2's support in
`eu-central-1` for the 119 nights before `CNB-C-149` existed was `CNB-C-126` and `CNB-C-127` and nothing
else** — the two controls that failed — **with the sixty-eight nights sitting inside those hundred and
nineteen.**

**The conclusion.** The service auditor concluded that the deviations did **not** mean P4.2 was not achieved
for the period taken as a whole, and issued an **unmodified opinion with the exception disclosed in Section
IV** together with the evaluation above.

## 4. What the alternative would have looked like

**A qualification would not have been a worse report. It would have been a different one**, and stating what
it would have said is the only way to show that the outcome was reasoned rather than assumed.

A qualified opinion takes the form *except for* — the practitioner states the matter giving rise to the
qualification and expresses the opinion subject to it. Here it would have named P4.2, the sixty-eight
nights, the region, and the conclusion that the controls stated for that criterion did not operate
effectively throughout the period to provide reasonable assurance that it was achieved. **The other sixty
criteria would have been unaffected.** The description would have been unchanged, the assertion would have
been unchanged, the other eight exceptions would have appeared exactly as they do, and the distribution
under **O2** would have run on the same dates.

**What would have changed is the sentence a customer's vendor risk analyst reads first**, and the
consequences of that are commercial rather than professional. Management is not the party who weighs them,
and did not.

## 5. The paragraph that makes this chapter worth writing

> **The reasoning in §3 is the service auditor's and management neither endorses nor contests it.** It is
> reproduced here because a portfolio that showed the outcome and not the reasoning would teach a reader
> that unmodified opinions happen, which is not a lesson. **DEC-906**, taken by Karim Haddad on 2027-02-27,
> records the decision to publish it in full, including the three facts that point the other way.
>
> **It was close, and the engagement partner said so at the closing communication.** **A different
> engagement team, on the same facts, could have reached the other answer — and would not have been wrong
> to.** Everything on the *against* side is true, none of it was disputed, and none of it is the kind of
> fact that gets smaller when it is looked at again: sixty-eight consecutive nights is sixty-eight
> consecutive nights, and a control set that could not see the failure could not see it whichever way the
> opinion went.
>
> **The distance between an unmodified opinion and a qualified one, in this engagement, was one judgement
> about one denominator.** Not a judgement about the facts, which were agreed by everybody in the room on
> the day they were found. Not a judgement about the remediation, which was complete, verified and inside
> the period. One judgement about whether sixty-eight nights on one rule in one region means a criterion
> served by that rule was not achieved across six months.
>
> **Any account of this programme that presents the outcome as earned rather than reasoned is an account
> that has stopped being useful.** **ADR-0041.**

## 6. What this chapter does not claim

**It does not claim the countervailing facts were decisive.** The five facts for and the three against are
both the service auditor's, reproduced from Section IV as published and from **GOV-34**, and neither set is
management's case. `08.11` §5 recorded an asymmetry when the engagement team's January concerns existed only
as two unminuted sentences inside CloudNimbus's own minute of a status meeting — **GOV-34 of 2027-02-10
supplied what Phase 08 said was missing**, and the asymmetry it reported is dissolved rather than argued
away. What is left is a count, and **a count is not a weight**: five facts against three is the shape of an
evaluation, not evidence about which way it should have gone.

**It does not claim detection was a success.** The failure was found by somebody assembling an evidence
sample for an examination. Had the examination not been running, the sample would not have been assembled,
and nothing in the control library was going to raise its hand. **`CA-07-02` is open on the cause**,
`CNB-C-149`'s blind spot on partial non-deletion is recorded on the face of `ADR-0031`, and `IS-33` still
owes an enumeration of how many other controls in the library have the same shape — an operating condition
that a record was written, rather than what the record says.

**It does not claim the notification of 41 customers was required.** It was not. `GOV-26` assessed the
condition against **SC-02**, **O4**, **O6** and **O7** and recorded that none of them was engaged: nothing
left `eu-central-1`, no access outside the normal processing path occurred, and the disclosure register held
no entry. The notification was **ADR-0032**'s decision, taken over a minuted dissent, and a decision taken
against a dissent is not evidence that the decision was easy.

**And it does not claim the question is settled for the next period.** The opinion covers 2026-07-01 to
2026-12-31. `CNB-C-149` has 65 nights of window population and ten further weeks behind it at this vantage,
**its alerting limb has still never fired**, and a detective limb that has never fired has not been shown to
fire. `PR-42` carries that, unchanged and open.
