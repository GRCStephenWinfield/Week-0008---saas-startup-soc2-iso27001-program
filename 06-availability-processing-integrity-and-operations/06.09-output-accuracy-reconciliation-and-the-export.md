## 1. The last three hundred metres

06.08 took the inputs and 06.07 took the engine. This chapter takes what happens between a derived amount
and a payroll provider's acknowledgement, which is where **PI1.3** — processing complete, accurate, timely
and authorised — hands over to **PI1.4** — output complete, accurate and distributed to meet the entity's
objectives.

Four controls operate across that handover, and they are arranged as a sequence of refusals rather than as
a sequence of checks. `CNB-C-107` refuses to release an export whose reconciliation is outside tolerance.
`CNB-C-108` refuses to leave an exception in the queue past two business days. `CNB-C-111` refuses to
deliver a file whose period totals sit outside the trailing three-period band until a named person at the
tenant says it is right. `CNB-C-110` refuses to treat an unacknowledged delivery as delivered. **Each of
the four has an observable consequence with a timestamp**, which is what 04.06 meant when it said that the
commonest defect in a Processing Integrity library is a row that restates PI1.3 and calls itself a control.

## 2. `CNB-C-107` — the reconciliation record and the four held exports

`CNB-C-107` requires the nightly calculation run to write a reconciliation record comparing input hours and
rule version against output amounts, and **any variance outside tolerance blocks the export**. It operated
on all 92 nights of the quarter and wrote a record on all 92.

**Twenty-six reconciliation exceptions were raised across the quarter.** They divide as follows.

| Disposition | Count | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Outside tolerance — export blocked under `CNB-C-107` | **4** | All four cleared and exported the same day, delaying delivery by between 40 minutes and 6 hours |
| Inside tolerance — raised as a queue item under `CNB-C-108` | **22** | Worked at the weekly calculation quality review |
| **Total** | **26** | |

The four blocked exports are the ones worth being careful about, because a held payroll file looks like a
service failure and is not one.

**SC-07 carries no timing commitment.** The commitment is that calculation output is complete, accurate and
reproducible against the configured rules; there is no clause in it about when the file arrives, and the
only availability commitment in the description — SC-01, 99.9% monthly — is about the platform, not about
an export cycle. **A file held because its reconciliation did not balance is the control performing the act
it was designed to perform**, and a programme that reported the four holds as incidents would be reporting
its own preventive control as a fault. The delay was real and it was between forty minutes and six hours.

**The four were released by CloudNimbus, and not by the tenant.** `CNB-C-107` blocks the export; what lifts
the block is the **reconciliation exception being cleared under `CNB-C-108`** — CloudNimbus's own work on
its own queue — and all four cleared and **exported the same day**. That is worth stating precisely, because
`CNB-C-111`'s named-payroll-contact confirmation is a **different mechanism on a different population**: the
31 band-check holds in §5. Reading the four as customer-released would credit a tenant with lifting a hold
they were never asked about.

**Who was told, and how, is a separate question and gets a separate answer.** Each affected tenant was
notified by its Customer Success manager on the day of the hold, as an operational practice rather than as a
step the control statement compels. What the four leave behind as evidence is the **reconciliation record
under EC-12** and the **exception record under EC-13** — raised timestamp, tenant, variance, assignee and
cleared timestamp — not a tenant confirmation. None of that makes a held export a breach of anything.

The inverse reading is the one that would be dangerous, and it is worth stating so that nobody reaches for
it later. If the four holds were reported as failures, the pressure the report creates points in exactly
one direction: widen the tolerance. A tolerance wide enough never to block an export is a tolerance that
has stopped being a control, and the artefact — the reconciliation record — would still be produced nightly
and would still look like evidence.

## 3. `CNB-C-108`, the one that was late, and the cadence that could not deliver it

`CNB-C-108` requires reconciliation exceptions to be triaged and cleared **inside two business days**, with
the open queue worked at the **weekly calculation quality review** where an item past its service level is
escalated to the calculation engine product owner.

**Twenty-five of the twenty-six were cleared inside the two-business-day service level. One was not.**

| Field | The late exception |
|---|---|
| Raised | **2026-08-27 at 23:41** — a Thursday night |
| Condition | A rule-version pin mismatch, after a customer changed an overtime rule mid-period |
| Cleared | **2026-09-02** |
| Elapsed | **Four business days** against a two-business-day service level |
| Recorded as | **`D-06-03`** — one deviation in a population of 26 |

The diagnosis this chapter owes the reader is not "somebody was slow", because that is not what happened
and it would send the corrective action to the wrong place.

**The control's service level is stated in business days and its queue is worked at a weekly review held on
Tuesdays, and those two things do not agree.** Count it out. An exception raised at 23:41 on Thursday
2026-08-27 has business day one on Friday the 28th and business day two on Monday the 31st. The weekly
review at which the queue is worked fell on **Tuesday 2026-09-01**, which is business day three — already
past the service level before anybody looked at the item — and the clearance landed on Wednesday the 2nd,
business day four. **An exception raised late on a Thursday cannot be cleared within two business days by a
queue that is worked weekly on a Tuesday.** The arithmetic settles it before any question of diligence
arises, and it reaches further than the worked example does. Count the whole week. An exception raised on a
**Tuesday after the review** has business day two on Thursday and does not reach a queue until the following
**Tuesday, business day five**. One raised on a **Wednesday** reaches the queue on **business day four**.
One raised on a **Thursday** — the case above — reaches it on **business day three**. **For three of the
seven days in the cycle, and not two, the service level in the control statement is unreachable by the
mechanism in the same control statement**, and `CA-06-02`'s exposure is therefore half again what a
two-day-in-seven reading of it would suggest.

That makes this a **design defect, not an operating failure**, and the same distinction 06.05 draws about
`CNB-C-096` applies here in a quieter register: the control operated the way it was written, and the way it
was written cannot always meet the commitment it contains. Twenty-five of twenty-six were cleared in time
because twenty-five of twenty-six arrived on days from which the Tuesday review is reachable inside two
business days. **The clean majority is a property of when exceptions happened to arrive, not evidence that
the design works.**

Clause **10.2** corrective action **CA-06-02** is open against it, owned by Grete Lindqvist, and it is
framed as a question rather than a fix: **which of the two is wrong — the service level or the cadence?**
Both answers are defensible. A two-business-day clearance genuinely requires the queue to be worked daily,
which is a real operating cost on a team of the size CloudNimbus has. A weekly review genuinely supports a
five-business-day service level, and a five-day service level on an exception that is by definition inside
tolerance and not blocking an export may be the honest commitment. What is not defensible is leaving the
published statement carrying both. The corrective action is not closed at this vantage and 06.13 carries it
forward; **the deviation stands on the record either way**, because a control amended in October did not
operate in August.

## 4. `CNB-C-110` — 5,171 export files

`CNB-C-110` checksums the payroll export and delivers it to the customer's payroll provider endpoint, and
an export not acknowledged inside the delivery window is retried and then alerted to the integration
on-call engineer.

The quarter's population derives from the customers' own payroll frequencies, and it is set out rather than
asserted because a total with no derivation is the kind of figure that turns out to have been the figure
somebody wanted.

| Payroll frequency | Customers | Cycles in Q3 | Files |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly | 168 | 13 | 2,184 |
| Biweekly | 302 | 7 | 2,114 |
| Semi-monthly | 121 | 6 | 726 |
| Monthly | 49 | 3 | 147 |
| **Total** | **640** | | **5,171** |

168 + 302 + 121 + 49 = **640**, which is every customer. 2,184 + 2,114 + 726 + 147 = **5,171**.

| Measure | Q3 2026 |
|---|---|
| Export files delivered | **5,171** |
| Rejected by a receiving payroll provider | **0** |
| Retried after an unacknowledged delivery window | **17** |
| Acknowledged on retry | **17 of 17** |
| Escalated to the integration on-call engineer | **0** |

**Zero rejections is a clean figure and a narrow one.** A payroll provider rejects a file for structural
reasons — a malformed header, an unrecognised pay code, a schema version it does not accept — and zero
rejections in 5,171 files says the integration contracts held across four frequencies and every provider
CloudNimbus delivers to. It says nothing at all about whether the amounts inside those files were right. A
file full of wrong numbers in the correct format is accepted, acknowledged, and paid.

The seventeen retries are the more instructive row. All seventeen were acknowledged on retry, so none
escalated, and the control worked exactly as its escalation path describes. It is also worth naming what
they were: an unacknowledged delivery window is usually the receiving endpoint, not the platform, and the
endpoint belongs to the customer under **CUEC-11**.

The criterion to read that against is **PI1.4's distribution limb** — output complete, accurate and
**distributed** to meet the entity's objectives — which is where `CNB-C-110` is mapped, and not A1.2. A1.2
is about environmental protections, backup and recovery infrastructure, and it does not reach a redelivery
arrangement to a third-party endpoint. **CUEC-11's A1.2 citation reaches the customer's own availability of
that endpoint and no further.** So the distribution limb runs against an endpoint CloudNimbus does not
operate, and where a customer has never tested theirs, the availability of the delivery path is not
something CloudNimbus's controls can establish.

## 5. `CNB-C-111` — the band check, and 31 files held

`CNB-C-111` compares tenant period totals against the trailing three-period band on each export cycle and
holds a file outside the band until the tenant's named payroll contact confirms it.

| Measure | Q3 2026 |
|---|---|
| Files held outside the trailing three-period band | **31** |
| Confirmed by the tenant's named payroll contact and released | **28** |
| Re-issued after the tenant corrected a source input | **3** |
| Mean hold duration | **3h 20m** |
| **Longest hold** | **9h 14m** |
| Holds that delayed a tenant's payroll cut-off | **0** |

Thirty-one holds in 5,171 files is 0.6%, and twenty-eight of the thirty-one were correct all along — a
seasonal shift pattern, an annual bonus cycle, a headcount change that moved a tenant's totals outside a
band derived from three periods that did not contain it. **A band check with a false-positive rate near
zero would be a band set so wide that it could not detect the case it exists for**, and the three that were
not false positives are the case: a source input was wrong, the tenant found it because the hold made them
look, they corrected it, and the file was re-issued before it reached a payroll provider.

The mechanism deserves one further sentence, because it is the only control in this family that requires a
human being outside CloudNimbus to act before an output moves. That is a design choice with a cost — a
tenant whose named payroll contact is on leave holds up their own file — and it was made anyway, because
the alternative is CloudNimbus deciding on the customer's behalf that an unusual total is fine. **The
mean hold of three hours and twenty minutes is a measure of how quickly customers answer**, and it is the
only figure in this phase that is substantially outside CloudNimbus's control.

**A mean without a maximum hides the case the reader is worried about, so the maximum is given: 9h 14m.**
That is the longest any one of the thirty-one files waited for a named contact to answer, and it is roughly
three times the mean, which is what a distribution driven by human availability looks like.

**No hold delayed a payroll cut-off.** The reason is structural rather than fortunate. The band check runs
on the **export cycle**, which sits ahead of the tenant's own submission deadline, and a held file is
released against **the tenant's cut-off** rather than against a CloudNimbus clock — so the slack the hold
consumes is slack the cycle was designed to carry. A nine-hour hold on a file produced a day before the
tenant submits it is a nine-hour hold that costs the tenant a conversation and nothing else. Had a hold ever
run into a cut-off, that would be a fact about the export cycle's placement rather than about the control,
and it would belong in this table.

## 6. CUEC-05 — the Phase 02 question, answered

Phase 02 asked, in 02.11 §5.1, what happens when a customer does not perform a complementary user entity
control, and named **CUEC-05 — validate the payroll export against your own records before submitting it to
your payroll provider** — as the most consequential entry on the list when it is not performed. It also
said, plainly, that this is the entry least likely to be performed properly, and gave three reasons: the
payroll cut-off leaves no slack, the volume makes line-by-line validation impossible for a large employer,
and eleven correct periods train the twelfth to be approved unread.

Customer Success put the question directly. **All 640 customers were asked whether they validate the
payroll export against their own records before submission.**

| Response | Customers | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed that they perform CUEC-05 | **411** | 64.2% |
| No response | **229** | 35.8% |
| Said they do not perform it | **0** | — |
| **Total** | **640** | |

411 + 229 = 640, and 411 / 640 = **64.2%**. In every case the answer came from **a single named payroll
contact at the tenant** — the same contact `CNB-C-111` holds a banded file for — and not from a survey of
the people who would perform the check.

> **The 411 confirmations are unverified self-assertions and they carry no assurance weight.** Nobody at
> CloudNimbus saw a validation performed, sampled a validated export, or asked what the validation consists
> of. 02.11 §5.1 already supplies the reason to distrust them in the aggregate: **the volume makes
> line-by-line validation impractical for a large employer, and eleven correct periods train the twelfth to
> be approved unread.** A contact who answers "yes, we check it" is describing an intention and a process
> they believe exists, which is a different thing from a control that operated on the period in question.
>
> **229 non-responses are not 229 failures, and they are not 229 successes either.** The description of the
> system will state what CUEC-05 requires and will not state how many customers perform it, because
> CloudNimbus does not know. **A complementary user entity control is a statement of what the user entity
> must do, not a claim about what user entities do.** The service auditor does not test CUECs; the user
> entity's own auditor does, against the user entity's own controls.

Three consequences follow and all three are recorded rather than argued around.

**The outreach does not move the register.** **R-14** — a customer does not perform CUEC-05 and a wrong
export reaches their payroll provider — was proposed for reduction at the September CAL-06 review on the
strength of these figures and **declined; it is held at 4 × 3 = 12**. A 64% response rate to a question
nobody answered "no" to is not evidence about likelihood, and the movement rule permits a rating to move on
likelihood only where there is evidence about likelihood. An outreach where the non-responses are
structurally indistinguishable from failures cannot supply it, and unverified self-assertions cannot supply
it either. 06.11 §6 records the decision and GOV-24 carries the review. It is worth saying what happened at
that review as a whole: **six reduction proposals, none accepted, and the only movement was a rating going
up** — R-08, which nobody had proposed. The register stands at **8 High · 17 Moderate · 12 Low = 37**.

**Nothing requires anybody to ask.** The outreach was performed because Customer Success chose to perform
it, and **the annual reconfirmation it is supposed to feed has no control behind it and no cadence in the
calendar.** No row in the library requires CUEC-05 to be raised with a customer, at onboarding, annually or
ever; no CAL entry places it in a quarter; no owner is named for the reconfirmation. **That absence is
itself the finding**, and it is recorded as **`IS-26`** and referred rather than closed by writing a control
in the same document that discovered the gap. The observation attached to it: **a CUEC nobody is required to
ask about is a CUEC the description will carry unexamined for as long as the description exists**, and the
2026 outreach will look in 2028 like evidence of a practice rather than what it was, a thing somebody did
once.

**The outreach was still worth doing**, and the reason is not the number. Four hundred and eleven named
payroll contacts have now had a conversation in which somebody explained why the check matters, which is
the mechanism 02.11 §6 described when it said that a CUEC disclosed only inside a restricted-use report has
been disclosed to the wrong person. The 229 who did not answer are the population the annual
reconfirmation exists for — which is the reconfirmation `IS-26` records that nothing requires.

None of that disturbs the disposition, which is unchanged and is right: **the description states what
CUEC-05 requires and does not state how many customers perform it.**

## 7. What this chapter does not claim

The deviation `D-06-03` is disclosed here, will be disclosed in Section IV of the report with the service
auditor's evaluation alongside it, and **is not a conclusion about the opinion.** The auditor evaluates
whether a deviation means a criterion was not met; that evaluation has not been performed, this phase does
not anticipate it, and 06.12 says the same thing about all six of the quarter's findings — the five
deviations `D-06-01` to `D-06-05` and the SC-01 commitment failure, which is not a control deviation.

Twenty-six exceptions is a population of twenty-six across three months of a six-month window, and the
window will produce more. One deviation in twenty-six is a Q3 rate and not a window rate, and the sentence
06.12 puts under its table applies here too: **half a period is not a period.**
