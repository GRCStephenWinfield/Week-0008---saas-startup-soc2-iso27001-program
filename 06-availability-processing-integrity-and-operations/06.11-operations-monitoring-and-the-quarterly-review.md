## 1. The six monitoring controls, and what they produced

`CNB-C-065` to `CNB-C-070` are the detection half of CC7, and five of the six operate continuously or
daily. That gives them the largest populations in the library.

| Control | What it does | Q3 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| `CNB-C-065` | Ships application, infrastructure, database and identity-provider logs to the region-partitioned `cnb-security` archive within five minutes, under an immutability policy | Operated throughout, including across the seventy-one minutes of `INC-2026-031` |
| `CNB-C-066` | Detection rules alerting on privileged role assumption, authentication failure bursts, cross-tenant query patterns and interruptions to log delivery | Operated; no interruption to log delivery raised in the quarter |
| `CNB-C-067` | Every alert acknowledged and dispositioned; a high-severity alert triaged to a disposition within thirty minutes, and an alert left **unacknowledged** past that point escalates automatically to the VP Security &amp; Trust | **5,102 alerts triaged** — 1,412 · 1,656 · 2,034. **148 high-severity, all triaged inside thirty minutes.** The automatic-escalation limb therefore had a **population of zero**. Separately, **38 alerts progressed to investigation** after disposition, which is a different activity — see 06.12 §3 |
| `CNB-C-068` | Synthetic transactions exercising sign-in, clock-in and report generation in each production region every minute; two consecutive failures page the on-call SRE | Operated; **paged at 14:24 on 8 September, two minutes after onset** — see §4 |
| `CNB-C-069` | The on-call analyst assesses each triaged event against the severity matrix, records on the ticket whether it is an incident and at what severity, and publishes a daily triage summary | 92 daily summaries; **1 event recorded as an incident** — `INC-2026-031`, Severity-1 |
| `CNB-C-070` | The weekly review of alert volume, false-positive rate and unactioned events, updating the detection backlog | **Ran 13 times** |

**`CNB-C-068` is the control that found the incident**, at 14:24 on 8 September, two minutes after onset,
and the `CNB-C-067` error-rate path corroborated it at 14:29. 06.05 owns the incident and sets out why that
is a better result than it first looks and a worse one than it sounds; 06.01 owns what it says about the
measurement architecture. What belongs here is the narrower operational fact: **the two write-exercising
signals did what they were built to do, and the measurement control did not.** Of 5,102 alerts across the
quarter, this was the one that mattered.

**All 148 high-severity alerts were triaged to a disposition inside thirty minutes**, the service level
`CNB-C-067` states, and **no alert went unacknowledged past thirty minutes, so the control's automatic
escalation to the VP Security &amp; Trust never fired.** A limb with a population of zero is stated here for
the same reason 06.06 §5.1 states it for `CNB-C-072`: a control that had no occasion to operate has not been
shown to work, and reporting the 38 investigations in its place would put a different activity behind the
limb's name. Neither figure is comfort about volume: 5,102 alerts in ninety-two days is fifty-five a day,
and the false-positive work that represents is what `CNB-C-070`'s weekly review exists to grind down.

**Thirteen occurrences of `CNB-C-070` is the whole quarter with no gaps.** It is the largest clean periodic
population in the phase, and it is unglamorous rather than reassuring: a weekly review is the cheapest
control in this chapter to keep on the calendar, and the controls that slipped this quarter were the
monthly and the emergency-path ones. **Cadence discipline is easiest exactly where it matters least.**

## 2. `CNB-C-069`, and the word "incident"

One event in ninety-two days was recorded as an incident, and that figure is only honest alongside the
definition behind it. Under **POL-13**, Severity-3 and Severity-4 tickets are **not incidents**: they are
operational tickets, worked and closed by the on-call rota without an incident record, an incident
commander or a post-incident review. The quarter produced plenty of them, and `CNB-C-069`'s daily triage
summary is where they are visible. **"One Severity-1 and zero Severity-2" is a statement about the two
severities the incident process governs, not a claim that nothing else happened.** Stating the definition
alongside the count is the difference between a figure and a figure that can be quoted.

## 3. GOV-23 — the Q3 operating review

The Trust Committee held its quarter-three operating review on **2026-09-28**, chaired by Marisol Vega with
Rahul Bhargava as secretary. It is the direct successor to GOV-20, and it had a harder job: GOV-20 reported
a clean month and had to resist reading it as a result, and GOV-23 reported a quarter containing a missed
service commitment, five deviations and a control whose design was found to be wrong — and had to resist
reading *that* as a result too. The committee's recorded position is the paragraph the review turns on.

> **Three months is half a period, and half a period is not a period.** Five deviations across three months
> is a fact about three months. The population the service auditor samples is six months, and a control
> with one missing occurrence in three has five occurrences still to run — which can improve the rate or
> worsen it, and this committee does not know which.

That is GOV-20's discipline applied in the opposite direction, and it is why this phase states `CNB-C-098`
as **2 of 3 occurrences to date** and states no window deviation rate anywhere. A rate computed across a
population that does not yet exist is a number invented to look precise, and it would be invented in the
direction that flatters, because one miss in six reads better than one miss in three and neither is a fact
yet.

The committee also declined to characterise any of the five deviations or the commitment failure as
material or immaterial. **That characterisation is the service auditor's**, made against the criteria
rather than against management's view of its own quarter.

## 4. Two divergences recorded rather than amended

Phase 05 found five control statements that did not say what it needed them to say and reconciled them at
source. This phase found two more of a different kind — **not statements that were wrong, but pairs of
statements that do not agree with each other** — and the treatment is deliberately different.

**The post-incident review window.** `CNB-C-102` requires a post-incident review inside **five business
days** of a Severity-1 or Severity-2 availability incident and cites A1.2 and A1.3. `CNB-C-075` requires
one within **ten business days** of closing a Severity-1 or Severity-2 incident and cites CC4.2 and CC7.5.
**They describe the same artefact for the same event under two different service levels.** The 2026-09-11
review, held three business days after the incident, satisfied both, so nothing turned on it this quarter.
The tighter window governs in practice, which is not the same thing as the library being right.

**The synthetic monitoring pair.** `CNB-C-096` requires synthetic probes against each of the three regions
every 60 seconds with error-budget burn paging the on-call SRE, and cites A1.1, A1.2 and A.8.16.
`CNB-C-068` requires synthetic transactions exercising **sign-in, clock-in and report generation** in each
production region every minute, with two consecutive failures paging the on-call SRE, and cites CC7.2. Two
continuous controls, the same cadence, the same pager, the same three regions — and **only one of them says
what the transactions must exercise.** On 8 September the one that says paged in two minutes; the one that
does not returned healthy for seventy-one and recorded no burn against the error budget.

This pair was **not** referred to the audit, because it is not a divergence between two statements. It is
one statement that is complete and one that is not, and the incomplete one has been completed: `CNB-C-096`
was amended under **DEC-609** on 2026-09-15 to require a write probe, aligning what it exercises with what
`CNB-C-068` has always named, and **Phase 04 was re-issued**. That is `D-06-02`, and it is closed as a
finding even though the deviation it produced stands in the record.

**The post-incident review windows are referred**, and the difference in treatment is deliberate.
`CNB-C-096` was silent and silence can be filled. `CNB-C-102` and `CNB-C-075` both speak, and they say
different things; deciding which is right is a judgement about the control framework rather than a
correction to it, and the people who noticed the divergence are the people who wrote it. A divergence
between two published control statements is a **documented information** problem under clause 7.5, and the
fastest route to making it disappear — quietly editing one row so the pair agrees — is also the route that
destroys the evidence that the divergence existed. **A library that corrects itself silently cannot
demonstrate that it was ever wrong.** What the audit made of it, and everything else about the audit,
belongs to Phase 08.

## 5. The CAL-06 cadence correction — DEC-602

04.11 committed the programme to a scheduling discipline: *quarterly and semi-annual controls are scheduled
early in their period*, enforced by "the assurance calendar, CAL-03 and CAL-06 to CAL-09". The argument
behind it is sound and this chapter does not disturb it — a quarterly control due "in Q3" that operates on
30 September has no margin.

**The line named one control it should not have named.** CAL-06, the quarterly risk register review, **has
operated late in its quarter wherever a register existed to review — 2026-06-15 and 2026-09-29** — and that
is right for it rather than a drift to be corrected. The two dates need their own note, because a reader
counting three occurrences would find only two that bear on the question. **The 2026-03 occurrence reviewed
the risk assessment *method* rather than a register**, as 01.11 §6 records, and a method review has no
accumulated evidence to wait for. And **2026-06-15 is mid-month, not month-end**: it was scheduled to
MS-09, the ISMS operational declaration, rather than to the end of the quarter, so the discipline it
evidences is "late enough to have something to review" rather than "on the last working day".

**A register review evaluates a quarter's accumulated evidence.** One held in the first fortnight of a
quarter reviews the previous quarter's evidence under the current quarter's label, which is worse than
late: it is a review filed against the wrong period. CAL-07 access review and CAL-08 sub-processor review
are different in kind — each certifies a **current state**, and certifying it early leaves margin to
correct what the certification finds, which is exactly the discipline 04.11 was arguing for.

**And CAL-07 was not early this quarter.** 04.11 §3 defines the discipline as **the first fortnight of the
quarter**, and CAL-07's Q3 occurrence was **2026-07-27 — week four**. A chapter correcting 04.11's cadence
line cannot describe CAL-07 as properly early in the same breath. It is recorded here as an **observation
and not a deviation**: the control's stated cadence is Quarterly, it operated inside the quarter, and
nothing in the library commits it to a week. What the observation costs is margin — an occurrence in week
four of a thirteen-week quarter has nine weeks left to slip into, where one in week one has twelve — and
margin is the whole of what 04.11 was buying.

**04.11's line is corrected to name CAL-03 and CAL-07 to CAL-09, with CAL-06 deliberately late, and Phase
04 is re-issued.** This is **DEC-602**, taken by Rahul Bhargava on 2026-09-02, and it is the second of two
Phase 04 amendments this phase produces.

The correction is small and the reason for making it in writing is not. A commitment published in Phase 04
and quietly not honoured in Phase 06 is the shape of finding an internal auditor produces from a five-minute
comparison of two documents. **Correcting the commitment costs a paragraph; being found not to have met it
costs the credibility of every other commitment in the same table.**

### 6.1 Why P-3 was refused, and why refusing it was the whole review

P-3 is the proposal this chapter would have been happiest to accept, and accepting it would have been the
worst thing the review did.

R-21 asks whether an event can be reconstructed from the logs. The proposal offered two reconstructions. One
of them — the September incident, reconstructed to the second across all seventy-one minutes, with 41,208
failed writes attributed across 318 tenants — is real, complete and good evidence. **The other is the
event R-21 describes.** 05.12 records that the June forensic search ran against a thirteen-month query
horizon on a defect that had existed for twenty-two months, so **nine months — 41% of the exposure — could
not be examined at all**, and that a clean result over 41% less than the period is a result about the
horizon rather than about the events. It also **concluded before 2026-06-22, when DEC-508 reviewed the
retention gap it exposed** — **nine days** before the observation window opened, so it was never in-quarter
evidence in the first place.

The internal test is what settles it. **P-4 was declined because DR-F1 is the event R-30 describes.** A
review that treats a materialised risk as evidence against one entry and as evidence for another is not
applying a rule; it is reaching a result and dressing it. What remains for R-21 is one reconstruction, and
**one event is not a period**. Held at 3 × 3 = 9, and Karim Haddad's clause 6.1.3 f) acceptance stands
unchanged because nothing moved.

### 6.2 R-08 rose, and nobody proposed it

**R-08 — a change is deployed to production without recorded peer review — moved from 4 × 3 = 12 to
5 × 3 = 15, Moderate to High.**

Nine of the quarter's 160 changes reached production without a code-owner review at merge, through the
designed emergency path under `CNB-C-082`. `03.02` §2 puts likelihood **4** at "expected at least once a
year" and likelihood **5** at "occurring now, or expected more than once a year". **Nine occurrences in
ninety-two days is 5.** It moves on likelihood; the consequence limb is unchanged.

**R-08 is the register's first likelihood-5 entry, and nobody has said so.** `03.04` §5's likelihood
distribution recorded **zero** at 5 and explained the emptiness: nothing in the baseline was judged to be
occurring at the time it was struck, and the two entries with the strongest evidential support for a high
frequency — R-08 among them — sat at 4 because that is what the evidence then supported. The anchor existed
and nothing had ever reached it. The top of the likelihood scale is now occupied, and it is occupied by a
counted frequency rather than by a judgement, which is the only way it should ever be occupied.

The re-rating arrived through the table's new **"Tested upward?"** column, and that column is the durable
output of this review. **A review that examines only the proposals put to it can only move a register
downwards**, because reductions are what people bring. Testing every entry whose described event actually
occurred against the anchors is a second pass in the other direction, and it is available only because
`03.02` §2 wrote the anchors as frequencies rather than as adjectives.

The consequence is governance rather than arithmetic. Under `03.02` §6 a **High** residual may not be
retained on the authority of the risk owner or of the VP Security &amp; Trust: it needs **the Chief Executive
Officer, Elise Fontaine, with the reasoning minuted**, over and above risk owner **Junia Okonkwo's** clause
6.1.3 f) acceptance. Both are recorded at GOV-24.

**The rule has two limbs and only one of them is complete.** `03.02` §6 requires a High residual to be
retained by the Chief Executive Officer **with the reasoning minuted**, and reported to the **Audit &amp; Risk
Committee with the reasoning minuted**. CAL-03's Q3 occurrence was held on **2026-09-24**, five days before
the register review, so Elise Fontaine's decision of **2026-09-29** could not have been reported to it.

> **Half of it fired.** The retention decision is taken and minuted; **the reporting limb is not complete at
> this vantage** and completes at the Audit &amp; Risk Committee's Q4 report. A rule that requires two things
> and has done one is a rule that has done one, and this phase is not going to write it up as though the
> second had happened. Carried as **`PR-40`** in the RAID log, owner Karim Haddad, due at CAL-03's Q4
> occurrence.

Clause 10.2 corrective action **`CA-06-03`**, owner Junia Okonkwo, is open at the vantage against the
emergency change path; 06.12 §5 sets out what it has to answer.

**`03.04` is not amended, because a baseline that is rewritten every time the register moves is not a
baseline.** It is the dated position at 2026-04-10 and its value to the examination is that it has not
changed. The current position is carried by the register workbook and by the CAL-06 review records, which
is where a reader should be sent and where GOV-24 sends them.

### 6.3 Nothing was reduced, and that is not the same as nothing being found

**A quarterly review at which nothing was reduced is not a review that found nothing.** It is a review whose
evidence was three months old in a six-month window, at an organisation that had just missed a commitment.
Every reduction proposed rested on a quiet quarter, and a quiet quarter is the shortest run of evidence
anybody in this programme has yet argued from. The review's output is one entry re-rated upward on counted
occurrences and six proposals sent back — which is a register doing what a register is for, and is a worse
headline than the one the phase would have preferred to write.

### 6.4 No new risk was admitted

**And that is ADR-0029.** **R-18 already describes the incident**, and admitting
an "R-39: an availability incident occurs" alongside it would be double-counting one exposure at two
entries, inflating the population and diluting every band count derived from it. **A register that grows
every time something happens is a log, not a register.** It grows on evidence that disproves an assumption,
which is what R-37 did in May and what nothing in this quarter did.
