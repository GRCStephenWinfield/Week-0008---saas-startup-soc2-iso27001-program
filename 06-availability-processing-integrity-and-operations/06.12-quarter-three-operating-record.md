## 1. What this chapter is for

Phase 05 published a table for July. This chapter publishes the quarter, and July's row in it is
**identical to GOV-20's** — 47 changes, 2 emergency, 1,412 alerts, 0 incidents — because a record that
restates a published month with different numbers has told the reader something about the record rather
than about the month.

The tables carry the data. The prose carries what the data does not say, and that is the larger half. **A
quarter of operating figures is the most persuasive artefact a compliance programme produces and the most
easily misread**, because every row invites the inference that the control behind it is effective, and
operating effectiveness is a conclusion about a period drawn by somebody else from a sample they select.

47 + 51 + 62 = 160. 1,412 + 1,656 + 2,034 = 5,102. 31 + 31 + 30 = 92, twice.

**Every column rises and none of the rises means anything on its own.** September carried 62 changes
against July's 47, and part of that is the incident response and the five actions arising from it; September
carried 2,034 alerts against July's 1,412, and part of that is the same event and the detection work around
it. A quarter in which the busiest month is the month something went wrong is a quarter behaving normally.
What would be worth investigating is the opposite — a flat September, which would mean the organisation had
absorbed a Severity-1 incident without generating any work.

**The one zero in the table is the row that matters most.** August's restore test did not happen, and the
`2 of 3` in the final column is not a rate. It is two occurrences out of three that have fallen due, in a
window that will hold six.

Five of those rows repay a sentence each.

**`CNB-C-067`'s two limbs are two controls wearing one identifier, and only one of them operated.** The
published statement requires every alert to be acknowledged and dispositioned, a high-severity alert to be
triaged inside thirty minutes, **and an alert left unacknowledged past that point to escalate automatically
to the VP Security &amp; Trust**. All 148 were acknowledged and triaged in time, so **the escalation limb has
a population of zero for three months of the observation window** — and a control with no occurrences
cannot be shown to work, which is the same thing 06.06 §5.1 says about `CNB-C-072` and SC-02. The **38** are
not that limb. They are alerts that, having been dispositioned inside the service level, were progressed to
investigation because the disposition warranted it. Reporting them under the escalation heading would put a
healthy number where an empty one belongs, which is the commonest way an empty population disappears from a
record.

**`CNB-C-087` running 92 times with zero unmatched deployments is the direct answer to ML-2.** The 2025
management letter recorded that change-management peer review was enforced by team convention rather than
by branch protection, and 04.05 explains that this daily reconciliation exists for no other purpose than to
catch the gap a convention-based control leaves. Ninety-two clean days is the first quarter of evidence
that the answer holds. It is also the control most likely to look boring in a report and hardest to
replace: **a convention-based failure is invisible until somebody counts.**

**Fourteen provisioning requests is a small population that is growing.** GOV-20 flagged three in July as a
denominator in which a single late approval is a large percentage; fourteen across the quarter is better,
and it is still small enough that one deviation would be seven per cent. All fourteen recorded manager
approval before provisioning, and all six leavers were revoked on the last working day.

**Four break-glass uses in ninety-two days, all reviewed inside 48 hours**, and three of the four fall on
the three days in the quarter when something unusual was happening — the exercise, the incident and the
September restore test. The fourth, 2026-07-14, is the one GOV-20 already reported. A break-glass
population concentrated on the days that required it is the right shape; a break-glass population spread
evenly across ninety-two ordinary days would be a standing access problem wearing an emergency label.

**`PT-09` and `PT-14` were closed on the 2026-08-14 mobile release**, and Ironwood Security Labs' retest on

`PT-15` — accepted with its reasoning recorded and revisited at the second engagement.

## 4. Due and performed in Q3 — the thin populations

§2 and §3 report the controls with large populations, where a quarter produces dozens or hundreds of
occurrences and a sample can be drawn. **This section reports the opposite end of the library**, and it is
the end that decides what the examination can reach: the controls whose cadence put **one or two**
occurrences in the quarter, where a single miss is a deviation rate of fifty or a hundred per cent and a
single occurrence is the whole of the evidence.

This chapter reports across control families rather than only the availability and processing-integrity
ones, so it cannot be defended as a partial record. Where an occurrence falls inside the quarter and is
reported elsewhere, the row says where.

### 4.2 `CNB-C-031`, and the four procedures that were not confirmed

`CNB-C-031` was performed on **2026-09-17**. Of the **147** procedures named across the policy suite —
sixteen of the nineteen policies name at least one — **143 were confirmed** as existing, owned and reviewed
inside their cycle. **Four were not.** 143 + 4 = 147.

All four were owned by the same departed manager: **two had no named owner** after the departure and **two
were unreviewed inside their cycle**. All four were re-assigned and reviewed by **2026-09-25**, before the
confirmation was signed, so the control's artefact records a complete inventory and the exceptions that had
to be cleared to produce it.

**This is the single most load-bearing annual occurrence in the quarter and it has nothing to do with
availability.** `04.11` §4.3 records that **`CNB-C-031` is the single control serving CC5.3**. Its natural
anniversary was June 2027, six months after the window closes; had it not been re-scheduled, **CC5.3 would
have had no testable occurrence at all** — not a thin population but an empty one, a criterion the
examination could not reach. Its operation on 2026-09-17 is the difference between CC5.3 being supported by
one occurrence and being supported by none, and the four exceptions it surfaced are the reason the
re-scheduling was worth doing rather than a formality: a confirmation that returned 147 of 147 would have
been a confirmation nobody had tested against a real inventory.

**And the re-scheduling did not achieve the thing it was re-scheduled for, which is a separate point and is
recorded rather than glossed.** `04.11` §4.3 moved `CNB-C-031` to 2026-09 **"so that the internal audit
meets a procedure inventory that has been confirmed current"**. The clause 9.2 internal audit ran
**2026-09-14 to 2026-09-25**. The control was performed on **2026-09-17 — mid-audit** — and the confirmation
could not be signed until the four exceptions cleared on **2026-09-25, the audit's last day**. For most of
the audit the inventory was not confirmed current, and on the day the confirmation existed the audit was
ending.

**The occurrence rescues CC5.3, which is what matters here, and it did not do the thing `04.11`
re-scheduled it to do.** Both are true and the second does not cancel the first. Whether the sequencing
mattered to the audit is not a question this phase can answer or should try to: the audit and everything it
produced belong to **Phase 08**.

## 5. The emergency change path — two limbs, two late approvals, and SR-11

Nine of the 160 changes were emergency changes under `CNB-C-082`, deployed ahead of approval and requiring
retrospective approval by the Director of Engineering (Platform) within five business days. **Seven were
approved on time. Two were not.**

### 5.1 The control has two limbs and two populations

`CNB-C-082` as published carries a second requirement that is easy to lose behind the first: **the emergency
change log is reviewed monthly and unapproved entries are escalated to the Chief Technology Officer.** The
control's stated cadence is Monthly, so the two limbs sample differently and must be reported separately.

| Limb | Population in Q3 | Deviations |
|---|---|---|
| Retrospective approval within five business days | **9** — one per emergency change | **2** — `D-06-04` |
| Monthly review of the emergency change log, unapproved entries escalated to the Chief Technology Officer | **3** — 2026-07-31, 2026-08-31, 2026-09-30 | **0** — and nothing to escalate at any of the three |

**The two limbs did not interact, and the record has to say so.** `CHG-2026-1147` was approved on
**2026-09-16** and `CHG-2026-1149` on **2026-09-17**. The monthly reviews fell on **2026-07-31**,
**2026-08-31** and **2026-09-30**, and **none of them falls on or before 2026-09-16**: by the time the only
review that could have seen the two entries ran, both had been approved. There was nothing for it to
escalate.

**The escalation of 2026-09-16 was made outside the monthly cycle.** When the 2026-09-15 due date passed
with both changes still unapproved, the **Director of Engineering (Platform)** escalated them to **Nathan
Oyelaran, Chief Technology Officer**, the following day. That is a person noticing a missed date. It is not
an occurrence of `CNB-C-082`'s review limb, and this record does not credit the limb with it.

**So the review limb's Q3 position is a population of three with no exceptions, and that is the whole of
it.** Three occurrences, all performed, nothing unapproved at any of them. That is a clean record and a
weak one, in the same way `CNB-C-067`'s escalation limb is: **a detective limb that has never had an
exception to find has not been shown to find one.** Reporting the 2026-09-16 escalation as though the
control's detective limb had caught its preventive limb failing would put a mechanism behind a result that
a person produced, and it is the flattering version of both facts rather than either of them.

### 5.2 The two changes, named, and where the clock starts

| Change | Deployed | Approval due | Approved | By |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CHG-2026-1147` — the rolling restart of the eleven services on the core write path | **2026-09-08 15:04** | 2026-09-15 | **2026-09-16** | Junia Okonkwo |
| `CHG-2026-1149` — a connection-pool configuration change | **2026-09-08 15:21** | 2026-09-15 | **2026-09-17** | Junia Okonkwo |

**Five business days run from deployment, not from incident closure**, which matters because the two are
different clocks and only one of them is the control's. Both changes were deployed on Tuesday
**2026-09-08**; five business days from that Tuesday is Tuesday **2026-09-15**. The approvals were recorded
on the sixth and seventh business day.

**That is an explanation and it is not an excuse, and the deviation stands.** In the intervening week the
same small group of people was writing the post-incident review, agreeing the five actions and deploying
`ACT-06-01`. The excuse is available and is superficially good: the emergency change procedure exists
precisely for the conditions under which it was missed. A retrospective approval requirement that is
honoured in ordinary weeks and missed in the week of an incident is a control that works when nothing is
happening, and **an emergency procedure nobody completes afterwards is a bypass with a name**. Two late
approvals in a population of nine is 22%, stated here rather than left for the sample to produce.

### 5.3 SR-11 — the Q3 position, stated plainly

`02.12` maps system requirement **SR-11** — *change promotion through an automated pipeline with recorded
peer review* — to **SC-01** and **SC-07**, and 06.00 promised this phase would address it. It is addressed
here.

**Nine of the quarter's 160 changes reached production without a code-owner review at merge**, by the
designed emergency path under `CNB-C-082`. The pipeline was used; the peer-review gate was not. **That is
not satisfaction of SR-11. It is an accepted departure from it**, accepted in advance in the sense that
`CNB-C-082` is the published route around the gate, and accepted at the vantage in the sense that no
alternative was in place at the time.

Three things follow and all three are recorded rather than argued away.

**It is the event R-08 describes**, and nine occurrences in ninety-two days is what moved R-08 from 4 × 3 =
12 to **5 × 3 = 15, Moderate to High**, at the September review. `03.02` §2 puts likelihood 5 at "occurring
now, or expected more than once a year".

**It is why `CA-06-03` exists.** The clause 10.2 corrective action, owner **Junia Okonkwo**, open at the
vantage, is to bring the emergency change path within a recorded-review discipline: a change reaching
production outside branch protection is to carry a **code-owner review after the fact** as well as a
retrospective approval, so the departure from SR-11 closes to a delay rather than an absence.

**It is not a control deviation.** `CNB-C-082` operated as described on all nine; what departed from SR-11
is the design of the path, not its operation. `D-06-04` covers the two approvals that were late, and
nothing in the deviation log covers the other seven, because there is nothing about them to record as a
control failure. **A description that reported the two and said nothing about the nine would be accurate in
every particular and misleading as a whole.**

**And one service commitment failure, listed separately because it is not a control deviation.**

| ID | Commitment | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| SC-01 | 99.9% monthly availability of the platform, excluding maintenance windows announced at least fourteen days in advance — as `02.12` reads following DEC-613 | **Not met in September 2026.** 71 minutes of unavailability, none of it in an announced window, against a 43.2-minute monthly allowance; the month closed at 99.84%. The separation is not presentational. **A control deviation is a control that did not operate as described;
a commitment failure is a promise to a customer that was not kept.** They are evaluated against different
things by different people: the deviations against the applicable trust services criteria by the service
auditor, the commitment against the contract by the customer. They are disclosed in different places too —
the deviations belong in Section IV of the report, and the September incident's disclosure is a **DC4**
matter in the description of the system, which 06.06 sets out. Filing one under the other is how a
programme loses track of which reader is being told what.

**`D-06-01` is the clearest illustration of why the two must not be merged**, and 06.06 makes the point at
length: the missed August restore test is a deviation and not a system incident. Nothing failed on 19
August; a control did not operate. Putting a control that did not operate under the incident criterion puts
it where no reader of a description looks for it — the same argument 04.11 made about the re-scheduled
annual controls and DC5.

**Four of the five carry clause 10.2 corrective actions open at this vantage.** **`CA-06-01`** addresses the
missed restore test, with the root cause recorded as the assurance calendar naming both CAL-09 and CAL-10 in
the same month and nobody owning the question of whether one discharged the other. **`CA-06-02`** addresses
`CNB-C-108`'s service level, and it asks a question rather than proposing a fix: the control's
two-business-day clearance and its weekly Tuesday review cannot both be right, and 06.09 §3 sets out the
arithmetic. **`CA-06-03`**, owner Junia Okonkwo, addresses the emergency change path and the departure from
SR-11 that §5.3 sets out. **`CA-06-04`**, owner Wes Delacroix, addresses the occurrence `CNB-C-136` did not
have. `D-06-02` is the fifth and is not one of them: it was addressed by amending the control statement
itself under **DEC-609**, which is a **correction** rather than a corrective action, and the deviation log
shows the clause 10.2 test that produces that answer.

**`D-06-05` was not back-dated**, and the reasoning is `D-06-01`'s exactly. The August occurrence of
`CNB-C-136` is gone; re-deriving the ICT continuity requirements in October and dating the record August
would close a gap in the record and nothing else. The population is one and it is empty, which on an annual
control is the worst arithmetic in the library — and it is worse still here, because `CNB-C-136` is the sole
carrier of A.5.30's planning limb and the Statement of Applicability reads `Implemented`. 06.10 §3 sets that
out.

## 7. What the record does not say

**Half the window is ahead.** Every figure above describes 92 days of a 184-day observation window. The
periodic controls have run between one and three of their occurrences; `CNB-C-098` has three still to come,
`CNB-C-112` has one, `CNB-C-113` and `CNB-C-103` have none, `CNB-C-073` and `CNB-C-076` have their only one
still to come, and the annual controls have between zero and one — `CNB-C-136`'s is the one that will always
be zero. The service auditor samples the whole six months and will compute rates over populations that do
not exist yet. **This chapter states occurrences to date and no rate over the window**, and that is a deliberate
refusal rather than an omission.

**The zeros are the weakest numbers here, as they were in July.** Zero security incidents in the quarter is
either a quarter in which nothing happened or a quarter in which something happened and was not detected,
and this phase cannot distinguish them from the inside — the same limit 05.13 recorded. It has a further
consequence worth naming: **SC-02's 48-hour customer notification path produced no evidence in Q3**, because
there was nothing to notify. That is a fact and not a comfort, and 06.06 records why it means R-22 cannot
move. Zero Critical or High vulnerabilities open past target at each month end is three photographs, not a
statement about the ninety-two days between them.

**And the five deviations plus the commitment failure are six findings and none of them is a conclusion
about the opinion.** Test deviations are disclosed in Section IV with the service auditor's evaluation
alongside them, and the auditor modifies the opinion only where deviations mean a criterion was not met.
That evaluation has not been performed. **Phase 06 does not offer one, does not anticipate one, and would be
wrong to.** It is ADR-0015's rule — no forecast until the harness has proved it reachable — applied to an
opinion instead of a register, and the reason it is applied here is that a programme willing to guess at its
own result is a programme whose other numbers are worth less.
