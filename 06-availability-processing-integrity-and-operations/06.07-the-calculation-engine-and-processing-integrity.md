## 1. The category where the product is

Processing Integrity has never been examined at CloudNimbus. The 2025 Type I covered Security alone, so
everything in this chapter and the two that follow it describes controls whose operating effectiveness no
service auditor has tested and whose design no service auditor has opined on. That is the starting
position, and it is worth naming before any figure is given.

It matters more here than the same sentence would matter at most service organisations. In a typical SaaS
examination Processing Integrity is a category added to satisfy a procurement questionnaire, and the
controls behind it describe a queue that does not lose messages. **At CloudNimbus the calculation engine
derives overtime, differential, accrual balances and reimbursement amounts for roughly 1.24 million end
users, and its output leaves the platform as a file a payroll provider pays from.** A payroll provider does
not sanity-check an amount. It pays it. The distance between a defect in this engine and money in a named
individual's bank account is one file and one submission.

So the commitment has to be read exactly. **SC-07 commits to calculation output that is complete, accurate
and reproducible *against the configured rules*.** The qualification is not a hedge added by counsel. It is
the true statement of what CloudNimbus can undertake: the engine applies the rules the employer configured,
and CloudNimbus is not entitled to second-guess an employer's pay policy. §8 returns to what that boundary
costs. 06.08 takes the inputs, 06.09 takes the outputs and the export, and this chapter takes the engine
itself, its specification and its stored results.

## 2. How the five criteria decompose over one engine

PI1.1 to PI1.5 are five criteria, not five restatements of one. They divide a processing chain by *stage*,
and the division is only useful if the controls are allocated by stage too rather than by the convenience
of the person writing the description. Phase 04's eleven Processing Integrity rows were written that way,
and the table below is the map from the criteria to them and to the chapters that report their Q3 operation.

| Criterion | Where it bites in this engine | Controls | Reported in |
|---|---|---|---|
| PI1.1 — definitions and specifications for processing | The rule catalogue: whether every rule type a tenant can activate has a current written definition, a worked example and a version | `CNB-C-103`, `CNB-C-104` | This chapter §4; 06.08 |
| PI1.2 — inputs complete, accurate, authorised | Clock events, employer feeds and rule-set activations, at the point of submission | `CNB-C-104`, `CNB-C-105`, `CNB-C-106`, `CNB-C-111` | 06.08 |
| PI1.3 — processing complete, accurate, timely, authorised | The nightly run, its reconciliation record, the exception queue and re-execution | `CNB-C-107`, `CNB-C-108`, `CNB-C-109` | This chapter §5; 06.09 |
| PI1.4 — output complete, accurate, distributed | The export file, its band check and its delivery to the payroll provider endpoint | `CNB-C-107`, `CNB-C-110`, `CNB-C-111` | 06.09 |
| PI1.5 — output stored completely and accurately | The stored result rows, the run ledger they reconcile to, and the constraint set that holds them | `CNB-C-112`, `CNB-C-113` | This chapter §6 and §7 |

Two features of that allocation are deliberate and are the reason it is set out before the figures.

**No control appears against a criterion merely because it is nearby.** `CNB-C-109` pins a rule version and
an immutable input snapshot to every run record, which makes a completed run re-executable; that serves
PI1.3, and it does not serve PI1.5, because storing an output faithfully and being able to recompute it are
different properties and a library that cited the same row against both would be double-counting one
mechanism as two.

**PI1.5 is served by two detective controls and no preventive one**, which 04.06 states and defends: the
uniqueness and referential constraints on the result tables are configuration rather than an operating
occurrence, and a constraint does not run on a date a sampler can select. §6 is where that design choice
meets a quarter in which something actually went missing, and it is the most interesting paragraph in this
chapter for that reason.

## 3. Ninety-two nights

The calculation engine ran a full nightly cycle on every night of the quarter: **31 in July, 31 in August
and 30 in September — 92 cycles, all completed.** `CNB-C-107` wrote a reconciliation record on all 92,
comparing input hours and rule version against output amounts for every tenant in the run.

A cycle is not a single computation. It is a per-tenant sequence — resolve the tenant's active rule
version, assemble the period's validated time records and employer-supplied employment data, derive the
amounts, write the results, write the reconciliation record, and release or hold the export. The unit a
service auditor samples is the cycle and its reconciliation record; the unit an engineer debugs is the
tenant within it. Both are retained, and 04.12's evidence class here is the reconciliation record rather
than the run log, because a run log records that something happened and a reconciliation record records
what it was compared against.

**Ninety-two occurrences in a quarter is the most comfortable population in this phase**, and the contrast
with the rest of the family is the point. `CNB-C-112` operates quarterly and has two occurrences in the
whole six-month window; `CNB-C-113` and `CNB-C-103` operate semi-annually and have one each. The daily
controls can absorb a slip arithmetically; the periodic ones cannot, and 04.11's table said so in advance.
**Three of this chapter's five controls are periodic**, and nothing in the clean figures for the daily ones
says anything about them.

## 4. The rule catalogue — `CNB-C-103`

`CNB-C-103` requires every rule type in the calculation catalogue to carry a written definition, a worked
example and a version identifier, and requires the catalogue to be re-approved semi-annually with any rule
type lacking a current definition withdrawn from tenant configuration. The re-approval was performed on
**2026-08-06**. **Two rule types were withdrawn** for lacking a current definition. **No tenant had either
of them active.**

That is the cheapest possible outcome and the least informative one, and it should be read as such rather
than as a result. A withdrawal that affects no tenant costs nothing, produces no customer conversation and
closes no exposure that was live.

What the occurrence does establish is that the catalogue was read against the configuration surface, which
is the part organisations skip. **PI1.1 is a specification criterion, not a code criterion.** A rule type
available for a tenant to activate, with no current written definition behind it, is a defect in the
specification of processing whether or not the code implementing it is correct — because there is then no
document against which the code's correctness could be judged. `CNB-C-103` cites no Annex A control, and
04.06 explains why: no Annex A control asks whether a catalogue of derivations is complete and its
definitions current. Citing the nearest plausible annex number would have been the mapping error 04.03
warns about.

The honest limit on the occurrence is its population. The control operates twice a year, the window holds
one occurrence, and a single clean semi-annual occurrence establishes that the control operated once.

## 5. Reproducibility — `CNB-C-109`, and the caveat that makes it honest

`CNB-C-109` pins the rule version and an immutable input snapshot identifier to every run record, so that a
completed run can be re-executed on demand and its output compared line by line with the original. In Q3
there were **12 on-demand re-executions — 9 investigating a reconciliation exception and 3 at a customer's
request. All 12 reproduced the original output line for line.**

That figure is worth having and is worth being precise about, because it is the figure most likely to be
misread. **Reproducing an output proves the engine is deterministic. It does not prove the output was
right.** A re-execution takes the same pinned inputs and the same pinned rule version and runs them through
the same code; if it produced a different answer, something would be badly wrong — a non-deterministic
ordering, an unpinned dependency, a rule version resolved at run time rather than recorded. Twelve of
twelve matching means none of those things is happening. It means nothing whatever about whether the amount
was the amount the worker should have been paid.

Determinism is what **SR-06** asks for — calculation runs reproducible from inputs and rule version, with a
reconciliation record retained — and it is a genuine requirement rather than a weak one, because the
alternative is an engine whose output cannot be investigated after the fact. **A number nobody can
reproduce is a number nobody can defend**, and the customer conversation that follows a disputed payslip is
unwinnable without it. Three of the twelve re-executions were exactly that conversation.

But **SC-07's accuracy limb is met against the configured rules**, and the configuration is the customer's
under **CUEC-04**. The engine can reconcile perfectly against a rule the employer configured wrongly, and
every CloudNimbus control in this family will pass. That is not a gap in the control set; it is the
boundary of what a processing engine can be asked to certify about somebody else's pay policy, and it is
disclosed rather than absorbed. 06.09 §6 takes it the rest of the way, to CUEC-05 and the outreach that
answered Phase 02's question about what happens when a user entity does not perform one.

## 6. Stored output — `CNB-C-112`, and the incident showing up here

`CNB-C-112` compares stored calculation results against the run ledger for every tenant each quarter, and
reports orphaned results, duplicate period rows and runs with no stored output to the data platform owner
for correction. The Q3 occurrence was performed on **2026-09-21** by Devon Ashby.

| Condition tested | Q3 result |
|---|---|
| Orphaned results — a stored result with no run in the ledger | 0 |
| Duplicate period rows — the same tenant, worker and pay period stored twice | 0 |
| Runs with no stored output | **2** |

**Both of the two fell inside the 71 minutes of `INC-2026-031` on 2026-09-08.** Each was an off-cycle
recalculation begun before 14:22, and each attempted to write its result rows into the window in which the
writer instance of the `core-us-east` cluster was refusing writes. The run existed; the output did not.
**Both were re-executed and their results stored on 2026-09-22**, the day after the finding, and the stored
results reconcile to the ledger. **No export had been issued from either run**, so no file derived from an
incomplete result reached a payroll provider. The finding is recorded against `INC-2026-031`, which is where
06.06 §4.1 takes it, because a disclosure drafted on 2026-09-09 saying no customer data was lost was drafted
before this control had run.

**This is an availability incident appearing in the processing integrity record, and the chapter says so
rather than filing it next door.** The categories are a way of organising an examination. They are not a
description of how a failure travels through a system. A connection pool holding a stale handle to a
demoted database instance is an availability defect by every reasonable classification, and its
consequences landed on stored output completeness, which is PI1.5. A reader who took the five categories as
five compartments would have looked for the whole of the 8 September incident in 06.05 and found ninety per
cent of it.

There is a second thing in the two runs and it is less comfortable. **The control that found them operates
quarterly**, and it found them thirteen days after they went missing. Had the incident fallen two weeks
later they would have been discovered in December. **PI1.5's detection latency is a quarter, and the
thirteen days were the calendar's doing rather than the design's.** 04.06's argument that a preventive
control here would have been a row that cannot be sampled still stands; what does not stand is any
suggestion that two detective controls at quarterly and semi-annual cadence give PI1.5 a short feedback
loop.

## 7. Data model conformance — `CNB-C-113`

`CNB-C-113` compares the constraint set on the result tables in all three regions — uniqueness on tenant,
worker and pay period, and the referential constraints to the run ledger — against the reference model
semi-annually, correcting any divergence by migration. It was performed on **2026-07-09** and found **no
divergence across `us-east-1`, `us-west-2` and `eu-central-1`**. The next occurrence falls due in
**2027-01**, outside the observation window.

Two notes. The check covers `eu-central-1` on the same terms as the other two regions, and that is not
symmetry for its own sake: `eu-central-1` recovers intra-region across availability zones only, under
obligation **O8** and commitment **SC-04**, so it is the region where a divergent constraint set would be
hardest to correct under pressure. And the population here is again one.

## 8. SC-07's boundary, stated once and not softened

**CloudNimbus's controls establish that the engine applies the configured rules faithfully, records what it
applied, can re-apply it, and stores what it produced.** They do not establish, and are not designed to
establish, that the configured rules express what the employer intended. That defect class — a rule
configured wrongly and applied perfectly — passes `CNB-C-104`'s schema validation because it is
schema-valid, passes `CNB-C-107`'s reconciliation because the reconciliation compares the output to the
inputs and the rule version actually used, and passes `CNB-C-109`'s re-execution because re-execution
reproduces it exactly. **Every control in this chapter is capable of returning a clean result on a wrong
paycheque**, and the reason is not that the controls are weak. It is that they answer a different question
from the one the employee asks.

Where that leaves the necessity is where 02.11 put it: **CUEC-04**, the employer's approval of its own pay
rules before activation, is the user-entity control PI1.2 needs on an input CloudNimbus does not author.
CUEC-05 is the late detective check at the point of submission and belongs to 06.09. Neither is a control
CloudNimbus operates, evidences or is tested on, and neither makes the controls above stronger or weaker.

## 9. What this chapter does not claim

Ninety-two nightly cycles is a large number about a quarter; **the population the service auditor samples
is the whole six months**, and the periodic controls in this family have between one and two occurrences to
date. One deviation is recorded against this family in Q3 and it belongs to 06.09 — `D-06-03`. Nothing in
this phase offers a view on how the service auditor will evaluate it. That evaluation belongs to the
examination, the disclosure belongs to Section IV, and the discipline is the one ADR-0015 imposed on
register forecasts: **no conclusion is published until it has been earned.**
