## 1. A chapter about a control that did not run, and two that are not yet due

Every other chapter in this phase reports something that happened. This one has to report something that
did not, and do it without dressing the absence up as a plan — and it has to be exact about which absences
are deficiencies and which are simply dates in the future, because the two look identical in a status
table.

**Two annual controls in this family have not operated, and neither is late.** `CNB-C-088` — the annual
review and reissue of the business continuity plan, which states the four-hour recovery time and
fifteen-minute recovery point objectives, names the recovery roles, and is reissued by the Director of
Infrastructure &amp; SRE — falls due in **2026-11**, ahead of the November exercise. `CNB-C-101` — the annual
business continuity exercise under **CAL-16**, which runs a scenario against the continuity plan with the
crisis team and logs every action arising with an owner and a due date — falls due in **2026-11** too.
`04.11` §4.1 fixes both dates and 01.11 has placed CAL-16 in November since the assurance calendar was
written in January. **Neither has slipped. Both are not yet due**, and a chapter that framed them as
deficiencies would be borrowing a finding it has not earned.

**The deficiency in this family is a third control, and it is real.** `CNB-C-136` — the annual re-derivation
of ICT continuity requirements from the business impact analysis — was re-scheduled by `04.11` §4.2 into
**2026-08**, inside the quarter, and **it was not performed**. That is `D-06-05`, and §3 sets out why it
matters more than its size suggests.

That is the position, and the rest of this chapter is about what it means for the one Annex A control this
family exists to answer.

## 2. A.5.30, and which rows in the library actually carry it

**A.5.30 ICT readiness for business continuity** is one of the eleven controls new in the 2022 revision and
the one this programme has had the most trouble evidencing. It requires ICT readiness to be **planned,
implemented, maintained and tested** against continuity objectives derived from a business impact analysis.
Four limbs.

03.12 §5.1 named A.5.30 in advance as "the contestable one" and "the member of the nine most open to
challenge" among the Annex A controls with no trust services criterion behind them. 04.07 then recorded a
correction the control change log had already made: `CNB-C-136` carried the planning limb and nothing
carried the testing limb, so the library was asserting implementation of a control whose testing
requirement no row implemented. `CNB-C-100` — the annual disaster recovery exercise — now cites **A.5.29
and A.5.30** together, which is where the testing limb went.

**On the library as published, exactly two rows cite A.5.30**: `CNB-C-136` and `CNB-C-100`. That has to be
said before anything else here, because the obvious neighbours do not carry it and the temptation to read
them as though they did is strong. `CNB-C-088` and `CNB-C-101` cite **A.5.29**, not A.5.30 — they are information
security during disruption, not ICT readiness. `CNB-C-097` and `CNB-C-099` cite **A.8.13** and **A.8.14** —
backup and redundancy. An Annex A control is answered by the rows that cite it, and a chapter that borrows
a nearby row to fill a limb has written a mapping the library does not contain.

| Limb of A.5.30 | Row in the library citing A.5.30 | Position at 2026-09-30 |
|---|---|---|
| **Planned** | **`CNB-C-136`** — ICT continuity requirements expressed per service tier as recovery time and recovery point objectives derived from the business impact analysis, re-derived annually. The **sole carrier** of this limb | **The re-scheduled 2026-08 occurrence was not performed** — `D-06-05`. The published objectives stand from the 2026-05 derivation: SR-10, four hours and fifteen minutes |
| **Implemented** | **None.** The capability is real and sits under `CNB-C-099` (A.8.14) and `CNB-C-097` (A.8.13), neither of which cites A.5.30 | The capability operated — `CNB-C-097` ran 92 times in the quarter — but the Annex A attribution for this limb rests on A.8.13 and A.8.14 rather than on A.5.30, so **on this table's own rule the limb is unattributed**. Recorded as **`IS-24`** and referred — see §3.2 |
| **Maintained** | **None. No row in the library cites A.5.30 for this limb** | Recorded as **`IS-24`** and referred to the clause 9.2 internal audit — see §3.2 |
| **Tested** | **`CNB-C-100`**, the CAL-10 exercise of 2026-08-19 | **Performed.** `us-east-1` → `us-west-2`, RTO 2h51m against 4 hours, RPO 4m12s against 15 minutes |

## 3. A.5.30 reads `Implemented`, and when that became true

**A.5.30 reads `Implemented` on the current issue of the Statement of Applicability**, and this chapter's
finding is not about the status field's value. It is about its date.

03.09 recorded A.5.30 as **`Planned`** at SoA v1.0. Phase 04's RAID entry **IS-13** records that **27 of the
29 Partial or Planned entries closed at the 2026-06-15 reissue, leaving only A.5.28 and A.8.32 Partial**.
A.5.30 was one of the 27. Any statement that it "remains `Partial`" is a statement about a superseded issue.

> **A.5.30 was moved to `Implemented` at the 2026-06-15 reissue on the strength of `CNB-C-100` having been
> given A.5.30 alongside A.5.29 — before `CNB-C-100` had operated.** CAL-10 was still two months away. The
> status ran ahead of the evidence, which is the failure mode clause 7.5 exists to prevent, and it is the
> same failure **R-30** describes: documented information that becomes out of date and is relied on as
> current. At **2026-08-19** the testing limb finally acquired the evidence the status had been asserting
> since June. **The status is now supportable where it was not, and nothing anybody did in September made
> it so.**

That is a better finding than the one it replaces and a less comfortable one. A status field corrected by
an event is a status field that was, for sixty-five days, a claim rather than a record — and the only
reason it is not still a claim is that an exercise scheduled in January happened to fall inside the gap.
05.13 carried two Statement of Applicability entries, `A.7.10` and `A.7.4`, that were `Partial` because a
document had not caught up with an operating position that had already moved: **stale in the safe
direction.** A.5.30 between June and August was stale in the **unsafe** direction, which is the difference
between a document to be corrected and a claim to be defended.

### 3.1 `CNB-C-136` did not operate in the one month the window gave it

`04.11` §4.2 re-scheduled `CNB-C-136` — the annual re-derivation of ICT continuity requirements from the
business impact analysis, and **the sole carrier of A.5.30's planning limb** — from its natural 2027-05
anniversary to **2026-08**, so that it would have a population inside the observation window. **It was not
performed.**

> **`D-06-05` · `CNB-C-136` · The re-scheduled 2026-08 occurrence of the annual ICT continuity re-derivation
> was not performed · 1 of 1 occurrence in the window.**

Owner **Wes Delacroix**. The treatment is `D-06-01`'s exactly: recorded, **not re-performed and
back-dated**, and carried with a clause 10.2 corrective action, **`CA-06-04`**. A re-derivation carried out
in October and dated August would close a gap in the record and change nothing about the period.

**And the sting is the arithmetic of the two facts sitting together. A.5.30 reads `Implemented`, and the
only row in the library carrying its planning limb did not operate in the one month the window gave it.**
The window's population for `CNB-C-136` is one, and it is empty. That is not a thin sample; it is no
evidence about the planning limb inside the period at all, and the status field says otherwise.

### 3.2 Two of A.5.30's four limbs have no citing row — `IS-24`

A.5.30 requires ICT readiness to be planned, implemented, maintained **and** tested. `CNB-C-136` plans it
and `CNB-C-100` tests it. **The other two limbs have no row in the library citing A.5.30 at all**, and
`IS-24` refers both.

**The maintenance limb has nothing behind it in any sense.** `CNB-C-088` looks like the answer and is not:
it reviews and reissues the business continuity plan, it cites A.5.29, and maintaining a plan is not
maintaining the ICT readiness the plan assumes.

**The implementation limb is the subtler of the two and was under-stated when `IS-24` was first raised.**
The capability is real — `CNB-C-097` and `CNB-C-099` supply it and operated throughout the quarter — but
they cite **A.8.13** and **A.8.14**, not A.5.30. §2 states the rule this chapter is held to: *an Annex A
control is answered by the rows that cite it, and a chapter that borrows a nearby row to fill a limb has
written a mapping the library does not contain.* Applied to A.5.30's own table, that rule leaves **two of
four limbs unattributed in the library**, not one, and an issue that referred only the maintenance limb was
applying the rule to somebody else and not to itself.

This is recorded as **`IS-24`**, widened to both limbs, **referred to the clause 9.2 internal audit** and
carried as a candidate for the next issue of the Statement of Applicability. **Phase 04 is not amended for
it**, and the referral is the point: IS-20 was referred rather than corrected on exactly this reasoning, and
a library that closes a gap the week it is found cannot demonstrate that the gap was ever there. What the
audit made of it belongs to Phase 08.

The consequence is carried openly: **A.5.30 is the weakest `Implemented` entry in the Statement of
Applicability at this vantage.** Two of its four limbs are carried by rows that cite it; **the other two are
carried by nothing that cites it** — one by rows citing something else, one by nothing at all — and the row
carrying the planning limb has an empty population inside the window.

## 4. A.5.29 — the control that was evidenced twice

**A.5.29 information security during disruption** asks whether information security is maintained during
disruption, and the quarter produced two disruptions to answer it against: the planned one on 2026-08-19
and the unplanned one on 2026-09-08.

**No control was suspended during either.** That is the whole of the evidence and it is worth more than it
looks, because **a continuity event is the commonest occasion on which a control is quietly turned off**.
The exercise ran in `us-west-2` with the same access controls, the same change gates and the same log
shipping in force as in `us-east-1`. During the incident the deployment pipeline remained the only route to
production under `CNB-C-080`, the rolling restart of the eleven services on the core write path went
through it, and the logs continued to ship to the region-partitioned `cnb-security` archive under
`CNB-C-065` throughout the seventy-one minutes.

The single elevation is the sharpest piece of that evidence. **Break-glass was used once during the
incident, at 14:47, to inspect connection state on the production cluster** — twenty-five minutes into an
outage, with the cause not yet identified, which is the moment at which an engineer is most likely to reach
around a control rather than through it. It went through the ordinary just-in-time path under `CNB-C-034`,
it alerted, and it was **reviewed inside 48 hours** like the other three break-glass uses in the quarter.
Four uses in ninety-two days, all reviewed: 2026-07-14, 2026-08-19 during the exercise, 2026-09-08 during
the incident, and 2026-09-23 during the September restore test. **Three of the four fall on the three days
in the quarter when something unusual was happening**, which is what a break-glass population should look
like.

## 5. A.5.27 — learning from information security incidents

**A.5.27 learning from information security incidents** is evidenced by the post-incident review held on
**2026-09-11** and the five actions it produced, `ACT-06-01` to `ACT-06-05`. `CNB-C-102` and `CNB-C-075`
both cite A.5.27, and 06.11 §4 records the divergence between their two review windows rather than
resolving it silently.

What makes the evidence a learning record rather than a minute is that **two of the five actions changed
something other than the thing that broke**. `ACT-06-01` fixed the mechanism — a write probe in the pool
health check and `max_lifetime` set to 900 seconds. `ACT-06-02` amended a published control statement,
`CNB-C-096`, because the detection control had operated exactly as designed and the design was wrong.
`ACT-06-03` wrote down a definition — availability means a read **and** a write succeeding in the measured
minute — which is not a repair at all but a correction to what the organisation thought it was measuring.
`ACT-06-05` changed the vocabulary on the status page, because "degraded" was displayed and the write path
was not degraded, it was down. Only `ACT-06-04`, the quarterly in-region failover game day from Q4, is a
new activity, and it is scheduled rather than performed.

**An incident review that produces only a code fix has learned about one defect. One that amends a control,
a definition and a customer-facing vocabulary has learned about a class of them.**

## 6. The technological controls this family rests on

The four Annex A technological controls in this area are cited only where Phase 04's library actually maps
them, and the mapping is worth setting out because it is where the continuity claim becomes concrete.

| Annex A control | Where the library cites it | Q3 position |
|---|---|---|
| **A.8.6** capacity management | `CNB-C-094` monthly capacity review, `CNB-C-095` quarterly re-set, `CNB-C-068` synthetic transactions | Three monthly reviews performed; the quarterly re-set on 2026-09-24 held one service open until 2026-09-29 |
| **A.8.13** information backup | `CNB-C-097` nightly snapshots and cross-region replication, `CNB-C-098` monthly restore test | 92 of 92 nightly cycles; restore tests **2 of 3 to date** — the August occurrence was not performed, `D-06-01` |
| **A.8.14** redundancy of information processing facilities | `CNB-C-099` multi-availability-zone placement and region-pinned recovery from the shared Terraform modules | Enforcement continuous; the annual re-approval that is the sampled occurrence falls in **2026-10** under DEC-409 and has not been performed |
| **A.8.16** monitoring activities | `CNB-C-066`, `CNB-C-067`, `CNB-C-068`, `CNB-C-070`, `CNB-C-096`, `CNB-C-127` | Operated throughout; `CNB-C-068` paged two minutes into the 8 September incident, and `CNB-C-096`'s design deficiency is `D-06-02` |

Two rows there carry findings and both are reported in full elsewhere — the missing August restore test in
06.03 and the synthetic probe's read-only design in 06.01 and 06.05. Naming them again here is deliberate.
**A continuity chapter that lists the backup control without the missed restore test would be describing
the design and not the operation**, and the design was never in question.

The A.8.14 row is the one a reader should notice for a different reason. Its enforcement runs continuously —
no production data store or node group is provisioned except from the shared modules — but the **operating
occurrence a sampler selects** is the annual re-approval with non-conforming resources rebuilt, and 04.11
§4.5 named this shape and required the row to say which of the two the cadence refers to. That occurrence
falls in October. At this vantage the window holds none of it.

## 7. What the region that cannot fail over does to all of this

`eu-central-1` recovers **intra-region across availability zones only**. Obligation **O8** and commitment
**SC-04** hold the personal data of the 41 EU-residency customers at rest in that region, and cross-region
recovery would move it. The August exercise therefore deliberately did not fail `eu-central-1` over, and
CAL-16 will not change that: a business continuity exercise can rehearse the crisis team's response to the
loss of a region it cannot recover, and rehearsing a response is not a recovery capability.

**R-20 — loss of `eu-central-1`, for which obligation O8 forbids cross-region recovery — sits at 2 × 5 = 10
and cannot move.** Phase 03 named it, with R-19, as immovable: the consequence has not changed and the next
likelihood step is 1, which the scale reserves for the not-reasonably-foreseeable. The best exercise result
in the programme's history did not move R-19 and could not have moved R-20, because the exercise proves the
recovery of two regions and cannot prove the recovery of the third. **That is a residual accepted on a
contractual constraint, restated at every vantage, and it is not going to be exercised away.**

## 8. What November has to produce, and what it cannot

CAL-16 is one exercise. `CNB-C-101` and `CNB-C-088` are both annual, so each has a population of **at most
one** inside the six-month window, and 04.11's arithmetic applies without softening: **a single late
occurrence on an annual control is a deviation rate of one hundred per cent, and a single occurrence that
happens on time is a sample of one.**

**What November cannot produce is anything about A.5.30.** On the library as published, `CNB-C-101` cites
**A.5.29** and CAL-16 does not touch A.5.30 at all. A clean exercise in November evidences information
security during disruption, gives the crisis team a rehearsal and gives `CNB-C-088` a reissued plan to be
exercised against — and it moves no limb of ICT readiness, because no row it operates cites the control. A
chapter that promised otherwise would be offering a scheduled event as coverage for a mapping the library
does not contain, which is the same error §2 has just corrected in the opposite direction.

**What would move A.5.30 is `CNB-C-136` operating and the implementation and maintenance limbs acquiring
citing rows.** The first is `CA-06-04`; the second is `IS-24` and belongs to the next issue of the Statement
of Applicability. Neither
is a November outcome, and this phase declines to describe either in advance as though it were, or to
forecast what any of it will find.
