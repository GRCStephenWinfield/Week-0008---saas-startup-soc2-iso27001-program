## 1. What 99.9% costs, in minutes

**SC-01 commits CloudNimbus to 99.9% monthly availability of the platform, excluding maintenance windows
announced at least fourteen days in advance.** That last clause was missing from the commitment as `02.12`
published it, and §1.1 sets out what was done about it. It is one of the four principal
service commitments sourced from the customer master services agreement directly rather than from the
obligations register, and it is the one a customer raises first, because customers commit staff to shifts
against this platform and a payroll cut-off missed is a payroll run missed.

The commitment is easier to hold and harder to meet than the number suggests, and the reason is that a
percentage is a poor unit for a promise. Stated as a percentage, 99.9% invites a reader to think of a
rounding. Stated in minutes it is an operating constraint that fits on one line:

| Month length | Minutes in the month | 0.1% of the month | The allowance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days | 43,200 | 43.20 | **43 minutes 12 seconds** |
| 31 days | 44,640 | 44.64 | **44 minutes 38 seconds** |

**Forty-three minutes is one incident.** It is not a budget that accumulates through a month of small
degradations; it is roughly the length of a single unplanned event handled competently from detection to
restoration. An organisation that commits to 99.9% monthly has committed to having **at most one bad
three-quarters of an hour per calendar month**, and the whole of the availability control set exists to make
that arithmetic survivable rather than lucky.

Two further properties of the wording matter and are stated here because they decide how the record in §3 is
read. **The commitment is monthly, so it does not average.** A quarter is not the unit; three months are
three separate tests, and passing two of them is not passing. And **the month resets**, which means an
allowance exhausted on the eighth day is exhausted for the remaining twenty-two, with no mechanism by which
a good second half of the month recovers it.

### 1.1 The exclusion `02.12` did not state, and its correction

The master services agreement's availability commitment **excludes maintenance windows announced at least
fourteen days in advance**, and `02.12` published SC-01 as "99.9% monthly availability of the platform"
without saying so. That is not a drafting nicety. **A 99.9% figure measured with an undisclosed exclusion is
not the figure the customer reads**, and a description of the system that carries the commitment without the
exclusion is incomplete under **DC2**. `02.12` has been corrected at source to read "**99.9% monthly
availability of the platform, excluding maintenance windows announced at least fourteen days in advance**"
and **Phase 02 has been re-issued**, under **DEC-613** of 2026-09-24, Marisol Vega.

The exclusion was engaged **twice** in this quarter, and stating it without stating the minutes it removes
would be half a disclosure.

**August.** The CAL-10 disaster recovery exercise of **2026-08-19** ran inside an announced maintenance
window of **06:00 to 10:00 UTC — four hours — notified to every customer on 2026-08-05**, fourteen days
ahead. **A window is a notified interval, not an achieved duration.** The exercise itself ran **06:00 to
08:51, 171 minutes**, finishing sixty-nine minutes inside its own window. **Customer-visible unavailability
during the exercise was 23 minutes**, in two cutover intervals: **14 minutes** at the failover to
`us-west-2`, of which DR-F1's four are the DNS overrun, and **9 minutes** at the read-path re-point on
failback into the same window. **The platform served production traffic from `us-west-2` for the other 148
minutes**, which is what a successful regional failover looks like. **Twenty-three minutes — not 171 — are
excluded from the August measurement.** 06.04 and GOV-21 carry the exercise itself.

**July.** `TP-20`, the availability architecture change for **R-18**, required a customer-notified
maintenance window under `03.06`. It was **delivered 2026-07-15 in a window notified 2026-07-01**, fourteen
days ahead, and consumed **4 customer-visible minutes**, all excluded.

EC-09's sampling unit carries a field for minutes excluded and it is now populated across the quarter:
**July 4 · August 23 · September 0**, giving measured denominators of **44,636**, **44,617** and **43,200**.
**An exclusion the programme relies on twice in three months is not an edge case**, and that makes the case
for correcting `02.12` stronger rather than weaker.

> **Twenty-three minutes did not change August's result, and four did not change July's.** The omission in
> `02.12` would have changed a reader's understanding of every month in the report, which is why it was
> corrected over twenty-seven minutes rather than left until a month when it mattered. **A carve-out is
> worth disclosing in the quarter it is trivial, because the quarter it is not trivial is the quarter
> nobody will believe the disclosure.**

**SR-10 is the system requirement behind SC-01**: an availability architecture supporting a **4-hour
recovery time objective and a 15-minute recovery point objective**. The distinction Phase 02 drew between
commitments and requirements is doing real work here. SC-01 is what CloudNimbus promised a customer; SR-10
is what the system has to be able to do for that promise to be keepable, and it is SR-10 rather than SC-01
that the architecture below was designed against.

## 2. The architecture the commitment rests on

CloudNimbus runs on AWS only, in three regions, with a deliberately small number of moving parts.

**`us-east-1` is primary.** The platform runs there across three availability zones: EKS node groups spread
across zones, Aurora PostgreSQL clusters with a writer in one zone and readers in the others, and object
storage that is regionally durable by construction. Multi-availability-zone placement is not a
configuration choice made per resource; `CNB-C-099` requires production data stores and node groups to be
provisioned **only from the shared Terraform modules that set multi-availability-zone placement and
region-pinned recovery**, with the module set re-approved annually and every non-conforming resource rebuilt
before sign-off. The enforcement point is the module rather than the reviewer, which is the same
architectural argument 05.12 made about the tenant predicate: a property guaranteed by the way resources can
be created is stronger than a property everyone remembers to set.

**`us-west-2` is the disaster recovery region** and is the target of the annual CAL-10 exercise under
`CNB-C-100`. It holds the standing Aurora global-database replica and the cross-region replicas of the
object stores that `CNB-C-097` maintains nightly.

**`eu-central-1` serves the 41 customers who require EU data residency, and it is not a disaster recovery
target for anything.** Obligation **O8** and commitment **SC-04** put the personal data of those customers at
rest in `eu-central-1` and keep it there; recovery is therefore **intra-region across availability zones
only**. This is not an oversight in the recovery design, it is the recovery design: a cross-region failover
that moved EU personal data to Oregon would restore availability by breaking a residency commitment, and
restoring a service by breaching a contract is not recovery. Phase 02 recorded the consequence as an
accepted residual and Phase 03 recorded it as **R-20** — the loss of `eu-central-1`, at 2 × 5 = 10, one of
the four retained risks and one of the two the register names as immovable.

### 2.1 The reader and the writer are two different promises

Aurora presents two endpoints. The **writer endpoint** resolves to the single instance accepting writes; the
**reader endpoint** load-balances across the replicas. In normal operation the distinction is an efficiency
— reporting and read-heavy paths go to the readers and leave the writer for the write path. In a failure it
is the whole story.

An Aurora failover promotes a replica and demotes the former writer. The data layer's own recovery is
measured in tens of seconds and is well inside anything SR-10 contemplates. What the failover does **not**
do is reach up into the application tier and tell it that the instance it is holding a connection to is no
longer the one that accepts writes. That gap between the data layer's recovery and the application's
awareness of it is where this quarter's Severity-1 incident lived, and 06.05 sets it out in full. It is
named here, in the architecture chapter, because it is an architectural property and not an operational
accident: **a platform whose read path and write path are served by different endpoints can be half
available, and a monitoring design that does not know that will not notice.**

## 3. The three months, region by region

**Availability is measured and reported per region.** EC-09's sampling unit is one calendar month **for one
region**, and that is not a presentational preference. The failure of 8 September took the **writer instance
of the `core-us-east` Aurora cluster in `us-east-1`** and nothing else; a platform-wide figure would have
averaged the region that failed against the region that did not.

| Month | Days | Region | Minutes excluded | Downtime | Availability | Allowance at 99.9% | Met |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| July 2026 | 31 | `us-east-1` | **4** | 9 minutes | **99.98%** | 44.64 minutes | Yes |
| July 2026 | 31 | `eu-central-1` | 0 | 0 minutes | **100%** | 44.64 minutes | Yes |
| August 2026 | 31 | `us-east-1` | **23** | 13 minutes | **99.97%** | 44.64 minutes | Yes |
| August 2026 | 31 | `eu-central-1` | 0 | 0 minutes | **100%** | 44.64 minutes | Yes |
| September 2026 | 30 | `us-east-1` | 0 | **71 minutes** | **99.84%** | **43.20 minutes** | **No** |
| September 2026 | 30 | `eu-central-1` | 0 | 0 minutes | **100%** | 43.20 minutes | Yes |

`us-west-2` carries no production traffic and is not measured against SC-01. It is the standing recovery
region, and the August exercise is the only occasion in the quarter on which customers were served from it.

**SC-01 was met for 41 customers and missed for 599.** The 41 EU-residency customers are served from
`core-eu-central` in `eu-central-1` and were untouched on 8 September; the 599 served from `us-east-1` were
not. A blended platform number would have spread seventy-one minutes across a population that includes 41
customers who lost nothing, and it would have done so in the direction that flatters — **the blended figure
would have concealed the missed commitment rather than disclosed it.** Reporting per region is what allows
this document to say who the commitment was missed for, and saying who is the disclosure.

### 3.1 Where July's and August's minutes come from

The measurement instrument that was blind to a write-path failure in September was the same instrument that
produced July's and August's figures, and a quarter cannot rest half on one basis and half on another. Under
`ACT-06-03`'s definition the **error-rate record for all three months was re-processed on 2026-09-24**, so
that every month in the quarter is derived the same way. The re-processing **raised July from the 4 minutes
the read-only probe had recorded to 9, and August from 6 to 13**. Say that plainly: **the old instrument
understated two months as well as missing one**, and the quarter's comparability was bought by re-deriving
all three months rather than only the one that mattered.

| Date | Region | Minutes | Event | POL-13 disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-09 | `us-east-1` | 4 | Read-path latency on the reporting service | **Severity-3** |
| 2026-07-22 | `us-east-1` | 5 | Read-path latency on the reporting service | **Severity-3** |
| 2026-08-06 | `us-east-1` | 13 | Partial failure of the notification service | **Severity-3** |

**The three rows are counted minutes and the excluded minutes are not among them.** July's excluded 4 belong
to the **TP-20 delivery window of 2026-07-15**, notified 2026-07-01, and August's excluded 23 to the CAL-10
window of 2026-08-19; neither appears above, because the table records unavailability that counts against
the commitment rather than unavailability the commitment carves out.

All three were dispositioned **Severity-3 under POL-13 and are therefore not incidents**, which is why
July's row in GOV-20 reads 0 while July's downtime here reads 9 minutes. That is coherent rather than
convenient: **SC-01 counts minutes and POL-13 classifies events, and the two thresholds are set for
different purposes** — one measures what a customer could not do, the other decides what has to be
commanded, reviewed and reported. Neither month is a commitment failure. Nine minutes and thirteen minutes
sit well inside an allowance of 44.64.

Three things follow from the September row, and none of them may be softened.

### 3.2 The mean is not the test

The three `us-east-1` months average **99.93%**, which is above the commitment. That figure is
arithmetically correct and it is not an answer to anything, because **SC-01 is a monthly commitment**.
There is no quarterly
commitment to average into. A quarter that averages above the line contains a month that did not meet it,
and averaging is precisely the arithmetic by which a missed commitment is talked away — one good month
subsidising a bad one in a calculation the contract does not authorise.

The number is published here anyway, alongside the three months it is derived from, because omitting it
would be its own kind of dishonesty. A reader who wants the quarterly mean can have it. What a reader
cannot have is the mean instead of the months.

### 3.3 The commitment was breached at 15:05 UTC on 2026-09-08

September's allowance was 43.2 minutes, and **cumulative September downtime passed it at 15:05 UTC**, with
twenty-eight minutes of the incident still to run. The 99.84% published at month end is therefore a
confirmation rather than a discovery. **06.05 §4.1 owns this argument and states it in full**, including why
no subsequent operational excellence in September could have changed the answer.

### 3.4 Availability was not measured by the control that exists to measure it

`CNB-C-096` is the availability measurement control and it feeds **EC-09**, and through all seventy-one
minutes of 8 September its probes ran on schedule and reported healthy, **because the probe performs a
read** and the reader endpoint was fine. The 99.84% in the table above was therefore reconstructed from the
error-rate record rather than produced by the control that declares it, and that silence in the control
statement about what a probe must exercise is `D-06-02` — a design deficiency, not an operating failure.
`CNB-C-096` was **amended under DEC-609 on 2026-09-15**, with the write probe deployed on **2026-09-19** as
`ACT-06-02` and **Phase 04 re-issued**; a statement is amended by the decision, not by the deployment, and
the definition sitting underneath it — that availability means a read **and** a write succeeding in the
measured minute — was recorded on 2026-09-11 under **DEC-607** and is **ADR-0027**. **06.05 §5 owns this
argument and states it in full**, including how a second continuous control paged at 14:24 on a platform
this one measured as available for seventy-one minutes.

## 4. What the availability control set is, and what it is not

Nine controls serve the Availability category, `CNB-C-094` to `CNB-C-102`, and the reason there are only
nine is the carve-out. **AWS performs the environmental protections, the facility power and the physical
redundancy** that A1.2 contemplates, and those are disclosed as **CSOC-01**, **CSOC-05** and **CSOC-08**
rather than claimed by CloudNimbus. What remains in CloudNimbus's hands is capacity (`CNB-C-094`,
`CNB-C-095`), measurement (`CNB-C-096`), backup (`CNB-C-097`), restore testing (`CNB-C-098`), recovery
configuration (`CNB-C-099`), recovery testing (`CNB-C-100`, `CNB-C-101`) and incident correction
(`CNB-C-102`) — and every one of those is reported on in this phase.

**A1.2 is served by five of the nine and A1.1 by three**, which is the right shape for a service that is
recovered from more often than it is prevented from failing. This quarter tested that shape from both
directions: `CNB-C-100` produced a clean exercise result in August, and `CNB-C-096` produced a design
deficiency in September, and both of them are evidence about A1.2.

**Nothing in this chapter is a statement that the availability controls operated effectively.** Three months
is half a period. The population the service auditor will sample is six months of monthly, quarterly and
continuous occurrences, and the September figure is a fact about September rather than a forecast about
December.
